# Continuity and the community that remains

**Issues:** #16 (closure of Voedselbos Eindhoven as a learning case) and #7 (what keeps green initiatives alive beyond their volunteers).
**Status:** desk research, public sources only, retrieved 2026-11-26. No stakeholder contact. Evidence labels: **[D]** documented in a retrieved source, **[C]** claimed by a single source, **[I]** inference, **[U]** not established, **[P]** provided by the commissioner (direct account, not public documentation).

---

## 1. What the record shows

Voedselbos Eindhoven occupied c. 0.7 ha at Elburglaan 8 (5651 EJ), "op het randje tussen bewoond gebied en Landgoed de Wielewaal" **[D]** ([Supportpunt portrait](https://www.supportpunt.nl/verhalen/voedselbos-eindhoven/)). The portrait records the shape of it: started by two founders, Lize and Arjan, roughly ten years before the portrait was written; Maartje Meerman-Kreike joined in 2017 and was asked to take over in 2019; around thirty volunteers; no pesticides or fertiliser; workshops, guided walks, nature education and a paid "Voedselbos Experience" for companies and schools; public access on Friday mornings, 10.00–12.30 **[D/C]**.

## 2. What was lost, what persists

**Lost.** The place: a twelve-year-old planted system. A food forest is not transplantable — the press makes the point explicitly, that after years of growth "niet zonder meer ergens anders opnieuw kan worden begonnen waar men was gebleven" **[C]**. Also lost: a public outdoor classroom on a Friday rhythm, and a free, low-threshold entry point in a city whose green offer is increasingly guided or programmed.

**Persists, documented.**
- The coordinator's practice. Natuurlijk Maartje continues as a business — coaching, masterclasses, wild-plant workshops, and still a "Voedselbos Experience" product for companies and schools — now run from an indoor/urban venue ("In de buurt", Philips Nederland) rather than a forest **[D]** ([agenda](https://natuurlijkmaartje.nl/agenda/)). The *practice* outlived the *place*; the place-specific pedagogy did not travel with it.
- An audience and a network: the newsletter, and the invitations the coordinator reports receiving to develop food forests elsewhere **[D/C]**.
- Municipal contact: a wethouder conversation in June 2026 and a municipal wish to "meedenken" about a new location **[C]**.
- Support infrastructure: Supportpunt, the municipal-backed support desk for Eindhoven foundations and associations (founded by the municipality in 2007), built the site's website and remains available **[D]**.

**Persists, status unclear.** The thirty-strong volunteer group: its existence is documented up to 28 Nov 2025; whether it still meets, and in what form, is **[U]**. The plantings presumably remain on private land and are now inaccessible — **[I]**, unverified.

**An artefact lag worth noting.** In Nov 2026 the coordinator's calendar still advertised a year-long training at "Voedselbos Eindhoven … Elburglaan 8" **[D]**. The initiative is dead as a place and a programme, but not administratively: no body to dissolve, no site to decommission, and the listings outlive what they describe.

**What the community says it wants next.** Only two things are public: a new location is being sought, and invitations to build elsewhere have arrived **[D/C]**. There is no public statement of preferences, conditions or red lines, nor of what people want the *community* — as distinct from the practice — to become. **[U]** This is the largest gap for #16, and it cannot be closed without talking to people.

## 3. Drivers: supported and not supported

**Supported by the evidence.** (a) *Tenure.* The driver named by both the coordinator's framing ("kon jarenlang van de grond gebruikmaken") and the press is insecure use of private land **[D/C]**. The commissioner reports the landowner sold the land **[P]**. (b) *Operator concentration.* Programme, public face, commercial products and volunteer coordination all ran through one person's practice; the 2019 handover shows succession is possible, but no second line is documented **[D/I]**. (c) *Capital in the wrong place.* Twelve years of soil, canopy, habitat and site knowledge were sunk into land the initiative did not control **[I]**.

**Not supported.** *Municipal policy change*: no evidence the municipality withdrew anything; it was in conversation in June 2026 and offered to think along. *Funding cut*: no subsidy to the food forest appears in any retrieved source. *Volunteer collapse*: contradicted by the November 2025 record of a full volunteer group. *De Wielewaal as cause*: the chronology is suggestive — the estate was bought by the municipality in 2022, opened to the public in Sept 2025, and a Natuurmonumenten visitor centre opened at Elburglaan 12 on 23 May 2026, two months before the food forest's closure notice **[D]** ([Omroep Brabant](https://www.omroepbrabant.nl/nieuws/6008437/natuurmonumenten-zet-dertig-gidsen-op-philips-priveparadijs-de-wielewaal)). But no retrieved source connects them, and "context, not cause" is the honest reading **[I]**.

## 4. Continuity risk register

Municipal instruments first, because they set the terms the others live under. Two documented cases, both single-candidate procedures under *Didam*:

- **Elburglaan 12 → Natuurmonumenten** (Gemeenteblad 2025, nr. 17171, 17 Jan 2025): lease of municipal building and parcel as a nature-education centre and volunteer base. The motivation is unusually explicit: professional steering is wanted; "De Gemeente is onvoldoende geëquipeerd om grote aantallen vrijwilligers te bereiken en te begeleiden"; the tenant is chosen for 7,000 volunteers, paid capacity, POM status and "financiële draagkracht" **[D]**. Current operation: c. 100 volunteers, 30 guides, 4 paid staff **[D]**.
- **Wasven** (Gemeenteblad 2026, nr. 14748, 15 Jan 2026): ground given in use to Stichting Groendomein Wasven for ecological management, agroforestry, day care, reintegration and volunteering; single candidate on continuity grounds **[D]**. Plus an additional municipal guarantee of €0.4 million in 2023 (document surfaced, snippet only; PDF not retrieved) **[C]**.

Against those, the food forest held nothing: private land, no lease, no named legal entity, no municipal guarantee.

## 5. What this implies for an ecosystem strategy

1. **The municipal instrument selects for the opposite of the target.** Both documented instruments reward a capitalised, professionally staffed partner with a legal personality; a volunteer-run initiative on private land has no matching route. An ecosystem that assumes its nodes are stable is assuming a form the city's own procedures do not create **[I, grounded in both gemeenteblad texts]**.
2. **Tenure is the leverage point, and it is not a "shared care" problem.** No amount of mutual support fixes a landowner's decision. What shared infrastructure *can* do: help groups find and negotiate a tenure instrument, carry insurance and liability centrally, hold a legal entity that outlives a coordinator, and archive site knowledge so a move is not a restart **[I]**.
3. **Support infrastructure already exists and is underused for exactly these questions.** Supportpunt offers administration, board advice, and legal and notarial partners, municipally funded since 2007. The food forest used it for a website, not for tenure or succession **[D]**. And loss is normal, yet the ecosystem has no ritual for it: the closure produced support and invitations elsewhere, but no public account of what twelve years on that soil taught — the cheapest thing a network could have captured **[I]**.
