## 2. Notes per family

**Soil.** The EU directive (EU) 2025/2360 fixes descriptors and methods — "SOC concentration (g per kg)", mineral-soil criterion "SOC/Clay ratio > 1/13", ISO 10694, ISO 11272, ISO 10390, ISO 11274 [D:5]. For biology it mandates none: "Use European or international standards where available; if such standard is not available, the methodology chosen shall either be available in the scientific literature or publicly available" [D:5]. Chemistry is standardised; soil life is not. The Dutch food-forest programme is the closest operational set: 33 food forests and 6 reference forests, 15–30 auger points, laboratory nematode determination [D:14] — research-grade. Lead risk is scenario-based (GGD/RIVM, 2024) [D:7].

**Biodiversity.** Protocols exist but are grid- or area-based. FLORON: "2 tot 3 bezoeken noodzakelijk zijn voor een (redelijk) volledige inventarisatie" [D:11]. BMP A: 8–12 rounds on 10–250 ha [D:10]. MUS volunteers "tellen in het voorjaar driemaal het aantal vogels … op tien vaste plekken in een postcodegebied … precies vijf minuten" [D:9]. On the operative variable the retrieved evidence favours structure: "the diversity of habitats and microhabitat heterogeneity contained in urban parks appears as the most decisive factor for the overall species richness", while "exotics constitute large shares, especially of plant species" [D:4]. Mixed native/non-native planting is definitional for food forests [D:2].

**Water.** No Dutch site-scale infiltration protocol was found. EU: "Option 1: LABORATORY: ISO 11274 … Option 2: ESTIMATION: apply pedotransfer functions" [D:5]. BRO registers groundwater only where "minstens één jaar lang" measured: "Incidentele metingen en metingen over een korte tijdspanne … vallen niet onder de registratieplicht" [D:18]. A garden dipwell is invisible nationally; national data are regional.

**Habitat connectivity.** "the species-area relationship are valid despite the manipulated 'nature' of parks" and "matrix effects are consistently found to affect species richness negatively" [D:4]. Surroundings are unstudied in food forests: "Food forest functioning can be greatly influenced by adjacent land use types, which can for example determine the influx of organisms into the food forest" [D:1]. At 0.7 ha only site structure is measurable — a municipal-green-structure contribution, not a connectivity effect [I].

**Climate resilience.** Canopy is cheap: "Completing 500-1000 points is suggested, although aiming for a standard error … less than +2.0 is recommended" [D:17]. Mortality and irrigation demand have no protocol; others "have not been addressed at all (e.g., microclimate regulation and erosion control)" [D:1].

**Review verdicts.** Roodhof et al. 2026 (scoping review): "We found ten studies that investigated how food forests compare to other land use types, but none that addressed the interaction between food forests and other land use types"; "studies on plant biodiversity did not quantify different biodiversity indices but were ethnobotanical inventories"; "half of the studies conducted in one food forest only" [D:1]. Five comparative studies touched biodiversity, three soil carbon; a full-text search of the review for "infiltration", "hydrology", "drought", "runoff", "soil moisture" returned no hits — it reports no water programme [D:1]. Gauly et al. 2025 (245 records → 21 sources): "Food forests and forest gardens are increasingly recognized as multifunctional agroforestry systems that enhance biodiversity, support community well-being and contribute to food security. However, the inconsistent terminology surrounding these systems and the lack of a uniform definition pose challenges for policy makers and researchers …" [D:2]. Its ecological themes ("improve soil", n=15; "improve biodiversity", n=13) are objectives in definitions, not measurements [D:2].

## 3. Counter-evidence

- **Edible ≠ biodiverse.** Urban plant richness is routinely exotic-dominated [D:4]; mixed native/non-native planting is definitional [D:2]. A high count can be a planting list.
- **The comparison decides the verdict:** "comparing food forests to nature conservation sites in terms of biodiversity could downplay their agricultural potential, whereas a comparison of their yield to more intensive agricultural systems could overlook their biodiversity contributions" [D:1].
- **Less biodiverse than the habitat replaced?** Not established either way: ten comparative studies, half of all studies single-site, surroundings unstudied [D:1].
- **Small patches suffer area and isolation effects** [D:4]; a 0.7 ha garden's richness says more about its neighbourhood than its design [I].
- **Structure, not origin, is the defensible variable** [D:4].

## 4. The ~20-hour minimum, and what must be refused

Defensible: one autumn soil-fauna search, three fixed 20×20×20 cm sub-plots, same fortnight yearly (3 h) [D:13]; one complete plant list, fixed route and June date, native/exotic column, unknowns photographed (4 h) [D:11]; three spring bird counts, five points × 5 min (4 h) [D:9]; canopy cover from free imagery every second year (1.5 h/yr) [D:17]; annual photo-census of dead/dying trees plus irrigation log (2 h); one lab sample for pH/SOC/Pb (2 h volunteer; **lab cost cannot come from a zero budget**) [D:6,7]. ≈17–20 h.

Refuse as claims: that the site *increased* biodiversity (no before-data, no control); that year 1 is a baseline of change; that the earthworm score is a soil-health index (coarse, postcode-referenced presence [D:13]); that richness equals conservation value [D:4]; that no dead trees means resilience; that metal results extend beyond sampled points; any cross-site comparison with unmatched method.

## 5. What is NOT established

No site-scale Dutch monitoring standard for a 0.1–1 ha food garden exists: NMVB targets a 30+ site programme [D:14]; NDFF/FLORON/Sovon are grid/area-based [D:9,10,11]. No protocol found for infiltration, in-situ water-holding, drought mortality or irrigation demand at garden scale [U]; KWR methods not retrieved [U]. No directional evidence that urban food forests are more biodiverse than the habitat replaced [D:1]. No cost figures beyond NEN 5754's €33 [D:6]. No site-specific baseline located; NDFF holdings are restricted and grid-referenced [D:9]. Adjacent, not researched: municipal planting records, PDOK imagery history.
