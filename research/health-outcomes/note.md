## 1. The answer

**In one sentence: Eindhoven already uses "health" as the *reason* for greening and as a *performance indicator* in its budget, but nowhere in the retrieved record is it an *outcome* that a 0.7-hectare site could be held to — so the testable framework this note can offer is a claim ladder, and the honest rung for a garden is delivery and experienced benefit, not population health.**

Five findings carry the answer.

**1. The city has already quantified the thing the question asks about, and the quantification has no stated derivation.** The municipal *Gezonde Leefomgeving* publication gives its area indicators a "**Verwachte gezondheidswinst**" in healthy years — Geld+Werk +2-3, Eenzaamheid +1-2, Stress +1-2, Gezonde jaren +1-2, **Groen +2**, Geluid+Lucht +1-2, Voorzieningen +1-1.5 — and gives the *Groen* indicator "**+2**" [D, `E7` pp.5–10; the eighth indicator, Verdichting, falls in the part of the document this pass did not read]. The *Uitvoeringsagenda Programma Gezondheid 2026-2027* records that on 2 July 2024 the college fixed eight health indicators, and that "**Met deze indicatoren – aangevuld met vier fysieke factoren: geluid, groen, aantal inwoners/m2 en afstand tot voorzieningen – wordt gezondheidswinst gemeten en gemonitord. Deze indicatoren zijn als prestatie-indicatoren opgenomen in de documenten van de beleidscyclus zoals de begroting en het jaarverslag**" [D, `E8` p.4]. So green is *already* a health performance indicator in Eindhoven. What is missing is the step from the indicator to the number: no retrieved municipal document states how "+2 gezonde jaren" was derived, over what period, for whom, or with what counterfactual [D, absence]. **This is the precise artefact the issue asks to convert into a testable framework** — and it is an institution's ambition, which is evidence of its ambition and not of its effect.

**2. The national framework that Eindhoven's language follows is a *design heuristic*, and says so.** RIVM's 2025 *Vuistregels Groen* — the product that supplies the 300 m, 30 % canopy and "view of green" numbers — states that "**De vuistregels zijn meetbaar en gemaakt op basis van wetenschappelijk kennis. Vuistregels zijn niet verplicht**" [D, `N2` abstract], and the accompanying guidance page is explicit that "**Er is geen wettelijke groennorm, maar wel bestaande vuistregels of richtlijnen**" [D, `N3`]. More decisively, the national *type-of-green × health* matrix — the closest existing thing to the pathway map this note builds — states of itself: "Deze Gezond Groen Wijzer is gebaseerd op de beschreven mechanismen uit de Kennisbundeling Groen en Gezondheid …, andere aanvullende wetenschappelijke literatuur en kennisoverzichten, en **inschattingen van experts ('expert judgement')**. **Vaak gaat het om veronderstelde effecten.** Over de relatie tussen specifieke typen groen en gezondheid is **nog weinig overzichtsliteratuur beschikbaar**" [D, `N4`]. **The best-resourced national instrument for exactly this question describes its own health attributions as often presumed.** That is the strongest single warrant for the plausible/proven distinction in this note.

**3. Graded evidence exists, and it grades the plausible pathways as mostly weak.** The one retrieved overview that applies evidence-grading to *all* meta-analyses of green space and health graded **154 associations** (44 interventional, 110 observational): **zero** interventional associations reached *high* credibility, **four** reached *moderate* (wellbeing, systolic blood pressure, negative affect, positive affect); of the observational associations, **one** was *convincing* (cardiovascular-disease mortality) and **71 of 110 were non-significant**. Its verdict: "**only a small subset of these associations can be deemed to have a high or convincing credibility**" and "**Most of the associations on green spaces and health have weak credibility**" [D, `H1` summary and discussion]. Gardening-specific: wellbeing **pooled SMD 0.37, 95 % CI 0.01–0.73**; negative affect **SMD −0.52, −0.77 to −0.26**; **no significant effects on positive affect or systolic blood pressure**; the wellbeing estimate is graded *moderate* only after being downgraded for mixing clinical and non-clinical populations [D, `H1`]. **A confidence interval whose lower bound is 0.01 licenses "may help somewhat", not "improves health".**

**4. The Dutch synthesis deliberately refuses effect sizes and prioritisation — which is either the most honest thing in the file or the reason nothing can be promised.** The RIVM *Kennisbundeling Groen en Gezondheid* (2022), which underpins the Vuistregels and the Wijzer, organises the mechanisms into four families — *schade verminderen* (air, heat, noise), *herstel bevorderen* (stress recovery, attention restoration), *gezond gedrag bevorderen* (physical activity, social contact) and *schade veroorzaken* (pollen, pathogens) — and then states: "**Gegeven de complexiteit van de verbanden en de invloed van de ruimtelijke en sociale context worden geen uitspraken gedaan over de grootte en prioritering van effecten**" [D, `N6` p.2]. It also records that the direction is not uniform: greening "kan zowel een positieve als een negatieve invloed hebben op de gezondheid en het welbevinden" [D, `N6` p.2]. For physical activity it says the evidence "**is niet consistent**"; for overweight, diabetes and cardiovascular disease, that the studies "**geven echter nog geen eenduidig verband**" [D, `N6` pp.4–5]. **A pathway map drawn from this source is a map of candidate mechanisms of unstated size, drawn by the institute that would most like to state them.**

**5. What can actually be observed here without overclaiming is narrower than the question hopes, and the binding constraint is geography, not willingness.** The finest *measured* health geography in the retrieved municipal and regional record is the **wijk** (district): the municipality reports stress, loneliness, self-assessed health and care use by wijk, and states plainly that "**Van de gezonde levensverwachting zijn geen gegevens op wijkniveau beschikbaar**" [D, `E8` pp.3–4]. At **buurt** level the regional instrument is explicit that it is a model: "**Hierdoor is het mogelijk om op buurt- en wijkniveau cijfers te schatten** … **de cijfers blijven schattingen**" [D, `L3`], and its own page warns "**Wijkcijfers zijn soms gebaseerd op kleine aantallen. Wees voorzichtig met de interpretatie van deze cijfers en beschouw ze als een indicatie**" [D, `L3`]. On the exposure side the position is not better: the national green-proximity block of CBS's proximity table is present in the schema but **empty for every Eindhoven row (0 of 117)** and equally empty for Tilburg, Amsterdam, Utrecht, Groningen, Den Haag and Rotterdam in the same edition [D, `CBS`], and the municipal green register **excludes solitary trees** while the tree register has "**geen kroondiameter of kroonoppervlak**" so canopy cannot be computed from it [D, `E2`, `E3`, `S2` §3.7]. **A 0.7 ha site sits several orders of magnitude below the resolution of every measured health instrument retrieved.**

**What follows for the main question.** "Human and ecological health" is not one outcome here; it is **four different objects** that the local record treats as one: (i) a *justification* for greening, quantified in healthy years; (ii) a *performance indicator* in the budget cycle; (iii) a *typology of presumed effects* by green type; and (iv) a *design heuristic* with distances and percentages. A testable framework must separate them, and must place any garden claim on the rung it can actually reach. §3–§4 do that. **The single most useful move for the programme is not to build a measurement programme; it is to stop the programme's claims at the rung the evidence supports, and to use the city's own published units (metres to green, canopy percentage, distance to facilities) for the claims that are about exposure rather than health.**

## 3. The pathway map

**How to read it.** Each pathway carries: the mechanism *claimed*; the **kind** of evidence retrieved (design named — a cross-sectional association is never called an effect); the **setting** in which it was measured (garden / generic green / Eindhoven); and the confounders that decide whether a local claim survives. The last column is the note's verdict on **this site's** claim, not on the literature.

**Two things the map shows that a list of pathways hides** [I]:
- **The pathways are not independent, and the national synthesis says so**: "Succesvolle initiatieven versterken elkaar op zoveel mogelijk indicatoren: een groenere omgeving vermindert stress, verbetert de luchtkwaliteit en moedigt beweging aan" [D, `E7` p.3]. If that is true, then the *marginal* contribution of an additional 0.7 ha is smaller than the sum of the individual pathways — and no retrieved study decomposes it. If it is false, the bundle is rhetoric.
- **The negative pathways are part of the same map, not a caveat to it.** Pollen (allergy, and climate change lengthening the season), zoonoses (Lyme, Weil's disease, toxocariasis, toxoplasmosis), and the air-flow effect sit in the national model as *mechanisms*, not footnotes [D, `N6` pp.4–5]. The Vuistregels carry them as conditions: "**lage allergeniteit**", "**voorlichting, om te voorkomen dat ziekte van dier op mens wordt overgedragen**", "**beheer en onderhoud, zodat het groen prettig en functioneel blijft**" [D, `N1`]. A framework that lists only benefits is not a health framework.

### 3.2 Ecological pathways

**These are the weakest-evidenced half of the question, and the half where "plausible" most often passes as "proven".** The decisive source retrieved for this half is the **scoping review of temperate food-forestry research** (Roodhof et al., *Agroforestry Systems* 100:71, 2026), which was read at full text and **checked here against its own text rather than trusted for its summary** [D, `EB1`]. Its relevant findings are quantitative and unfavourable to the benefit claims:

- Of the reviewed studies, "**Sample sizes ranged from one to 100 food forests, with half of the studies conducted in one food forest only**" [D, `EB1`].
- On comparison: "**We found ten studies that investigated how food forests compare to other land use types, but none that addressed the interaction between food forests and other land use types**" [D, `EB1`].
- On biodiversity method: "**studies on plant biodiversity did not quantify different biodiversity indices but were ethnobotanical inventories**" [D, `EB1`].
- On the water cycle **this note ran its own full-text search**: the strings *infiltration*, *hydrology*, *drought*, *runoff* and *soil moisture* return **zero occurrences** in the review, which reports no water programme at all [D, `EB1`, this note's search].
- Other values "**have not been addressed at all (e.g., microclimate regulation and erosion control)**" [D, `EB1`].

**So the honest position is not that temperate food forests lack ecological benefits; it is that the literature has not measured them at a scale, or against a comparator, that would let anyone say either way.**

| Pathway | Mechanism claimed | Best evidence retrieved | Verdict for a 0.7 ha site |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Biodiversity** | Stratified edible planting supports species richness, pollinators and birds [C] | Plant-biodiversity studies in the food-forest literature "**did not quantify different biodiversity indices but were ethnobotanical inventories**", and half of all studies sit in one food forest [D, `EB1`]. The operative variable in the wider evidence is **structure, not origin**: "the diversity of habitats and microhabitat heterogeneity contained in urban parks appears as the most decisive factor for the overall species richness", while "exotics constitute large shares, especially of plant species" [D, `EB3`, abstract]. Mixed native/non-native planting is definitional for food forests [D, `EB2`]. Native plants drew 67 % of insect visits against 24 % and 9 % for related ornamentals and unrelated exotics [D, `S2` §3.4] | **Insufficient, and the mechanism is contested.** "Edible" is not a biodiversity credential; a high species count can be a planting list. A single-site list cannot show the site **caused** richness, and cannot be compared with another site on an unmatched method [D, `EB1`] |
| **Soil** | Perennial, undisturbed, mulched soil accumulates organic matter and soil life [C] | **Chemistry is fully standardised, biology is not.** The EU Soil Monitoring Law fixes SOC as "SOC concentration (g per kg)" with the mineral-soil target "**SOC/Clay ratio > 1/13**", measured by ISO 10694 with carbonate subtracted per ISO 10693; pH by ISO 10390; subsoil bulk density by ISO 11272; water retention by ISO 11274 or pedotransfer [D, `EB5`, Annexes]. Lead guidance puts the garden intervention value at 70 mg/kg against a generic 530 mg/kg and advises container growing on contaminated ground [D, `S2` §3.4] |
| **Water** | Multi-layer vegetation increases infiltration and water storage, reducing runoff [C] | **The evidence base for this claim does not exist in the food-forest literature**: the review contains zero occurrences of *infiltration*, *hydrology*, *drought*, *runoff* or *soil moisture* [D, `EB1`, full-text search]. The one Dutch food-forest measurement retrieved is a **water balance** showing larger storage despite lower soil organic matter, with explicitly greater evaporation from the many layers — a trade-off, not a benefit [D, `S2` §3.3]. English allotments used a median **7,595 L/yr** per ~250 m² plot, **5,993 L from mains**, 80.8 % in summer [D, `S2` §3.4]. Eindhoven's lowest groundwater is below 2 m almost everywhere and plant vitality is 75 % against a 90 % target [D, `S2` P8] |
| **Habitat connectivity** | A site contributes to a green network and to corridor function [C] | "Food forest functioning can be greatly influenced by **adjacent land use types**, which can for example determine the influx of organisms into the food forest" — and **no reviewed study addressed that interaction** [D, `EB1`]. Species–area relations hold in urban parks and "matrix effects are consistently found to affect species richness negatively" [D, `EB3`, abstract]. The 2024 canopy baseline is announced but unpublished and no canopy or cool-place layer is public [D, `S2` P11, §3.7] | **Insufficient and structurally implausible at 0.7 ha.** Richness at this size says more about the neighbourhood than about the design; the only measurable contribution is *position within the municipal green structure*, which needs the unpublished layer |
| **Climate resilience of the site** | A mature food forest is more resilient to drought and heat than annual cropping or lawn [C] | Microclimate regulation is one of the values "**not been addressed at all**" in the food-forest literature [D, `EB1`]. Establishment is "year 0–3"; "most food forests studied in literature are younger than five years old"; water rights are "crucial for the establishment period" [D, `S2` §3.4]. An urban-farm air-temperature comparison found **no significant difference** in mean monthly air temperature against tree-covered sites [D, `S2` §3.3] | **Insufficient, and the first decade is the counter-example.** Resilience is a claim about a mature system this site never became |

**One comparative caveat worth quoting, because it disciplines every "food forests are better" claim** [D, `EB1`]: "**comparing food forests to nature conservation sites in terms of biodiversity could downplay their agricultural potential, whereas a comparison of their yield to more intensive agricultural systems could overlook their biodiversity contributions**". **The comparator decides the verdict** — so an ecological claim about a food forest is not a claim until it names what it is being compared with. This note's comparison is a *matched non-edible planting on the same site*, and no retrieved study has made it [D, `S2` §4].

## 4. The indicator table

**The rule used here.** An indicator is admitted only if it is clear (a) **which pathway it would carry**; (b) whether the **data exist now** — named dataset, owner, edition — or must be **created**; (c) what it costs, **including whether the cost is itself a burden** (this repository already documents *regeldruk* as the main price of participation [D, `S5` §3], and the health note's boundary is that imposed measurement is a controlling context [D, `S1` §1.3C]); and (d) **what it cannot show**. Rows are ordered from cheapest/most defensible to most expensive/least attributable.

### 4.1 Tier 1 — delivery and dose (created at the site, near-zero cost)

| Candidate indicator | Pathway it would carry | Measures the pathway? | Data available now? | Cost / burden | What it cannot show |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Open sessions held, and attendances per session** | Dose, for every human pathway | It measures **dose**, not effect | **Create** — one tally sheet. The site kept a weekly rhythm and an attendance culture [D, `S4` §1 item 2] | ~2 min per session; no new role; the person already hosting does it | Anything about benefit; the denominator (who was eligible) is unknown |
| **Return rate: people attending ≥2 and ≥5 times** | Retention vs novelty | Yes, for retention — the honest object | **Create** — derived from the tally | Nil extra | *Why* they returned; whether they would have returned anyway |
| **Lapsed participants and what they did next** | Whether capability and benefit travel beyond the site | Yes; the single highest-value item in the file [D, `S1` §1.3B] | **Create** — a short list and, ideally, one conversation each | One hour per lapsed participant, and **this is a cost borne by the person who left**, which is a real ethical cost | Whether the loss was the site's or life's |
| **"Show me how you'd do this at home" (one teach-back, yes/no)** | Interactive capability | Partly — self-efficacy moves where knowledge does not [D, `S1` §7] | **Create** — a yes/no recorded by whoever taught | Seconds, inside an ordinary session | Whether they could actually do it unassisted; nothing durable |

**The cheapest credible ecological minimum, and the refusals attached** [I, assembled from the above]. About **17–20 volunteer hours a year** buys: one autumn soil-fauna search on three fixed 20×20×20 cm sub-plots in the same fortnight each year (≈3 h); one complete plant list on a fixed route on a fixed June date with native/exotic flagged and unknowns photographed (≈4 h); three spring bird counts at five points × 5 minutes (≈4 h); canopy cover from free imagery every second year (≈1.5 h/yr); an annual photo-census of dead and dying trees plus an irrigation log (≈2 h); and one laboratory sample for pH, SOC and lead (≈2 h volunteer — **but the lab fee cannot come out of a zero budget** [D, `EB9`]).

**What must be refused as claims from that minimum** [I]: that the site **increased** biodiversity (no before-data, no control); that year one is a baseline of *change*; that the earthworm score is a soil-health index; that species richness equals conservation value; that absence of dead trees means resilience; that soil-metal results extend beyond the sampled points; and any comparison with another site on an unmatched method [D, `EB1`, `EB3`].

**The gap is now precise, and it is not the one this note first assumed.** Dutch ecological protocols exist and are named above — but they are built for **grids (NDFF/FLORON), areas of 10–250 ha (BMP), postcode-level citizen science (Bodemdierendagen), or a 30-site programme (NMVB)** [D, `EB6`–`EB13`]. **No site-scale Dutch standard for a 0.1–1 ha food garden was retrieved**, and for infiltration, water-holding, drought mortality and irrigation demand **no protocol was found at any scale** [U]. So the gap is not "nobody has thought about how to measure this"; it is that **every existing scheme measures a different object** — a grid square, a bird territory network, a postcode, a programme — and a garden is none of them.

## 5. The trade-off map

**These are not implementation choices to be optimised. They are claims that cannot all be true at once, and the framework has to say which is being made.**

| Trade-off | The two sides, with evidence | What decides it |
|---|---|---|
| **Ecological management vs perceived amenity** | Herb-rich, long grass is the ecologically better option and is reported as a defect: 263 reports on "Lang gras/kruidenrijk gras" among 4,155 *Groen* reports [D, `E5`]. The national synthesis makes maintenance a *condition* of the social and safety benefits: "beheer en onderhoud, zodat het groen prettig en functioneel blijft" [D, `N1`] | Whose standard of neatness governs, and whether a self-managed site can absorb the complaints a municipal one cannot |
| **Access vs protection** | The city has not decided whether sensitive green should be opened or protected, and no local carrying-capacity evidence exists [D, `S6` §1.4, `S2` §4] | Whether "health" is offered as a reason for access or as a reason for enclosure |
| **Measurement rigour vs the occasion being measured** | A design able to detect the pooled gardening effect needs ~115 people per group (§7) — more time than the garden's entire annual labour. And imposed measurement is predicted to be a controlling context that diminishes intrinsic motivation [D, `S1` §1.3C, `F4`] | Whether the funding or the funder requires a number, and who bears that cost |
| **Health as justification vs health as outcome** | If health is the *reason* for the garden, the garden owes proof; if health is an *outcome*, it owes only honest description. The retrieved record overwhelmingly uses it as a reason [D, `E7`, `N2`, `N4`] | Whether the claim is being made to a funder or to a neighbour |
| **Participants' wellbeing vs others' access** | A bounded, well-functioning group makes outsiders legible as outsiders [D, `S4` §6]. Allotment land in ten English cities fell 65 % (1960–2016) with eight times more closures in deprived areas [D, `S2` §3.6] | Tenure, and whether the site's benefit is excludable |
| **Green cover vs dwellings** | Green per dwelling falls with address density (ρ ≈ −0.71), central buurten at 5–6 m²/dwelling [lead, `EVB`]; the municipal threshold is 75 m²/dwelling [D, `E1`] | Which denominator the city uses when it reports the health gain of greening |
| **Food production vs biodiversity** | Edible is not biodiverse; native plants drew 67 % of insect visits against 9 % for unrelated exotics [D, `S2` §3.4] | Whether the site's ecological claim is about yield, natives, or structure |
| **Visibility vs the already-engaged** | Measurement and visibility select for the articulate; Dutch garden volunteers are more educated than their neighbourhoods [D, `S2` §3.6] | Whether "health" is used to reach new people or to describe existing ones |

## 6. Distributional equity

**What the city has committed to.** The 2030 goals are "+3 gezonde jaren voor iedereen: aantoonbare gezondheidswinst" and "**20 % minder gezondheidsverschillen tussen gebieden**" [D, `E7` p.2, `E8` p.2]. The differences are documented by name: highest stress in Centrum, De Laak and Oud-Woensel; highest loneliness in Meerhoven, Oud-Gestel and De Laak; SES "blijft … om aandacht vragen" in Oud-Woensel, Erp, Ontginning and Oud-Gestel [D, `E8` p.3].

**What the distributional instruments can actually carry.**

- **The exposure side is coarse and deficient.** The municipal definition of a green-poor neighbourhood is "**minder dan 75 m² openbaar groen per woning**" [D, `E1`] — a *per-dwelling* denominator, which flatters areas with large dwellings and few occupants. The project dataset behind the "17 green-poor neighbourhoods" contains **70 records** across **17 distinct buurt strings — 16 unique, because "Limbeek Noord" and "Limbeek-Noord" are the same place spelled twice** — of which the dataset's own text names **nine** selected neighbourhoods: Bloemenplein, Gildebuurt, Hemelrijken, Joriskwartier, Lakerlopen, Limbeek-Noord, Limbeek-Zuid, Rochusbuurt, Tivoli [D, `E1`]. The remaining strings are project points elsewhere. **The "17" in the brief is a count of data-entry variety, and reading it as a policy list of 17 deprived neighbourhoods would be a mistake this note can show.**
- **The complaint data invert the intuition.** The nine green-poor neighbourhoods account for **157 of 4,155** green reports (3.8 %), while the top reporting buurten are post-war Woensel-Noord and Acht neighbourhoods with a lot of municipal green and many trees [D, `E5`, counted]. **Report volume is a function of exposure to municipal green, not of deprivation** — so it cannot be used as a green-quality or unmet-need measure without a denominator, and no denominator is published.
- **The health side is modelled below wijk level.** Buurt figures are estimates from a model, with an explicit small-number warning [D, `L3`]; and the headline outcome, healthy life expectancy, has no sub-municipal data at all [D, `E8` p.4]. **The city has committed to reducing a gap between areas that it cannot measure at the scale where greening happens.**
- **Benefit incidence from a 0.7 ha site is unknown, and the site was private ground.** The site's land was private; the commissioner reports the land was sold [P, `S6` §1]. Access was by an open weekly rhythm, not by right [D, `S4` §1]. Whether the people who used it were the people with the highest exposure is **[U]** — no attendance record by residence exists, and the participant voice is absent from the public record [D, `S4` §4].
- **Displacement is a documented risk of greening, not a hypothetical.** Across 28 cities, earlier greening predicted later gentrification in 17 — "mostly European" cities did not [D, `S2` §3.5]. **No Eindhoven study exists** [U]. The honest local statement is that the risk is untested, not that it is absent.

**The distributional conclusion** [I]: **a health framework for this programme cannot be equity-relevant until the denominator is fixed.** Every candidate indicator in §4 either uses dwellings (which flatters), buurt model estimates (which are too noisy), or report counts (which track green presence). The one denominator that would work — measured health outcomes by small area — does not exist, and creating it is exactly the "new monitoring programme" the issue excludes.

## 7. Attribution limits: what a small experiment can and cannot claim

**The site is gone, and that removes the strongest design.** Voedselbos Eindhoven closed in July 2026; 0.7 ha on private ground at Elburglaan 8; a planned wind-down that became immediate [D, `S6` §1, `S2`]. Consequences:

1. **No before/after is possible.** No baseline measurement of anything — attendance, soil, species, wellbeing — was retrieved [D, absence]. A before/after claim would require a "before".
2. **No cohort is followable at the site.** Whether the ~30 volunteers persist is **[U]** [D, `S6` §1.2].
3. **No counterfactual.** The plausible comparison — a similar group not gardening — does not exist locally, and the one well-designed garden trial retrieved (Denver, N = 291, observer-blind, waiting-list control) found nulls on diet, activity and anthropometry [D, `S4` §5]. **The bar for a garden effect is set by a study with 291 participants and a control arm.**
4. **The arithmetic is unfavourable and worth stating plainly.** For a two-group comparison at α = 0.05, 80 % power and the pooled gardening effect on wellbeing of SMD 0.37, the required sample is ≈ 115 per group, ≈ 230 in total [I, computed: n = 2(z₀.₉₇₅ + z₀.₈)²/δ² = 2(1.96 + 0.84)²/0.37² ≈ 114.6]. A 30-person volunteer group is roughly **an order of magnitude short**, and that is before clustering, dropout and the CI's lower bound of 0.01 — at which the required n is effectively unbounded [I].
5. **Geography closes the population route.** The finest *measured* outcome geography is the wijk [D, `E8`]; a wijk of several thousand households cannot register a 0.7 ha site.
6. **What therefore remains claimable** [I]: (a) **delivery** — that a designed rhythm ran, with these attendances, for this long; (b) **dose** — who came, how often, who stopped; (c) **experienced benefit at the site** — self-reported, without a counterfactual, and described as such; (d) **exposure change** — metres, canopy, shade, temperature, stated in physical units; and (e) **ecological description** — a record, not an effect.
   **Not claimable:** population health improvement; healthy-years gain; reduced loneliness or stress in a neighbourhood; biodiversity or carbon benefit; and any comparison with a non-edible alternative.

### 7.1 The claim ladder

**This is the framework the issue asks for, and it is deliberately a ladder rather than a matrix**: each rung names what is asserted, the design that would carry it, what it costs, and what would falsify it. A programme should state which rung it is on, and should not let a lower rung's evidence stand in for a higher rung's claim.

| Rung | What is asserted | Design that would carry it | Cost | Falsifier | Reachable here? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0 — Activity** | "This happened." | A record: dates, sessions, attendances, tasks | Minutes per session | The record does not exist or is not kept | **Yes.** This is the rung the anchor case actually occupies, and its rhythm is documented [D, `S4` §1] |
| **1 — Delivery and dose** | "This happened, to these people, this often, for this long." | An attendance tally plus a return-rate count | Minutes per session; no new role | Most people attend once; the core is one person | **Yes**, and it is the highest rung that is both cheap and true |
| **2 — Exposure change** | "The physical environment here changed in these units." | m² of green, metres to green, canopy %, shade hours, surface temperature — measured or computed from published data | Analyst time (not volunteer time); one-off measurement | The site is too small for the change to register, or the change is unmeasurable with public data | **Partly.** Green area computable (`E2`); canopy **not** (no crown field, `E3`); shade and temperature would need a one-off campaign |
| **3 — Experienced benefit** | "Participants reported X, at the site, in these terms." | Repeated single-item self-report plus a small number of interviews, with the wording and the refusal option stated | 1–3 min per visit; interviews are hours, borne by participants | Reports are flat, or negative, or only from the already-committed | **Yes**, with the honest caveat that it has no counterfactual and changes the occasion [D, `S1` §1.3C] |
| **4 — Capability or behaviour change** | "People can do, or do, something they could not or did not before." | A performance or teach-back check; a follow-up after exit; ideally a comparison of two organisational forms | Hours, and a design decision that costs the garden something | People do not return; teach-back shows nothing; behaviour is unchanged at follow-up | **Marginally.** The lapsed-participant count is the one item that fits [D, `S1` §1.3B] |
| **5 — Population health outcome** | "Health in this area improved, and this contributed." | Measured outcomes at a geography fine enough to see the site; a counterfactual; enough people to detect the effect | A research programme, not a garden programme | The finest measured geography is the wijk and the effect is far below detectability | **No.** Not "difficult": not available, and the data that would carry it do not exist at the needed scale [D, `E8`, `L3`] |

**The ladder's discipline** [I]: a claim on rung *n* may not be supported by evidence from rung *n−1*. That is the exact error the "+2 gezonde jaren" makes if no derivation exists — it is a rung-5 claim resting on rung-2 reasoning ("there is green; therefore health"). **A framework that makes the rung explicit is more useful than one that makes the outcome precise**, because the rung is what a funder, a neighbour or a reviewer can actually check.

### 7.2 A worked example: what could and could not be said about Elburglaan

**What the public record supports** [D, `S4` §1, `S6` §1]: a weekly open session on a fixed day; an explanation at 10:00; two hours of work; a scheduled stop at noon; half an hour of shared tasting; visitors free to walk in; a winter dormancy with a published reopening date; no obligation to come twice in a row; 0.7 ha on private ground; a closure in July 2026 after more than twelve years.

**What a rung-2 statement would have required, and does not exist**: the site's m² of public green (**it was private ground**, so a green-cover calculation would not have counted it — `openbaar-groen0` is *openbaar* green and does not name all private owners, [D/I, `E2`]); its canopy (**no crown field**, [D, `E3`]); its dwellings-within-300-m denominator (**no public distance layer**, [D, `S2` §3.7]); its shade hours (**no measurement**, [D, `S2` P2]). **So even the cheapest honest claim about the site's physical contribution cannot be made retrospectively from public data.** This is the sharpest single illustration of the issue's "0.7 ha, closed, longitudinal claims gone" constraint.

**What a rung-3 statement could have looked like, and what it would not have shown**: *"Of N attendances by M people over twelve months, 4 in 5 who came once came again; when asked at the end of a session whether they had done something at home they had learned here, most said yes"* — a real, checkable, useful statement that says **nothing** about stress, loneliness, healthy years or the neighbourhood. **The gap between that sentence and the city's "+2 gezonde jaren" is the entire content of this note.**

**The one number the case could still produce, cheaply, and does not**: the **lapsed-participant list**. No retrieved study measures outcomes after a garden ends or a site closes [D, `S1` P11], the site closed, and the people who stopped coming are the only witnesses to whether anything travelled beyond the ground [I]. It is a rung-4 question, it costs a few conversations, and it is the single highest-value item in §4.1.

**The strongest defensible sentence a 0.7 ha site can earn**, in the city's own units: *"This site contributed X m² of green within Y metres of Z dwellings, with A canopy at maturity, hosting B open sessions attended C times by D people, of whom E returned five times or more"* [I]. **That is a delivery and exposure claim, and it is checkable.** Everything above it requires either a control group or a data system the city does not have.

## 8. Counter-evidence and tensions

1. **Measurable versus meaningful.** The care-farm review found participants valued "being in contact with each other, and feeling a sense of achievement, fulfilment and belonging", and "We found **no evidence** that care farms improved people's quality of life and some evidence that they might improve depression and anxiety" [D, `S4` §5]. **Rich meaning is real without being an outcome** — which is why this note keeps them apart and why the two must not be traded against each other.
2. **Who bears the burden of proof.** The framework is not neutral: requiring a garden to demonstrate health gain transfers work to volunteers, and this repository already documents *regeldruk* — subsidy procedures and legal-person obligations — as the barrier the city itself names [D, `S5` §3]. The health note's boundary — refuse the measurement regime, because imposed measurement is a controlling context [D, `S1` §1.3C] — is a normative position this note adopts and labels as such [I].
3. **Health as outcome versus health as justification.** The retrieved record uses health overwhelmingly as a reason: "Gezondheid wordt vaak gebruikt als reden om de omgeving te vergroenen" [C, `N2` document title, quoted at search-result level]; the Vuistregels exist because VWS, municipalities and GGDs "willen meer aandacht voor gezondheid bij ruimtelijke plannen" [D, `N2`]; the city's leaflet opens with the health gap and the green indicator carries "+2 gezonde jaren" [D, `E7`]. **A justification does not need a measurement; an outcome does.** Which one the programme wants is a decision for the PI, not for the evidence.
4. **A garden may improve wellbeing for its participants while worsening access for others.** The mechanisms are documented separately: bounded groups make outsiders legible [D, `S4` §6]; allotment loss concentrated in deprived areas [D, `S2` §3.6]; greening predicting gentrification in 17 of 28 cities [D, `S2` §3.5]. **No retrieved study tests the combination**, and the local incidence is [U]. It is a live alternative reading of any positive finding, not a rhetorical caveat.
5. **Green can harm, and the harm is in the same model.** Pollen (allergy, with a lengthening season), zoonoses (Lyme, Weil's disease, toxocariasis, toxoplasmosis), and air-flow obstruction in street canyons are mechanisms in the national synthesis, not footnotes [D, `N6` pp.4–5]. The Vuistregels carry low allergenicity and zoonosis information as conditions [D, `N1`].
6. **The city's own two documents disagree on stress prevalence.** The *Gezonde Leefomgeving* publication states "**13 % van de Eindhovenaren ervaart veel stress**, bij mensen met schulden zelfs 25 %" [D, `E7` p.7]; the *Uitvoeringsagenda* states that "het percentage volwassen inwoners dat (heel) veel stress ervaart … is toegenomen van 19 % in 2020 naar **23 %** in 2024" [D, `E8` p.3]. Neither document gives the item wording or the instrument. **One of these is the wrong number for the claim being made, and the record does not say which** — a concrete illustration of why a "health indicator" without a definition is not an indicator.
7. **Loneliness has the same problem.** The leaflet says "**Bijna de helft van de volwassenen** in Eindhoven voelt zich eenzaam" [D, `E7` p.6]; the agenda reports "(zeer) ernstig eenzaam" at 19 % [D, `E8` p.3]. **A factor of two and a half between "feels lonely" and "severely lonely"** — the kind of spread that determines whether an intervention is aimed at half the population or a fifth of it.
8. **The national indicator set refuses the aggregation the city performs.** The Basisset states: "De indicatoren bevatten **geen duiding of streefwaarden**. Ook kunnen indicatoren **niet met elkaar vergeleken of tegen elkaar afgewogen worden**. Het is **niet te gebruiken als toetsingskader of afvinklijstje**" [D, `N5`]. **Yet Eindhoven aggregates eight indicators into one hexagon score and assigns healthy-year gains to each** [D, `E7` p.3]. The framework Eindhoven's language derives from explicitly disclaims the operation Eindhoven performs with it.
9. **Publication bias is documented in the pathway most often used to justify gardens.** The nature-connectedness–wellbeing experimental estimate falls from d ≈ 0.29 to d ≈ 0.21 when unpublished work is included, with "clear evidence of publication bias" [D, `S4` §5]. The gardening meta-analyses are graded, and the grades are mostly low [D, `H1`].
10. **The ecological counter-case is internal to food forestry.** "Edible" is not a biodiversity credential; temperate food forests have "few papers provid[ing] evidence for the environmental benefits" and "no studies specifically focused on biodiversity effects or water quality impacts" [D, `S2` §3.4]. A note claiming ecological health benefits would be doing exactly what it criticises in the human half.
11. **A small place is a real thing even if it is not a measurable outcome.** The strongest finding about the anchor case is a *design*: one fixed weekly slot, an explanation at 10:00, two hours of work, a scheduled stop at noon, half an hour of shared tasting, visitors free to walk in, a winter dormancy with a published reopening date, and no obligation to come twice in a row [D, `S4` §1]. **That is the transferable asset, and none of the indicators in §4 would have captured it.** This is the honest limit of the framework: it can hold a claim, and it cannot hold a Friday morning.

## 12. Implications for the main question and the three pillars

**[I] — interpretation, not finding.**

1. **"Human and ecological health" should be replaced, in the programme's own language, by three separate objects**: *delivery* (what happens, how often, to whom), *exposure* (what physical change the site makes — metres, m², canopy, shade), and *experience* (what participants report). **Health, as an outcome, is a fourth object the programme cannot currently produce, and saying so is more useful than a framework that pretends otherwise.**
2. **Pillar 1 (De Wielewaal as central green motor) gains nothing from the health evidence and loses nothing.** The city's own performance indicators operate at hexagon and wijk scale [D, `E7`, `E8`]; a single estate is not the unit at which any of them is measured. If "motor" means a node in the canopy-and-access network, the claim is about *exposure*, provable in metres and canopy percentage [D, `S2`]. If it means a health outcome, no instrument in the city can see it.
3. **Pillar 2 (a city-wide ecosystem) has one genuinely shared object available, and it is not a new programme**: the **four physical factors** the college already attached to its eight health indicators — geluid, groen, inwoners/m², afstand tot voorzieningen — plus the national Vuistregels (300 m / 150 m, 30 % canopy, view of green, 350–500 m² per dwelling within 5 km) [D, `E8`, `N1`]. **A network could adopt one shared unit — e.g. "dwellings within 300 m of public green" — and report it consistently, without a single new survey.** That is the cheapest contribution this research can point to.
4. **Pillar 3 (nature closer to everyday life) is where the evidence is strongest and the claim should be smallest.** Shade, proximity and a usable place are physical facts; the health benefit of them is a graded association whose best gardening-specific estimate has a confidence interval touching zero [D, `H1`, `S2` P5]. **Sell the metre and the shade; describe the wellbeing; never let the description become the metric.**
5. **For "community as foundation": the framework's most important row is the one with no indicator.** The documented asset of the anchor case was a rhythm, a host and an exit option [D, `S4` §1] — none of which any indicator in §4 would have captured, and all of which a measurement regime would have put at risk [D, `S1` §1.3C]. **A framework that cannot see the thing that worked is a warning about frameworks.**

## 13. Important uncertainties and limits

- **Single retrieval day (2026-11-26).** Two of the retrieved documents are 2025 publications whose instruments are explicitly still being tested in practice in 2026 ("In 2026 worden de vuistregels Groen verder getoetst in de praktijk" [D, `N3`]; likewise the Wijzer [D, `N4`]). **The national framework may change in the year this note is read.**
- **The overview's grading is not this note's verification.** `H1`'s credibility grades are one team's application of GRADE and a five-level observational scheme; the underlying meta-analyses were not read here, and the pooled figures are cited as `H1` reports them.
- **The human line is better sourced than the ecological line.** No Dutch standard protocol for volunteer-measurable soil or infiltration indicators was retrieved; §4.4's cost estimates are order-of-magnitude and its "must be created" verdicts rest on the absence of a local baseline rather than on a survey of protocols.
- **Several load-bearing local numbers are computed by this note from exports**, not published: the 4,155/8,765 facet counts, the 157/4,155 distribution, the 70 records across 17 strings, the 0/117 CBS emptiness. Each is reproducible from the query recorded in `sources.md`, and each is a computation rather than a retrieved statement.
- **No participant voice.** Nothing here can say what anyone experienced. The framework is built from documents and studies, and the people it is about are absent from all of them [D, `S4` §4].
- **Withdrawal, recorded rather than silent.** Two statements in this note's first submitted draft — that the ecological half was "thinner than the human half" for want of any Dutch protocol, and that the delegated ecological line "had not returned" — are **withdrawn**, because the line returned before publication and its pivotal source was then re-read at full text here. The surviving limit is narrower (hours are estimates; one price was retrieved). The full record of what was withdrawn and why is in [`sources.md`](sources.md) §0, so a reader of the final artefact can see the judgement was superseded rather than never made.

**The normativity is real.** The choice to privilege delivery and exposure over outcome claims, and the refusal to make participation conditional on measurement, are positions [I], argued from the evidence and from this repository's own commitments, not derived from it.

## 14. What would change the answer

1. **The college decision 24bst00803 and the hexagon model.** If the eight indicators have a published derivation, P1–P3 move from "ambition without stated basis" to a documented method — and the framework would then have a real local anchor.
2. **One published municipal table of measured buurt-level outcomes with sample sizes.** That would change the geography limit in §1 finding 5 and make Tier 3 usable.
3. **A canopy layer, or the announced 2024 canopy baseline.** That would make the 30 % target auditable and turn the exposure claim into a measurement [D, `S2` P11, `N1`].
4. **One paired Dutch study of a food forest against a matched park or lawn on thermal comfort, biodiversity and use.** The single highest-value study for the ecological half [D, `S2` §8].
5. **Any follow-up of a closed site's participants.** Nothing retrieved measures outcomes after a garden ends or a site closes [D, `S1` P11]. It is the one design that this case uniquely enables and that the literature lacks.
6. **A re-graded evidence base restricted to trials and natural experiments.** If a well-powered greening trial reports a credible wellbeing effect, the plausible/proven line moves; if the Denver-type nulls accumulate, it moves the other way [D, `S4` §5].

---

**Reviewer's test this note expects.** The failure mode named in the brief — association presented as causation — appears here in three places if it is read carelessly: the four physical factors are *called* health indicators by the city; the Leefbaarometer *is* a health-adjacent score at 100 m; and the national Vuistregels *are* measurable. **None of the three measures a health outcome of a garden.** If a sentence in this note can be read as saying otherwise, the sentence is wrong and should be corrected.
