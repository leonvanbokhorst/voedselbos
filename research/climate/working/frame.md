# Research frame — issue #22 (written before retrieval)

**Question in one sentence.** Can a community-driven, distributed network of edible green spaces in a densifying Eindhoven deliver measurable climate adaptation and thermal comfort, and if not (or not mainly) for cooling, what does it deliver — and under what conditions?

**What a useful answer would change.** It would let the main question stop treating "food forest" as inherently a climate intervention, state which part of the climate claim is supported for Eindhoven, and name the test that would distinguish a cool refuge from a garden that happens to be green.

## Propositions to establish, contest or bound

| # | Proposition (to test) | What would falsify it |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | Eindhoven heat exposure is real, projected to rise, and unevenly distributed across districts and housing types | A city series or municipal statement showing no projected increase; a distributional study showing no social gradient |
| P2 | The city's heat picture is modelled (PET map), not measured; no Eindhoven urban temperature series exists | An urban station network or measurement campaign for the city |
| P3 | Multilayered edible vegetation cools mainly by shade (radiant load, MRT), secondarily by evapotranspiration; the air-temperature effect is small | Studies showing large air-temperature effects, or shade-dominated results for non-edible vegetation being equally strong (would relocate the mechanism, not remove it) |
| P4 | A food forest's cooling is not documented as distinct from generic urban green/woodland; its distinctive value is social/productive, not thermal | A study isolating edible/agroforestry plots against matched non-edible controls on the same site |
| P5 | Food forests carry specific constraints (slow canopy establishment, irrigation in drought, soil contamination, food safety, maintenance) that make them a poor first-choice cooling intervention on heat-critical sites | Evidence that edible systems establish as fast as alternatives, or that irrigation demand is met without municipal water |
| P6 | Proximity ≠ use: a distributed network of small cool places only works if route shade, seating, water, opening hours and perceived safety are present | Evidence that proximity alone predicts use, or that a single large refuge serves the same population |
| P7 | Shared care builds social resilience (weak ties, mutual aid, routine presence) but is not evidence of emergency preparedness and cannot replace statutory responsibility | Documented cases where volunteer care substituted for professional heat response, or evidence of care collapse under heat/drought |
| P8 | Eindhoven's own policy record contains ambition (3-30-300, adaptation programme 2027–2032) but no baseline, measures or funding — so cooling by food forests cannot be claimed as delivered | A published canopy/access baseline and funded heat measures |

## Independent subquestions (delegated)

1. Cooling mechanisms and measured magnitude for urban agroforestry / food forests (literature, measurement method, PET/UTCI).
2. Comparison with alternative interventions, and the agronomic/ecological constraints of edible systems (establishment, water, soil, food safety, habitat).
3. Who is exposed, who reaches and uses cool places; thermal equity, green gentrification, distributed-network evidence.
4. Social resilience, shared care under heat/drought, and the limits of volunteer capacity.
5. (Lead) Eindhoven's own evidence: heat map service, KNMI'23 context, adaptation programme, 3-30-300, densification, existing edible sites.

## Source families

Official/municipal (raadsinformatie, Gemeenteblad, data.eindhoven.nl, klimaateffectatlas, RIVM/KNMI); peer-reviewed measurement and modelling studies; Dutch and international grey literature on cool places and urban agroforestry; counterevidence (null results, critique of community-garden claims, green gentrification); lived experience where documented.

## Boundaries

Desk research only. No stakeholder contact, no field measurement, no implementation, no site allocation. No clinical claims, no causal attribution from overlays, no assumed cooling from the label "food forest", no claim that volunteers replace statutory duties. Existing sibling notes (#19, #20) are not read for steering; #20 is unmerged at the time of writing.

## Access risks known in advance

Municipal pages behind Cloudflare (403 on some), ArcGIS MapServer may be JSON-only, ed.nl paywall, academic full texts behind licences, English/Dutch terminology mismatch ("voedselbos" vs "food forest" vs "urban agroforestry").

## Stop rule

Stop when: the four delegated lines have returned and their pivotal citations have been checked; the Hittekaart 2050 service metadata is verified or its failure classified; the municipal adaptation documents are read; and further searching in the identified families is unlikely to change the answer. Record what remains open rather than expanding the question.
