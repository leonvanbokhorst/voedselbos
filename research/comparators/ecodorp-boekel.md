# Ecodorp Boekel as a comparator for Voedselbos 2.0

**Issue:** #18. Public sources only, retrieved 2026-11-26; no stakeholder contact. Labels: **[D]** documented, **[C]** single-source claim, **[SA]** the community's own 2025 resilience self-assessment, **[I]** inference, **[U]** not established.

## 1. What the case actually is

Ecodorp Boekel sits at the edge of Boekel (NB), between De Maashorst and De Peel against protected forest, on ground the municipality offered in 2012. It is **36 social-rental homes in three circles**, built 2019–21, home to **43 adults and 14 children** (own site; 44 + 15 in a 2024 interview — current count **[U]**). Homes cost c. €128,000 each; the community was its own developer and landlord; social rent repays a **30-year loan** **[D]** ([Gebiedsontwikkeling.nu, 6 Mar 2024](https://www.gebiedsontwikkeling.nu/artikelen/ecodorp-boekel-laat-zien-dat-het-kan-zelf-bouwen-met-brede-maatschappelijke-impact/)). Funding history per the community's own timeline: €50,000 provincial start subsidy (2013), crowdfunding, subsidies, later a bank loan **[D/C]**; a provincial loan of the size implied is unverified **[U]**.

**Two figures need flagging.** The food garden is **0.6 ha** of former farmland in three sources (own garden page, own resilience tracker, issue brief) but **600 m²** on one own page — a tenfold discrepancy; use 0.6 ha **[D]**. And the 2024 claim that the village draws **"60% of its food" from the food forest**, free, by wheelbarrow each Friday **[C]**, is contradicted by the community's own 2025 assessment, which scores food self-provision **1/5: "less than 20% of its own food"** **[SA]**. Treat food-production claims as unverified.

**Initiators.** Ad Vlems (co-initiator; public face — EU committee invitations, ministry advisory, author of the network's VrijCoop explainer) and **Monique Vissers**, the spelling in both retrieved sources that give a surname ([HUMAN, 15 Apr 2017](https://www.human.nl/artikelen/lokale-helden---monique-vissers); Gebiedsontwikkeling.nu 2024); no retrieved source writes "Visser". Her documented role is **co-initiator**, publicly framed as Ad's wife and an account manager by trade — not a board title; formal roles for either are **[U]**.

**Governance:** property sits in a **Vastgoedvereniging (VaVer)** with exactly two members — the residents' **Bewonersvereniging (BeVer)** and **VrijCoop**; homes are never sold; once the loan is repaid, surplus rent is lent to new communities, modelled on the German **Mietshäuser Syndikat** ([GEN-NL, by Ad Vlems](https://gen-nl.nl/vrijcoop-is-betaalbaar-wonen-en-startfinanciering-voor-ecodorpen/)). Decisions use "holarchie": responsibility placed low, work in circles and working groups, every resident in at least one, a three-month joining trajectory **[D]**.

**Labour:** residents give at least 8 hours a week (a 2024 interview says two days); the **tuinploeg is eight residents averaging one day a week**, plus fixed volunteers and a Saturday work day; garden days are Monday, Friday, Saturday **[D]**.

**Ecology:** poor sandy soil improved over years by an own composting system, permanent cover and green manures; January 2023 a **13-species native hedge** on the south and east sides; from November 2023 the outer zone becomes food forest to a plan by Jade Koop (Jadereforestry), pond planned **[D]**. The community's own verdict is sober: native biodiversity **1/5**, regenerative-agriculture impact **1/5** ("modest") **[SA]**.

**Visibility:** 1,100 visitors a year at one weekly tour, two weekly by 2023; monthly open days; EU SDG Award 2023 (winner, SDG 11, one of 17 jury-chosen from 91 finalists); a Gouden Piramide **nomination** in 2022, not a win **[D]**. The **Wereldhuiskamer** — community house and learning space — was permitted only on 19 Feb 2026, with volunteer build weeks April–November 2026, funded by neighbours approaching funds and by donated materials **[D]**. The public-learning infrastructure is still being built.

## 2. Comparator

| | Ecodorp Boekel | Voedselbos Eindhoven / 2.0 |
|---|---|---|
| Land | Owned by the community's VaVer; municipal offer 2012 | Private land, informal permission; use ended July 2026 |
| Legal form | VaVer + BeVer + VrijCoop membership; never sold | No entity named publicly; a coordinator's practice |
| Decision rights | Holarchie, circles, working groups | One coordinator; volunteers |
| Labour | Residents ≥8 h/week; tuinploeg of 8; Saturday work day | c. 30 volunteers; one Friday morning |
| Money | Rent → 30-year loan; provincial start subsidy; crowdfunding | Earned income (workshops, company experiences); no subsidy found |
| Ecology | 0.6 ha permaculture, 13-species hedge, young food forest; self-scored 1/5 on natives | 0.7 ha, twelve years of mature plantings, lost with the site |
| Visibility | Tours, award, education centre under construction | Local reputation; absent from the national open day |

## 3. Transferable, with conditions

1. **Tenure before planting.** Boekel's garden sits on ground the community controls; Eindhoven's did not. *Condition:* a tenure instrument needs a legal person to hold it and a counterparty willing to grant it — the Wasven use agreement shows the municipal form exists.
2. **A legal body that outlives its founders.** The VaVer/BeVer split answers "who owns this when we are gone", and VrijCoop extends it into peer lending. *Condition:* members, bookkeeping, statutes; borrow the pattern, not the housing structure.
3. **Cross-subsidy, not food sales.** Housing rent, not vegetables, carries the food work; food is given away. *Condition:* a food forest alone rarely yields a surplus — pair it with a revenue function (education, care, hospitality) or accept grant dependence openly.
4. **Labour as an obligation attached to membership.** A named weekly team on a fixed roster produces reliability that open volunteering does not. *Condition:* Boekel's version needs people living there; an urban forest needs another binding mechanism — seasons, teams, a paid coordinator hour.
5. **Hosting as a discipline.** Tours, open days and a visitor route create legitimacy, recruits and funder confidence. *Condition:* needs capacity and a host; Boekel scores backup skills 1/5, so this is thinner than it looks.
6. **Ask the municipality for in-kind value, not only cash.** Preparing the ground itself reportedly saved c. €1.5 million; the assessment lists *Right to Challenge* for green management — taking over the budget the municipality would spend on a contractor. *Condition:* needs a business case and an official willing to move.

## 4. Not transferable

1. **Residence.** Eight hours weekly from people whose home it is cannot be reproduced by an urban place whose users go home.
2. **Rural land economics.** Former farmland at a village edge, no competing development claim — the opposite of Eindhoven's land.
3. **Bankability.** Thirty-six homes are collateral; a food forest is not. The 30-year loan has no analogue for planting.
4. **Founder capacity.** Fifteen years of founders' time, national media, EU invitations, ministry advisory: an input, not a method.
5. **Novelty.** Award juries reward firsts. A second urban food forest is not a first.

## 5. Selection effects and founder centrality

Boekel is a **survivor**: 100 interested families in 2008 became 20, then 36 households. The award, not the attrition, is what the record shows. The group is, by a national newspaper's observation, predominantly white, middle-aged and highly educated **[D]**; it scores its own social diversity 3/5 and asks "how do you attract more men?" **[SA]**. Founder centrality is high and openly so: the network explainer, the SDG timeline and most published framing come from the initiators — read self-descriptions as claims. Most useful of all, the community scores itself **1/5 on backup skills, conflict resolution, shared purpose, monitoring and collective memory** **[SA]**. A funded, award-winning, fifteen-year-old ecovillage reports that its knowledge sits with a few people and that it has little shared sense of purpose. Those are the fragilities that ended Voedselbos Eindhoven — and they persist *after* the land question is solved.

## 6. Questions worth putting to the initiators

What did the 100-to-20 attrition teach? What does the garden cost per year and who pays? Who holds the tuinploeg's knowledge when its members leave? Without the housing asset, would you still have started — and would you advise an urban group to seek ownership or a use agreement?

**Not established:** the loan's lender, size and terms; whether provincial support was grant, loan or both; any formal board role for either initiator; the garden's yield and operating cost; whether the Wereldhuiskamer opened; whether the food forest is fenced from public access (the assessment cites a "hek om voedselbos"). Bij Albrecht, Phood and Victoriapark were out of scope here.
