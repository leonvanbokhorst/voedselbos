# Documented first-hand voices — Voedselbos Eindhoven and comparable green-care cases

Working note for issue #19 (what makes caring for green places, other people and the living world
meaningful; how community grows through shared care). Line of investigation: **what people who took
part say publicly**.

- All retrievals **2026-11-26** via `source_read` (URL, sha256, retrieval time recorded by the tool).
- Status key: **retrieved** = page opened and read in full as served; **surfaced** = seen in search
  results / linked from a retrieved page, content not read; **blocked** = access boundary, not bypassed.
- Quoting rule used here: Dutch text is copied exactly from the retrieved representation, with an
  English gloss. Nothing in quotation marks is a paraphrase.
- Evidence type is marked per row: **[P]** participant/first-hand testimony, **[C]** coordinator or
  organiser speaking about participants, **[O]** organisation's claim about itself or its participants,
  **[J]** journalist's characterisation (no verbatim source voice). This distinction matters more than
  anything else in this note: **almost everything retrieved about Voedselbos Eindhoven is [C] or [O];
  direct volunteer [P] testimony was not found in any public source.**

## 2. Synthesis

### 2.1 What the retrieved evidence actually supports

**Established (documented, retrieved):**

1. **The anchor case's public record is written in the coordinator's voice.** Across the site, the
   Supportpunt portrait, the Trefpunt Groen interview and the newsletter, the speaking subject is
   Maartje Kreike/Meerman-Kreike. Every statement about what volunteers felt is hers.
2. **The organiser explicitly describes plural, non-identical motives** — mental clarity for one,
   the being-together for another; ages 72 and 23 (quote 13). This is the strongest retrieved evidence
   against any single-story account of why people came.
3. **Low-commitment participation was a design feature, not a failure**: "Iedereen is vrij om de ene
   keer wel mee te doen en de andere keer niet" (quote 14). The group nonetheless grew from 3 to 20 in
   the 2020/21 winter–spring.
4. **The claimed mechanism for community is shared practical work plus a planned pause.** Work 10:00–12:00,
   then a half hour of making/tasting/herbal tea together; people "stilstaan om met elkaar te praten";
   "gehoord worden" named as the good (quotes 15, 16, 20). Rhythm — every Friday, same hours — is the
   carrier.
5. **Sensory and embodied language dominates the self-description**: hands in the earth, smelling,
   feeling, tasting, "alle zintuigen wakker" (quotes 8, 21). Health appears as *ease, rest, clearer mind,
   energy* — never as a measured outcome (quotes 7, 9, 13).
6. **Learning is peer-to-peer and distributed**: tastes, plant families, making things; "We delen graag
   onze kennis" (quote 16). Curiosity is reported both as an entry route ("gewoon wilt ontdekken wat een
   voedselbos precies is", quote 22) and as an energy input from newcomers (quote 19) and expats (quote 17).
7. **The loss is framed as grief and as the loss of a gathering**, not primarily as loss of produce:
   "Het verdriet is groot" (4), "gemeenschap" grown (24), "Eindhoven heeft géén voedselbos meer" (25).
   The one concrete continuity mechanism named is the coordinator's own mailbox/network and the
   mailing list: the community is claimed to persist as an addressable list and as an intention
   ("Wat zou jij graag willen zien in ons volgende hoofdstuk?", 26), and the successor is described as
   **a herb garden**, not a food forest.
8. **Comparators confirm the plural-motives pattern but give it different weights.** In Bennekel, a
   resident-volunteer ranks climate first, place-attachment second, neighbourhood connection/safety third
   (31). In Wasven's own survey summary, work-learning and *atmosphere/social contact* are the valued
   goods, with "zinvol" as the provider's word (36–38). Among food-forest course participants, the
   valued goods are hope, breadth of knowledge and years of pleasure afterwards (39–42).

**Contested / ambiguous:**

- Whether "community" survived the closure. The coordinator asserts a community grew and that people
  wrote to her (3, 24); no public document from any volunteer corroborates this, and no volunteer group
  statement was found. Treat "the community persists" as the coordinator's claim, not as documented
  participant fact.
- Whether the Eindhoven case was a *food forest* or a *programme hosted on someone else's land*. The
  retrieved self-descriptions emphasise activities, workshops, experiences and meeting; the volunteer
  Friday is one element among many. The press account (eindhoven.nieuws.nl, 25-08-2026) characterises the
  Henri Dunantpark case as "wezenlijk anders van opzet … niet een uitgebreid activiteitenprogramma op
  particulier terrein, maar een groen bewonersinitiatief in een stadspark".
- The coordinator's claim "Eindhoven heeft géén voedselbos meer" (25) is factually contested by the
  Henri Dunantpark residents' initiative described in the same press coverage, which the coordinator's
  sentence does not acknowledge. That is a rhetorically strong but locally inaccurate framing.

**Inference (mine — not a source statement):** the pattern in the retrieved material is that meaning is
ascribed to *the Friday* — a recurring, low-obligation, bodily, sociable slot — more than to the forest as
an ecological object. If that is right, a replacement site would not automatically restore it, and a
herb garden with workshops might. **This is an inference from organiser statements only and is not
established by participant testimony, because participant testimony was not retrievable.**

### 2.3 The silences

1. **No verbatim volunteer quote exists in any public source retrieved.** Not on the site, not in the
   Supportpunt portrait, not in the Trefpunt Groen interview, not in the closure coverage, not in the
   newsletter. Volunteers appear only in the third person and in the aggregate ("een hechte groep",
   "30 vrijwilligers", "iedereen is vrij om …"). The nearest thing to participant first person in the whole
   anchor case is an *organisational* partner (Boes Bos/Itility, quotes 27–28).
2. **Nobody publicly describes stopping, drifting away, or being bored.** No ex-volunteer voice, no
   complaint, no "it was cliquey", no "I preferred working alone". The design explicitly allowed people to
   skip weeks (14), which means the population of lapsed participants certainly existed; none is audible.
3. **No conflict is public.** Not about the land, the owner, the coordinator's role, the closure decision,
   or the municipality. Everything in the public record is harmonious, which is implausible for a
   12-year volunteer project on private land that ended abruptly; the silence should be read as an
   artefact of the channels (see §4), not as evidence of harmony.
4. **Children, school groups and dagbesteding participants are entirely silent.** The site says schools and
   care groups came ("Voor inwoners, scholen, bedrijven en instellingen"); no child, teacher, or day-activity
   participant is quoted anywhere retrieved. Consent for such voices would not normally be given publicly —
   so this silence is expected and should not be read as absence of experience.
5. **Nobody says what they lost in their own words.** The only loss testimony is the coordinator's.
6. **The sector's own reporting quotes no one.** The national Open Voedselbossendag report (43) and the
   Boer&Buur met Natuur / Natuur en Milieufederaties materials describe curiosity and enthusiasm in the
   aggregate without a single visitor sentence.

### 2.4 Case-specific vs generic

**Case-specific to Voedselbos Eindhoven:**
- A *single charismatic coordinator* who is also the practitioner (Natuurlijk Maartje), the newsletter
  owner, the workshop provider and the closure announcer. Meaning and continuity are person-shaped.
- A *hosted* place: private land at Elburglaan 8, adjacent to a municipal/Natuurmonumenten estate, with
  a programme (workshops, wildpluk walks, qigong, cooking, Foodforest Experience, DDW, Lichtbos) layered
  on top of the volunteer Friday.
- *Paid and free registers coexisting*: €35 workshops and corporate experiences alongside free Friday
  volunteering. Comparators either do free community gardening (Bennekel, Buurttuin vol Kleur) or
  funded care/work activity (Wasven, Phood Farm).
- *Immediate, unexplained closure* of a place that had just started a new project (a kitchen garden,
  spring 2026) and had just been incorporated into the Lichtbos programme.

**Generic (appears across the comparators):**
- Plural, individually-specific motives inside one group (13 ↔ 31 ↔ 32).
- Neighbourhood connection and safety as an explicit good (15, 31).
- Learning-and-hope as what courses deliver (39–42), work-plus-atmosphere as what day activity delivers (36–37).
- Adoption of a small patch, often for a child or a specific plant interest (32).
- Small, incomplete, ongoing places ("bouwplaats", 35; "nog volop in ontwikkeling").

## 3. Closure and loss

What the retrieved sources establish:

- Closure was **immediate** (28-07-2026), after an earlier expectation of stopping "na de zomer" on the
  current site (eindhovenvandaag.nl, 18-06-2026, lede only).
- The site's own footer now reads "Permanent gesloten sinds juli 2026" on every page; the address and the
  programme pages remain online (an artefact lag also visible in the agenda, which still listed
  Elburglaan activities after closure).
- The coordinator's public loss language: grief, mailbox overflowing, "niet alleen", a door closing and a
  path opening elsewhere (1–5), and community/friendships as the thing that had grown (24).
- **No participant is quoted about the loss anywhere retrieved.** The press coverage of the closure
  describes the network that formed — "In twaalf jaar tijd ontstond rond het voedselbos ook een netwerk
  van vrijwilligers, bezoekers en mensen die deelnamen aan lessen en workshops" (eindhoven.nieuws.nl,
  25-08-2026) — but quotes no one.
- **Whether the community persists:** the only evidence of persistence is (a) the coordinator's statement
  that people wrote to her with memories and invitations (3), (b) the November 2026 mailing-list
  newsletter and the 26-11-2026 "Voedselbos Community – Workshop" at Student Base, High Tech Campus
  (which the newsletter announces, from the list held by Natuurlijk Maartje), and (c) the mailing list
  itself, which belongs to the individual practice rather than to any association. **No volunteer group,
  legal entity, or collective statement was found.** Persistence is asserted and institutionally hosted,
  not documented as a participant fact.
- The successor object changes: the new location is to be developed "als een kruidentuin", and the
  newsletter says "Eindhoven heeft géén voedselbos meer" (25) — a narrowing of ambition from food forest to
  herb garden, while the food-forest ambition relocated into the individual's paid "Voedselbos Experience".
