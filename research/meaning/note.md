# What makes shared care meaningful — and how community grows through it

Issue #19. Desk research, 26 November 2026. Public sources only; no stakeholder contact; no fieldwork; no intervention or software proposal. Marks as in the rest of the repository: **[D]** documented in a retrieved source, **[C]** claimed by a party or a self-description, **[P]** provided by the commissioner (direct account, not public documentation), **[I]** inference of this note, **[U]** not established in the sources searched. Source list with URLs, read depth and access status in [`sources.md`](sources.md); the raw delegated working notes are in [`working/`](working/README.md) and are not evidence in themselves.

## 1. The answer in short

1. **The public record cannot tell us what participants felt.** The entire documented voice of Voedselbos Eindhoven is the coordinator's. No verbatim volunteer, workshop, wildpluk, day-activity or neighbour account exists in any source reachable without contacting people **[D — negative finding]**. Everything below about *organisation* is documented; everything about *experience* is either a coordinator's characterisation or a literature finding from elsewhere. Misreading that boundary is the main way this note could mislead.

2. **What the case documents is a designed rhythm, not a spontaneous community.** One fixed weekly slot; an explanation at 10:00; two hours of work; a scheduled stop at noon; half an hour of shared tasting and tea; visitors free to walk in from 10:00 to 12:30; a winter dormancy with a published reopening date; and an explicit rule that nobody is obliged to come twice in a row **[D]**. That design is the most transferable thing the case produced, and it is a design, not an outcome.

3. **The most defensible claim about how community grows through shared care is modest and relational.** Across the literature, the motive that brings people in is not the one that keeps them: joining is about the practice (gardening, food, nature, childhood), continuing is disproportionately about people and organisation **[D, N=148 Michigan; D, 23 interviews across 6 Melbourne gardens]**. The best Dutch evidence — seven community gardens — finds that people talk, get to know each other and help each other *even when they are not socially motivated*, and that the social side adds value for the vegetable-motivated too **[D]**. Meaning here is emergent, not a precondition.

4. **Meaning and measured benefit come apart, and should be kept apart.** The systematic review of care farms found people valued "being in contact with each other, and feeling a sense of achievement, fulfilment and belonging" and found **no evidence** that care farms improved quality of life, with some evidence on depression and anxiety **[D]**. That is the single most useful result for this issue: rich meaning is real without being an outcome, which is exactly why #10's attribution question must stay separate.

5. **The binding agent in this case was one person, and the community that survived was an audience.** Organisation, teaching, hosting, communication and the mailing list all ran through one practice; no legal entity, no second named keeper, no committee is documented **[D]**. When the land was sold **[P]** and the site went, the invitations arrived in one mailbox and the newsletter went out under one practice's name **[D]**. A community can persist as audience and practice while failing to persist as a body — and "community as the foundation" inherits that problem.

6. **For the three pillars the implication is about occasions and keepers, not about a new site, organisation, network or platform.** The documented mechanism for relationship is repeated shared work with a low entry threshold, a bounded time cost, an easy exit, someone who hosts, and a scheduled moment where people talk **[D for the design; I for the transfer]**. Nothing in this evidence base requires a central node, a city-wide layer or software; several findings suggest those would be the wrong thing to build first.

## 4. The participant silence — the central limit of this answer

- **No participant voice exists in the public record.** Searches in Dutch and English, the initiative's site and news archive, the Supportpunt portrait, Trefpunt Groen's coverage, local and regional press, and the November 2026 newsletter produced **no verbatim statement by a volunteer, workshop participant, day-activity participant, neighbour or child**. The only first-person participant account found anywhere is organisational and promotional (a company that did a "voedselbos-experience") **[D]**.
- **No ambivalent or negative voice either.** Nobody is on record as bored, lapsed, excluded or obliged. That absence should be read as an artefact of the channels — a login-walled Facebook page, an Instagram account that renders as a script shell, a paywalled regional paper, a Wayback capture that served a CAPTCHA — not as harmony **[I]**. The design explicitly permitted sporadic attendance, so lapsed participants certainly existed; they are simply not audible.
- **Therefore:** the question "what draws people in and what does it mean to them" is *answerable only with people*, and the issue excludes exactly that in this phase **[U]**. What this note can do is (a) establish how the care was organised, (b) establish what the literature says about such settings in general, and (c) specify the smallest set of conversations that would settle the local question (§8).

## 5. What the literature supports, and how strongly

Every row below is reported at the depth stated; only the cited item's own claims are summarised.

**Joining is not sustaining.**

| Finding | Evidence | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Initial motives were "helping the environment and learning"; **commitment** was predicted by "social factors and project organisation" | Ryan, Kaplan, Grese et al. (2001), *J Environ Planning & Management* 44: 629–648; cross-sectional survey, N=148 long-term stewardship volunteers, Michigan **[D, abstract]** | Best direct support; single state, self-report, no baseline |
| Gardener joined for the practice (childhood, fresh food, nature, politics); "some participants cited social reasons for joining, however this was more a driver for sustaining involvement" | Kingsley, Foenander & Bailey (2019), *BMC Public Health* 19: 745; 23 interviews, 6 gardens, Melbourne **[D, full text verified]** | Strong qualitative fit; self-selected, retrospective, no leavers interviewed |
| Social cohesion and mutual help arose "even if people are not particularly driven by social motivations", and the social side "bring[s] added value for those participants who are motivated primarily by growing vegetables" | Veen, Bock, Van den Berg, Visser & Wiskerke (2016), *Local Environment* 21(10): 1271–1287; 7 Dutch community gardens, comparative qualitative **[D, abstract verified at the WUR repository]** | **The best Dutch evidence in this note**; interaction observed, not network change measured |

**Pleasure, not health, is the stated pull.** Among 5,766 UK gardeners, "improving health… was not the prime motivator to garden, but rather the direct pleasure gardening brought" **[D, Chalmin-Pui et al. 2021, *Cities* 112: 103118, cross-sectional]**. In the anchor case the *offer* was health-framed while the *activity* was pleasure-framed (blossom, herbs, Szechuan pepper, tea); the contrast is an inference about framing, not about motive **[I]**.

**Meaning and measured outcome are different objects.**

- Care farms: participants valued "being in contact with each other, and feeling a sense of achievement, fulfilment and belonging"; "We found no evidence that care farms improved people's quality of life and some evidence that they might improve depression and anxiety" **[D, Murray et al. 2019, *Campbell Systematic Reviews* 15(4), verified verbatim]**.
- Volunteering and health: cohorts show favourable effects on depression, life satisfaction and wellbeing (mortality RR 0.78, 95% CI 0.66–0.90) but "these findings were not confirmed by experimental studies" **[D, Jenkinson et al. 2013, *BMC Public Health* 13: 773, 40 papers, abstract]**.
- The one community-gardening RCT (N=291, Denver, observer-blind, waiting-list control) found no significant effect on diet quality, activity, BMI or waist, and modest differences in perceived stress and anxiety whose printed confidence intervals straddle zero **[D, Litt et al. 2023, *Lancet Planetary Health*, abstract]**.
- Nature connectedness correlates with self-reported wellbeing at r≈0.20–0.24 and with pro-environmental behaviour at r≈0.37, but the experimental estimate falls to d≈0.21 once unpublished work is included, with "clear evidence of publication bias" **[D, Pritchard et al. 2019/20; Mackay et al. 2019, abstracts]**.

**How many relationships a place can actually hold.** Personal networks are layered at roughly 5–15–50–150 with rising maintenance costs and falling intimacy **[D, Sutcliffe, Dunbar, Binder & Arrow 2012, *Brit J Psychol* 103(2): 149–168, abstract]**. Applied to a 30-person Friday group, that implies a handful of close ties and a wider ring of acquaintance **[I]**. It is the strongest available constraint on any claim that a place "builds community" at scale, and it matters for Pillar 2.

**Learning is peer-to-peer, and it is the underrated benefit.** Melbourne gardeners: "hard learning out of books", learning happens "around other gardeners" **[D]**; allotment-holders in Newcastle learn from neighbours and some "carry their plots with them for decades" **[D, Schoneboom 2013, *ephemera* 13(1): 137–150, ethnography + 35 interviews]**. In the anchor case the practice was explicitly organised for this — "kijken wat iemand wil doen en waar zijn/haar talenten liggen", and a half hour in which "de ene persoon leert ons over verschillende soorten smaken, een ander weet veel over plantenfamilies" **[C]**.

**Commons are institutions, not goodwill.** Long-lived commons share eight design regularities — boundaries, congruence with local conditions, collective choice, monitoring, graduated sanctions, cheap conflict resolution, recognised rights, nested enterprises — and failures lack them **[D, Ostrom 2009 Nobel lecture, reading Cox et al.'s >100 cases]**. Free-riding and uneven contribution appear as ordinary garden problems in the same Melbourne interviews ("inconsistent contributions by and conflict between members") **[D]**. For the anchor case the interesting columns are the ones that were **absent**: no documented membership boundary, no documented collective decision over what to plant, no monitoring, no conflict arena, and — the structural one — no recognised right to the ground **[D/I]**.

## 6. Counter-evidence and rival interpretations

The issue asked for the contradictions, so they lead here rather than trailing.

1. **Care as pleasure and care as obligation are not opposites.** In one allotment ethnography the same caregiver calls the plot "incredibly relaxing and fruitful" and "stress-inducing at the same time", describes guilt when it is neglected, and faces the site's own "weed warnings" **[D]**. Obligation there is the condition of the pleasure, not its negation. For the anchor case there is **no evidence** that anyone felt obliged **[U]** — but the design feature that would have prevented it ("vrij om de ene keer wel… en de andere keer niet") is also, precisely, what makes close ties unlikely **[I]**.
2. **Belonging and conformity are the same mechanism seen from two sides.** Gardeners describe finding "like-minded people"; the recognition literature locates esteem in "networks of solidarity and shared values"; the commons literature makes clear boundaries between users and non-users its first principle **[D]**. A community that makes some people legible makes others legible as outsiders. No source records who was outside at Elburglaan **[U]**.
3. **Shared space does not guarantee encounter.** The social-infrastructure optimism could not be verified at source here (Klinenberg and Oldenburg were not retrievable), and what was retrieved argues the opposite: a peer-reviewed critique insists that outcomes of shared space "cannot be assumed" and are shaped by power, procurement and antagonism **[D, Horton & Penny 2023, abstract]**; a feminist critique argues the concept depoliticises care by naming spaces rather than labour, gender and reproduction **[D, Hall 2020, abstract]**.
4. **Stated meaning may be post-hoc justification.** Retrospective reports of changed outlook and of meaning are equally compatible with genuine change and with accounting for years already given; no retrieved study tests this by comparing entry-time and exit-time accounts **[D/I]**. A future inquiry that asks people afterwards will inherit the problem.
5. **Participation staged as display is not participation.** The canonical ladder distinguishes manipulation and therapy from real power sharing, and describes advisory bodies "trotted forward at appropriate times" **[D, Arnstein 1969]**. If a successor's visibility work shows participants without giving them decisions, this is the exact failure mode it enters — a *power* diagnosis, not an image problem.
6. **Meaning may be place-bound and non-transferable.** The only retrieved bodies of work that take place loss seriously are solastalgia and environmental-change distress, which concern landscapes and homes, not voluntary associations losing a plot; the concept's own scoping review calls the field conceptually unsettled **[D]**. Nobody has studied grief for a lost community garden, allotment or volunteer community **[U — genuine gap, not absence of the experience]**. So the step from "the coordinator grieved" to "the community grieved" is analogy **[I]**, and the step from "we grew a community" to "2.0 inherits it" is unwarranted on this evidence. The trigger matters for that reading: the practice did not end because interest ran out but because the ground was sold **[P]**, which is a tenure event, not a participation event.
7. **The strongest rival to all of it: the green setting is the pretext and people come for company.** The Dutch garden study supports this more than the nature-motivation story does, and the commitment evidence points the same way **[D]**. The counter-consideration is that 5,766 gardeners named the direct pleasure of gardening, not company, as their prime motive **[D]**. Both can be true at different stages, which is what the staged models of volunteering predict **[D]**.

**A specific rival interpretation of visibility (#17) worth carrying forward.** If visibility is *mutual recognition among people and initiatives who share a practice* rather than publicity to an audience, then the mechanism is recognition, and the theory says esteem is granted inside "networks of solidarity and shared values"—an outside audience can confer attention, not esteem **[D, Honneth 1995, translator's introduction]**. No retrieved study tests this among garden or food-forest initiatives **[U]**. It should be treated as a hypothesis with falsifiers, not a finding. The falsifiers that would settle it are stated in [`working/relational-counterevidence.md`](working/relational-counterevidence.md) §4; the most practical one: if exit and resentment cluster around moments of being displayed (a photo, a visit, a grant application) rather than around workload, recognition rather than publicity is the live problem.

## 7. Against the three pillars, and the "community as foundation" premise

Consistent with the rest of this repository, these are implications for how the pillars are *framed*, not proposals.

**Pillar 1 — De Wielewaal as central green motor.** This evidence adds nothing to the case for centrality and something against it: the documented generator of relationship was a *weekly slot with a keeper*, not a large site **[D/I]**. A 145-hectare estate run by a national NGO with 4 paid staff and ~100 volunteers **[D, elsewhere in this repository]** already possesses the thing the food forest lacked; what it does not possess is a low-threshold slot for a neighbouring group to do its own work in its own rhythm. If the pillar means "a motor that hosts others' practices", that is a concrete hosting question. If it means "the centre of the network", the meaning evidence does not support it.

**Pillar 2 — connect existing initiatives into a city-wide ecosystem.** The one mechanism with Dutch evidence behind it is shared practice, not shared infrastructure: people get to know each other by working alongside each other, and mutual help emerges from that **[D]**. A city-wide layer that produces listings and meetings produces acquaintance among organisers **(which is real)** but not the peer learning or mutual help that the gardens produced **[I]**. The tie-limit evidence also says a city-sized network cannot be a community: expect a handful of close relations per node and a wide ring of recognition **[I]**. Cheapest faithful form: recurring joint work-and-taste occasions between two initiatives, of which this repository's experiment C is already an example.

**Pillar 3 — nature closer to everyday life.** The case offers a documented, low-cost design for exactly this: a fixed weekly window, a stated end time, no commitment, visitors free to walk in, a seasonal reopening date, and something to taste **[D]**. Note what is *not* documented: any measure that this increased anyone's contact with nature, any reach figure, any retention figure **[U]**. The design is transferable; the claim is not.

**"Community as the foundation" (the premise in QUESTION.md).** The case supports a corrected version. What the food forest produced was a practice, an audience and a set of relationships held by one person; what it did not produce — in any retrieved source — was a body able to hold a lease, make a collective decision, or speak for itself after the site went **[D]** **[I]**. "Community first" is right as a value and insufficient as a structure: the community that existed could not be the party that secured the ground, and the ground was what ended the community's practice. That is the same finding as the continuity note, arrived at from the meaning side.

## 8. Conversation prompts, for when there is consent

Written to be genuinely open — no prompt assumes that participation was meaningful, that the person misses it, or that community is desirable. These are for a later, separately agreed phase; nothing here has been asked of anyone.

1. **First time:** "How did you end up at the food forest the first time — and what did you do that morning?"
2. **What kept you:** "Was there a moment you decided to come back? What was it?" *(and, for anyone who stopped: "What made you stop?")*
3. **The work itself:** "Describe a Friday you remember. What were you doing, who was there, what did it smell or taste like?"
4. **Use and competence:** "Did you learn something there you now use somewhere else? Did you teach anyone anything?"
5. **Being useful or being in the way:** "Was there a moment you felt useful? Was there a moment you felt in the way, or that you didn't know why you were there?"
6. **The social half-hour:** "What was the half hour after the work like for you? Was it the point, or was it something you could take or leave?"
7. **Since July:** "What do you miss, if anything? What have you not missed at all?"
8. **The successor:** "If something starts again, what should it keep from the old place, and what should it do differently?" *(Not: "would you join?" — an offer dressed as a question.)*
9. **Who was not there:** "Who would you have liked to see at the food forest who never came? What would have made it easier for them?"
10. **Stopping:** "What would have made you decide to stop coming?"
11. **For neighbours who never came:** "What did the food forest do to your street? Did you ever go through the gate?"
12. **For other initiatives:** "Where do you get advice about your plants/your ground/your volunteers? Who do you ask?" *(A recognition question that is not a networking pitch.)*

## 12. Dead ends, and what would change the answer

**Dead ends (searched, not found — absence of evidence, not evidence of absence):**

- Any verbatim participant account for the anchor case, in any medium reachable without a login.
- Any ambivalent, lapsed, excluded or critical participant voice for the anchor case or its Eindhoven comparators.
- Any study of **grief for a lost garden, allotment or volunteer community**; solastalgia and ecological grief concern landscapes and homes and are lenses here, not evidence.
- Any application of self-determination theory to community gardening, food forests or nature volunteering that tests continuation or quitting.

**What would change the answer, in order of value:** (1) ten to fifteen entry-and-exit accounts from people who came to Elburglaan, including at least three who stopped coming — this alone would convert most of §4–§6 from inference to evidence; (2) any record of the November 2026 workshop and what participants asked for; (3) the council's PvdD file on the food forest, which is the likeliest place a public body has described the community; (4) a longitudinal volunteer study with baseline measures at entry — none exists for any comparable Dutch initiative; (5) evidence that the mailing list functions as a community rather than an audience (a reply rate, a shared channel, a decision taken by subscribers).

## 13. Adjacent questions noticed, not researched

- **#9 "who is the community"** — this note assumes the category is contested; the case shows at least four candidate communities (volunteers, visitors, paying Experience groups, day-activity participants). Not resolved here.
- **#5 reciprocity** — the design created a knowledge exchange (tasting, teaching) but no documented material exchange; whether that counts as reciprocity is that issue's question.
- **#14 history** — the founding by two people around 2013 (the Supportpunt portrait says "tien jaar geleden" when written after October 2023; the closure says "meer dan 12 jaar") and the 2019 handover are documented only in the coordinator's telling; the founders' account would matter for the "how community grows" question.
- **The municipal participation report for Natuurcentrum De Wielewaal (Elburglaan 12, May 2025)** — neighbours' written reactions to the neighbouring nature centre; about parking and a pavilion, not the food forest, but the closest public record of neighbours' voices in the immediate area.
- **The initiative's claim to be "onderdeel van het Lichtbos"** (November 2025) — a municipal-programme relationship that no retrieved municipal document confirms.
