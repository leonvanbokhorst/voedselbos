# Three low-threshold experiments that would actually test shared care

**Issue #11.** Desk design only, 26 November 2026; no stakeholder contact, no implementation. Labels: **[D]** documented in `research/`, **[C]** claim, **[I]** inference, **[U]** not established, **[A]** assumption of this note. Every proposal is conditional on consent from every named party; none assumes willingness.

## 1. What the evidence forces on the design

1. **Tenure is not a shared-care problem** (`../continuity/note.md`). Mutual help does not overturn a landowner's decision, and the municipal route is closed at the top end: `Beleidsregel` art. 10 rejects community management of green of "middel of hoog" value, directing it to nature managers **[D]**. No experiment should position initiatives as prospective managers of municipal green.
2. **The burden that breaks people is administrative** (`../participation/note.md`): `regeldruk` — subsidy procedures, legal-person duties — named by the municipality's own volunteer programme **[D]**. Anything needing an entity, bank account, permit or budget line is not low-threshold.
3. **Volunteers want bounded, exitable, visible, social commitments**; the base is ageing and the "supervrijwilliger" holding three offices is normal **[D]**. Every experiment below has an end date and a one-line exit.
4. **Operator concentration, not volunteer collapse, ended the food forest**; a funded fifteen-year ecovillage still scores itself 1/5 on backup skills and collective memory **[D]**. Second-line capacity and knowledge are the scarce goods.
5. **Coordination infrastructure already exists, cheaply**: the greening-wishes register records resident proposals (124) **[D]**; Trefpunt Groen runs eight Groen Cafés a year with Wasven, Bibliotheek, KNNV and IVN **[D]**; Supportpunt advises foundations on administration and boards **[D]**; the International Green Café runs English walks and movie nights **[D]**.

   **Correction, 26 November 2026** — this item previously also counted "the Klusbus … a scheduled municipal neighbourhood circuit (`locaties-klusbus`, 22 locations 2023/2024)" as existing coordination infrastructure. That is wrong: the Eindhovense Klusbus is an **energy-poverty service**, run with the housing corporations on a national SPUK for "de aanpak van energiearmoede", which visited 15,759 households before it stopped ("De Klusbus stopt maar krijgt wel een vervolg in een ander jasje"), and the 22 rows in the dataset are **neighbourhoods, not depots** **[D, Raadsinformatiebrief 24bst00251; eindhovenduurzaam.nl Klusbus]**. The correction is recorded here rather than silently replacing the sentence; it was raised by issue [#5](https://github.com/leonvanbokhorst/voedselbos/issues/5) and verified at source by the Lead. The point that cheap coordination infrastructure exists still stands on the three items above, which are unaffected — see [`../reciprocity/note.md`](../reciprocity/note.md) §8.7 for the corrected inventory.

Precedent to copy: Ecodorp Boekel's reliability comes from *a named team on a fixed roster* — labour attached to a commitment — while its knowledge stays with a few people **[D]**. Both halves are usable.

## 2. Selection criteria

All six must hold: (a) **≥2 named initiatives**; (b) **low burden** — no new entity, budget line or permit, ≤1 coordination day per party; (c) **reversible** — stoppable after one instance with nothing to unwind; (d) **voluntary** — individual opt-in, any party may veto without explanation; (e) **a learning question that could come back negative**; (f) **fair risk/benefit** — whoever carries most operational risk is not whoever gains least. Two hard limits throughout: **ecological** (no work on sensitive habitat; tasks agreed with the site's own manager) and **safeguarding** (no care participants moved between organisations' care regimes; no identifiable images without per-person consent).

## 3. Experiment A — the half-day swap (Wasven × the Voedselbos community)

**What.** Two mirrored dates. First, up to six Voedselbos volunteers join one of Wasven's existing Saturday mornings (klusochtend 2nd/4th Saturday 09:30–12:30, or the Natuurwerkgroep's 1st/3rd) **[D]**. Then the same number of Wasven volunteers join one bounded session the Voedselbos community itself defines — the herb-garden plot if one exists **[A/U]**.

**Hypothesis.** Cross-initiative labour transfers only if the host can absorb strangers into an existing routine without creating a supervision task. If induction exceeds ~30 minutes per volunteer, or a task must be designed for them, the economics fail even with goodwill.

**If it worked.** A one-page induction sheet exists (safety, tools, what not to touch, who to ask) and the next step is a standby list held by the hosts — not a new organisation.

**Success signals.** ≥5 volunteers each direction; the host asks for a second date unprompted; ≤1 coordination day per party; no task redesign; one volunteer reports something usable back home.

**Stop rules.** Stop after one instance if induction exceeds 45 minutes per person, a coordinator spends more than half a day preparing, a task has to be invented, or a safety/insurance question cannot be answered before the date.

**Resources [A].** ~12 volunteer hours; ~4 hours host coordination; ~2 hours community organiser; €0–50. No software: a text thread and one A4.

**Risk.** The host carries supervision, insurance and site safety; volunteers carry travel and unfamiliar tools. Since Wasven is staffed and insured and the Voedselbos community is not, the riskier direction is the Voedselbos one and it runs second.

**Consent and safeguards.** Whether the volunteer group still exists is **[U]**, so the experiment must be able to run as Wasven × a named set of individuals instead. No photographs, no individual names, tasks confined to tuinderij/klus areas and not ecologically managed zones.

## 4. Experiment B — "Twaalf jaar op één A4" (Supportpunt × Trefpunt Groen × the Voedselbos community)

**What.** One Groen Café slot (TGE programs eight a year with partners **[D]**), one Supportpunt advisory session, one two-page document: what twelve years on that soil taught, what a group should secure before planting, and a handover checklist (tenure, insurance, entity, second person, archive). Authored or approved by the community, edited by Supportpunt, hosted and circulated by TGE.

**Rationale.** The cheapest network product is the lesson from a loss; the closure produced sympathy and invitations but no public account of what was learned **[D/I]**. Supportpunt exists for exactly this and was used only for a website **[D]**.

**Hypothesis.** A network can convert a closure into transferable knowledge at near-zero cost and other groups will use a checklist they did not commission. The negative result is equally useful: if nobody uses it, "shared learning" is a story the ecosystem tells about itself.

**If it worked.** TGE's network gains a continuity resource; Supportpunt gains a repeatable clinic; the next group negotiating private land has something to hold. A second edition by a different author is the real signal.

**Success signals.** ≥1 other initiative adopts a checklist item within six months (observable without asking: a lease, a named second person, a public status page); the document is linked by a body other than its authors; the community says the published version is accurate.

**Stop rules.** Stop if the community declines, authorship becomes contested, Supportpunt's help would require a client relationship not agreed, or the draft drifts from "what we learned" to "what we achieved".

**Resources [A].** An existing Groen Café slot; ~8 hours Supportpunt; ~6 hours author time; ~4 hours TGE; €0–150. No new software: an existing site and a PDF.

**Risk.** Representational: the community's closure becomes teaching material. Mitigation: authorial control, a right to withdraw at any time, no fundraising attached, no framing of the closure as a named person's mistake.

**Consent.** Two separate consents — to be a case, and to be named. If refused, the format runs on another case (Wasven's 2026 use agreement; a Dunantpark organiser), which still tests the mechanism but loses the twelve-year comparison.

## 5. Experiment C — shared hosting, English, twice (International Green Café × Wasven, room at LAB-1)

**What.** Two English evenings co-hosted by the **International Green Café** (walks, movie nights, cleanups; currently recruiting co-organisers **[D]**) and **Wasven** (site, substance, volunteers), in a hired room at **LAB-1** (Expats strand, weekly conversation clubs **[D]**). Format: a short walk, one film, thirty minutes with the people who do the work; content from what the hosts already maintain.

**Hypothesis.** Co-hosting distributes cost rather than multiplying it: each party contributes under four hours, and the audience differs measurably from any single party's own (more internationals, more first-timers).

**If it worked.** A quarterly slot owned by no one, with a rotating substantive host — the cheapest form of movement infrastructure. Wasven or Phood host the second edition; the food forest's successor is a segment, not the subject.

**Success signals.** All three want a second evening; ≤4 hours prep each; ≥30% of attendees new to all three (a show of hands, not a tracking system); one attendee signs up for a work morning.

**Stop rules.** Stop if any party's prep exceeds a day, if room cost forces ticket pricing that excludes the neighbourhood audience, if attendance is only the already-converted, or if content slides into promoting one organisation.

**Resources [A].** LAB-1 room hire (real cost; **[U]**, perhaps €150–400, possibly in-kind); ~4 hours per party; a title already screened by a partner to avoid a licence; ~€50 refreshments. No new software: existing newsletters and accounts.

**Risk.** LAB-1 carries commercial risk, Wasven site/staff time, the Green Café reputational risk. Benefit is shared and roughly equal, which is why this is the safest to run first.

**Consent.** Each party's own communications decision; English-first is a distributional choice (`../visibility/cultural-intermediaries.md`) and should come with a Dutch-language version of any walk; no filming without consent at the door.

## 6. Rejected candidates

| Joint funding bid / shared procurement | (b) entity and months; (c) commits parties before trust exists |
| Umbrella foundation for legal-person duties | (b) — the municipality's `Vrijwillig voor elkaar` is already designing it; duplicating policy is not testing care |
| Shared tools / seed / compost depot | (b) needs a site, keyholder, liability owner; (e) learning question already answered elsewhere |

## 7. What would make us stop

Stop all three if: participation costs a party a volunteer it could not spare; the same two or three people appear in every experiment (the supervrijwilliger pattern reproduced at network level **[D]**); an experiment needs a coordination role nobody will fund after the pilot; consent is given by an organisation on behalf of individuals who were not asked; a site's manager says the visits are too much for the ground; or the only output is content. One experiment failing its own rules is a result. Two failing because the network cannot spare the people is an answer to the main question.

## 8. Where AI helps

A one-page roster and a text thread do everything these need; software would create the burden the design avoids. The narrow exception is **transcription and translation**: one spoken session into a bilingual A4 (Experiment B) saves a real hour, with a human checking names and claims. Anything with memory — a live directory, a matching engine, an agent answering enquiries — becomes a maintained object, i.e. the `regeldruk` these proposals exist to avoid.
