**Verbatim quotations used**

1. Pfeffer & Salancik's central proposition as stated by B3: "The theory's central proposition is that, in order to reduce uncertainty in the flow of needed resources from the environment, organizations will try to manage their resource dependencies with a variety of tactics, such as coopting or absorbing the sources of dependence into the organizational structure (Pfeffer and Salancik, 1978)."
2. The standard critique, in the authors' words (abstract): "Despite the ubiquitous references to Pfeffer and Salancik's (1978) classic volume, resource dependence theory stands in organizational discourse as an appealing general metaphor, rather than a foundation for testable empirical research."
3. "However, in spite of the evident appeal of resource dependence imagery, empirical support for the key prediction stemming from the theory is surprisingly limited (Pfeffer, 1987)."
4. "Consequently, resource dependence theory has acquired the status of a powerful general metaphor, but has been marginalized as a foundation for testable empirical research (Pfeffer and Salancik, 2003)."
5. The reformulation's core: "Under conditions of power imbalance, the dependent organization will be willing, but not able to take action to reduce constraint."
6. Hypothesis 1: "The likelihood of constraint absorption operations is negatively associated with power imbalance."
7. "In contrast with the predictions advanced in the original formulation of resource dependence theory, we hypothesize that higher levels of an organization's asymmetric dependence on other organizations will actually reduce that organization's ability to take action to manage environmental dependencies."
8. Mechanism: "power imbalance reduces the frequency of exchange among social actors by hindering conflict resolution (Lawler et al., 1993)."
9. "Unequal power, therefore, introduces issues of legitimacy and fairness concerning the distribution of payoffs from the exchange, thus complicating the bargaining agenda…"
10. Definitional basis, Emerson quoted by B3: "power is a property of the social relation; it is not an attribute of the actor" (Emerson, 1962: 32).
11. Dependence defined: "An actor i, therefore, is dependent upon actor j (1) in proportion to i's need for resources which j can provide, and (2) in inverse proportion to the ability of other actors to provide the same resources to i."

**Verbatim quotations used**

1. "Its main claim is that 'Increasing concentration enhances the life chances of specialist organizations' (Carroll and Hannan 1995, p. 217)."
2. "Along with the proliferation of specialists, the theory claims that, as a consequence of increasing concentration, the niche overlap and competition between specialists and generalists decreases (i.e., resource partitioning)."
3. Core sentence 4 of the theory, quoted by B5 from Carroll & Hannan 1995: "This lessened crowding of generalists and their move to the center opens up small pockets of resources on the periphery of the market, and it is here that specialist forms usually appear and thrive."
4. Core sentence 7, quoted by B5: "When the market is not highly concentrated, specialist organizational forms will not do as well as they do when it is highly concentrated."
5. The theory's scope condition: "Notice that for resource partitioning theory, it does not matter whether resources, e.g., consumer tastes, change while generalist organizations do not, or whether generalist organizations change their position with respect to the resource base." and "The key predictive variable in the model is the overall level of market concentration."
6. Inertia assumption: "In organizational ecology, organizations are seen as inert, which means most of them cannot adapt flexible to their environment (Hannan and Freeman 1984) … Generalists do not become specialists or vice versa."
7. **Verbatim quotations used**

2. "There are many ways to construct preference relations from observed choices."
3. The behaviourist position: "Many (but certainly not all) economists have interpreted the dominant revealed preference theory in behaviourist terms … Furthermore, they also reject the interpretation of preferences as causes of choice, instead insisting that they only capture choice patterns."
4. The mentalist objection: "Mentalists, in contrast, insist that choices and preferences are entities of quite different categories: Preferences are states of mind whereas choices are actions."
5. The objecting argument that matters for us: "Another argument against behaviourist interpretations points to the apparent existence of preferences over alternatives that one cannot choose between — for example preferences for winning a certain prize of a lottery, or for particular configurations of Paradise. 6. **The decisive passage for this study's warning:** "mentalists also distinguish between those agents who indeed have preferences as states of minds — e.g. humans, and maybe higher animals — and those agents who do not — e.g. machines, plants or institutions."
7. Choice depends on the option set (Sen, quoted by B6): "More generally, property α has been contested with reference to cases when alternatives are preferred for their position in an alternative set, when the set of alternatives itself constitutes important information about the alternative chosen, or when certain alternatives provide the chooser with the freedom to reject them (Sen 1993, 501–503)."
8. Preference construction: "Instead they claim that total preferences are constructed at the moment of elicitation, and thus influenced by contexts and framings of the elicitation procedure that are not encoded in pre-existing partial preferences."
