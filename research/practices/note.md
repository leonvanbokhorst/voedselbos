# Which existing Eindhoven practices create conditions for people to enjoy caring for their environment together?

Issue #20. Desk research, 26 November 2026. Public sources only; no stakeholder contact; no fieldwork; no platform or intervention design. Marks as in the rest of the repository: **[D]** documented in a retrieved source, **[C]** claimed by a party or a self-description, **[P]** provided by the commissioner (direct account, not public documentation), **[I]** inference of this note, **[U]** not established in the sources searched. Per-case detail, source tables and access logs are in [`working/`](working/README.md); the source list is [`sources.md`](sources.md).

## 1. The answer in short

1. **The four named practices are real, but they are not four versions of the same thing.** They are four different *hosting arrangements*: a 25-year residents' association with a management agreement on ground that has had a green zoning since 2007 (De Vergeten Tuin); a housing corporation's disused yard turned into a buurttuin by a commissioned social designer with paid concierges since 2023 (De Buurtwerf); a volunteer-maintained garden that rides on a municipal buurthuis currently in a transfer year (Buurttuin Philipsdorp at Bellefort); and a 100 m² plot made available by a temporary project platform, handed to a WhatsApp group within months (De Block Acker) **[D]**. Treating them as comparable "community gardens" would flatten the very differences that matter.

2. **Hosting and coordination recur in the cases; their relationship to durability is a hypothesis, not an established cause.** The four descriptions identify a municipal green zoning and management agreement, a housing corporation, a municipal buurthuis arrangement and a developer's living lab, together with named coordination roles or contacts **[D/I]**. The most securely documented long-running case is De Vergeten Tuin; the temporary-platform case has the least documented tenure and its 2026 status is unknown **[U]**. These four differently documented cases suggest that continuity of hosting and care deserves investigation. They do not establish that enthusiasm or other human motivations matter less, nor that Block Acker stopped.

3. **Some practices make room for presence without productivity.** Free adoption of a plot maintained collectively (Buurtwerf), a recurring open work day (Vergeten Tuin) and stated open access with monthly work days (Block Acker) document different entry arrangements **[D/C]**. The Philipsdorp tuinteam explicitly describes daily non-gardening uses: children playing, yoga, reading, laptop work, sunbathing and dog walking **[D/C]**. This is particularly relevant to a green place as everyday social infrastructure. Whether people feel welcome without contributing in the other cases remains **[U]**.

4. **Visible work is a candidate recruitment mechanism.** From the Philipsdorp tuinteam: "Door actief aanwezig te zijn in de tuin en te werken komen veel buurtbewoners kijken en vragen stellen, zo werven wij weer nieuwe mensen die een handje mee willen helpen" **[C]**. This is a practitioners' account, not a measured comparative effect. Whether it transfers to the other cases and what it costs are open.

5. **The public record documents hosting much more fully than lived experience.** The retrieved first-person material includes a resident-organiser explaining her motives (Laura Astola, Buurtwerf, 2023), a resident-organiser describing children's art at Bellefort (2026), a volunteer's brief recollection (Vergeten Tuin, 2025), the collective first-person association account and curated Wasven quotations. These are valuable but partial voices **[D/C]**; no sustained set of current gardeners' accounts across the four cases was retrieved **[U]**. Organiser and intermediary descriptions cannot stand in for all participants' experiences.

6. **The counterexamples do not take the shape the issue expected.** Of five candidates for "struggled, changed or stopped", only Voedselbos Eindhoven is documented as stopped. **Struikroven** is documented as having changed form — "begon ooit als bewonersinitiatief en is nu uitgegroeid tot een gewoonte binnen het Duurzaamheidspact" (gemeente Eindhoven, 25 January 2021) and now a national foundation working on commission **[D]**. **Buurttuin vol Kleur** turns out to be running, not stopped, as a youth-work programme with paid supervisors and a €5,000 funder record for spring 2026 **[D]** — the earlier reading of it as a one-season garden was wrong and should not be repeated. **Institutionalisation, not collapse, is what the record mostly shows**; and that means the community character changes even when the garden survives **[I]**.

7. **What this can and cannot teach 2.0.** It can teach the conditions under which *hosted* practice works: a host with continuity interest, one named keeper, a bounded recurring slot, free and low-commitment entry, visible work, small scale, and permission to be present without producing. It cannot teach what any of it meant to the people involved, because they are not in the record. The next step is not more desk research; it is a small number of conversations, listed in §8.

## 2. Method, and the date problem the issue predicted

- **Scope.** The four named cases plus contrasting practices, Wasven as an institutional reference, and Voedselbos Eindhoven as the repository's existing reference case. Public sources only, retrieved 26 November 2026 with signed `source_read` requests. No contact, no forms, no logins, no closed groups.
- **The date problem is worse than the issue warned.** The Block Acker description is a Trefpunt Groen network page whose text is **unchanged since at least 6 December 2023** (identical Wayback digests), so the only substantial account of that garden is a late-2023 snapshot **[D]**. Buurttuin Philipsdorp's live site carries no dates at all; its founding document is from 2012 and its realisation document from July 2022 **[D]**. De Vergeten Tuin's own history page stops around 2007 and its homepage still advertises a membership price the association's own "Word lid" page contradicts **[D]**. The 2023 Buurtwerf article and the housing corporation's magazine version are **the same copy and the same quotes** — one witness, not two **[D]**.
- **Evidence discipline.** Participants' own words, organisers' promotional claims and this note's inferences are kept apart, and absence of a public record is reported as exactly that. Membership and volunteer numbers are reported as reported and never used to infer vitality, motive or relationship. Where two sources disagree (membership fee; who may enter the Vergeten Tuin), both are given with their dates and the disagreement is left open.

## 3. The four case portraits

### 3.1 De Vergeten Tuin, Gestel — a residents' association with recognised ground

A 1,800 m² enclosed heemtuin behind the Palingstraat, "opgezet en onderhouden door Vereniging Veldwerk, een groepje vrijwilligers uit de buurt rondom de Palingstraat" **[D, association site]**. Origin: the plot "dreigde lang een openbare vuilnisbelt te worden. Omwonenden hadden in **2001** een beter idee"; Trefpunt Groen adds the trigger — remediation of contamination from the **Capax factory** was scheduled, and "omwonenden organiseerden zich in Vereniging Veldwerk en wisten het veld na de sanering te behouden" **[D]**. The association's own history page dates the design to 2002 and opening to 2006; the **25-year celebration was 6 September 2026**, visited by mayor Dijsselbloem **[D]**.

What makes it structurally different from the others is the ground: "In **2007** kreeg de grond waarop de Vergeten Tuin ligt **een groenbestemming**… **Vorig jaar ondertekende Vereniging Veldwerk een beheersovereenkomst met de gemeente**" **[D]**. Land ownership is unstated in every source **[U]** — the security is a zoning decision plus a management agreement, not ownership.

Practice: open **every first Sunday of the month, 12:00–16:00**; membership €7.50 a year (the homepage says €5 — the "Word lid" page and Trefpunt agree on €7.50, so the homepage is out of date) **[D]**; opened to the public only on those days — and the sources differ on whether neighbours may enter at other times, Trefpunt saying the garden is "alleen toegankelijk voor de mensen die eromheen wonen, behalve op de Open Veldwerkdagen" while the association's own page says access is only on open work days **[D, unresolved]**. Scale of care: **~180 members and ~25 core volunteers** on undated association pages; an independent 2007 print source records **150 members**, mostly neighbours **[D]**. So roughly one in seven members carries the work — and the association's news carries a "**Penningmeester gezocht**" notice from 4 September 2024 **[D]**.

Voice: the association writes in the collective first person — "De meesten van ons waren niet bijzonder bezig met de natuur voordat we hier kwamen wonen, maar door de tuin zijn we er bij betrokken geraakt. Een ander effect van de tuin is dat buurtgenoten elkaar erdoor ontmoeten en beter leren kennen." That is the closest thing to a participant account in the whole set, and it is an association statement rather than a named person's testimony. A volunteer of the first hour, Kees de Kort, is quoted in a regional paper via the site: "Dat er in de binnenstad zoiets is, ja, dat is echt een cadeautje" **[D]**.

### 3.2 De Buurtwerf, Bennekel — an installed practice

A former Woonbedrijf yard behind a row of houses next to community centre De Dommel, which "jaren lag het er ongebruikt en leeg bij" **[D]**. The decisive sentence for this issue is the reason it did not happen earlier: "**Het idee hiervoor lag er al langer, maar de motivatie in de buurt ontbrak**" **[D, 30 May 2023]**. What changed was not motivation but commissioning: "**Sociaal ontwerper Lian Kroes**… **kreeg vanuit Woonbedrijf de opdracht** om hier een levendige plek van te maken en bewoners te betrekken", with the corporation renovating the buildings and providing water, power and a meeting room **[D]**. An already-organised resident — Laura Astola of Groen Bennekel — became the resident anchor **[D]**.

Practice: residents "adopteren hier **gratis een stukje tuin**" and grow their own, "maar uiteindelijk **onderhouden en delen ze de tuin samen. En leren ook van elkaar**" **[D]**. Open **Saturdays 11:00–14:00** in 2023, and more often "dankzij **Doe mee met 4W**": a social-work company of Ergon with Woonbedrijf and care organisation Archipel whose workers "hebben sinds april een **conciërgerol** op de woensdagmiddag en vrijdagochtend. Ze beheren de Buurtwerf en helpen met klussen" **[D]**. By 2026 the yard is still the anchor for neighbourhood action days (45 m² of tiles lifted there on 27 September 2025) and for a weekly Sunday clean-up group that meets and ends there, "vrijblijvend": "Je bepaalt zelf wanneer, hoe vaak en hoeveel minuten je meedoet" **[D]**. Infrastructure is material: a rainwater tank, an old window frame turned into a greenhouse, planters built with a contractor, a table and benches made by High Tech Campus volunteers **[D]**.

Voice: Laura Astola's three reasons are the richest first-person statement retrieved anywhere in this research. Climate crisis; then a philosophy of place — "**Ik denk niet: ik ga de beste plek ter wereld vinden en daar wonen. Maar: ik wil de plek waar ik woon de beste plek ter wereld maken**"; and third, "voor de **verbinding en veiligheid in de buurt**. Je buurtbewoners meer leren kennen" **[D]**. Note the ordering: connection is explicitly the third reason, not the first.

Counter-signal from the same neighbourhood: a resident had to remove parts of her street garden after neighbours complained — "wat voor de één een groene oase is, is voor de ander een obstakel of rommel" **[D, Trefpunt Groen, 17 July 2026]**. The garden as shared good is contested at the scale of the street.

### 3.3 Buurttuin Philipsdorp / Bellefort — a garden on derivative tenure

Planned by three residents in 2012 ("het kernteam Sophie Vermeulen, Annemieke Koppens en Rob Verhoeven"), with subsidies requested from the Oranje Fonds, Strijp binnen de Ring and Woonbedrijf; **opened on 17 July 2022** — a ten-year gap the live site does not show **[D, both documents recovered from the Wayback Machine because the original PDFs now 404]**. Opening attendance: 130 residents at the opening, 40 at a preceding buurtborrel, 350 at the primary school's summer party **[D]**.

Practice in 2022: "**12 vaste buurtbewoners** actief in het 'tuinteam'", who maintain the garden and "stimuleren buurtbewoners en organisaties om gebruik te maken van de tuin en zelf activiteiten te organiseren" **[D]**. Daily use is the most interesting finding in this case: "Tussen deze activiteiten door wordt buurttuin dagelijks bezocht door spelende kinderen, hun ouders, buurtbewoners die willen bewegen (buitenyoga), ontspannen (boekje lezen, sigaretje roken), werkenden uit de buurt (met laptop aan picknicktafels), hondenbezitters maken ommetje… Jongeren (max 2 personen) die even komen chillen. Mensen die op een matje zonnen" **[D]**. Joining is one mobile number — the same number published in 2012 **[I]**, i.e. one individual has been the public point of contact for fourteen years.

Tenure is the weak point, and it is invisible on the garden's own page: Bellefort is one of **seven municipal buurthuizen** managed by Lumens, and 2026 is an explicit **transition year** — "Bellefort: extra inzet vanuit WIJeindhoven (Buurt in Bloei) en CKE om te komen tot vrijwillig beheer. Een plan van aanpak wordt opgeleverd in april 2026" **[D, B&W raadsbrief 9 December 2025]**. No legal entity for the garden was found, and whether its ground sits inside the municipal lease to Lumens or on another arrangement is **[U]**. The garden's tenure is therefore **derivative**: it rides on the buurthuis arrangement rather than on any agreement of its own **[I]**.

Voice: no gardener's account was found **[U]**. The nearest first-person material from the same address is Claire, who runs the free drop-in *Kinderkunst* at Bellefort: "Ieder kind is welkom en kan gratis meedoen… Kinderen vinden het zó leuk dat ze terug blijven komen. Zo bouwen we echt een band op met elkaar… Gewoon beginnen. Zo leg je de lat niet te hoog en geef je jezelf ruimte om te groeien" **[D, October 2026]**. It is about children's art, not the garden — but it is the best local statement of how a recurring, free, low-bar activity turns into a group, and it comes from the same building.

**The integration the issue asks about is not documented.** Bellefort's weekly programme does include €1 lunches three days a week, free *Kinderkunst*, taalles, coffee mornings, an iftar, "samen koken, samen eten" **[D]** — but no retrieved source shows a shared meal or cultural activity taking place *in* the garden, or gardeners and non-gardeners doing anything together. Co-existence at one address is documented; integration is **[U]**.

### 3.4 De Block Acker, Strijp-S — the weakest tenure, the most honest account

Founded **May 2023** by the "creatief collectief" of YellowSunshine (Frans, Suus, Kat) on **100 m² "beschikbaar gesteld"** by The New Block, a circular living-lab platform in the SFS building on Strijp-S, for residents of Strijp-S and the adjacent Lijsterbesbuurt **[D, Trefpunt Groen network page, frozen since ≤ December 2023]**. Monthly work days; five held by late 2023; harvest eaten together; then "**overgedragen aan de vrijwilligers zelf**", coordinated in a WhatsApp group **[D]**. No legal entity, no lease, no term, no rent: the ground is hospitality from a project-phase platform, and ownership and conditions are **[U]**.

The account is unusually candid, and it is the only place in this material where an organiser admits the recruitment problem: "**Het was moeilijk om vrijwilligers te activeren om hiernaar toe te komen.** Ondanks verscheidene flyer pogingen was de opkomst niet zo groot als gehoopt. 'Maar toch hebben we er een fijne club van kunnen maken'" **[D]**. There is also a hard physical constraint: "na 30 centimeter een betonnen laag onder de grond" **[D]**.

Two cautions. The motto — "Voor de mensen, de planten, de bloemen en de bijen. **Zorg goed voor de tuin en voor elkaar!**" — is documented only as the initiative's stated mission, relayed by an intermediary **[D/C]**. The "close-knit group" is the organisers' retrospective, quoted by that intermediary, sitting immediately beside the admission that turnout disappointed; the Dutch says *fijne* (nice), not *hechte* (close-knit), so "close-knit" overstates it **[I]**. And **the garden's present status is [U]**: the only description is three years old, the host platform's domain now resolves to a parked domain, and the newsletter archive has been deleted. Dormancy is plausible; existence in 2026 is not established either way.

Three readings of the table, offered as inferences **[I]**:

1. **Continuity of land and hosting is a plausible enabling condition, not a comparative causal finding.** The 25-year case has recognised green zoning, a recent management agreement and a membership; the temporary-platform case has unclear tenure and no recent public account. The latter is not evidence of cessation. Different ages, documentation and participant perspectives prevent inference that land or keepers matter more than enthusiasm. Continuity of a place, continuity of its organised practice and continuity of relationships are distinct outcomes **[I/U]**.
2. **Almost every "community" practice here is hosted.** The free-standing civic model is rare: a municipality, a corporation, a care organisation or a developer is underneath each case. That is not a criticism — it is a factual description that changes what "support existing initiatives" means, because the host's continuity is a condition of the practice's continuity.
3. **A bounded recurring slot and visible work are candidate enabling conditions.** The cases document these arrangements, but do not establish their comparative costs, causal effects or durability **[D/I]**.

## 5. Counterexamples and the honest negatives

- **Only one practice is documented as stopped: Voedselbos Eindhoven.** The expectation that the city's public record would contain several cautionary tales is not met, and that absence should not be read as health — it is what a promotional and institutional record looks like **[D/I]**.
- **Struikroven changed form, not fate.** It "begon ooit als bewonersinitiatief en is nu uitgegroeid tot een gewoonte binnen het Duurzaamheidspact" (gemeente Eindhoven, 25 January 2021), with sessions run by "een ervaren struikrover en een groenexpert"; Trefpunt now describes a "snelgroeiende, landelijke stichting" working "in opdracht van woningcorporaties, projectontwikkelaars of gemeenten" **[D]**. Institutionalisation is a survival route — and it moves the practice from residents' initiative to commissioned service, with no participant voice in the record **[I]**.
- **Buurttuin vol Kleur was misread in an earlier pass and is not a stopped garden.** The €5,000 Postcode Loterij Fonds entry (period "Voorjaar 2026", Stichting Kansen voor Kinderen) is a funding record; the garden runs as a youth-work programme under GroenKLUP with paid supervisors **[D]**. A grant record is not a garden's life, and a lapsed grant is not its death.
- **"Two years of meetings for one planting."** Voedselbosje bij De Splinter is documented as having spent two years in meetings, "op gemeentegrond wat zo zijn drempels met zich meebracht", before "groen licht"; the page is undated and present status is **[U]**. The slow municipal route is a documented cost, not a documented failure.
- **Wasven shows that shared care can be lonely.** The care farm — three ANBI foundations, municipal ground on continuity grounds, 45 day-activity participants at end-2024, 10 volunteer working groups — reports that "**het contact met andere deelnemers blijkt voor veel deelnemers lastig**", that volunteer policy is "nog niet van de grond gekomen", and that team leaders have their "handen vol" **[D, delegated line; not independently verified]**. Participants themselves are never quoted; their views arrive only as an organisational summary. Shared activity and shared company are not the same thing, and the one organisation here that measures anything admits the gap.
- **The retrieved material does not provide a complete enumeration of the city's green practices.** Trefpunt Groen's network map yields six extractable nodes with no published total; its 2022/2024/2025 annual reports carry no *achterban* or partner list, and resident initiatives appear as an hour line (14 in 2022, 5 in 2024) against 53 tree-pruning advices and 33 environmental-plan advices in 2025 **[D, delegated line]**. The map's extraction limits do not establish what Trefpunt knows. Any city-wide inference rests on a handful of cases, not a population.

## 6. What is transferable, and what is not

**Transferable insights** (conditions, not templates):

- A **host with a continuity interest** in the ground is a plausible enabling condition suggested by the documented long-running practices; the four-case comparison does not establish its necessity or relative importance **[D/I]**.
- **One named keeper**, preferably with a role rather than a phone number, and ideally with paid hours for the hosting dimension: the social designer and the 4W concierges are the clearest documented examples of paid hospitality, and the treasurer vacancy is the clearest documented consequence of its absence **[D/I]**.
- **A bounded, recurring slot** ("every first Sunday 12–16", "Saturdays 11–14", "monthly work day") that is short enough to be entered and long enough to contain a conversation **[D]**.
- **Entry that does not require productivity**: free plot adoption, open gate, drop-in, "vrijblijvend", and — most importantly — documented tolerance of people being present without gardening **[D]**.
- **Visible work as a possible recruitment mechanism** — the Philipsdorp practitioners' account of how newcomers arrive **[C]**, not a measured cost or cross-case effect.
- **Small physical scale** (100 m², one yard, 1,800 m²) and a material constraint that shapes the design (concrete 30 cm down; remediated soil; a disused yard) **[D]**.
- **A "why" that is not the garden**: climate, a philosophy of place, neighbourhood safety, or simply a reason to know the neighbours **[D]**.

**Site-specific conditions, not directly transferable:** the 2001 contamination dispute preceding Vereniging Veldwerk; a mayor's visit and a 25-year anniversary; a housing corporation with a disused yard and resources for a social designer; Strijp-S's developer platform and its 100 m²; the municipal buurthuis arrangement and Lumens' position in it; and an established resident anchor. Copying the visible form without understanding these conditions risks missing what makes it meaningful **[I]**.

**Unresolved**: whether any of the four produces durable relationships; whether tenure derivative on a buurthuis is safer or riskier than a lease; whether paid presence substitutes for or suppresses resident initiative (the Buurtwerf concierges could do either) **[U]**.

## 7. Against the three pillars, and Voedselbos 2.0

- **Pillar 1, De Wielewaal as central green motor.** The retrieved evidence does not establish centrality, but suggests a concrete hosting function worth testing: supporting another community's own recurring slot. Vergeten Tuin documents formal municipal recognition of a small group's management of green space **[D]**. Whether an estate can support another group's rhythm and whether scale affects relationship formation remain open **[I/U]**.
- **Pillar 2, connect existing initiatives into a city-wide ecosystem.** Two corrections. First, the connective layer cannot currently enumerate what it would connect (six nodes, no total, no *achterban* list) **[D]**, so "mapping" is a data problem before it is a network problem. Second, the documented connective mechanisms here are **material and practical**, not relational: struikroven moves plants between demolition sites and gardens; a Groen Café convenes; a landlord's repair budget enables. If connections are to mean something, they should look like a shared work day, a plant exchange or a co-hosted slot — the same conclusion the meaning note reached from the literature **[D/I, cross-reference #19]**.
- **Pillar 3, nature closer to everyday life.** The best documented example in this city is not a garden visit but **daily unpurposed use** — children, yoga, laptops, sunbathers in a buurttuin — plus a building next door offering €1 lunches, free children's art and language classes **[D]**. That is the design worth studying: a green place that does not require a purpose, attached to a place that already has a routine. The integration of the two is not documented **[U]**.
- **Voedselbos 2.0.** The cases suggest that a supportive host, continuity of coordination, a predictable rhythm, accessible entry, permission to be present without a task and visible care are worth discussing with the community **[I]**. They are not a necessary or sufficient checklist. The current land arrangements and succession capacity of 2.0 remain incompletely documented; no inference is made about its community's survival or willingness. Ask what participants value, which conditions they want to preserve and what would enable those relationships in a successor practice.

**Review qualification (26 November 2026).** These conditions concern the possibility of meaningful participation, not a demonstrated recipe for durable community. The value of an unpurposed visit is not contingent on whether it later becomes volunteering. Land security and hosting can facilitate relationships, but cannot establish their existence or explain their meaning without participant evidence. No sources or access limitations have been changed in this revision.

## 8. What requires listening, not more desk research

The issue asks which questions need participants rather than further desk work. These do:

1. **Who comes, and who does not?** For each garden: of the people who live within five minutes' walk, who has never been inside, and what would it take? No public source can answer this, and no amount of counting volunteers will.
2. **What does the monthly open day actually do?** Vergeten Tuin: does the first-Sunday rhythm recruit, or does it serve an existing membership? Who decided to open only then?
3. **Does derivative tenure feel secure?** Buurttuin Philipsdorp: does the tuinteam experience itself as part of Bellefort, or as a separate thing that will be re-let with the building?
4. **What did the social designer leave behind?** Buurtwerf: would the yard still be worked if 4W's concierges stopped coming? What did residents do before the designer arrived, and what do they do now that she has gone?
5. **Who is doing the work, and do they want to be?** Vergeten Tuin: 25 of 180 is a heavy skew; is that a fair division or a quiet burden? The treasurer post has been advertised publicly since September 2024.
6. **Does Block Acker still exist?** One message to a known contact would settle three years of silence — but that is contact, and out of scope here.
7. **Was the shared meal real?** Bellefort: is there any occasion when gardeners and non-gardeners eat together in the garden, or is the integration only architectural?
8. **What do neighbours who never enter think the garden is for?** The Bennekel complaint about street planting suggests the answer is not always benign.
9. **How do people describe what they get?** The available collective and individual accounts are partial; listening to a wider range of participants, including people who stopped or never joined, would help establish what the work and presence mean to them.
10. **What would make you stop?** The question this repository's decision note already asks of the food forest's organisers, transposed to each garden's keeper.
