# Case portraits: De Vergeten Tuin (Gestel) and De Buurtwerf (Bennekel)

**Line of work:** case portraits for issue #20 — "What can existing community gardens, neighbourhood initiatives and other shared green practices in Eindhoven teach us about the conditions under which people come to enjoy caring for their environment together, and meaningful relationships emerge through that care?"
**Author:** researcher (cases-gestel). **Retrieval dates:** all sources retrieved 26 November 2026 unless stated otherwise.
**Method:** desk research only; public pages retrieved with `source_read`. No contact, no forms, no logins, no closed groups, no subagents. Robots.txt treated as non-blocking per PI direction; no bulk crawling. Paywalls, login walls and consent/JS gates were recorded, not bypassed.

**Scope note.** Two cases only. De Buurtwerf turned out to be embedded in a wider Bennekel greening ecology (Groen Bennekel, Buurtplatform Bennekel Belang, Vele Groene Handen, Doe de Dommel, 4W/Ergon, Yuverta). Those are used as context for the Buurtwerf portrait and are **not** separately researched here (see "Adjacent questions" at the end).

---

## 1. De Vergeten Tuin, Palingstraat, Gestel

### 1.1 Origin and date

An enclosed inner area of **1,800 m²** behind the early-20th-century workers' houses on the Palingstraat, entered between nos. 21 and 23. The plot had a green function for most of the twentieth century except **1959–1978**, when the firm Capax built factory halls there and polluted soil and groundwater (initiative's own history page, undated). After Capax left, the site lay fallow and was used informally by residents:

> "Omwonenden hebben het terrein samen gebruikt en onderhouden. Zonder duidelijk plan werd er opgeruimd, gemaaid, gesnoeid gezaaid en geplant." — Vereniging Veldwerk, history page (initiative text; undated, internally written circa 2007)

In **2001** the neighbourhood learned the soil would be remediated and that housing demolition was also threatened; residents founded **Vereniging Veldwerk** "om de tuin en de huizen te behouden". After visits to other Dutch nature gardens and a design by garden architect **José Kennis (De Slatuinen, Amsterdam)**, the plan was executed in **2002** when remediation was postponed. The garden was "regelmatig opengesteld" **from 2006**; B&W gave the site a green zoning ("groenbestemming") at the **start of 2007**.

**Timeline as supported by more than one source:**

| Year | Event | Source(s) |

| pre-2001 | informal resident tending of the fallow plot; used as dog-walking ground; slated for housing | Vergeten Tuin history page; *Vlinders* 4 (2007) |
| 2001 | remediation announced; Vereniging Veldwerk founded | history page; Trefpunt Groen 2 Sep 2026; obituary 2019 ("het begin van de oprichting van de Vereniging Veldwerk in 2001") |
| 2002 | design executed after remediation postponed | history page |
| 2006 | garden regularly opened to visitors | history page |
| early 2007 | municipal green zoning | history page; *Vlinders* 4 (2007) ("het gebied heeft van de gemeente een groenbestemming gekregen") |
| 2025 | management agreement ("beheersovereenkomst") signed with the municipality | Trefpunt Groen 2 Sep 2026 ("Vorig jaar ondertekende Vereniging Veldwerk een beheersovereenkomst met de gemeente") |
| 6 Sep 2026 | 25-year celebration; mayor Dijsselbloem visits at 14:00 | initiative news 25 Aug 2026; Trefpunt Groen 2 Sep 2026 |

**Does the "25 years" check out?** Yes, with a precise referent. It counts **25 years since the founding of Vereniging Veldwerk and the organised garden project in 2001** — not 25 years since the current design (2002), and not the start of residents' informal use (older, undated, "decennialang"). Both the initiative and Trefpunt Groen phrase it as "al 25 jaar onderhouden door vrijwilligers", which slightly overstates: the *association* is 25 in 2026, the *designed garden* is 24, and neighbourhood tending is older but undocumented as to start date. The 2025 management agreement ("vorig jaar" in a September 2026 text) is a clean internal date check and is consistent.

### 1.2 Legal form and who holds the ground

- **Legal form:** a members' association (*vereniging*), **Vereniging Veldwerk**, with a board. Board members named publicly in the 2019 obituary: Maud, Kees, Anneke, Ilse, Roel, Sjanie.
- **Who holds the ground:** **not established.** No public page states the owner or the holder of a lease/erfpacht. What is documented: the municipality gave the plot a green zoning (2007) and, per Trefpunt Groen (2 Sep 2026), the association signed a **management agreement with the municipality in 2025**. Management is therefore contractually the association's; title is unstated in every source read. The *SNTP* garden register lists the address as Palingstraat 31 with contact person Kees de Kort, which is the association's correspondence address, not evidence of ownership.
- The association also exists to protect the surrounding workers' houses: "Vereniging Veldwerk is opgericht om de oude arbeidershuisjes aan de Palingstraat te behouden en het veld daarachter om te vormen tot een (beperkt) toegankelijk groengebied."

### 1.3 What members do, how often, who coordinates

- **Rhythm:** maintenance *is* the public moment. "Op de dagen dat we bezig zijn met werken in de tuin, de eerste zondag van de maand, stellen we de tuin open voor het publiek" (Vereniging page). Open **first Sunday of the month, 12:00–16:00** (homepage; SNTP; Trefpunt Groen). The site's agenda independently confirms the rhythm running forward: *Open Veldwerkmiddag december 2026, 2026-12-06, 12.00–16.00* (retrieved 26 Nov 2026 — an upcoming dated event, i.e. the rhythm was live at retrieval).
- **Work content (seasonal, skilled, low-frequency):** "Twee keer per jaar wordt de bloemenweide met een zeis gemaaid"; autumn leaf-raking; pruning with brash bundled into dead hedges; composting; netting the pond in autumn (Werkzaamheden page). Also amphibian inventory ("Ook inventariseren we dieren en planten in de tuin, zoals de amfibieën"), an annual "amfibieënavond", school classes with teaching materials, and nature news/photos on the site (Uitgangspunten page).
- **Who coordinates:** a **kerngroep of about 25 active volunteers** inside a membership of "bijna 180 leden" (Vereniging page, undated). A chair gives the anniversary speech and tour (Trefpunt Groen 2 Sep 2026); the secretary is the public contact (SNTP: Kees de Kort; site: secretaris@vergetentuin.nl). The board is the coordinating body; there is no paid staff visible in any source.
- **Governance load is public and visible:** a **treasurer vacancy** was posted 4 Sep 2024 ("Vereniging Veldwerk … is op zoek naar een nieuwe penningmeester … Ook als je geen groene vingers hebt, kun je de Vergeten Tuin helpen") and was still listed in the archive at retrieval (re-dated 2024-12-14). Trefpunt Groen notes the association "weet steeds weer nieuwe enthousiaste mensen te vinden voor het bestuur en de vrijwilligersgroep (al kunnen er altijd meer bij)".

### 1.4 What participants themselves say

I found **no individually attributed, first-person account by a current volunteer about becoming involved or about neighbours**, other than the short quotation below. The strongest participant-voice material is (a) a collective "we" statement by the association and (b) a former resident's memoir about the *pre-garden* plot.

**(a) Collective first-person statement (organiser-as-participant; the association's own voice):**

> "De meesten van ons waren niet bijzonder bezig met de natuur voordat we hier kwamen wonen, maar door de tuin zijn we er bij betrokken geraakt. Een ander effect van de tuin is dat buurtgenoten elkaar erdoor ontmoeten en beter leren kennen."
> — Vereniging Veldwerk, "Wat de tuin voor ons doet", Vereniging page (undated)
> *Gloss: most of us were not particularly engaged with nature before we came to live here, but through the garden we got involved. Another effect is that neighbours meet each other through it and get to know each other better.*

This is exactly the proposition issue #20 asks about — and it is an **interested party's self-description**, not independent testimony. Treat as claim, not finding.

**(b) A volunteer of the first hour, quoted in local print media (reproduced on the initiative's site):**

> "Dat er in de binnenstad zoiets is, ja, dat is echt een cadeautje"
> — Kees de Kort, volunteer "van het eerste uur", quoted in *Groot Eindhoven*, excerpt posted 29 Aug 2025 (full article not retrieved — see §7)

**(c) A former resident's memoir (about the plot before the garden existed) — the richest sensory/formation account found:**

> "Ik heb als kind vele uren in de tuin doorgebracht, eerst met mijn opa, die er een grote moestuin had. Als kleuter had ik al een eigen afgebakend stukje moestuin met aardbeien, radijs en tomaten. Elke ochtend met opa de tuin in om te zien hoe de zaken er voor stonden."
> "Als de 68-jarige van nu, kan ik gerust zeggen dat die tuin mij de liefde voor de natuur en het buiten zijn hebben gegeven, de tuin is een soort basis, waar ik heel veel aan terugdenk."
> — **Trini Post**, former resident of Palingstraat 19, letter published 3 Nov 2019
> *Gloss: as a child I spent many hours in the garden, first with my grandfather who had a large kitchen garden there; as a toddler I already had my own demarcated patch with strawberries, radish and tomatoes. Every morning into the garden with grandpa to see how things stood. / As the 68-year-old I am now, I can say that garden gave me the love of nature and of being outdoors; the garden is a kind of base I think back on a great deal.*

Important: this is **not** testimony about the current community garden. It is about a leased market garden on the same plot in the 1950s–60s, and about a *family* transmission of care, not a neighbourhood one. It does, however, document that the plot has a ~70-year layering of residents' growing and that the "love of nature" narrative attached to this place predates the association. Do not let it stand in for the community-garden case.

**(d) A visitor's reported reaction, 2007 (third-party reported):**

> "Het was, zoals iemand zei: 'Een nacht om nooit meer te vergeten'."
> — René Marcelis, *Vlinders* 4 (2007), on the National Moth Night in the garden, 7 Sep 2007

### 1.5 What the organisation claims

- Public value: water buffering, clean air, summer cooling, biodiversity; four ponds with alpine and smooth newts, frogs and toads; the wild camera recorded hedgehog, pine marten and woodcock; "bosuil, braamsluiper en ijsvogel" have been spotted (Trefpunt Groen 2 Sep 2026; association post "Biodiversiteit en de Vergeten Tuin", 16 May 2025).
- Social value: "Dan is het een plek waar mensen uit de buurt elkaar in de tuin ontmoeten. Vrijwilligers die de tuin onderhouden genieten van het samen werken in het groen" (Trefpunt Groen, describing the open days).
- Educational openness: garden open to school classes, an annual amphibian evening, monthly open work day ("waarop iedereen welkom is").
- Deliberate restraint as a design value: "We streven … naar een natuurlijk evenwicht zodat planten en dieren goed gedijen en mensen uiteindelijk zo min mogelijk hoeven in te grijpen" (Beheer page).

### 1.6 How someone joins, what it costs, accessibility

- **Membership:** €**7,50** per year, by bank transfer, membership "definitief nadat je de contributie … hebt overgemaakt" (Word lid page, undated). Trefpunt Groen (2 Sep 2026) states the same €7,50 and links to that page. **Note the discrepancy:** the homepage still says "Word dan lid voor maar vijf euro per jaar" — an older text not updated; treat €7,50 as current. A higher amount is welcomed ("Een hoger bedrag is natuurlijk ook welkom"), and a direct debit mandate is encouraged.
- **Volunteering:** open invitation (homepage, Vereniging page, contact address verenigingveldwerk@vergetentuin.nl). Joining the working group in practice means turning up on a first Sunday.
- **What membership *means*:** the Word-lid page gives no rights beyond the contribution letter and newsletter; no voting, plot, key or access privilege is stated. **Membership is financial support, not access.** What it can show: a recurring willingness-to-pay base of ~180 people. What it cannot show: involvement, attachment strength, or use.
- **Accessibility — the crucial, easily-missed point:** this is **not a public park**. "De tuin is alleen op open veldwerkdagen vrij toegankelijk. Ook het onderhoud is op die tijden" (Uitgangspunten). Trefpunt Groen puts it more sharply: "De tuin is alleen toegankelijk voor de mensen die eromheen wonen, behalve op de Open Veldwerkdagen." That second sentence is a **claim I could not verify elsewhere** — no page states a residents-only rule, and the open days are advertised to "iedereen". The verified fact is: closed except one Sunday a month. Access is *gated by rhythm*, not by membership.
- House rules visible on the site: stay on the paths, no animals kept, dogs on lead only, free entry on open days (SNTP: "Toegang: Gratis").
- Physical accessibility of the entrance (steps, gate width, surface) is **not described in any source**; the entrance is a gate between two house numbers in a narrow street.

### 1.7 Present status (2026)

**Active and publicly celebrated.** The 25-year celebration took place on Sunday 6 September 2026, 12:00–16:00, with information stations along a route, volunteers explaining flora and fauna, snacks and coffee/tea/lemonade, the mayor visiting at 14:00, a speech and guided tour by the chair, and a jubilee postcard set and new folder presented (initiative news 25 Aug 2026; Trefpunt Groen 2 Sep 2026). A further open work afternoon is scheduled for **6 December 2026**. The site is live, the membership route works, and the association holds a 2025 management agreement. I found **no** independent report of the 6 September event itself (see §7) — so "the celebration happened as planned" is **not established**; "it was planned and advertised by two organisations" is.

## 2. De Buurtwerf, Bennekel

### 2.1 Origin and date

An old **yard ("werf") of Woonbedrijf** behind a row of houses and next to community centre **De Dommel** (Lumens), Bennekelstraat 129A. It lay unused for years. Woonbedrijf **commissioned social designer Lian Kroes** to turn it into a lively place and involve residents, around the themes "groen, ontmoeten en bewegen". Woonbedrijf fixed up the neglected outbuildings and provided water, power and a meeting room. The transformation is dated "**sinds 2022**" by Woonbedrijf's own magazine.

> "De Buurtwerf in de wijk Bennekel was 2 jaar geleden nog een verwaarloosde plek. Maar dit is sinds 2022 langzaam veranderd in een mooie moestuin en ontmoetingsplek." — *Buurten* (Woonbedrijf residents' magazine), zomer 2023, p. 6

Critical detail for the "conditions" question: the idea was not new and did not self-start.

> "Het idee hiervoor lag er al langer, maar de motivatie in de buurt ontbrak." — Woonbedrijf news, 30 May 2023

So: **a landlord's commission + a professional social designer + a resident already organised in Groen Bennekel** produced the initiative. This is a hybrid, not a pure resident initiative.

### 2.2 Legal form and who holds the ground

- **Ground and buildings:** Woonbedrijf (housing corporation) property; a former yard. No lease, membership or user agreement is described publicly.
- **Legal form of the garden:** none. There is no association, foundation or formal membership. It is a **corporation-owned site animated by a volunteer group (Groen Bennekel, a working group of Buurtplatform Bennekel Belang) plus a commissioned designer plus, since 2023, a social-work team (4W/Ergon)**. Groen Bennekel is described by Trefpunt Groen as "een werkgroep van het Buurtplatform Bennekel Belang".
- **Who organises what:** Woonbedrijf = owner/base infrastructure; Lian Kroes = concept and, per 2023 reporting, the operational lead on the ground; Laura Astola = resident leader of Groen Bennekel ("het initiatief Groen Bennekel dat wordt geleidt door Laura Astola", Trefpunt Groen network page); 4W (Ergon, with Woonbedrijf and Archipel) = several-times-weekly host presence and handyman help.

### 2.3 What participants do, how often

**2023 (Woonbedrijf, 30 May 2023 and *Buurten* zomer 2023):**
- Residents "adopteren hier gratis een stukje tuin, waar ze zelf op kweken. Maar uiteindelijk onderhouden en delen ze de tuin samen. En leren ook van elkaar."
- Crops in 2023: "boerenkool, koolraap, peterselie, aardappel, bramen… Maar ook kruiden en bloemen. Allemaal biologisch en pesticidevrij."
- Infrastructure: large rainwater tank, an old roof window converted into a greenhouse for small plants, large planters built with contractor Van der Meijs, a table and benches made by High Tech Campus volunteers.
- Opening: "De buurttuin is elke zaterdag open, tussen 11.00 en 14.00 uur", extended "dankzij Doe mee met 4W" with a caretaker role on **Wednesday afternoon and Friday morning** from April 2023.

**2024–2026 (other sources):**
- 17 March 2024, Vele Groene Handen action day: "het Buurtwerf in Bennekel verzorgde koffie, thee, muziek en gezelligheid" (Trefpunt Groen, 22 Mar 2024) — the site functioning as hospitality base for a wider neighbourhood action.
- 27 September 2025, Vele Groene Handen/Burendag: the target was "het vergroenen van de oprit naar De Buurtwerf en de voortuinen van drie adressen in de wijk"; 45 m² of tiles lifted; named participants "hoveniers Herman, Patrick en Jules", "vrijwilligers Jannie en Laura en coördinator Linde", three Yuverta Green Lyceum pupils who also ran old-Dutch games for children; leftover topsoil was taken away by neighbours with wheelbarrows; the day closed together around 17:00 (Trefpunt Groen, 7 Oct 2025).
- **Current (undated page, live at retrieval):** "Aan de Bennekelstraat ligt de Buurtwerf waar 4W een paar keer per week bezoekers en buurtgenoten verwelkomt met een kop koffie of thee. Samen met de vrijwilligers werken de medewerkers 4W mee om de Buurtwerf om te toveren tot een natuurlijke groene ontmoetingsplaats midden in de wijk." (4W, *Locaties*)
- **Current (event listing, next occurrence 29 Nov 2026):** litter-cleanup group "Groep Groen Bennekel" starts at the Buurtwerf **every Sunday 10:30–11:30**: "Meedoen is altijd op een vrijblijvende basis. Je bepaalt zelf wanneer, hoe vaak en hoeveel minuten je meedoet. Na afloop staan in de Buurtwerf hapjes en thee voor je klaar." (Troep Troopers Eindhoven)

So the Buurtwerf in 2026 is best evidenced as **a place that hosts other people's rhythms** — 4W's coffee mornings several times a week, the Sunday cleanup group's start and finish — rather than as a garden with published opening hours. **No 2024–2026 source states the garden's own opening hours**; the 2023 "Saturday 11.00–14.00" cannot be confirmed for 2026.

### 2.4 What participants themselves say

The only resident quoted by name in the sources retrieved is **Laura Astola**, resident of Bennekel and leader of Groen Bennekel. All quotations below are as published by Woonbedrijf's communication department (or, for the last one, Trefpunt Groen); I could not find a first-person text by her.

> "We laten mensen graag kennismaken met planten, tuinieren en biodiversiteit." — Laura Astola, Woonbedrijf, 30 May 2023

Her three reasons for the work — the most substantive resident statement in either case:

> "De eerste is de klimaatcrisis. Het vergroenen van de wijken helpt bij het opvangen van CO2. De tweede is vanuit een levensbeschouwing. Ik denk niet: ik ga de beste plek ter wereld vinden en daar wonen. Maar: ik wil de plek waar ik woon de beste plek ter wereld maken. En de derde is voor de verbinding en veiligheid in de buurt. Je buurtbewoners meer leren kennen." — Laura Astola, Woonbedrijf, 30 May 2023

*Gloss: the first is the climate crisis … the second from a worldview: I don't think "I'll find the best place in the world and live there", but "I want to make the place where I live the best place in the world". And the third is connection and safety in the neighbourhood: getting to know your neighbours better.*

Note the ordering: **connection is explicitly third**, after climate and worldview. Attachment-to-place ("de plek waar ik woon") is doing the motivational work, and neighbour-relations are named as an *effect*, not the initial driver. That is a useful corrective to a romantic reading of the case.

The designer's words (Lian Kroes — **not a resident**, a commissioned professional):

> "Eigenlijk een combinatie van een buurttuin, moestuin en bouwplaats … Groen en ontmoeten zijn de hoofdzaken." — Woonbedrijf, 30 May 2023

> "Van jong tot 50-plus … En allemaal met een eigen interesse in groen. Van het kweken van groenten tot geneeskrachtige kruiden en bloemen. Zo kweekt één bewoonster goudbloemen. Maar ze wil ook een stukje adopteren voor haar zoontje en zijn vriendjes. Zodat ze daar groenten kunnen kweken die ze lekker vinden." — Lian Kroes, Woonbedrijf, 30 May 2023

That last passage is the best available evidence of **differentiated participation** (medicinal flowers for one, a child's patch for another) — observed by the designer, not spoken by the participants.

A student's words from the adjacent Yuverta/Bennekel programme (context, not the Buurtwerf):

> "Wij vinden het leuk en leerzaam om aan deze opdracht te werken, omdat we nu beter begrijpen hoe vergroening invloed heeft op de leefomgeving en gezondheid van bewoners." — Jill, Yuverta student, quoted by Trefpunt Groen, 17 Jul 2026

### 2.5 What the organisation(s) claim

- Woonbedrijf: the Buurtwerf "komt letterlijk tot bloei"; residents "onderhouden en delen de tuin samen. En leren ook van elkaar"; it makes the neighbourhood greener, healthier, and "mooie plekken om elkaar te ontmoeten".
- Trefpunt Groen (17 Jul 2026), the strongest current claim: "Het paradepaardje is De Buurtwerf, een buurttuin die pal naast wijkcentrum De Dommel is opgestart. Het is een laagdrempelige plek geworden waar buurtbewoners niet alleen leren tuinieren, maar waar ook eenzaamheid wordt bestreden bij een kop koffie tussen de planten."
  - **"eenzaamheid wordt bestreden" is an unevidenced outcome claim.** No loneliness measure, no participant testimony about loneliness, no numbers. It is an NGO's characterisation. Flag as claim only.
- 4W: the site is being turned "tot een natuurlijke groene ontmoetingsplaats midden in de wijk".

### 2.6 How someone joins, what it costs, accessibility

- **Joining:** no membership, no fee stated anywhere. The 2023 route: adopt a free patch ("gratis een stukje tuin") by turning up on a Saturday. In 2026, the visible entry points are the 4W coffee presence and the Sunday cleanup group (which says "Aanmelden: NEE" and "Meedoen is altijd op een vrijblijvende basis" while also inviting an email to info@bennekel.nl — mildly contradictory).
- **Cost:** growing space free; no charge documented. Costs of infrastructure were borne by Woonbedrijf and partners (water/power, planters with Van der Meijs, benches by High Tech Campus volunteers).
- **Accessibility:** ground-level yard next to a community centre; **physical accessibility (surface, thresholds, toilet, shade, seating) is not documented**. The site is behind a row of houses, reached via an alley/driveway ("Van buitenaf zie je alleen huizen" is said of another garden, but the Buurtwerf is likewise described as "achter een rij huizen"). It is not a through route: **visibility from the street is low**, which is a plausible but unproven barrier to casual discovery.

### 2.7 Present status (2026)

**In use, publicly described as thriving, but with no self-published presence.** Evidence: (i) 4W's live page describes a several-times-weekly coffee/tea presence with volunteers; (ii) a litter-cleanup group's published schedule starts and ends at the Buurtwerf every Sunday, next dated occurrence 29 Nov 2026; (iii) Trefpunt Groen's 17 Jul 2026 article calls it the neighbourhood's "paradepaardje". **Not established:** its own opening hours, how many households currently grow there, whether the individual adoption plots still operate, who holds keys, and whether the 4W arrangement continues in 2027.

## 4. Established / claimed / not established

### 4.1 De Vergeten Tuin

**Established (on retrieved sources, at least one of which is independent of the association):**
1. An enclosed 1,800 m² inner-area nature garden exists at Palingstraat 21–23, Eindhoven, open to the public **one Sunday a month, 12:00–16:00**, free entry (homepage; SNTP; Trefpunt Groen; agenda dated 6 Dec 2026).
2. **Vereniging Veldwerk was founded in 2001**; the design was executed in 2002; the garden was regularly opened from 2006; the site received a municipal green zoning in early 2007 (history page; *Vlinders* 2007 independently confirms the 2007 zoning and the residents' re-landscaping).
3. The association **manages** the garden under a **management agreement with the municipality signed in 2025** (Trefpunt Groen, 2 Sep 2026). It is a members' association with a board and no paid staff visible.
4. Membership is **€7,50 per year** (Word-lid page; Trefpunt Groen 2 Sep 2026), and confers no access rights — access is by calendar, not by card.
5. Around **150 members in 2007** (*Vlinders*, independent), **"bijna 180 leden" and ~25 core volunteers** at an undated moment (association's own page).
6. The garden was **publicly celebrated as 25 years old on 6 September 2026**, with the mayor attending, per two organisations' pre-event announcements.
7. Maintenance is **episodic and seasonal** (two scythings a year, autumn leaf clearance, pruning, compost, pond netting) and happens **on the same first Sunday as the public opening**.

**Claimed but not independently evidenced:**
- That neighbours meet and get to know each other through the garden, and that volunteers "genieten van het samen werken in het groen" (association and Trefpunt Groen — both interested parties; Trefpunt Groen is a partner organisation that lists the garden in its network).
- That the garden is open only to surrounding residents on non-open days (Trefpunt Groen phrasing). No rule to that effect found on the initiative's own pages; the site says "alleen op open veldwerkdagen vrij toegankelijk".
- Biodiversity and climate benefits (water buffering, cooling, air quality): repeated by the association and by Trefpunt Groen, unsourced to measurement.
- That the association "steeds weer nieuwe enthousiaste mensen" finds for board and volunteers — while a treasurer vacancy from September 2024 remained posted.

**Not established (from public sources searched):**
- **Who owns the land**, and the terms/duration of the 2025 management agreement. No public document found; the agreement's text is not online.
- **How many people actually visit** on open days, or how many of the ~180 members ever attend. No visitor counts published.
- Any **individual, first-person current-volunteer account** of becoming involved with nature or of getting to know neighbours *through this garden*. The one first-person nature-formation account (Trini Post) concerns the *pre-garden* plot in the 1950s–60s.
- Whether the **6 September 2026 celebration actually took place as planned** — no post-event report found from any source.
- Whether the garden's social mix resembles the neighbourhood's (age, tenure, ethnicity): nothing published.

**Explicit note on numbers.** "180 leden / 25 vrijwilligers" can show there is a dues-paying base and a small working core. It **cannot** show vitality, motive, intensity of involvement, or relationships. In 2007 the same organisation reported 150 members; the growth to ~180 over ~19 years is a membership count, not a participation trend, and the two figures come from different decades with different definitions ("leden" both times, but no audit).

### 4.2 De Buurtwerf

**Established:**
1. The Buurtwerf is a **former Woonbedrijf yard** behind a row of houses next to community centre De Dommel, at **Bennekelstraat 129A**, converted into a neighbourhood garden/meeting place **from 2022** (Woonbedrijf 30 May 2023; *Buurten* zomer 2023; 4W; Troep Troopers).
2. The conversion was **initiated by the landlord commissioning a social designer (Lian Kroes)** and depended on an already-organised resident (Laura Astola, Groen Bennekel); Woonbedrijf supplied buildings, water, power and a meeting room (Woonbedrijf 2023).
3. In 2023 residents could **adopt a free growing patch**; the whole was described as collectively maintained and as a place of mutual learning (Woonbedrijf 2023 — interested party).
4. Since April 2023 the site has had a **several-times-weekly staffed presence** via 4W (Ergon, with Woonbedrijf and Archipel): caretaking Wednesday afternoon and Friday morning in 2023; "een paar keer per week … koffie of thee" in the current 4W description.
5. In **2024** and **2025** the Buurtwerf functioned as the **hospitality base** for neighbourhood greening action days (coffee/tea/music in March 2024; the target of the September 2025 tile-lifting, with a joint 17:00 close).
6. In **late 2026** it is still an active anchor: a weekly Sunday litter-cleanup group (10:30–11:30) uses it as start and end point, with snacks and tea afterwards (next dated occurrence 29 Nov 2026); Trefpunt Groen (17 Jul 2026) calls it the neighbourhood's "paradepaardje".

**Claimed but not independently evidenced:**
- That residents "uiteindelijk onderhouden en delen ze de tuin samen. En leren ook van elkaar" (Woonbedrijf).
- That loneliness is being countered there ("eenzaamheid wordt bestreden bij een kop koffie tussen de planten", Trefpunt Groen 2026) — no measure, no testimony.
- That participation spans "van jong tot 50-plus" (designer's observation, 2023).
- That the garden is a "buurttuin, moestuin en bouwplaats" in one (designer's framing).

**Not established:**
- **Present opening hours and present growing arrangements in 2026.** The 2023 "every Saturday 11.00–14.00" is not confirmed by any 2024–2026 source; whether individual adoption plots still exist is unknown.
- **How many households currently garden there**, and how patches are now allocated.
- **Any first-person account by a participant other than Laura Astola** — and hers comes through corporate communication. No resident describes, in their own published words, what the garden does for them.
- Whether the initiative would survive without the **commissioned designer + 4W staffing + landlord investment**: the only evidence points to a professionally supported hybrid, and no source describes a resident-only mode of operation.
- Any formal user agreement, lease, or governance body for the garden itself. Nothing found.

**Explicit note on numbers.** There are, in fact, **no participation numbers at all** for the Buurtwerf in the public sources retrieved — only areas (45 m² of tiles in 2025), counts of named helpers (three gardeners, two volunteers, one coordinator, three pupils in 2025), and one narrative phrase ("het groepje vrijwilligers"— which in the magazine refers to a *different* garden, Moestuin Tivoli, growing to 8 and shrinking to 4). No figure exists to check vitality against; the vitality evidence is **institutional persistence** (4W page live, cleanup schedule live, NGO description in 2026), not participation data.

## 5. What bears on the conditions named in issue #20

### 5.1 Informal hospitality
- Vergeten Tuin: "Er zijn hapjes en koffie, thee en limonade voor de bezoekers" (anniversary, 2026); the 2019 obituary records that "Ambtenaren en buurtbewoners, pers en politieke partijen en ook oud-bewoners werden uitgenodigd en gastvrij ontvangen"; the same text remembers "gezellig napraten" as part of the workday. Hospitality here is **episodic and event-bound**, attached to open days and to a named person's style.
- Buurtwerf: hospitality is **institutionalised and routinised** — a social-work team whose job is to pour coffee several times a week; "hapjes en thee" waiting after the Sunday cleanup; coffee/tea/music supplied to a neighbourhood action day. This is the clearest structural contrast between the two cases: one relies on members' goodwill on one Sunday a month; the other has **paid hosting capacity**.
- What cannot be told: whether either hospitality is offered *by* participants to each other, or *to* visitors by a small group. The sources consistently describe hosts and guests, not a shared table.

### 5.2 Regular rhythms
- Vergeten Tuin: a **monthly** rhythm (first Sunday, 12–16), plus annual (amphibian evening) and seasonal (two scythings) beats. The rhythm is old enough to be a habit: it is announced on SNTP, on Trefpunt Groen, and in the site agenda a fortnight ahead.
- Buurtwerf: a **weekly** beat in 2023 (Saturday), now a **multi-weekly** staffed beat plus a **weekly Sunday** cleanup rhythm. The garden's own weekly opening is the part that is no longer documented.
- Both cases show rhythms that are **legible to outsiders** (fixed day, fixed hours) — a plausible enabling condition, though neither source argues the causal link.

### 5.3 Autonomy and freedom to participate in different ways
- Best explicit formulation found, from the cleanup group that uses the Buurtwerf: "Meedoen is altijd op een vrijblijvende basis. Je bepaalt zelf wanneer, hoe vaak en hoeveel minuten je meedoet" (Troep Troopers).
- Vergeten Tuin offers **differentiated roles without hierarchy of virtue**: dues-payer, newsletter reader, volunteer on workdays, board member, and explicitly "Ook als je geen groene vingers hebt, kun je de Vergeten Tuin helpen" (treasurer call, 2024). Membership is deliberately cheap (€7,50) and buys no privileges, which lowers the price of belonging.
- Buurtwerf offers **free, individually held growing space inside a collective frame** ("adopteren hier gratis een stukje tuin, waar ze zelf op kweken"), and the designer's examples show people using that freedom for quite different purposes (medicinal flowers; a patch for a child and his friends).
- Counterpoint: autonomy is bounded by rules and by neighbours. See 5.7.

### 5.4 Shared meals
**Not established for either case.** The strongest evidence is coffee, tea, lemonade, "hapjes", and one mention of music. No source describes a shared meal, a cooking or eating collective, or food distributed from either garden. Kitchen garden produce is described as grown by individuals for themselves ("waar ze zelf op kweken") — and in the Vergeten Tuin's case the planting rules exclude vegetables altogether (heemtuin: native plants and bulbs only). Anyone using issue #20's "shared meals" category should not attach it to these two cases on the present evidence.

### 5.5 Practical work
- Both cases put **visible, physical, completable work** at the centre: scything and raking in Gestel; lifting 45 m² of tiles, preparing beds, barrowing soil in Bennekel.
- Work is also the **social occasion**: the open day *is* the workday at the Vergeten Tuin ("Ook het onderhoud is op die tijden"); the 2025 Bennekel day ended with a joint close at 17:00, "Moe, maar met een voldaan gevoel".
- Skill is present and valued: a scythe, a graafmachine (the obituary's affectionate "je graafmachine skills"), hoveniers Herman/Patrick/Jules giving "kennis en kracht", a resident with a horticultural background who wrote the Tivoli planting plan.

### 5.6 Learning
- Vergeten Tuin: the founders **went and looked at other Dutch nature gardens** before designing; they hired an architect; they inventory amphibians; they host school classes with lesson materials; they publish species lists. Learning is **project-shaped and outward-facing**.
- Buurtwerf: "leren ook van elkaar"; Groen Bennekel "laat mensen graag kennismaken met planten, tuinieren en biodiversiteit"; Yuverta students do neighbourhood assignments; IVN gives planting advice at action days; a resident with a horticultural background made a soil-recovery plan for another garden in the same series.
- Note that in both cases the **knowledge enters from outside the participant group** (a garden architect; a commissioned designer; hoveniers; IVN; a green school). The learning is real, but it is not self-generated — an important condition, not a spontaneous property.

### 5.7 Sensory experience and seasonal change
- Vergeten Tuin's self-description is explicitly sensory and restorative: "Een oase van rust midden tussen drukke doorgaande wegen"; visitors are invited to "ontdek dat er het hele jaar door van alles te zien is"; seasonal posts ("Het sneeuwt!", "Hoezo winterslaap?", autumn leaf work, the pond net). A local journalist's framing, reproduced by the association: "Het voelt een beetje als Alice in Wonderland."
- Buurtwerf: "koffie tussen de planten", a rainwater tank "nodig voor de hete zomerdagen", an old roof window as a greenhouse. Sensory detail is thinner because there is no participant-authored text.
- The seasonal argument is **stated, not demonstrated**: no source reports what participants experience across seasons.

### 5.8 Conflict, exclusion and uneven care burdens — the counter-evidence
This is where the two cases teach most, and it is thin in the promotional sources.

1. **A garden can be someone else's nuisance.** In the Bennekel: "Toen Anna na klachten van buurtbewoners delen van haar tuintje moest opruimen, legde dat een voor TGE herkenbare discussie bloot: wat voor de één een groene oase is, is voor de ander een obstakel of rommel" (Trefpunt Groen, 17 Jul 2026). A resident's street-garden was partially removed after complaints. Greening from below is **not universally welcomed**, and the sanction fell on the most enthusiastic person.
2. **Motivation can be absent, and the fix was external.** "Het idee hiervoor lag er al langer, maar de motivatie in de buurt ontbrak" (Woonbedrijf, 2023). The Buurtwerf's start required a commissioned designer and a landlord's investment — i.e. the "condition" was **institutional sponsorship plus one already-active resident**, not latent neighbourly energy.
3. **Care burden concentrates.** A handful of names recur across years and tasks in Bennekel (Laura; Jannie; coordinator Linde; hoveniers trio). Trefpunt Groen describes the working group as running weekly litter rounds *plus* facade gardens, planters, front-garden help and green strips — a wide portfolio on few shoulders. In Gestel the core is "zo'n 25" of ~180 members, and the treasurer's post went unfilled in public from September 2024.
4. **Key-person dependence and its cost.** The 2019 obituary of co-founder and secretary Olaf Beelen shows how much of the work — writing responses to municipal plans "tot diep in de nacht", hosting officials and press, running the workdays — rested on one volunteer, and that the group's continuity was carried by personal relationships ("je luisterend oor, je vriendschap"). Friendship is documented here as an *outcome* of shared work; so is fragility.
5. **Access can exclude by design or by drift.** The Vergeten Tuin is closed 29 days out of 30, and one source describes it as being for people living around it. A garden optimised for fauna (one open day a month so as "zo min mogelijk verstoren") necessarily limits the casual, repeated visits through which neighbours usually meet. The association names this trade-off openly; the social cost of it is not discussed anywhere.
6. **Professionalisation displaces or protects volunteers — the sources cannot say which.** 4W's caretaking kept the Buurtwerf open more often ("We zijn nu dankzij Doe mee met 4W vaker open"), and simultaneously means the routine care is partly **paid work** by people in a social-employment scheme. Whether that deepened or thinned resident ownership is not addressed by any source.
7. **No source in either case reports exclusion by ethnicity, income, language or disability**, and none reports conflict over plot allocation, noise, or opening hours at the Buurtwerf. This is an absence of public record, not evidence of harmony.
8. **Uneven gender/age visibility:** nearly all named participants in the retrieved sources are women (Laura, Jannie, Linde, Anna, Trini, the chair, the secretary Maud/Anneke/Ilse/Sjanie); named men appear as hoveniers, a student-worker, an ecologist and the late co-founder. Whether that reflects who does the caring or who gets quoted cannot be determined from these sources.

## 6. Cross-case reading (interpretation, explicitly labelled)

**INTERPRETATION (mine, from the above):** the two cases describe two different routes to the same outcome, and the contrast is more instructive than either case alone.

- **Gestel: crisis → organisation → design → rhythm.** A shared informal practice existed for decades without a name; it became an institution only when the ground was threatened (2001 sanitation/demolition plans). The condition for durable care was a **threat that made the loss imaginable**, plus a small group willing to do bureaucratic work, plus a professional design that converted sentiment into a maintainable object, plus one fixed monthly moment that doubled as work and welcome. Care here is *custodial*: the garden's needs come first, humans are guests once a month.
- **Bennekel: commission → designer → resident leader → staffed hosting.** Care was *installed*: a landlord decided the yard should live, hired a designer, and the social infrastructure (coffee several times a week, a caretaker, a cleanup group) is partly paid. The condition for participation is low cost and low commitment (a free patch, no membership, "bepaal zelf wanneer, hoe vaak en hoeveel minuten"), and the social benefit is explicitly named by the resident leader as connection and neighbourhood safety.
- Read together, the cases suggest that **relationships emerge as a by-product of recurring, bounded, useful work with a low entry price and a visible host** — and that the fragile parts are the same in both: a few people carry the load, an unfilled treasurer's post or an absent designer changes everything, and the surrounding neighbours are not automatically allies.

**INFERENCE (weaker):** the Buurtwerf's dependence on paid hosting is a risk to the "relationships emerge through care" mechanism: if the coffee is someone's job, the mutuality may be thinner than the Trefpunt's "eenzaamheid wordt bestreden" implies. This is a hypothesis the retrieved sources cannot test.
