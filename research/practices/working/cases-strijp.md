# Two Strijp cases: Buurttuin Philipsdorp (Bellefort) and De Block Acker (Strijp-S)

Working note for issue #20. Desk research, public sources only. Retrieved 26 November 2026. Author: `cases-strijp` (delegated case line). Scope: the two named cases only; no other initiatives researched.

**Read this first — the date problem.** The brief warned that some public accounts date from 2023. Both cases turned out worse than that:

- For **Buurttuin Philipsdorp** the founding document is from **2012** (an *Iepenblaadje* article), the realisation document from **July 2022**, and the live site carries **no publication dates at all** (Wix `lastmod` 2026-11-24 is a site-wide rebuild stamp, not a date of writing).
- For **De Block Acker** the only substantive description is a **Trefpunt Groen network page whose text has not changed since at least 6 December 2023** (Wayback digests identical 2023-12-06 and 2024-04-21; text identical today, only the footer address changed from Klokgebouw 146 to 159). That page describes a garden founded in May 2023 that had held "five work days" and had just been handed to volunteers "last month". It is, in substance, a **late-2023 snapshot**, not a 2026 status report.

Every claim below is labelled: **[D]** documented in a retrieved source; **[C]** one party's claim; **[I]** my inference; **[U]** not established in the sources I could reach.

## 1. Buurttuin Philipsdorp / Bellefort

### Origin and dates

- **January–autumn 2012: plan.** Three residents began planning a "buurttuin" on the ground around wijkcentrum 't Bellefort, Iepenlaan 40, targeting realisation "dit najaar" (that autumn). Signed by "het kernteam Sophie Vermeulen, Annemieke Koppens, en Rob Verhoeven". Subsidy was requested from Het Oranje Fonds, stichting Strijp binnen de Ring and Woonbedrijf. Practical support: "Jan Konings, verbinder van Buurt in Bloei" and "Jacqueline van Heumen, coördinator van wijkcentrum 't Bellefort". **[D]** — *Iepenblaadje* 4e kwartaal 2012, PDF hosted by Strijp binnen de Ring (primary text recovered from the Wayback Machine; the live file now 404s).
- **17 July 2022: opening.** "Buurttuin Philipsdorp is een feit!" — the garden opened with a swap of plants and cuttings, insect-hotel making, pot painting and seed planting, attended by 130 residents, followed by a second neighbourhood *borrel*. **[D]** — undated-author update dated 24-7-2022, also hosted by Strijp binnen de Ring.
- **Ten years between plan and opening.** The 2022 document speaks of "deze nieuwe ontmoetingsplek" and of residents "kennis gemaakt" with it, i.e. it presents the garden as new in 2022. Whether any earlier 2012–13 iteration was ever built is **[U]**; the 2012 plan's own target was autumn 2012. That the 2012 article was still sitting on the neighbourhood site in August 2022 (Wayback capture 2022-08-14) suggests it was being used as the project's history at opening time **[I]**.
- **2025–2026: the host institution is in a transfer year, not the garden.** **[D]** — see "who holds the ground".

### Legal form and who holds the ground

- Bellefort is one of **seven municipal buurthuizen** whose management and operation the municipality contracted to **Lumens**; the 2025 arrangement was an "incidentele subsidie" because "Lumens heeft eerder aangegeven dat de bestaande constructie financieel niet haalbaar was". The municipality's stated principle: "vrijwillig beheer, tenzij professionele ondersteuning noodzakelijk of gewenst is". **[D]** — Raadsbrief "Overgangsjaar beheer en exploitatie buurthuizen Lumens in 2026", B&W 9 December 2025, inboeknummer 8086297_3.4.1, dossier 25.50.103.
- **2026 is an explicit transition year:** "worden de huidige huurovereenkomsten met Lumens voortgezet en is Lumens bereid het beheer en exploitatie in 2026, als overgangsjaar, voort te zetten", with a new construction sought for 2027 onwards. **[D]** — same.
- **Specifically for Bellefort:** "Bellefort: extra inzet vanuit WIJeindhoven (Buurt in Bloei) en CKE om te komen tot vrijwillig beheer. Een plan van aanpak wordt opgeleverd in april 2026." **[D]** — same. The April 2026 plan itself was not found publicly **[U]**.
- The site's own account matches: "In 2025 en 2026 vinden er veranderingen plaats in onder andere het beheer, de inrichting en invulling van activiteiten." **[D]** — bellefort.info homepage, retrieved 26 Nov 2026. A 10 December 2025 meeting ("De koers van Bellefort in 2026") had the municipality explain "hoe het overdrachtsjaar eruitziet", Stichting Lumens explain "rol in het beheer en de dagelijkse gang van zaken in 2026", and working groups on *beheer*, *activiteiten* and *interieur* report. **[D]** — bellefort.info post, 4 Dec 2025.
- **No legal entity for the garden itself was found.** "Buurttuin Philipsdorp" appears as a volunteer group with a named contact, not as a stichting or vereniging **[U]**. Whether the garden ground is inside the municipal lease to Lumens or held on another arrangement is **[U]** — the raadsbrief covers the *buildings* ("buurthuizen"), not the garden parcel. **[I]** The garden's tenure is therefore derivative: it rides on the buurthuis arrangement, not on any agreement of its own.

### What participants do, how often, and who coordinates

- **Present tense, 2026 site:** "De Buurttuin is een groene oase rondom Bellefort die volledig onderhouden wordt door vrijwilligers. Hulp is altijd welkom!" and, on the homepage, "De grote tuin wordt vrijwillig bijgehouden door Buurttuin Philipsdorp." **[D/C]** — bellefort.info/buurttuin and homepage. This is the site's claim, not an observation; it is consistent across two pages of the same site, so it is one witness, not two.
- **Joining:** by phoning the named contact — "Wil je helpen tuinieren? Neem dan contact op met Sophie: 06 83 27 70 70". **[D]** — bellefort.info/buurttuin.
- **Same phone number, 2012 and 2026.** The 2012 article signs off with "Sophie: 06-83277070"; the 2026 buurttuin page gives "06 83 27 70 70". **[I]** Almost certainly the same person, i.e. one individual has been the public point of contact for fourteen years. This is the single most structurally significant fact about the garden's continuity, and it is an inference from a phone number, not a statement by anyone.
- **The garden in daily use (2022):** "Tussen deze activiteiten door wordt buurttuin dagelijks bezocht door spelende kinderen, hun ouders, buurtbewoners die willen bewegen(buitenyoga), ontspannen (boekje lezen, sigaretje roken), werkenden uit de buurt (met laptop aan picknicktafels), hondenbezitters maken ommetje (en ruimen bijna allemaal hondenpoep op!) Jongeren (max 2 personen) die even komen chillen. Mensen die op een matje zonnen." **[D]** — 24-7-2022 update. This is the closest thing in either case to a description of *unpurposed* shared use: people in the garden not gardening.
- **Numbers:** "Er zijn momenteel 12 vaste buurtbewoners actief in het 'tuinteam'. Zij onderhouden de tuin en gaan verder met nieuwe aankleding en inrichting (denk aan insecten-bijenhotel maken, wanden vlechten, ontwikkeling 'blote voetenpad' en bloementuin)." **[D]** — 24-7-2022. Current (2026) number of volunteers is **[U]**.

### Recruitment mechanism — the most transferable finding in this case

> "Door actief aanwezig te zijn in de tuin en te werken komen veel buurtbewoners kijken en vragen stellen, zo werven wij weer nieuwe mensen die een handje mee willen helpen."
> — tuinteam update, 24-7-2022 **[D]**. English: *By being visibly present and working in the garden, many neighbours come and look and ask questions; that is how we recruit new people who want to lend a hand.*

This is a mechanism claim by the practitioners, not a measured effect. It is consistent with the 2012 article's own recruitment problem — a call for "een tiental vrijwilligers" for short announced jobs and "met spoed … 2 vrijwilligers" for the core team **[D]** — and with the observation that the garden works as a *stage* before it works as a *membership*.

### The mix: gardening, meals, culture — and how far it is actually integrated

- **What the garden was meant to host (2012):** "'buurt BBQ', 'plantenruildagen', 'Engelse High Tea', 'buitenyoga', 'kruidenazijn maken', 'schilderen in de tuin', 'zelf compost maken' en nog veel meer…." **[C]** — a wish list, not a record.
- **What actually happened around the opening (2022):** a residents' *buurtborrel* on 17 June (40 visitors) organised by residents of Iepenlaan/Klimoplaan; the primary school's summer party on 14 July (350 visitors); the opening on 17 July (130); a second *borrel* "die tot laat in de avond door ging"; and planned *burendag* (Stichting De Kunsterij) and a *monumentendag* walk including the garden (Architecturencentrum Eindhoven). Residents' own ideas recorded at that point: "buurt BBQ, koffie en thee ochtenden, plantenruildag". **[D]** — 24-7-2022.
- **The wider site, 2026:** Bellefort's weekly agenda runs *Kinderkunst* (free, drop-in), *lunches at €1* (open inloop) three days a week, *taalles* (Taalkracht), Dutch-language coffee mornings for women of different cultural backgrounds, an iftar with neighbours, a games evening, theatre rehearsals, meditation, "Samen koken, samen eten" (cooking together, 29 June, 10.30–12.30). **[D]** — bellefort.info homepage and blog, retrieved 26 Nov 2026. Named "medemogelijkmakers": Buurt in Bloei, CKE, Strijp binnen de ring, Buurtkeuken, Woonbedrijf, St. Geef om elkaar NL. **[D]**
- **Crucial limit:** the record shows the *mix* at the level of the **Bellefort site**, and shows the *garden* as a separate volunteer-maintained strand. **No retrieved source documents a shared meal, a cooking session or a cultural activity taking place *in* the buurttuin, or gardeners and non-gardeners doing those things together.** The 2012 wish list and the 2022 "ideas of residents" come closest; both are intentions, not events. So: gardening plus meals plus culture **co-exist at one address** **[D]**; their **integration** is **[U]**, and should not be asserted.

### What participants themselves say

The only first-person account I could retrieve from a Bellefort participant concerns the site's cultural programme, not the garden:

> "Ieder kind is welkom en kan gratis meedoen."
> "Kinderen vinden het zó leuk dat ze terug blijven komen. Zo bouwen we echt een band op met elkaar."
> "Gewoon beginnen."
> "Zo leg je de lat niet te hoog en geef je jezelf ruimte om te groeien."
> — **Claire**, who runs *Kinderkunst* with Anna and Patricia every Wednesday afternoon at 't Bellefort, interviewed by Stichting Doede Goed **[D]**. English: *Every child is welcome and can join for free. / Children enjoy it so much that they keep coming back. That is how we really build a bond with each other. / Just start. / That way you don't set the bar too high and you give yourself room to grow.*
> Interview retrieved at doedegoed.nl; Bellefort's own post pointing to it is dated **28 October 2026**. The interview itself carries no visible date **[D/date partly U]**. The post says Kinderkunst "bestaat 3 jaar", i.e. since about 2023.

This is a resident-organiser's account of how a recurring, free, drop-in activity became a group — "just start", low bar, children returning. It is *not* about the garden, and it is not evidence about gardeners. It is the best available local statement of the conditions under which people come back, and it comes from the same address.

**No first-person account by a Buurttuin Philipsdorp gardener was found.** **[U]** — absence of a public record, not evidence of absence.

### Present status in 2026

- Garden exists and is presented as volunteer-maintained **[D/C]** (2026 site).
- Host institution is mid-transition: municipal transfer year, plan of action due April 2026, new operator sought from 2027 **[D]**. 2026 was also a year of "veranderingen in … beheer, de inrichting en invulling van activiteiten" at the site **[D]**, with a residents' survey open **[D]**.
- Volunteer count, planting content, opening regime, and whether the 2022 "blote voetenpad"/flower garden/insect hotel were built: **[U]**.

## 2. De Block Acker, Strijp-S

### Origin and dates

- **May 2023: founded** by the "creatief collectief" of **YellowSunshine** — "Frans, Suus en Kat" — as a 100 m² shared food garden at **The New Block**, for residents of Strijp-S and the adjacent **Lijsterbesbuurt**. **[D]** — Trefpunt Groen network page (content frozen since ≤ Dec 2023).
- YellowSunshine describes itself as "Een creatief gelijkgezind en getalenteerd collectief dat graag werkt aan community-based projecten" with three people: Frans (project leader), Suus (event manager), Kat (art director/storyteller). **[D]** — yellowsunshine.nl, retrieved 26 Nov 2026; site carries "© 2024", no garden page, and no Block Acker project entry in its work list.
- **By late 2023:** five joint work days held; the garden handed over "aan de vrijwilligers zelf". **[D]** — same page.
- **The page is the initiative's only substantial public description and it is a third party's.** Trefpunt Groen Eindhoven is an advisory city-wide green network, not the initiative. It labels the case "Type: Bewonerinitiatief". **[D]** No site, blog, Instagram bio or newsletter text authored by the Block Acker volunteers themselves was retrievable **[U]**.

### Legal form and who holds the ground

- **No legal entity** found: not a stichting or vereniging in any retrieved source; coordination is a **WhatsApp group** ("Zij hebben een whatsapp groep waarin zij contact onderhouden en afspraken maken wanneer zij samen in de voedseltuin gaan werken") **[D]**. Whether any entity exists: **[U]**.
- **Ground:** "Bij The New Block is een stuk grond van 100 m² beschikbaar gesteld" — *made available*, verb and term unspecified: no lease, no duration, no rent, no conditions **[D for the fact; U for the terms]**.
- **What The New Block is:** a circular "living lab" and platform in the **SFS building, Kastanjelaan, Strijp-S**, with — per its project architect's page — "Opdrachtgever: Park Strijp Beheer / Volker Wessels / StdK", "Organisatie en projectleiding: De Conceptenbouwers", "Samenwerkingsverband: Yellow Sunshine / bygg architecture / Plug-in-City", partners including "Gemeente Eindhoven" and "Woonbedrijf", and (at the time of that page) "Fase: Structuurontwerp" **[D]** — burokade.nl project page, retrieved 26 Nov 2026. In 2020 wethouder Rik Thijs signed a cooperation agreement making The New Block the base for **Klimaatnetwerk040** **[D]** — same page.
- **Who owns the 100 m² and under what title is not established [U].** The garden's tenure appears to be hospitality from a temporary, project-phase platform — the weakest possible form of ground tenure, and structurally similar to the fail point in the Voedselbos case **[I]**.

### What participants do, how often, and who coordinates

- **Rhythm:** "Maandelijks komen vrijwilligers bijeen om in de moestuin te werken en sociaal contact op te doen." **[D]** — monthly, i.e. low-frequency, with social contact named as an explicit purpose alongside the work.
- **What was grown and eaten:** "zijn er al o.a. aardappels, courgettes, eetbare bloemen, uien, pompoenen en kruiden geoogst uit de moestuin", and at work days there was "geplant, getuinierd, geoogst en zelfs van de oogst gegeten" — eating the harvest together is documented, at least on work days **[D]**. Tools and infrastructure: "een regenton, gieter en hark" **[D]**.
- **Openness:** "de tuin is altijd open en vrij toegankelijk voor iedereen"; "Ook anderen mogen deelnemen" **[C]**.
- **Who does the work now:** since the handover, the volunteers themselves, coordinated in WhatsApp, with YellowSunshine still the **public contact** ("Neem dan contact op met suus@yellowsunshine.nl") **[D]**. That is an unresolved ambiguity: operations handed over, the front door not. **[I]**

### The motto and the "close-knit group": documented, but reported, not first-person

> "De missie is om wat toe te voegen aan de wereld. 'Voor de mensen, de planten, de bloemen en de bijen. Zorg goed voor de tuin en voor elkaar!'"
> — quoted as the initiative's mission on Trefpunt Groen's network page **[D]**. English: *For the people, the plants, the flowers and the bees. Take good care of the garden and of each other.*

> "'Het was moeilijk om vrijwilligers te activeren om hiernaar toe te komen.' Ondanks verscheidene flyer pogingen was de opkomst niet zo groot als gehoopt. 'Maar toch hebben we er een fijne club van kunnen maken'."
> — two anonymous quotations inside Trefpunt Groen's account **[D]**. English: *It was difficult to mobilise volunteers to come here. … But even so we managed to make a nice club out of it.*

**Assessment.** The motto is **documented as the initiative's stated mission**, but only at second hand (Trefpunt reporting it) and with no date on the quotation. The "close-knit group" — *een fijne club* — is **the organisers' own retrospective claim, quoted by an intermediary, not a participant's account**, and it sits immediately beside the admission that turnout was disappointing. So: **promotional/self-reported, not independently documented**; and the honest reading is a *small* club that was hard to assemble, not a demonstration that community formed at scale. The word is *"fijne"* (nice/pleasant), not *"hecht"* (close-knit) — the English "close-knit" overstates the Dutch.

**Nothing in the record is in a volunteer-gardener's own voice.** Members' words exist only as these two un-attributed quotes relayed by Trefpunt **[U]** for anything independent.

### Joining, cost, accessibility

- **Joining:** email suus@yellowsunshine.nl, or "neem eens een kijkje bij de moestuin zelf" (just come and look) **[D]**. Entry criteria are stated inclusively: affinity with vegetable gardens, *or* wanting social contact, *or* wanting to learn about growing food **[D]** — i.e. explicitly not only gardeners.
- **Cost:** none stated anywhere; the garden is described as freely accessible **[D/C]**. No membership, subscription or fee appears in any retrieved source **[U]** for whether one exists.
- **Accessibility:** ground-level 100 m² plot on Strijp-S, "altijd open" **[C]**. Physical accessibility (surface, raised beds, seating) is **[U]**; the "betonnen laag" 30 cm below the surface constrains what can be grown **[D]**.

### Present status in 2026 — not established

- The Trefpunt page is live and the case is still listed as a *bewonerinitiatief* in the network **[D]**.
- **But its text is unchanged since at least December 2023** **[D]** (Wayback digests), and every operational detail on it is late-2023.
- **The New Block's own domain is gone:** `thenewblock.nl` fails with a TLS certificate mismatch — the certificate presented belongs to `sold.domainorder.nl`, i.e. the domain is parked/for sale **[D]** (retrieval attempt 26 Nov 2026, no content read).
- **The newsletter archive the brief points to is unavailable.** `us7.campaign-archive.com/?u=257c1a7baa8345ba49e5c0e33&id=0ab8072e6c` now redirects to Mailchimp's generic static page ("You probably found this page because one of our subscribers used Mailchimp…") — the campaign is deleted or the list archived **[D]**. No Wayback capture exists **[D]**. Search engines still index the title *"Nieuwsbrief Mei & Juni | The New Block"* **[D, snippet only]**, and the same list ID serves **Klimaatnetwerk040** newsletters **[D]** — so the garden's newsletter trail is a shared Klimaatnetwerk040 list, not an initiative archive.
- **Conclusion:** whether the Block Acker garden still exists, is maintained, or has been built over in 2026 is **[U]**. There is **no public account after 2023**. Given that the host platform's web presence has lapsed and the intermediary's page has been frozen for three years, dormancy is plausible **[I]** — but a garden can persist unphotographed, and I found no evidence either way.

## 4. Established / claimed / not established

### Buurttuin Philipsdorp

**Established [D]**
1. A buurttuin around wijkcentrum 't Bellefort, Iepenlaan 40, Philipsdorp, was **planned from January 2012** and **opened on 17 July 2022**, with 130 residents at the opening.
2. It is **maintained by volunteers** — stated by the site in 2026 and by the tuinteam in 2022; the 2022 team was **12 regular neighbours**.
3. In 2022 the garden was in **daily, largely non-gardening use**: children playing, parents, yoga, reading, laptop workers at picnic tables, dog walkers, young people, sunbathers.
4. Activity around the garden is **embedded in a dense site programme**: a residents' *borrel* (40), a school summer party (350), a second *borrel*, plus a weekly agenda of free/€1 lunches, language classes, art workshops, coffee mornings, an iftar, games evenings and theatre.
5. The **host institution is municipally contracted to Lumens** and **2026 is an explicit transition year**, with extra WIJeindhoven (Buurt in Bloei) and CKE input to reach voluntary management, and a plan of action due April 2026.
6. **One named contact, reachable by mobile, is how you join** — and the number is the same one published in 2012.

**Claimed [C]**
- "volledig onderhouden … door vrijwilligers" / "Hulp is altijd welkom" — the site's own framing; no independent observation.
- That the garden was designed as "een opstap naar een bruisend hart van Philipsdorp waar bewoners elkaar kunnen vinden en zich samen (weer) inzetten voor de wijk" (2012 ambition).
- The activity wish list (BBQ, High Tea, plant swap, herb vinegar, compost) — intended, not evidenced.

**Not established [U]**
- Volunteer numbers in 2024–2026; whether the 2022 plans (blote voetenpad, flower garden, insect hotel, woven screens) were built.
- Whether the garden ground is inside the municipal lease to Lumens, or held otherwise; whether "Buurttuin Philipsdorp" has any legal form.
- Whether shared meals or cultural activities happen **in** the garden, or involve gardeners and non-gardeners together.
- What the April 2026 plan of action says; whether the garden is named in it.
- Any first-person account by a gardener.
- Any local journalism on the garden: **no public account found** in the searches run (queries naming *indebuurt*, *Studio040* and general Dutch phrasing returned nothing on this garden).

### De Block Acker

**Established [D]**
1. **Founded May 2023** by three people from YellowSunshine (Frans, Suus, Kat) on **100 m² made available by The New Block** at Strijp-S, for Strijp-S and Lijsterbesbuurt residents.
2. **Monthly volunteer work days** with social contact as a stated purpose; five work days had happened by late 2023; potatoes, courgettes, edible flowers, onions, pumpkins and herbs were harvested, and the harvest was eaten together.
3. **Handed over to the volunteers themselves** by late 2023, coordinated through a **WhatsApp group**; YellowSunshine remained the public contact.

## 5. What bears on the issue's themes

**Informal hospitality.** Bellefort's €1 open-inloop lunches, free drop-in art workshops and coffee mornings are the strongest documented instance of low-threshold hospitality in either case **[D]**. Block Acker's "altijd open" garden and "kom eens kijken" invitation are the same idea in weaker form — a claim without an observed practice **[C]**. Note that in both cases the hospitality is *hosted by an institution or a named individual*, not generated by the group as such.

**Regular rhythms.** Three different rhythms are visible and they are not interchangeable: **daily unpurposed presence** (Bellefort garden, 2022) **[D]**; **weekly programmed activity** (Kinderkunst, lunches, taalles) **[D]**; **monthly collective work** (Block Acker) **[D]**. The monthly rhythm is the thinnest — too sparse to build familiarity on its own, which is **[I]** consistent with the organisers' difficulty in retaining volunteers, though the sources do not make that link.

**Autonomy.** Block Acker's handover to a WhatsApp-coordinated volunteer group is the clearest case of delegated autonomy in either case, and it came with the founders still holding the public contact address **[D/I]**. Bellefort's 2026 process is the reverse movement — the municipality and WIJeindhoven building *towards* voluntary management, with working groups on beheer/activiteiten/interieur **[D]**. Autonomy granted (Block Acker) and autonomy constructed (Bellefort) are different things and produce different accountabilities.

**Shared meals.** Documented twice, at two different scales: harvest eaten together on Block Acker work days **[D]**, and "Samen koken, samen eten" plus €1 lunches at Bellefort **[D]**. In neither case is the meal documented as an *outcome* of the garden; at Bellefort the two run in parallel at one address **[D/I]**.

**Practical work and visible labour.** The Bellefort tuinteam's own mechanism claim — visible work draws questions, questions draw helpers **[D]** — is the single most useful practitioner proposition retrieved. It implies the garden's recruitment surface is its *legibility*, not its programme.

**Learning.** Block Acker names "samen te leren over het verbouwen van voedsel" as a purpose and recruits explicitly on wanting to learn **[D]**. Bellefort's 2012 plan had the primary school wanting the garden "voor de uitvoering van educatieve activiteiten" **[C]**, and the 2022 record shows the school's summer party actually happening there **[D]** — but no educational use of the garden is documented.

**Sensory and seasonal experience.** Almost nothing. The Block Acker page mentions edible flowers, pumpkins, a rain barrel, a watering can, a rake; the Bellefort record mentions a barefoot path (planned), a flower garden (planned), insect hotels **[D/C]**. No source describes smell, taste, weather, or seasonal change as experienced. Anyone writing about sensory or seasonal benefit from these two cases would be writing from outside the evidence.

**Freedom to participate differently.** This is where the two cases diverge most usefully. Bellefort's 2022 text is the only source in either case that describes people using a garden **without gardening** — sitting, reading, working on a laptop, sunbathing, walking a dog **[D]**. Block Acker's entry criteria ("affinity *or* social contact *or* learning") are inclusive on paper **[D]**, but every documented activity is a work day.

**Conflict, exclusion, uneven burden, withdrawal.** Documented: mobilisation failure and disappointing turnout (Block Acker) **[D]**; a concrete layer thwarting the plan (Block Acker) **[D]**; a founding core of three plus "met spoed" two more, and a standing team of 12 doing the maintenance while stimulating others (Bellefort) **[D]**. Not documented anywhere: an argument, an exclusion, a resignation, a burnout, a theft, a vandal. **Absence of a public account of conflict is not evidence of harmony** — volunteer groups rarely publish either.

**The care-burden signal that matters most.** In Bellefort the entire public interface of a fourteen-year-old garden is **one mobile number**. In Block Acker the entire interface is **one email address at the founder's company**. Neither case shows a second named keeper or a collective front door **[D/I]** — which is the same structural finding the main brief reached from the Voedselbos closure, now visible in two *living* cases as well.
