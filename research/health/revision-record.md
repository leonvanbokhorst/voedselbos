## 1. How the question and its scope changed

**Not at all.** The issue's question, scope, exclusions, geography (Eindhoven and the region), population and period are unchanged. No stakeholder was contacted, no form submitted, nothing registered, no fieldwork, no intervention designed, no clinical or nutritional advice given, no personal profiling. The revision was desk research on the same authorised question with an explicit list of corrections from the PI.

**What changed is the inferential standard.** v1 systematically converted *the absence of a study* into *a conclusion about the mechanism*. v2 adopts four evidential states (supports / contradicts / insufficient / measure-unsuitable), puts a State column on every proposition, and follows three rules stated in `note.md` §2.1–2.2:

1. "Not studied" is never reported as "implausible".
2. A null is never generalised beyond its measured outcomes, population and dose.
3. "This instrument cannot answer the question" is never transferred to "the hypothesis is false".

| # | Hypothesis | Why it is still unresolved | What would resolve it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **H1** | A shared practice produces task capability, not portable capability | No retrieved study measures capability after exit; the two studies that look hardest (`D6`,`D19`) find confident participation and weak transfer, and JArDinS could not see it (dose near zero for half, no capability outcome) | A garden cohort measured at exit and 12 months later on a task performed at home, with a comparison |

## 5. How the answer changed

**v1's answer:** *a shared green practice is a plausible place for people to become more capable at food and at asking, and an implausible vehicle for health literacy; the most defensible thing it does for health is to demand less of people than the health system demands.*

**v2's answer:** *a shared green practice is a plausible setting for the relational and practical part of health literacy — asking, doing, judging a situation with other people — and it has never been studied as a health-literacy intervention. The absence of that study is not evidence against the mechanism. What the evidence establishes is narrower and more useful: that a garden can supply the conditions in which interactive capability is exercised, that the population instrument cannot measure whether it happens, and that the two conditions most likely to defeat the mechanism are dose and organisational form, not disbelief.*

**In one line:** v1 said *the evidence says no*; v2 says *the evidence does not say yes, and does not say no — and here, precisely, is what would.*

**What did not change:** every factual finding about the Dutch number and its instrument; the DE STAP architecture and its fragility; the directory classification; the asking-for-help gradient; the participation evidence table; the exclusion and qualification-trap analysis. The revision **narrows or withdraws conclusions drawn from those findings**; it does not revise the findings.
