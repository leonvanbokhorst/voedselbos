## 1. The answer

**Short answer: a shared green practice is a plausible setting for the *relational and practical* part of health literacy — asking, doing, judging a situation with other people — and it has never been studied as a health-literacy intervention at all. The absence of that study is not evidence against the mechanism. What the retrieved evidence does establish is narrower and more useful: that a garden can supply the conditions in which interactive capability is exercised (a person present, a recurring occasion, low demands, no qualification), that the population-level health-literacy instrument cannot measure whether it happens, and that the two conditions most likely to defeat the mechanism are dose and organisational form, not disbelief.**

The issue's proposition has three links: participation → health literacy → self-directed behaviour change that carries into everyday life. **v1 declared the middle link "implausible" on the strength of not finding a study. That was closure from absence and it is withdrawn.** Read against the retrieved evidence, the three links are in different evidential states, and the differences are the answer:

- **The middle link is not studied.** Five recorded searches (four in v1, one documentary sweep in this revision) found **no study, anywhere, that uses an adult community garden, allotment, urban farm or food forest deliberately as a health-literacy intervention with health literacy as an outcome** [U, `A` §5; `F`/`nl-literature.md` §2 Q5]. "Green health literacy" is not an established construct; the nearest real family is environmental/climate/planetary health literacy, which is professional-facing and unvalidated — 28 studies, **17 distinct concepts**, "requires empirical validation" [D, `A27`]. A claim that a garden strengthens health literacy is an inference *across constructs* — but **the absence of the study is a gap in the literature, not a result about gardens** (§2.2, §5).
- **The third link has one well-powered attempt, and it found nothing — for the outcomes it measured, in the population it measured, at the dose it achieved.** JArDinS followed 66 people entering a community garden against 66 pairwise-matched non-gardeners for twelve months: "no significant impact, **in spite of sufficient statistical power**" [D, `B7` — re-read at full text this revision, `revision-record.md` §2]. Four qualifications are now recorded and they matter: power was computed for **one** outcome (fruit and vegetables, +1 portion/day), the sample was **75.8% university-educated**, the two groups differed at baseline (education p = 0.049, BMI p = 0.046), and **half the gardeners harvested nothing all year**. JArDinS tested what *joining* a garden does. It did not test what *practising in* one does, and it measured nothing about literacy, appraisal, asking or cooking [D/U, `B7`].
- **The first link is where the evidence is, and it is weaker and more design-dependent than the issue assumes.** Gardening programmes do produce small-to-moderate improvement in self-reported mood and social contact; the pooled estimate is carried by the weakest designs (before/after *d* = 0.60 against gardener/non-gardener *d* = 0.32), and the reviewers' own verdict is that "evidence quality overall was low" and effects "may be overestimated" [D, `B1`, `B4` — Lead-verified].

So the honest answer is **not** "yes, under conditions" and **not** "no". It is:

> **The mechanism is unstudied, its two most plausible carriers — relational learning and low-demand participation — have supporting evidence from neighbouring fields, and the specific claim "a garden builds health literacy" cannot be made from anything retrieved. What can be said is conditional, and the conditions are stated in §1.3.**

### 1.1 Ten findings carry the answer

1. **The headline number the issue inherits measures the wrong thing, and the record says so.** "About half of Dutch adults have limited health literacy" is **44.3%** (Nivel panel, HLS19-Q12, fieldwork November 2025) to an upper end that is itself uncertain, **52.3–52.8%** (RIVM, HLS19-Q47, fieldwork 6 June–6 July 2025 — the report states 52.8%, Pharos restates it as 52.3% in one place; §6.1) [D, `A4`,`A6`,`E6` — Lead-verified]. The spread — 8 points on the RIVM report's own figures, 5.7 if 52.3% is the right one — is mostly **instrument length**: the same 11,441 respondents score 52.8% on Q47 and 46.6% on Q12 [D, `A4`]. The step up from 29–36% (2012–2023, HLS-EU-Q16) is described by the national statistics portal as a consequence of the new instrument [D, `A7`]. The cut-offs are "**conceptueel vastgesteld**", not validated; the score is a rescaled mean of a four-point difficulty item, so 50 is the arithmetic neutral midpoint [D, `A4` — Lead-verified]; and RIVM states plainly that this "meet **ervaren moeilijkheid, niet objectieve vaardigheid**" and that the questionnaire was "hoogstwaarschijnlijk alleen ingevuld door mensen die kunnen lezen" [D, `A4` — Lead-verified]. **This is a finding about the instrument, not about gardens** (§7).
2. **The Dutch field has already moved to the demand side, which changes what a garden would even be doing.** RIVM's operational definition is relational — "een **dynamisch samenspel** tussen individuele capaciteiten en de complexiteit van het zorg- en informatiesysteem" [D, `A4`] — and Pharos states the doctrine without hedging: "Gezondheidsvaardigheden worden niet alleen bepaald door wat iemand zelf kan, maar ook door de eisen die de omgeving stelt" [D, `A1`, **Lead-verified on the factsheet**]; "Dat zoveel mensen beperkte gezondheidsvaardigheden hebben ligt niet aan hen" [D, `A2`]. On this definition, a garden's relevant property is not only what it teaches but also **how little it demands**: no referral, no form, no fluent reading, no registration, no measurement. **v1 read that as a claim about *access* instead of *literacy*. This revision reads it as a claim about the capability the setting requires — which is what COM-B calls opportunity and what Nutbeam calls the social skills of the interactive level** (§3, §5).
3. **Food literacy is the construct that contains doing, and its gardening evidence base is real but is about children in schools.** Gardening is an established vehicle for **food literacy** (planning, managing, selecting, preparing): a preregistered Danish school-garden protocol, a Tasmanian kitchen-garden realist evaluation, and a systematic review of school food-and-nutrition-literacy interventions already organised by Nutbeam's functional/interactive/critical levels [D, `A28`,`A29`,`A31`]. The Dutch operationalisation is the **schooltuin**, and "Voedseleducatie is niet verplicht en daarmee geen structureel onderdeel van het Nederlandse curriculum" [D, `A26`]. **Health literacy contains *appraising*; food literacy contains *doing*.** A food forest plausibly produces the second — that is a hypothesis with a neighbouring evidence base, not a finding (§3.3, §10 H1).
4. **Participation buys relationship, not capability, and the benefit is site-bound — with one Dutch finding that points the other way.** The Dutch evaluation of care-and-neighbourhood gardens is modest, self-reported, and — in one detail — directly on point: year after year **9 in 10** participating volunteers agreed "ik heb het afgelopen jaar nieuwe mensen leren kennen in de buurt", **5 in 10** that "sta ik eerder voor andere mensen klaar", and **4 in 10** that "**durf ik meer mensen om hulp te vragen**" [D, `E8`, Lead-verified]. **All three items are retrospective self-reports of change with no baseline and no comparison group, in one funded programme — so they cannot show a level or a measured rise.** What they do show is a gradient in *reported* change, and asking is the one health-literacy-adjacent skill in the set and the one least often reported as moving. The qualitative material from another country and language is consistent with that, not decisive: new gardeners "also described feeling shy and hesitant to ask for help", and those who could draw on relatedness were more likely to find the garden worthwhile [D, `F5`]. Coordinator burden is measured, not anecdotal: volunteer coordinators carry "the greatest burdens on their time and wellbeing … fatigue and feelings of over-commitment" [D, `B23`]. **Capability and benefit are attached to the place, and the repository's own anchor case is a place that ended.**
5. **Dutch applied research on growing and health exists, is modest, and does not measure literacy.** The RIVM-authored review in *Bodem* concludes that community gardens "op een gezonde bodem kunnen bijdragen aan de gezondheid van bewoners": people move more, eat more of their own vegetables, and there are "aanwijzingen dat stress afneemt en er meer sociale contacten in de buurt ontstaan" — and it reports its own counter-findings, that gardens "dragen niet per definitie bij aan integratie", that groups may work *next to* rather than *with* each other, and that a tight group can make other residents feel excluded [D, `F6`, full text — **new in this revision**]. The same article reports that RIVM developed a **questionnaire to measure health effects** of urban agriculture — a Dutch context-specific instrument measuring movement, consumption, stress, social contact and cohesion, **and not literacy** [D, `F6`]. The Dutch PhD thesis on the subject reaches the same shape of conclusion from seven cases and two years of observation: gardens "do not necessarily foster a more inclusive society; they often attract people with relatively similar socio-economic backgrounds"; "Most participants from place-based gardens eat from their gardens only occasionally; others never do so"; and whether produce reaches the plate depends on whether the participant is organised around **growing** or around **shopping** [D, `F8`, abstract read in full].
6. **The regional infrastructure is real, bigger and thinner than the issue says, and it is mid-handover.** The partner count is **193**, not 189 (the about page, the counter and 193 listed names agree) [D, `E1`,`C3` — Lead-verified; the Fontys side of the same landscape was re-verified live this revision, §8]; growth ran 100 → 127 → 158 → 193 across municipal and Fontys documents [D, `C24`,`C25`,`C30`]. But DE STAP is not a foundation: its operator **Coöperatie Slimmer Leven was dissolved on 1 January 2026**, **GGD Brabant-Zuidoost holds a one-year penvoerderschap for 2026**, the relevant subsidies total **€1.2 million and end in 2026**, and "structurele borging van deze themalijnen bij een passende organisatie na 2026" is still to be decided [D, `C26` — Lead-verified]. There is **no public annual report, accounts, board or evaluation** of DE STAP [U, `C` §4]. Among the 193 partners sit Fontys, GGD BZO, the municipality, IVN and — decisively for the main question — **Wasven**; **no food forest, community garden or citizen group appears in the list** [D, `C3`, absence].
7. **The directory shows what the movement already does with green and food, and it files it as meeting, not as health or learning.** Of 147 Eindhoven listings, **14 are green or food (~10%)**, 99 are meeting-and-advice (~67%), and **every garden sits under the category *Ontmoeten*** [D, `E2`,`C7` — Lead-verified count]. The Irisbuurt garden is **De Iristuin** at Buurthuis De Blokhut — a decade old, four named volunteers, herbs and tomatoes, and a neighbourhood pizza oven that uses the harvest — described on DE STAP with no address, no opening times, no cost and no contact [D, `C9`,`E3`]; and **'t Kruidenkafé is a GGzE day-activity venue** whose "Kruiden" is the *Kruidenbuurt*, with no growing of any kind [D, `C10`,`C11`,`C12`; the GGzE attribution is Line C's retrieval, not re-verified]. The food activities are mostly **institutional kitchens that cook for and with residents** at €0–7; the green activities are maintenance and nature visits. The two rarely meet.
8. **The participation door is cheaper than the partnership door, and its own criteria contain the anti-qualification principle the issue asks for.** An activity can be listed by **anyone with a name and an e-mail**, free, subject to four published content criteria — one of which is "**Het is zonder verwijzing of indicatie toegankelijk**" [D, `C6`]. Partner status, by contrast, requires an *organisation* ("Naam organisatie") and leads to the Paco partner community; no fee, obligation or governance right is published anywhere [D, `C4`,`C5`]. So the route from a garden to visibility in the region's health movement is already open, free, and needs no entity — and the route into its governance is closed to anything that is not an organisation.
9. **COM-B and self-determination theory are now retrieved, and they point at different things than v1 assumed.** COM-B puts **capability, opportunity and motivation on equal footing** — "This system places **no priority** on an individual, group, or environmental perspective — intra-psychic and external factors all have equal status" [D, `F1`] — so "a garden cannot build health literacy" is not a finding about capability but a claim that one of three co-equal conditions does not matter. SDT predicts the opposite risk from the one v1 worried about: that attaching a health goal, a target or a measurement to a garden makes it a **controlling context**, and controlling contexts diminish intrinsic motivation and persistence [D, `F4`]. **Both are theory, not evidence about gardens:** no retrieved study applies either framework to a community garden as a health intervention [U], the one retrieved COM-B application is to *promoting* front gardening and is explicitly un-evaluated [D, `F2`], and the one retrieved SDT application is a qualitative study **nested inside the JArDinS cohort that found nothing** [D, `F5`]. (§4)
10. **The experiential mechanism the issue posits is coherent, partially supported, and testable cheaply — but it has never been tested where the issue needs it.** Nutbeam's interactive level requires social skills and negotiation; situated learning says capability is acquired by doing alongside someone who knows; the Dutch evidence is at least consistent with asking being the harder skill to move — 4 in 10 volunteers report daring to ask more often, against 9 in 10 reporting new contacts [D, `E8`]; teach-back — the cheapest relational check — improves **self-efficacy** (SMD 2.40, very low certainty) and **short-term adherence** (SMD 1.04, low certainty) while showing, at **very low certainty, no clear pattern of effect on knowledge** [D, `F9`]. Assembled, that supports a **hypothesis with stated conditions** (§5), not a conclusion.

### 1.2 What is established, what is contradicted, what cannot be adjudicated

Stated once, in the four states defined in §2.2, so that no reader has to infer the verdict from prose:

| The claim | State | What licenses it |
|---|---|---|
| Adults who garden report better mood and more social contact while they garden | **Supports** | `B1`,`B3`,`B4`,`B12` — but design-dependent and low certainty |
| Community gardening raises household fruit-and-vegetable supply in free-living adults | **Contradicts** (for this population and this dose) | `B7` full text: 66 pairs, 12 months, p = 0.999; and half harvested nothing |
| A garden produces **health literacy** | **Insufficient to adjudicate** | No study retrieved; `A` §5, `F`/`nl-literature.md` Q5 |
| A garden produces **food-literacy** capability | **Insufficient to adjudicate** in adults; **supports** in school-age children | `A26`,`A28`,`A29`,`A31`; `F8` on produce reaching the plate |
| Participation raises **interactive** health literacy — asking, negotiating, getting help | **Insufficient to adjudicate** | No retrieved study measures interactive literacy as an outcome of any green setting. The one Dutch item that asks (§5.3) has no baseline and no comparator, and the qualitative material is consistent with the hypothesis rather than decisive either way (`E8`,`F5`) |
| A single retrospective change item can show whether asking **rose over time** | **Measure-unsuitable** | `E8` asks whether the respondent "sinds het afgelopen jaar" dares ask more people for help — retrospective, no baseline, no comparison group, so it cannot report a level or a measured change |
| HLS19 / HLS-EU / HLQ / AAHLS can serve as a garden's outcome measure | **Measure-unsuitable** | `A4` pp.18, 31; `A6` sheet 15; `S23`,`S24` via `A` |

**Stated position on the balance of evidence [I], held consistently from here on.** On the neighbouring evidence — **clinical (teach-back) and single-programme (the JArDinS cohort)** — relational participation is **weakly positive for interactive capability and self-efficacy and unstudied for health literacy as an outcome**: the first is why the mechanism deserves a test (§5.3), the second is why no claim may be made (§2.2). The weak positive is not a substitute for the missing study, and the missing study is not a reason to discount the weak positive.

### 1.3 Conditional conclusions

**v1's four categorical recommendations are replaced.** Each is now a claim with conditions, and each condition is one that could fail visibly. The protective intent of v1 — no mandatory health intervention, no deficit framing, no gating on health status — is **kept, as a boundary on how the practice is offered, not as a verdict on the mechanism**.

**A. Under what conditions could the health-literacy claim be made at all?** Every row must hold for the claim "this garden strengthened health literacy" to be defensible [I, from the retrieved evidence]:

| Condition | Holds when | Fails when | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Population** | participants include people who *declined or dropped out of* referred health offers, and people with limited literacy — not only the already-engaged | the participant base is the volunteer base in the Dutch evaluation: "ongeveer de helft … universitaire opleiding of … hoger beroepsonderwijs" against "ruim een derde" of the neighbourhood [D, `E8`]; or a JArDinS-type sample at 75.8% university [D, `B7`] | `E8`,`B7`,`H2` |
| **Dose and organisational form** | a recurring, weekly, supervised occasion with a shared task and a person who knows — the form in which the gardening literature does find effects [D, `B3`,`D9`] and which the JArDinS authors themselves name as the more likely vehicle ("multicomponent programs are more likely to modify health outcomes") [D, `B7`] | one year of nominal membership with low involvement — **half of JArDinS gardeners harvested nothing** [D, `B7`]; or a site-dependent commons whose benefit ends when the site ends [D, `B23`] | `B7` full text; `B3`; `B23`; `F8` on growing vs shopping practices |
| **Staffing** | someone is present whose role includes instruction and *being asked* — the role `F5` finds carries competence, and which the volunteer-coordinator evidence shows is the binding cost [D, `B23`,`D2`] | the coordinator is already at capacity: "het aantal begeleiders heeft moeite dat bij te houden" [D, `D2`]; link workers' "capacity is stretched" [D, `D9`] | `B23`,`D2`,`D9`,`F5` |
| **Outcome** | the outcome is specified **before** the activity and is one of: task capability or asking checked by performance or teach-back; a **context-sensitive qualitative or participatory account** of asking and appraising, with its own stated falsifier; or a measure of **interactive** capability from a setting-appropriate instrument [D, `F9`,`F8`,`F7`,`D22`] | the outcome is a population HLS19/HLQ score, a dietary biomarker, or "health literacy" as a latent trait — **or** an unspecified impression gathered after the fact, which cannot fail | `A4`,`F9`,`F8`,`F10`,`F7`,`D22` |
| **Design** | a comparison of *organisational forms* or a *dose* contrast, with follow-up after exit; or a realist/participatory design answering "for whom, under what conditions" [D, `F7`,`D22`] | a before/after self-report, which the meta-analytic gradient shows inflates (*d* 0.60 vs 0.32) [D, `B1`] | `B1`,`F7`,`D22` |

**B. What could be tested cheaply, reversibly, and without turning the garden into a research site** [I]:

- **A dose-and-form comparison inside one garden**, not a new intervention: does a weekly staffed session with a named person produce more return visits and more "I did this at home" reports than open access to the same plot? Uses the garden's own attendance record and one question. This is the cheapest test of the whole mechanism and it is reversible.
- **One teach-back question inside an ordinary session** — "show me how you'd do this at home" — recorded as a yes/no by the person who taught it. `F9` says this is where self-efficacy moves and knowledge does not; a null here is informative and costs nothing.
- **A lapsed-participant count.** Who stopped coming, and what did they do afterwards? No retrieved study measures the after-the-end period [U, `B` §4.3]; the garden already knows the answer informally, and writing it down is the single highest-value item on this list.
- **An offer-side check against DE STAP's own four criteria** [D, `C6`], specifically "zonder verwijzing of indicatie" — a passive, free, reversible test of whether the offer is as low-demand as it claims.
- **A refusal to measure anything else.** If the above produces nothing, the honest finding is "no observable change on these indicators", which is a result — not a failure to be fixed by adding instruments.

**C. What a community should refuse — the boundary, kept** [I, with the evidence that supports each]:

1. **Refuse to make participation conditional on a health goal.** The SDT prediction is explicit: imposed goals and pressured evaluation are controlling and diminish intrinsic motivation [D, `F4`]; the Dutch evidence shows gating on a health problem deters the people the offer claims to serve (§6.9, `D7`,`D14`).
2. **Refuse deficit framing.** A garden is not a remedy for other people's lack. The Dutch field's own doctrine says the demands are the problem [D, `A1`,`A2`], and the critique literature calls the individualising move "health literacy reductionism" [D, `A14`].
3. **Refuse to gate on health status, literacy level, or referral.** DE STAP already writes this into its intake [D, `C6`]; a garden that adds a gate is going backwards.
4. **Refuse the measurement regime.** No pre/post battery as a price of entry: the population instruments are unsuitable (§7), the burden lands on the least able (`F2`'s Equity criterion; `D9`), and imposed measurement is itself a controlling context [D, `F4`].
5. **Refuse to be the intervention.** v1's "do not run a health intervention in a garden" is **withdrawn as a verdict and retained as a boundary**: the garden may host a study if it wants one, co-created and on its own terms [D, `D22`,`F7`]; what it should not do is become one by default, because the request came from outside.

## 2. Status of the propositions

### 2.1 Marks and the four evidential states

The repository's marks (**[D]**, **[C]**, **[I]**, **[U]**, **[P]**) say *how a claim is held*. The four **states** below say *what the retrieved evidence does to a hypothesis*, and they are deliberately independent of the marks — a proposition can be [D] and still be *insufficient* (documented absence is still absence). Every row of §2.3 now carries one.

| State | Meaning | Typical form |
|---|---|---|
| **Supports** | retrieved evidence is consistent with the hypothesis and would be surprising if it were false | an effect measured in a design able to detect it |
| **Contradicts** | retrieved evidence is inconsistent with the hypothesis, in a population and design that could have detected it | a null in a powered test of *that* outcome |
| **Insufficient** | no retrieved study adjudicates it — including "nobody has looked" | the absence of a study, stated as absence |
| **Measure-unsuitable** | a particular operationalisation or instrument cannot answer the question, whatever the answer is | an instrument measuring felt difficulty used as an outcome for a setting |

**Three rules this note now follows, and v1 did not:**

1. **"Not studied" is never reported as "implausible".** v1's §1 sentence — that a shared green practice is "an implausible vehicle for *health literacy*" — is **withdrawn**. The retrieved evidence is that no study exists; that is **Insufficient**, not **Contradicts**.
2. **No generalisation of a null beyond its measured outcomes, population and dose.** JArDinS licenses "no effect on household food supply, accelerometer physical activity, mental and social wellbeing in a highly educated French entry cohort at low achieved dose". It does not license "no effect on health literacy", which it never measured.
3. **Measure-unsuitable is not transferred to the hypothesis.** That HLS19 cannot measure a garden's effect (§7) is a fact about HLS19. It says nothing about whether a garden has an effect.

### 2.2 The absence-of-evidence distinction, stated where it is used

The single most consequential correction in this revision, so it is stated as a rule rather than a caveat:

- **Absence of a garden-specific health-literacy study** → **Insufficient** for the mechanism (P1, P27–P29).
- **A null in a garden study on dietary, activity and wellbeing outcomes** → **Contradicts** those outcomes, in that population and dose (P10, re-scoped).
- **A population instrument that measures felt difficulty on conceptual cut-offs** → **Measure-unsuitable** for a garden's outcome (P6, P29).
- **Dutch grey literature claiming food forests improve health without measuring it** → **Insufficient** and, where the claim is about cultivation rather than exposure, **unsupported by the source** (`F11`).

The distinction has a practical consequence: **(1) and (3) together do not add up to (2)**. v1 added them up. That is the error this revision corrects.

## 3. Constructs: a family, not a hierarchy

**v1 had no construct section; it used "health literacy" and "food literacy" as rivals for the same claim.** This section lays the family out, states their relations and overlaps, says what each can be measured with, and says **which construct this report now claims for a garden and which it does not**.

### 3.1 The six constructs and their relations

| Construct | What it is | Relation to the others | Measurable with what (retrieved) | What it cannot carry |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Health literacy — functional** | decoding and following health information: reading a leaflet, a label, an appointment letter [D, `A9`,`S18` via `A`] | the floor of the family; the level the population instruments mostly measure | HLS19-Q47/Q12, HLS-EU-Q16, HLQ, AAHLS (`A4`; `S23`,`S24`,`S33` via `A`) — all self-report of difficulty | it is a *demand-side* measure: it records what the setting asked, not what the person can do |
| **Health literacy — interactive** | social skills: asking, discussing, negotiating, acting with others; "mainly interactive health literacy was associated with patients' perceived ability to organize care, interact with healthcare providers and perform self-care" [D, `S18` via `A`] | **the level a garden could plausibly reach**, because it is the one that requires other people | no validated garden-suitable instrument retrieved; qualitative observation and teach-back are the nearest [D, `F5`,`F9`] | not separable empirically from the other levels in the short instruments (`A6` sheet 15) |
| **Health literacy — critical** | "information appraisal, understanding the social determinants of health, and collective action" [D, `A9` via `A`] | the level that would make a garden *political*, which is exactly why it is rarely claimed and rarely measured | AAHLS subscales, contested (`S33`); critical-HL interventions are "low" evidence and concentrated in schools [D, `S20` via `A`] | nothing retrieved shows a garden producing it; 167 CHL articles yielded only 28 with behavioural associations, "some" of them *reduced* behaviour [D, `S19` via `A`] |
| **Food literacy** | "kennis en vaardigheden om eten te kunnen plannen, regelen, kiezen en bereiden" [D, `A26`, citing Vidgen & Gallegos 2014 — original not retrieved] | contains **doing**, where health literacy contains **appraising** [I, `A` §4.3] | SPFL, 29 items, 8 domains, validated on 90.7% female / 59.1% highly educated adults [D, `A25`] | a self-perceived scale in a selected sample; and Dutch adult food education through growing is not established (`nl-literature.md` §4) |
| **Health-related learning** | the acquisition of knowledge or skill *about health* through an activity — the issue's own framing | the broadest and weakest term: it can be claimed by anything, and is therefore the term most in need of an outcome | nothing standardised retrieved; observation, artefact and interview are the retrievable methods [D, `F8`,`D6`] | without a specified task it cannot fail, and a claim that cannot fail is not a claim |
| **Self-efficacy** | belief in one's capability for a *specific* task | distinct from capability (capacity) and from competence (a need, in SDT); the construct that relational teaching most reliably moves | measured as an outcome of horticultural therapy (*g* = 0.52, very low certainty, `B12`) and of teach-back (SMD 2.40, very low certainty, `F9`) | it is a belief: it rises without knowledge rising (`F9`), so it cannot stand in for learning |
| **Situated capability** | "knowledge-in-practice": what a person can do in a setting, with its tools and people — legitimate peripheral participation, communities of practice | explains *where* capability lives; predicts that it is context-bound, not portable | participant observation over two years in seven Dutch gardens [D, `F8`]; CoP review [D, `D19`]; critique literature [D, `D20`,`D21`] | it does not specify what is learned, and its health-promotion record shows practice change without outcome change [D, `D19`] |
| **Sustained behaviour change** | the behaviour persists after the intervention or the setting ends | the outcome the issue actually wants, and the one with the least evidence | no retrieved study measures it after a garden ends [U, `B` §4.3]; the nearest is the free-offer cliff [D, `D14`] and closure harms [D, `B23`] | nothing retrieved can support it; it is the honest [U] of the whole note |

**Two overlaps that matter and are usually collapsed** [I]:

- **Self-efficacy ≠ capability.** `F9` is the clean demonstration: teach-back raised self-efficacy (SMD 2.40) and short-term adherence (1.04) and showed **no clear pattern of effect on knowledge**, at very low certainty. A garden that makes people feel able has done something real, and it has not thereby made them able.
- **Interactive health literacy ≠ social contact.** The Dutch gradient (`E8`: 9 in 10 report meeting people, 4 in 10 report daring to ask for help) shows the two *reported* differently by the same volunteers in the same programme. Social contact is the *opportunity*; asking is the *skill*. **What the item cannot show is that the first fails to deliver the second**, because it is a retrospective change question with no baseline: it records how many people affirm a rise, not whether asking rose (§5.3).

### 3.2 What this report claims, and what it does not

Stated explicitly, because v1's implicit claim was too large in one direction and, after correction, could become too large in the other:

- **Claimed [I, as a hypothesis with conditions]:** a shared green practice is a plausible setting for **interactive health literacy and food-literacy capability** — asking, doing, judging a situation with others, planning and preparing food — because those are the constructs that require other people and hands, and because the setting's low demand profile matches what the Dutch demand-side analysis says matters.
- **Not claimed:** that a garden produces **functional** health literacy — no retrieved study shows a reading demand that would exercise it, the documented activities being doing, maintaining and harvesting (`C7`,`C9`,`E3`); **critical** health literacy (nothing retrieved; and claiming it would make the garden a political actor it has not agreed to be), **self-efficacy gains that imply capability**, or **sustained behaviour change** after exit.
- **Explicitly refused:** treating these constructs as a hierarchy in which food literacy is the "consolation prize" for failing to reach health literacy. They are a family with different evidential status — food literacy has a school-age evidence base (`A26`–`A31`), interactive health literacy has a mechanism and no study, critical health literacy has neither, sustained behaviour change has neither and is the one the issue most wants.

### 3.3 The cross-construct step, stated honestly

A claim that "a garden strengthens health literacy" moves from a setting that produces **doing** to a construct defined by **appraising**. That step is not forbidden — appraisal happens in real situations (deciding whether a plant is ready, whether a recipe is safe, whether advice from a neighbour is trustworthy) — but **no retrieved study has tested that step**, and the instruments that would measure the destination were not built for the origin (§7). The step is therefore **Insufficient**, and the candidate bridge is interactive literacy: asking, checking and deciding with others is closer to appraisal-in-a-situation than anything else in the family (§5).

## 4. COM-B and self-determination theory, used critically

**v1 §3.3 admitted the gap**: "No line retrieved the primary sources for either, and this note therefore makes no claim from them" [U]. **The gap is now closed.** Full detail, quotations, access log and read depth: [`working/com-b-and-sdt.md`](working/com-b-and-sdt.md). What follows is the use, not the summary.

### 4.1 COM-B: three co-equal conditions, one of which is the garden

Michie, van Stralen & West's COM-B system defines capability as "the individual's psychological and physical capacity to engage in the activity concerned. It includes having the necessary knowledge and skills"; opportunity as "all the factors that lie outside the individual that make the behaviour possible or prompt it"; and motivation as "all those brain processes that energize and direct behaviour" [D, `F1`]. The sentence that matters most for this issue is the one about status:

> "This system places **no priority** on an individual, group, or environmental perspective — intra-psychic and external factors all have equal status in controlling behaviour." [D, `F1`]

**What this does to v1's conclusion [I].** v1 concluded that a garden is an implausible vehicle for health literacy because no study showed capability change. On COM-B that inference is malformed: it treats one condition (capability) as the whole model and ignores the condition the garden actually supplies (opportunity — a place, tools, a task, other people, permission, a recurring time). The correct COM-B statement is: **a garden is an opportunity intervention with a capability component, and whether the capability component produces anything is an empirical question nobody has asked.** That is **Insufficient**, not **Contradicts**.

**And the framework's own limit, in its authors' words [D, `F1`]:** "Research is needed to establish how far the BCW can lead to more efficient design of effective interventions." COM-B audits and designs; it does not establish effect. A COM-B audit of a garden is a legitimate next step and is not evidence that the garden works.

**The nearest application, and what it shows.** The BCW has been applied to gardening exactly once in the retrieved record — to *promoting* front gardening among UK householders — and its authors state that "to the best of our knowledge, it has had no application within the context of promoting gardening", that the study produced twelve recommendations, and that "the next steps include evaluating the proposed interventions in practice" [D, `F2`]. Two things follow [I]: the direction of the behaviour is the opposite of this issue's (getting people to garden, not what gardening does to them), and the intervention functions that stakeholders preferred were "educational and supportive (social and practical) strategies (e.g., community gardening workshops, front gardening 'starter kits') over persuasive and motivational approaches" — i.e. **the functions a community garden already performs informally**. That is a hypothesis worth holding, not a finding.

**The critique, kept.** Ogden argues that the systematisation the BCW performs "may be neither feasible nor desirable", that the translation from a coded protocol to actual behaviour leaves gaps, and that "we should celebrate rather than remove this variability" [D, `F3`, abstract]. Applied to this note, that is a discipline on **the researcher**: a desk COM-B audit of a garden nobody has observed is precisely the "coding a protocol" move Ogden criticises. Applied to the health partners, it is a caution against requiring a garden to become legible as an intervention before it may be taken seriously.

### 4.2 Self-determination theory: the autonomy risk, not the motivation deficit

Ryan & Deci postulate "three innate psychological needs — **competence, autonomy, and relatedness** — which when satisfied yield enhanced self-motivation and mental health and when thwarted lead to diminished motivation and well-being" [D, `F4`]. Three of their findings bear directly on this issue:

> "…not only tangible rewards but also **threats, deadlines, directives, pressured evaluations, and imposed goals diminish intrinsic motivation**… In contrast, choice, acknowledgment of feelings, and opportunities for self-direction were found to enhance intrinsic motivation…" [D, `F4`]

> "…people will be **intrinsically motivated only for activities that hold intrinsic interest for them**…" [D, `F4`]

**What this does to the issue's framing [I].** v1 treated the risk of a health framing as ethical and reputational — participation made conditional on a health goal. SDT gives it a **mechanism and a prediction**: a garden that carries an imposed health goal, a behavioural target or a measurement regime is a *controlling* context, and controlling contexts reduce intrinsic motivation and persistence. The prediction also bounds itself: SDT's cognitive evaluation theory "does not apply" to activities that hold no intrinsic interest in the first place [D, `F4`], so it says nothing about whether a garden can attract someone who does not enjoy gardening. That is a separate (and open) question.

**The empirical application, and its real status.** The one retrieved application of SDT to community gardening interviewed **30 new gardeners** in Montpellier and Denver and found that "relatedness as a motivational feature carried through the interview data at both sites", that "participants' success or failure to relate to other gardeners was a major influence for how autonomous and competent as a community gardener they expressed feeling", and that competence grew through instruction by others [D, `F5`]. It also found the asking problem: "Others also described feeling shy and hesitant to ask for help" [D, `F5`].

**Critical caveat, recorded prominently:** that study is **nested inside the same two cohorts** as the JArDinS trial and the Litt et al. Denver RCT [D, `F5` §2.1.1–2.1.2]. **It is one witness family, not two.** Where its participants say "we do our shopping differently, and we really eat differently" [D, `F5`] and JArDinS measures no change in household food supply (`B7`), the disagreement is *within* a single research programme — and the honest reading is that self-reported change and measured change diverge, which is why neither is decisive alone.

### 4.3 Where the SDT ↔ construct mapping is informative and where it is analogical

| Mapping | Informative? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| competence ↔ self-efficacy ↔ capability | **Informative, with care** | three different things (a need, a belief, a capacity) that move separately — `F9` shows self-efficacy rising without knowledge |
| autonomy ↔ the intrinsic-value boundary | **Informative** | supplies the mechanism by which a health framing could *harm*, not merely intrude |
| relatedness ↔ interactive health literacy | **Informative and load-bearing** | interactive literacy requires other people; relatedness is the motivational precondition for asking them — this is the mechanism the report now hypothesises (H5) |
| autonomy ↔ "self-directed behaviour change" | **Analogical only** | autonomy is about *why* someone acts; the issue's phrase is closer to internalisation, which SDT treats as a separate continuum and no garden study measures |
| relatedness ↔ "social support" ↔ "social prescribing" | **Analogical only** | imports an institutional referral model the theory says nothing about |

### 4.4 What each framework would require to be tested in Eindhoven

- **COM-B:** a structured audit of one garden's actual offer — what is available, to whom, when, with whom, at what demand — performed **with** the garden, plus a test of whether removing one opportunity barrier changes who comes. Requires no instrument, no ethics approval and no money; requires the garden's consent and a researcher who does not systematise it into oblivion (`F3`).
- **SDT:** a measure of the three needs in a garden population with a comparator, and the design the theory most needs — **an open-entry versus health-framed entry to the same activity**, which would test the controlling-context prediction directly. The scales exist (Basic Psychological Needs scales) but **were not retrieved here** [U]; the design has no retrieved instance [U].

## 5. Experiential and relational learning → interactive and critical health literacy

**This is the mechanism the issue posits and v1 did not investigate.** It is built here from what is retrieved, and it stays a hypothesis with conditions.

### 5.1 What the construct requires

Nutbeam's interactive level is not a nicer version of the functional one; it requires different things [D, `A9`,`S18`,`S34` via `A`]:

- **social skills** — the capacity to extract information in conversation, to ask, to negotiate a course of action with another person;
- **a person to have the conversation with**, and a situation worth conversing about;
- **appraisal in context** — deciding whether the advice is any good, which is the first element of the critical level ("information appraisal, understanding the social determinants of health, and collective action", `A9`).

Critical health literacy adds **collective action** and understanding of structural determinants — which is a governance claim, not a learning outcome, and which no retrieved garden study measures [D/U, `S19`,`S20` via `A`].

**The Dutch definition supplies the bridge.** Health literacy is "een **dynamisch samenspel** tussen individuele capaciteiten en de complexiteit van het zorg- en informatiesysteem" [D, `A4`] — a *samenspel*, a playing-together. If the construct is relational by definition, then a setting whose defining feature is other people doing something together is not an odd candidate for it; it is the kind of setting the definition points at. **[I]** That is an argument from the construct's definition, not evidence of effect.

### 5.2 What the retrieved evidence says about each link in the mechanism

| Link | Retrieved evidence | State |
|---|---|---|
| Doing alongside someone who knows produces capability | "Competence frequently resulted from feeling related to others… When instruction was readily available via engaged leaders and instructional classes and events, new community gardeners described feelings of relatedness and enjoyment" [D, `F5`]; cooking interventions succeed through "provide instruction" and "**prompt practice**", with only 14 of 59 reporting long-term outcomes [D, `D17`] | **Supports** (as self-report and short-term confidence) |
| Situated learning produces *portable* capability | CoPs changed practitioner practice and "**None of the studies described the impact of CoPs on public health outcomes**" [D, `D19`]; the Dutch garden-and-kitchen case rates self-reliance at "+ (few examples…)" [D, `D6`]; Veen finds produce reaches the plate only where growing is a *routine practice*, not where the garden is organised around shopping [D, `F8`] | **Contradicts** (portability) / **Supports** (context-bound capability) |
| Participation produces the *social* precondition for asking | 9 in 10 report meeting new people, 5 in 10 standing ready for others, **4 in 10** daring to ask more for help [D, `E8` — all three retrospective change items, no baseline]; "shy and hesitant to ask for help" [D, `F5`] | **Supports** (social contact) / **Insufficient** (asking) / **Measure-unsuitable** (change over time) |
| Relational teaching moves the person, not just the knowledge | teach-back: self-efficacy SMD **2.40** (0.37–4.44), 5 RCTs, n=416, **very low** certainty; adherence SMD **1.04** (0.45–1.64), 7 RCTs, n=571, **low** certainty; **no clear pattern for knowledge, very low certainty** [D, `F9`] | **Supports** (self-efficacy, adherence) / **Insufficient** (knowledge) |
| Participation, not referral, is the active ingredient institutionally | "**Successful participation, rather than referral alone, appears to be the principal mechanism** underlying these benefits" [D, `D12`] | **Supports** |
| The Dutch field's own account of what matters in a green intervention | residents value green "vooral … als een plek om te ontspannen, fysiek en mentaal tot rust te komen en elkaar te ontmoeten"; success lies in "het aansluiten bij de leefwereld van bewoners, hun wensen en behoeften" [C, `F7` — project self-report, not peer-reviewed] | **Supports** (as a claim by the project) |

### 5.3 The hypothesis, stated so it could fail

**H5 — Relational learning carries interactive health literacy, and the garden supplies the relationship but not the literacy.** (The report's stated position on the balance of evidence is in §1.2; this hypothesis is why the mechanism deserves a test, not a conclusion from it.) A recurring, low-demand, shared task in which a person who knows is present and askable produces **interactive** capability — the willingness and skill to ask, check and decide with someone — and this is the *only* health-literacy-adjacent outcome a garden plausibly produces. It does **not** produce functional literacy (no reading demand), and it does not produce critical literacy (no collective-action frame).

- **What would support it:** participants who, unprompted and after leaving, report asking someone for help with something health-related outside the garden; a **repeated** measure of help-seeking in the same people over time (same question at entry and after a year, with a comparator), showing a rise; a teach-back check showing task transfer to home.
- **What would falsify it — a test that has not yet been run:** (a) a repeated measure of help-seeking, in a setting with a comparator, showing no movement in asking while reported contact rises; (b) a qualitative account of asking **at the point of need** — someone needed help with something health-related, could have asked, and did not — in a setting where asking was structurally possible; (c) participants reporting that they ask *inside* the garden but not outside it, a narrower form of the same test; or (d) a garden where asking is structurally discouraged because the only knowledgeable person is unavailable — the "leader … has a lot of other things to do and not enough time" case reported in `F5`. *`E8` cannot serve as any of these and should not be cited as one*: it is a cross-sectional retrospective change item with no baseline, no comparator and no denominator for "flat", so it can neither confirm nor contradict the hypothesis.
- **What would make it untestable:** measuring it with a population instrument. The short forms do not distinguish skill types ("verschilt niet of nauwelijks naar type vaardigheid", `A6` sheet 15), so the instrument cannot see the distinction the hypothesis is about.
- **The cheap test:** one question asked of people who have stopped coming, and one asked of people who still come — *did you ask anyone for help with anything in the last month, and who?* — plus the garden's own attendance record. Reversible, no form, no battery.

### 5.4 What this section does not establish

- **[U]** Any study measuring interactive or critical health literacy as an outcome of a garden, a kitchen, a walking group or any green practice.
- **[U]** Any peer-education evidence in an adult community-gardening setting. Peer education is retrievable for adolescents and for mental-health literacy in schools (`F` search: 15 hits, the closest a qualitative realist evaluation of a school Peer Education Project) — **not for this setting**, and nothing here rests on it.
- **[U]** Any Dutch study of *ervaringsleren* (experiential learning) leading to health capability in adults. The Dutch search surfaced adjacent material — recognition of informal learning (`Dialogisch Valideren`), practice-theory work on gardening (`F8`) — and nothing that measures the learning.

## 6. The evidence

### 6.1 The number, and what it is a number of

The figure the issue inherits ("approximately 44–53% … depending on the 2026 survey and instrument") is three small errors away from the record, and all three matter for how it may be used [D, `A1`,`A4`,`A6`,`A7`; Lead read the Pharos factsheet and the RIVM report]:

- **The band runs from 44.3% to an upper end that is itself uncertain: 52.8% or 52.3%, stated throughout this note as 52.3–52.8%.** The RIVM report read in full (**2026-0076**) reports **52.8%** on the Q47 [D, `A4`,`E6`]. The Pharos factsheet summarising the *revised* report gives **52.8%** in its headline range and **52.3%** in its own explanatory paragraph, both attributed to the Q47 instrument [D, `A1`,`E7`]. **Which of the two is right is not resolved here** — the discrepancy is observable and is reported as a discrepancy, not explained away; the revised report (2026-0107) that the factsheet cites was not read in full (record and abstract only), so a revision of the report is a plausible but unverified explanation [U]. **No figure outside 52.3–52.8% is used for the upper bound anywhere in this note.**
- **Both surveys did fieldwork in 2025**, not 2026. RIVM fielded 6 June–6 July 2025 (n = 11,441); Nivel's panel in November 2025 (n = 733). 2026 is the publication year.
- **It is not two surveys giving a range.** It is one population survey read at two lengths — Q47 = 52.8% (52.3% in Pharos' explanatory paragraph), Q12 = **46.6% in the same respondents** — plus one small panel on the short form (44.3%). The authors' own explanation, quoted by Pharos: the Q12 items "was gebaseerd op het conceptuele raamwerk en niet op basis van uitkomsten van de Q47", and "De Q12 is dus geen inhoudelijk equivalent van de Q47" [D, `A1`].

The rise from the pre-2024 series is the larger issue. The same national series reported 29% / 36% / 25% / 35% on HLS-EU-Q16 across 2012–2023 [D, `A4` p.29]. De Staat van Volksgezondheid en Zorg attributes the jump: "**Daardoor** is het percentage hoger dan in eerdere jaren, toen het percentage schommelde tussen 29 en 35% maar waar gebruik gemaakt werd van de HLS-Q16" [D, `A7`]. A series whose level changed when the instrument changed cannot carry a claim about Dutch society becoming less capable.

Two further properties bound what the number can be used for. The cut-offs are "**conceptueel vastgesteld** op basis van het oorspronkelijke HLS-EU-Q47 model" [D, `A4` p.18, Lead-verified], with no retrieved validation against behaviour or morbidity; and because the score is a rescaled mean of a four-point item anchored *heel moeilijk* → *heel makkelijk*, a score of 50 is the neutral midpoint between "difficult" and "easy", so "about half" is close to what a population rating itself near the midpoint would produce by construction **[I]**. And the measure is of felt difficulty, in a literate sample [D, `A4` p.31, Lead-verified].

**Consequence for this issue [I].** The number cannot be used as a description of a neighbourhood (it is national; no Eindhoven-specific percentage exists in any retrieved source [U, `C` §7.3, **and the Dutch dashboard that might hold one returns no readable values — `nl-literature.md` §4**]), as a diagnosis of the people a garden would reach, or as a baseline against which a garden could be credited with change. What it can support is a single, safer statement: **a large share of Dutch adults report finding health information hard to use, and the Dutch field's own reading of that is that the information and the system are hard.**

### 6.2 The demand side, which changes what a garden would be for

The Dutch operational definition is not a trait list [D, `A4` p.11, Lead-verified]:

> "Gezondheidsvaardigheden vormen een **dynamisch samenspel** tussen individuele capaciteiten en de complexiteit van het zorg- en informatiesysteem."

Nivel states the same as a mismatch between "persoonlijke vaardigheden en de complexiteit van de omgeving" [D, `A6`]. Pharos turns it into doctrine and into practice: "Gezondheidsvaardigheden worden niet alleen bepaald door wat iemand zelf kan, maar ook door de eisen die de omgeving stelt. Naarmate een situatie ingewikkelder is of meer stress veroorzaakt, kan het voor mensen moeilijker worden om hun vaardigheden optimaal te gebruiken, **ook als zij die vaardigheden in principe wel hebben**" [D, `A1`, Lead-verified]; and it operationalises the answer as ten characteristics of a *gezondheidsvaardige organisatie* [D, `A3`].

The critique literature says the same thing from outside, and it is current: Okuhara, Okada & Furukawa (2026) name "**health literacy reductionism**" — "the tendency to preferentially translate complex health communication and health promotion problems into a lack of individual health literacy among patients or the public" [D, `A14`]. Chinn's older charge is that the concept "stretches the idea of 'literacy' to an indefensible extent" [D, `A9`].

**What this does to the issue's premise [I].** If the deficit is substantially in the demands, then a garden is not a place that supplies a missing skill to deficient people; it is a setting with a *different demand profile*. **v1 stopped there and called this an access claim rather than a literacy claim. This revision goes one step further, and it is the step the demand-side definition licenses:** if health literacy is the *samenspel* between capacity and demand, then a setting that deliberately lowers demand is not outside the construct — it is exercising the other half of it. What a garden does *not* thereby do is raise the person's capacity. The distinction is between **a setting that asks less of people** (documented, `C6`,`C9`,`E2`) and **a capability that travels** (unstudied, §5). Both are health-relevant; only the second is what the issue's wording claims.

### 6.3 The construct that actually contains doing

Food literacy is defined in Dutch practice as "het hebben van kennis en vaardigheden om eten te kunnen plannen, regelen, kiezen en bereiden ten behoeve van een gezonde, veilige en duurzamere voedingsinname" [D, `A26`, citing Vidgen & Gallegos 2014 — the original was not retrieved]. Its Dutch instrument (SPFL, n = 755) reduces 50 items to 29 across eight domains including planning, managing and preparing [D, `A25`], validated on a sample that was **90.7% female and 59.1% highly educated** — a caution for exactly the population at issue **[I]**.

The difference is the one that decides this report's claim [I, from `A` §4.3]: **health literacy contains appraising; food literacy contains doing.** A garden plausibly produces the second. Claiming the first requires an extra step through information appraisal that the construct does not supply, and that no retrieved study has tested in a garden (§3.3).

Where gardening does have a food-literacy evidence base, the population is children in schools: a preregistered Danish quasi-experimental protocol on six school-garden sites (FoodACT, NCT05839080) [D, `A28`]; a Tasmanian kitchen-and-garden realist evaluation [D, `A29`]; a systematic review of school food-and-nutrition-literacy interventions organised by Nutbeam's three levels [D, `A31`]. The Dutch route is the schooltuin, and it is not a structural curriculum requirement [D, `A26`]. **Nothing retrieved transfers this to an adult community garden in Eindhoven** [U].

**What the Dutch evidence adds, and v1 did not have** [new this revision]: the mechanism by which growing reaches the plate is not the *presence* of a garden but the *organisation* of the practice. Veen compared Dutch allotments with alternative-food-network gardens and found that allotment participants "are involved in the practice of gardening… The gardening practice requires structural engagement, turning it into a routine. The produce is a result of that routine and is easily integrated into daily meals", whereas in the shopping-type gardens "every visit to the garden requires a conscious decision" and produce is "less easily integrated in daily life" [D, `F8`]. That is a **dose-and-form finding** in Dutch, matching the JArDinS authors' own reading of their null (`B7` full text), and it is the strongest single support for the conditional conclusion in §1.3A.

### 6.4 What participation yields, and how strongly

The strongest evidence in the whole retrieval is that gardening programmes make people feel better while they are in them, and that the size of the finding depends on how it was measured [D, `B1` — Lead read Table 2]:

| Subgroup | Effect (Hedges' *d*) | Comparisons |
|---|---|---|
| All | 0.42 (95% CI 0.36–0.48); 0.35 after trim-and-fill | 76 |
| **Wellbeing** vs health variables | **0.47** vs 0.31 | 58 / 18 |
| **Horticultural therapy** vs non-therapy | **0.61** vs 0.31 | 33 / 43 |
| **Before/after** vs gardener/non-gardener | **0.60** vs 0.32 | 32 / 44 |
| **Patients** vs non-patients | **0.61** vs 0.32 | 28 / 48 |

The effect is not nothing; it is *design-dependent*. Every subgroup with less protection against regression to the mean, expectancy and enthusiasm carries the larger number, and the authors' own Egger test found publication bias, with trim-and-fill reducing the pooled estimate from 0.42 to 0.35 [D, `B1`].

The reviews that look hardest are blunt. Fifty-three studies: "Effects appeared positive for fruit and vegetable intake, some psychosocial and community outcomes, but mixed for physical health outcomes. **Evidence quality overall was low**" and "poor evidence quality suggests the effects of community gardening may be **overestimated**" [D, `B4`]. Fifteen studies: 13 of 15 carry selection bias, 3 of 15 used objective measurement [D, `B5`]. An all-RCT review of horticultural therapy found **four eligible RCTs** and "insufficient evidence" [D, `B11`]; a 27-study meta-analysis in older adults reports *g* ≈ 0.52 for self-efficacy and self-esteem but rates certainty "**very low**" [D, `B12`]. And the strongest dietary trial — 23 schools, 641 children, 18 months, 24-hour food diaries — found an adjusted effect of **−40 g/day** (95% CI −88 to 1; p = 0.06) and concluded there was "very little evidence" for the claim [D, `B16`].

The Dutch-specific result is the most useful single calibration: among 121 allotment gardeners and 63 neighbours, those **aged 62 and over** scored "significantly or marginally better on all measures of health and well-being", while younger gardeners "did not differ from younger neighbors" [D, `B9` — Lead-verified]. The authors add that "the findings may be limited by self selection" [D]. **In the one Dutch study of its kind, the effect is an age effect in a self-selected group, not a gardening effect with a general population.**

### 6.5 The transfer question, which is the pivot — and JArDinS reassessed

**v1 treated JArDinS as the decisive test and called the transfer question closed. This revision re-read it at full text and narrows that reading: JArDinS is an important null, but it is a null about *joining*, and it measured none of the constructs the issue is about.** What the full text adds [D, `B7` full text, PMC7690132]:

| Question the PI asked of the evidence | What JArDinS actually did |
|---|---|
| **Population** | 66 gardeners vs 66 pairwise-matched non-gardeners, Montpellier, 2018–19. **75.8% female, 75.8% university degree, mean age 44.0.** Inclusion required being "able to read French"; exclusion criteria included **having a chronic disease** and past community-gardening experience in the household. Gardeners had **lower** education and BMI and ate fewer meals out at baseline (p = 0.049; p = 0.046; p = 0.033). The authors concede the sample is "more concerned about their health than the general population" and that "the majority of participants held a university degree and displayed healthier dietary patterns at baseline than the French general population (398 vs 264 g/d per person)" |
| **Design** | Quasi-experimental, **not randomised** — "Random sampling was not achievable in our setting because new membership and plot renewal in community gardens are under the control of local authorities or private managers"; a convenience sample of new gardeners, compared with volunteers from a population food survey; matched on age, sex, income band and household composition |
| **Power** | A target of **160 participants (80/80)** was computed to detect **one portion of fruit and vegetables per day per person** at 80% power, α = 0.05, with a 30% attrition allowance. 296 approached; 155 at baseline; 14 lost to follow-up; **66 pairs analysed**. The paper's "in spite of sufficient statistical power" applies to **the primary outcome only**; the secondary outcomes — physical activity, BMI, WEMWBS, loneliness, nature relatedness, food-waste sensitivity, environmental impact, expenditure — have **no stated power calculation**. The authors' own limitation section concedes: "it can also be argued that insufficient statistical power and inability of the data collection tools to detect change could explain the lack of results" |
| **Measured** | Household **fruit and vegetable supply** (g/day/person, from a one-month food diary plus receipts — the primary outcome), MAR, MER, Healthy Purchase Index, **accelerometer** physical activity (PAEE, inactivity, light, MVPA), self-reported BMI, **WEMWBS**, **UCLA Loneliness**, food-waste sensitivity, **Nature Relatedness**, GHGE/acidification/eutrophication, food expenditure |
| **Result** | Fruit and vegetables 402.4 → 400.0 g/d (gardeners) vs 433.6 → 445.6 (controls), group × time **p = 0.999**; WEMWBS p = 0.546; loneliness p = 0.227; nature relatedness p = 0.395; PA and BMI showed **time** effects in both groups (activity fell, BMI rose) and no group × time effect |
| **NOT measured** | **health literacy, information appraisal, asking for help, cooking capability or food preparation skill, self-efficacy, knowledge, transfer after exit, or the after-the-end period.** The only competence item is a single self-rated gardening level ("beginner/intermediate/advanced"), used as a baseline descriptor |
| **Dose actually achieved** | **"At t1, half of gardeners (n = 32) had not retrieved any fruit or vegetables from the garden"**; among the others the mean harvest was **38.9 g/day/person** (SD 44.1). The authors: "The observed **low gardener involvement** might explain the lack of effect"; a post-hoc analysis restricted to active gardeners found no effect either, "but these findings are to be taken with caution because the reduction of the sample also reduces statistical power" |
| **What the authors themselves conclude** | That "the health benefits of community gardening previously reported by cross-sectional studies **might be confounded by selection bias**"; that "strong evidence of the health benefits of gardening mainly comes from gardening programs in school or medical settings where the activity is supervised and carried out regularly"; that in those settings gardening "is often accompanied by other actions such as … nutrition education courses, cooking classes…"; and that "**multicomponent programs are more likely to modify health outcomes**" |

**What the null licenses, and what it does not** [I, from the above]:

- **Licensed:** no effect of *entering* a community garden for twelve months on household food supply, accelerometer-measured activity, BMI, wellbeing, loneliness, nature relatedness, food-waste sensitivity, environmental impact or food expenditure, in a health-interested, highly educated French cohort, at the dose achieved; and a warning that cross-sectional garden-health associations may be selection effects.
- **Not licensed:** that gardening has no effect on health literacy, asking, appraisal, cooking capability or transfer — **none was measured**; that a garden with a supervisor, a weekly rhythm and a shared task would produce the same null — the **authors themselves** point to supervised, multicomponent programmes as the more likely vehicle; that the null generalises to a less-educated population — the authors say the opposite ("one can assume that the recruitment of less educated, low-income gardeners would have led to different conclusions"); or that a longer follow-up would have found nothing — they concede "a follow-up longer than 1 year might be needed".
- **A correction exists** (BMC Public Health 21:15, 4 January 2021) and its content was **not isolated** in the retrieved text; no claim here rests on a number it might have touched [U, `F13`].

**No retrieved study measures the after-the-end period.** Across the reviews read, follow-up is at the end of the intervention, with the garden still running [U, `B` §4.3]. The nearest adjacent evidence is that closures hurt most those with least: COVID closures had "disproportionate impacts … on vulnerable groups" [D, `B23`]. That is evidence of **dependence on the site** — the precise failure mode this repository documented when Voedselbos Eindhoven closed ([#16](../continuity/note.md)).

**The literature that would show transferable capability does not exist in retrievable form.** Situated learning's community-of-practice form has been reviewed in public health: twelve studies of ten communities of practice, changing practitioner practice where they offer "structured problem-solving, reflective practice and networking", and "**None of the studies described the impact of CoPs on public health outcomes**"; "the true impact of CoPs on population health remains unknown" [D, `D19` — Lead re-read]. In the one Dutch garden-and-kitchen case with participants in the record, the qualitative evaluation rates self-reliance at "+ (few examples from participant and supervisor)" [D, `D6`]. And the Dutch thesis on the subject finds that where the garden is organised around growing as a routine, produce does reach the plate, and where it is organised around shopping, it does not [D, `F8`] — which is a **condition on transfer**, not evidence of it.

**And the health-literacy half of the claim was never attempted.** Gardening as a *health-literacy* intervention does not appear in the literature at all: three abstract-level hits for health literacy + garden, none an intervention; a full-text sweep added nothing at title level [D/U, `A` §5.1–5.3]; the Dutch search added nothing [U, `nl-literature.md` Q5]. **The absence is not refutation — it is the absence of anyone having tried or published, and this note now says so in those words.**

### 6.6 The regional layer: what exists, who owns it, and what it is for

**DE STAP naar gezonder** is, in its own words, "de naam van een **beweging**" with 193 organisational partners across Eindhoven and De Kempen [D, `E1`, Lead-verified]. It is also the city's own policy vehicle: "samen zetten we De STAP naar gezonder" (Gemeente Eindhoven, 2024) [D, `C24`]. What it is *not* is an organisation with a public existence: no legal entity, board, annual report, accounts or whole-movement evaluation was found [U, `C` §4]; the partner list contains "no food forest, community garden or citizen group" [D, absence, `C3`]; and its operational owner **Coöperatie Slimmer Leven was dissolved on 1 January 2026**, leaving **GGD Brabant-Zuidoost as penvoerder for one transition year** on **€1.2 million** of subsidies that run to the end of 2026, with "structurele borging … na 2026" requiring a decision that has not been taken [D, `C26`, Lead-verified]. The municipal health budget for 2026 sits inside a €4,229,000 package that is 85% SPUK-GALA, and the document says the cover for 2027 "valt veel lager uit" [D, `C25`].

**Fontys** is the complementary expertise the issue asks about, and it is current — with one important dating correction, and one 2026 re-verification. The CoE Health page (live 2026, **re-retrieved this revision**) confirms the three themes verbatim: "**gezondheidsvaardigheden, gezonde fysieke en sociale leefomgeving en samenzorg voor de toekomst**", with health literacy defined around "informatievaardigheden en digitale vaardigheden" and "**inclusief onderzoek en co-creatie met gebruikers met lage gezondheidsvaardigheden**", and with a green-environment research line including "**Groene stedelijke leefomgeving** — Onderzoek naar de vraag: wat is het effect van een groene stedelijke omgeving op de gezondheid?" [D, `E5`, re-verified 2026-11-26T21:13:51Z]. Its board is a public-private partnership of "**Slimmer Leven, Pharos, GGD-BZO, Transvorm, Fontys Mens en Gezondheid en Fontys Paramedisch**" [D, `E5`] — which places the Dutch health-literacy institute (Pharos) and the regional GGD one governance step from the university that owns DE STAP's learning workstream. Note two dating problems: **Slimmer Leven is still listed as a board member months after its dissolution** [D, `E5`; `C` §6.2], and the 2023 article's claim that Fontys is "eigenaar van de bouwsteen 'lerend vermogen'" is **not repeated in any 2025–2026 source** [U, `C34`,`C35`].

Fontys' involvement continues in practice: the 2025 annual report and a 2 December 2025 BRON article name three professional learning workplaces — **Achtse Barrier, Eckart/Vaartbroek (Woensel) and Son en Breugel** — working "onder de vlag van" DE STAP on health, vitality and equal chances, with Toegepaste Psychologie among the student routes [D, `C34`,`C35`,`C33`]. **Tongelre, named in 2023, has since dropped out** [D, absence]. The funding is one-year at a time, described as a "pioniersfase" [D, `C34`].

**The municipality and the GGD** supply need and vocabulary but not an instrument for growing. Loneliness among adults rose 13% → 19%, stress 19% → 23%, and "voelt zich niet zo gelukkig of ongelukkig" 4% → 7% between 2020 and 2024, with healthy life expectancy flat at 70.6–71.9 years and inequality named by neighbourhood [D, `C25`]. The 2030 goals are "drie jaar langer in goede gezondheid" and a 20% reduction in health inequalities [D, `C25`]. Green is a municipal health instrument **as space**: the "Gezonde Buurt" approach, beweegtuinen, calisthenics parks, and an adopted "**Groenarm**" indicator [D, `C25`,`C24`]. **No municipal document retrieved names community gardens, buurtmoestuinen, voedselbossen or urban food-growing as a prevention instrument** [D, absence, `C25`,`C24`]. The "Preventieakkoord" that once convened "ruim 50 partners" no longer exists as a programme; it was absorbed in 2024 [D, `C25`].

**Two consequences [I].** First, a food forest entering municipal health policy would be entering through a door that is not a prevention instrument: green space yes, cultivation no. Second, the regional movement it might join is *deciding what it will be after 2026* during 2026 — which is a reason to be a listing rather than a partner, and a reason not to build anything that depends on the movement's continuity.

### 6.8 Frameworks, used critically

Three framework-level findings, each retrieved:

- **Positieve Gezondheid**, the vocabulary behind the region's six site sections (which are *not* the instrument's six dimensions [D, `C` §1.3]), is a conversation and agenda-setting tool, not an outcome measure. iPH itself points to a meetset "under development" [D, `C37`]; a TSG methodological paper states "Dit instrument is niet ontwikkeld, en daardoor niet bruikbaar, als meetinstrument" [D, `C40`]; a psychiatric journal review states "de vragenlijst is niet gevalideerd en het wetenschappelijke bewijs dat positieve gezondheid 'werkt', ontbreekt tot nu toe" [D, `C41`]; and the concept carries a documented individualisation critique — "Wie ondanks alle positiviteit toch nog klachten houdt of er niet in slaagt zijn gedrag vol te houden, kan daar zelf voor verantwoordelijk worden gehouden" [D, `C42`].
- **Situated learning** is the right theory for what a garden does — and its health-promotion record is a change in *practice* with no demonstrated change in *outcomes* [D, `D19`], with the movement from periphery to core organised by the institution rather than the practice [D, `D20`] and power relations "displaced by a managerial preoccupation" [D, `D21`]. Read for a garden, the radical part of the theory is *who is allowed to move from the edge to the centre* — a governance question, not a curriculum. The Dutch practice-theory account adds the useful correction: what people are organised around determines what they do with the produce [D, `F8`].
- **Social prescribing** is the closest institutional analogue, and its best evidence is pre/post. The England-wide programme reached 8,339 people and improved all four ONS-4 items, with an SROI of £2.42 per £1 [D, `D9`]; its own evaluators record that "it is unknown how representative the data is" and that "**evidence for GSP is not sufficiently compelling or rigorous**" [D, `D9`]. The systematic-review layer is weaker still: 15 evaluations of link-worker schemes, "**All were rated as a having a high risk of bias**" [D, `D11`]. There is **no NICE guideline recommending nature-based social prescribing** [U, `D` §2].

### 6.9 Exclusion, and the qualification trap

The issue asks this to be addressed directly, and the retrieved evidence supports a structural rather than attitudinal account.

- **The overlap is large and not mainly linguistic.** Stichting Lezen en Schrijven: 18% of Dutch adults (2.5 million) struggle with reading, writing and/or arithmetic; 25% of adults have limited health literacy; "**Alle laaggeletterden hebben beperkte gezondheidsvaardigheden**"; and "**Ongeveer 55% van de laaggeletterden heeft geen migratie-achtergrond**" [D, `D23`]. An *anderstaligen*-only framing would miss most of the group. (The factsheet's primary citations sit on a page not retrieved; treat 18/25% as second-hand until re-sourced **[I]**.)
- **Access to nature is stratified before anyone is invited.** In Natural England's MENE data (2009–2012, so dated), frequent nature visitors are 38% of ethnic-minority adults against 57%, 43% of the urban deprived, and 42% of people with a disability or long-term illness; "the demographic factors that account for most of the variation … are, in descending order: **whether or not someone has a disability**; whether people are members of the BAME population; socio-economic status; age" [D, `D24`]. **Disability is the strongest single factor** — and that is a design requirement (raised beds, paths, seating, a toilet), not an invitation. The Dutch material agrees from the practice side: the RIVM review records that "niet iedereen vindt moestuinieren leuk en sommigen moeten misschien stoppen als ze er niet fit genoeg (meer) voor zijn", and that gardening can produce low-back complaints "te voorkomen door bijvoorbeeld perken in hoge bakken aan te leggen" [D, `F6`].
- **Qualifying for the offer excludes.** Welzijn op Recept requires a GP referral for psychosocial complaints, and Dutch action research found it stalling even at experienced sites, with two of four drifting off the model's own definition and multi-problem clients better served by a broader offer [D, `D7`]. In UK qualitative work people decline because "they are afraid of being stigmatised" and because "Patients expect to be referred to an investigation, or a drug, not to SP!" [D, `D14`].
- **A free offer with an end date creates a cliff.** When free sessions stopped: "What is the point then if it's not free anymore?" [D, `D14`].
- **Burdens land on the person who hosts.** Volunteer coordinators carry the greatest time and wellbeing cost [D, `B23`]; a Dutch walking programme states "het aantal begeleiders heeft moeite dat bij te houden" [D, `D2`]; link workers' "capacity is stretched" [D, `D9`]; and the national directory of 1,300+ green-health initiatives, *Beter in het Groen*, went offline in 2020 [D, `D5`].
- **A garden can exclude from the inside.** The two Dutch sources that look hardest at this both find it: gardens "dragen niet per definitie bij aan integratie", groups may work *next to* rather than *with* each other, and a cohesive group can make other residents feel excluded [D, `F6`]; and "community gardens do not necessarily foster a more inclusive society; they often attract people with relatively similar socio-economic backgrounds and may support not one, but several communities", with one garden harbouring two communities that "assign others to categories ('us' and 'them') on the basis of place of residence" [D, `F8`]. **This is a counter-example to the inclusion assumption that comes from Dutch gardens themselves, and v1 did not have it.**

**Assembled from the retrieved evidence rather than quoted from one source, a genuinely low-threshold offer looks like this [I]:** free at the point of use; weekly and drop-in, so a missed week is not an exit; self-referral accepted; a person who makes first contact; no measurement burden; no end date; and physical access designed for people who cannot bend, stand long or travel far. **A garden can meet most of those conditions more cheaply than a programme can — and the two that it meets worst are the last two.**

## 7. Instruments versus assessment: two claims v1 merged

**v1 said both of these, and treated them as one:** (i) population instruments are unsuitable as a garden's outcome measure; (ii) therefore no appropriate assessment exists. **(ii) does not follow from (i).** Full working: [`working/assessment-methods.md`](working/assessment-methods.md).

**Claim (i) — established, and it is about measurement, not about gardens.** HLS19-Q47/Q12, HLS-EU-Q16 and (on the same self-report logic) HLQ and AAHLS measure **felt difficulty** on cut-offs that are "conceptueel vastgesteld", in self-completion samples that exclude non-readers; the short forms are "geen inhoudelijk equivalent" of the long form; Q12 subscales have 3–4 items and the skill-type distinction is not reproduced at that length [D, `A4`,`A1`,`A6`; `S23`,`S24` via `A`]. Using them as a garden's outcome would measure the instrument and the respondent's position. **State: Measure-unsuitable.**

**Claim (ii) — contradicted.** Assessment of the kind the issue needs does exist, at a range of costs [D]:

| Method | What it establishes | Burden | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Teach-back** | whether someone can reproduce a task or explanation | seconds, no literacy demand, inside the activity | `F9`: 18 RCTs — **no clear pattern of effect on knowledge (very low certainty)**; self-efficacy SMD **2.40** (0.37–4.44), very low certainty; short-term adherence SMD **1.04** (0.45–1.64), low certainty |
| **Observation and qualitative work** | what happens, who asks, who stops, what it means | high in time; low in literacy demand | two years of participant observation in seven Dutch gardens (`F8`); 30 interviews in two countries (`F5`); a Dutch MSc thesis with 16 interviews (`D6`) |
| **Participatory / realist evaluation** | for whom, under what conditions; whether the partnership changed | high: co-governance and trust | `D22` (CBPR), `F7` (Dutch realist review + participatory action research) |
| **Performance-based, context-specific instruments** | whether a person can perform specified tasks | high: 320 recruited across six clinics plus spirometry | `F10` — exists, is research-grade, and its scores showed **no significant association with disease severity or quality of life** |
| **Self-efficacy scales** | confidence in a specific task | low–moderate | `B12` (*g* = 0.52, certainty very low); `F9` |
| **Food-literacy scale (SPFL)** | self-perceived planning, managing, selecting, preparing | moderate, reading-based | `A25` — validated on a 90.7%-female, 59%-highly-educated sample |
| **Short forms as process measures** | direction of travel, with stated limits | low | `A4`,`A6`; `S23`,`S24` via `A` |
| **Think-aloud / cognitive interviewing** | whether a text is understood as intended | low–moderate | **[U] no source retrieved — not described here** |

**What a community could reasonably do without becoming a research site** [I, and the practical payload of this section]:

1. **One question, asked by a person:** "show me how you'd do this at home." Cheapest defensible check; produces a conversation, not a dataset.
2. **Count what the garden already produces:** who came, who came back, who stopped, how much was harvested, who asked for help. JArDinS's most important finding — half harvested nothing — came from data a garden itself could hold.
3. **Refuse the battery.** No pre/post questionnaire as a price of entry: unsuitable instruments (§7), burden on the least able (`F2` Equity; `D9`), and imposed measurement is a controlling context in SDT terms (`F4`).
4. **If a study is wanted, let it be co-created and someone else's burden** — the Dutch precedent exists (`F7`), the CBPR evidence on what it takes exists (`D22`), and its cost is documented (`D9`).
5. **Say what the check cannot show.** "People came, people came back, people could show me the task" is available. "Health literacy improved" is not. **The distance between those two sentences is the whole of this section.**

### 8.2 Is the expertise complementary? What is established

**Yes in subject matter, and not yet in practice** [I, from the above]:

- **Complementary in subject:** Fontys CoE Health is the only actor in the retrieved record that names **health literacy** as a research theme *and* has a board including **Pharos** (the Dutch health-literacy institute) and **GGD BZO** [D, `E5`]. DE STAP is the only actor with a **city-wide directory and partner network** in which gardens already appear [D, `E2`,`C3`]. The GGD is the only actor with **population data and prevention infrastructure** [D, `C25`,`C29`]. Those are three different things and none of them substitutes for another.
- **Complementary in environment:** Fontys has **places where students and residents work together in neighbourhoods** [D, `C33`,`C34`,`C35`]; a garden is a place where residents work together without students. Whether a learning workplace could sit in a garden is **[U]** — no retrieved document names a garden as a Fontys learning environment, and the workplaces named are neighbourhoods, not gardens.
- **Not complementary in evidence:** neither side has participant-level outcome data for a green practice [U, `C` §4; `D` §10]. So the complementarity is *of capability*, not *of findings*: one side has measurement and co-creation method, the other has reach and a directory, and neither has a result.
- **The nearest thing to a joint learning object is the *green environment* theme, and it is about exposure, not cultivation:** "wat is het effect van een groene stedelijke omgeving op de gezondheid?" [D, `E5`]. A food forest is a green urban environment *and* a cultivation practice; only the first half is inside anyone's stated research question [I].

### 8.3 What each could plausibly learn from the other, and what is not established

**Kept as possibilities, explicitly not as proposals** [I]:

- **Fontys could learn** what the Dutch field says it lacks: what happens when health-literacy expertise meets a setting with **no reading demand at all**. The CoE's own framing is "informatievaardigheden en digitale vaardigheden" [D, `E5`]; a garden exercises neither. That is a useful negative case for the theme — and it is also a caution, because importing the theme's vocabulary into a garden would re-create the demand the Dutch analysis says is the problem (`A1`,`A2`).
- **DE STAP could learn** what its own directory already contains but does not describe: a garden with a four-person crew and a pizza oven is filed under *Ontmoeten* with no times, no cost and no contact [D, `C9`,`E3`]. The gap between what the listing says and what the practice is (`C` §2.2) is a documentation problem the movement could fix without a partnership.
- **The GGD could learn** whether the people its prevention programmes fail to reach are reachable through an offer that requires nothing — the question its own Welzijn op Recept pilot raises (`C25`; `D7`).
- **What is not established:** that any of these organisations wants this, has capacity for it, or would change anything; that the 2023 "lerend vermogen" role still exists; that a garden would welcome a student presence (the volunteer-coordinator burden evidence is directly relevant here, `B23`); and that the F-fontys relationship is anything other than the movement's partner list plus a project-funded learning workplace [U].

**Where v1 established facts, this revision verified rather than re-litigated:** P16–P19 stand as v1 found them, and the CoE page was re-retrieved live to confirm P19 and P33 [D, `E5` re-retrieved 2026-11-26T21:13:51Z, sha256 `8885d9ebd2737d7a…`].

## 9. Counter-evidence and tensions

1. **The strongest counter-evidence is still that the garden may not be a health setting at all — but it is weaker than v1 said.** JArDinS is null on its measured outcomes [D, `B7`]; the strongest dietary RCT is null [D, `B16`]; the pooled gardening effect halves when designs improve [D, `B1`]; and where the Dutch evidence is strongest, the effect belongs to being over 62 rather than to gardening [D, `B9`]. **What v1 did not record: JArDinS's own dose finding and its authors' proposal of supervised, multicomponent programmes as the remedy.** The counter-evidence is against *unsupervised, low-dose, single-component* gardening, which is a condition, not a verdict.
2. **The construct the issue wants may be the wrong *entry* construct, and the field's own move supports the correction** — but the correction is to *interactive*, not to *nothing*. Health literacy now means skill × demand [D, `A4`], the Dutch institute says the problem is not the person's [D, `A1`,`A2`], and the critique calls individualising it "reductionism" [D, `A14`]. Building a garden argument on a deficit reading would use the construct in the sense the field has spent fifteen years abandoning; building it on the demand-side half is the move the definition actually licenses (§6.2).
3. **The regional partner is not the one the issue names.** Wasven — a staffed, care-contracted, ANBI-structured organisation — is a DE STAP partner [D, `C3`]; no food forest, community garden or citizen group is. The existing bridge runs through **institutionalised** green care, not through volunteer growing.
4. **The movement is weakest exactly where the issue needs it strongest.** Its operator dissolved, its coordination is a one-year transition, its money ends in 2026, it has no public accounts or evaluation, and it abandoned its own attempt to measure positive health [D, `C25`,`C26`; U, `C` §4].
5. **The municipal instrument is space, not cultivation.** Green appears as play, beweeg and a "groenarm" indicator — not as growing [D, `C24`,`C25`].
6. **The issue's own framing carries a motivational risk, and SDT names it.** Asking a garden to build health literacy makes participation conditional on a health goal, in a setting whose documented value is that it asks nothing — and SDT predicts that imposed goals and pressured evaluation diminish intrinsic motivation and persistence [D, `F4`]. The Dutch evidence on stigma, on the free-offer cliff and on coordinator burden is the empirical half of the same warning [D, `D14`,`B23`].
7. **Evidence quality is the ceiling, and it is low by the reviewers' own verdicts.** "Evidence quality overall was low" [D, `B4`]; "certainty … very low" [D, `B12`]; "Generally the quantitative research was poor" [D, `B15`]; "All were rated as a having a high risk of bias" [D, `D11`]; teach-back's own meta-analysis reports "very low" certainty for self-efficacy and "low" for adherence [D, `F9`].
8. **A tension inside the Dutch numbers that could not be resolved.** Both 2025 measurements find **men** reporting more health-literacy problems than women (54.9% vs 50.9% in RIVM; the same direction at Nivel), against a policy framing that treats the issue as concentrated among women, older people and the lower-educated [D, `A4`,`A6`; unexplained, U].
9. **The two survey figures disagree with each other in the record.** RIVM cites Nivel's panel figure as 45.2%; Nivel's own report, Pharos and Staat VenZ all give **44.3%** [D, `A4`,`A6`,`A1`,`A7`; U which is right].
10. **A contradiction preserved rather than repaired.** The issue brief states 189 partners; the page, its counter and its name list say **193**, while municipal and Fontys documents give 100 (early 2023), ">95" (June 2023), 127 (later 2023), 158 (2024–25) and "zo'n 150" (2025). The direction is consistent; **no figure is audited** [D, `E1`,`C3`,`C24`,`C25`,`C30`,`C35`].
11. **New tension: self-report and measurement diverge inside one research programme.** The JArDinS cohort produced a quantitative null on household food supply and qualitative accounts of changed shopping and eating ("we do our shopping differently, and we really eat differently") [D, `B7`,`F5`]. **Neither can be used to cancel the other**, and the divergence is the most instructive single fact about what a garden study can and cannot see. It is also a warning against treating any single qualitative account — including a favourable one — as evidence of effect.
12. **New tension: the Dutch literature on gardens is more sceptical about inclusion than the English-language programme literature.** The RIVM review and the Dutch thesis both document gardens as socially selective and sometimes internally divided [D, `F6`,`F8`], against a programme-evaluation literature (GSP) that reports reaching 57% from the most deprived areas and 21% from ethnic minorities [D, `D9`]. The difference is plausibly the organisational form — referred and staffed vs volunteer and open — and it is a second reason to treat "organisational form" as the load-bearing variable [I].

## 10. Hypotheses and counterexamples

Five hypotheses, each written so that it could come back negative. These are **not** proposals and require no one's consent to be *false*; the first two can be tested on public evidence alone.

**H1 — Task capability, not portable capability.** A recurring, free, bounded session with one task and one person who knows produces **task capability** ("I can do this here, with someone around") but not portable capability, and the difference shows up as soon as the person is asked to do it alone. *If it worked:* people who have attended three times do the task unaided in a different setting. *If it failed:* people are confident and helpful inside the session and never do it at home — which is the VTTB finding [D, `D6`], the situated-learning verdict [D, `D19`], and Veen's contrast between growing-as-routine and shopping-as-practice [D, `F8`]. *Falsifier:* a garden whose participants report doing the task at home without prompting, verifiable without asking them to fill anything in.

**H2 — The demand profile is the health contribution.** The health-relevant property of a shared green practice is that it is a **lower-demand setting** than the health system: no referral, no form, no reading, no measurement, no appointment. *If it worked:* the people who come include people who have declined or dropped out of referred health offers. *If it failed:* the participants are the same already-engaged, already-educated group as the volunteer base in the Dutch evaluation, which was "ongeveer de helft … een universitaire opleiding of een opleiding in het hoger beroepsonderwijs" against "ruim een derde" of the neighbourhood [D, `E8`] — or, as in JArDinS, three-quarters university-educated [D, `B7`]. *This is the one hypothesis that can be checked retrospectively from a garden's own participant description, without a new study.*

**H3 — The one learnable health-literacy skill is asking.** If anything health-literacy-adjacent is produced, it is **interactive** literacy: asking a question, getting help, navigating a person rather than a system. The one Dutch item that asks about it is a retrospective change question with no baseline: **4 in 10** volunteers affirm that they now dare ask more people for help, against **9 in 10** who affirm new contacts [D, `E8`] — consistent with the asymmetry, and not a measurement of it. *If it worked:* an increase in "I asked someone for help", **measured repeatedly in the same people**, would be the only defensible indicator — and since it is a **giving/asking asymmetry**, the garden would have to be producing askers, not only givers. *If it failed:* the garden reproduces the asymmetry the retrospective item is consistent with. *Note the direction: this hypothesis predicts the garden is worse at the health-literacy skill than at the social one.*

**H4 — The listing, not the partnership.** An activity listing (free, anyone, four criteria, one of which forbids a referral requirement) reaches more of the intended population than partner status (organisation-only, governance-facing, obligations unpublished) — and it costs nothing. *If it worked:* a listed activity with no organisational form behind it gets enquiries from people who are not already in the network. *If it failed:* listing generates no contact, and the "movement" is a directory with no traffic. *Falsifier:* enquiry data, which the movement would have to hold; **not obtainable** [U].

**H5 — Relational learning carries interactive capability, and the garden supplies the relationship but not the literacy** (new this revision; full statement at §5.3). *If it worked:* people who have left the garden report asking someone for help with something health-related, outside it. *If it failed:* a repeated measure shows no movement in asking while reported contact rises; or asking happens only inside the garden, with the one knowledgeable person, and not outside. (`E8` cannot serve as this test — §5.3.) *What would make it untestable:* measuring it with a short-form population instrument, which cannot see the distinction (`A6` sheet 15).

**Six counterexamples to the premise, from the retrieved record:**

1. **The Vergeten Tuin** has run for twenty-five years with ~180 members and ~25 core volunteers, a green zoning since 2007 and a management agreement, and it makes **no health claim at all** [D, [`../practices/note.md`](https://github.com/leonvanbokhorst/voedselbos/blob/research/20-local-practices/research/practices/note.md) §3.1]. If long-lived gardens produced health literacy, the practice most likely to have noticed has not.
2. **'t Kruidenkafé** is named for herbs and contains no herbs: a GGzE day-activity venue whose participants are mental-healthcare clients [D, `C10`,`C12`]. It is the clearest local case of the name doing the argument's work, and it is genuinely valuable as a *low-demand social venue* — which is the different and better claim.
3. **The eight cooking listings** already exist, cost €0–7, and are classified as meeting, not learning [D, `E2`,`C7`]. If cooking together produced food literacy that anyone could see, the region's own health platform would have a reason to say so.
4. **Fontys' professional learning workplaces** in Achtse Barrier and Eckart/Vaartbroek are the region's documented instance of an institution entering a neighbourhood to learn — and the learning runs **from** the institution **to** the neighbourhood (student routes, research, "versterken van gezondheid … en gezondheidsvaardigheden") [D, `C33`,`C34`]. Reciprocity is documented in participatory *research* [D, `D22`], not in this programme class [U, `D` §6].
5. **The England-wide green social prescribing programme** — the best-resourced attempt anywhere, 8,339 people, £5.77m — is pre/post only, and its own evaluators say the evidence is "not sufficiently compelling or rigorous" [D, `D9`]. A volunteer food forest in Eindhoven cannot outperform the design of a national programme with professional link workers.
6. **New: the Dutch gardens that have been studied most closely are socially selective and sometimes internally divided** — "they often attract people with relatively similar socio-economic backgrounds", one garden harbouring two communities that classify each other by residence [D, `F8`], and groups that "niet mét elkaar, maar naast elkaar" work [D, `F6`]. A garden is not automatically a commons.

## 11. Open questions for a later, separately agreed conversation

Not questions for a stakeholder to answer on the researcher's behalf; questions whose answers would change the note. **No one has been asked any of them.**

1. **What does "health" mean in the practice's own words?** The Voedselbos archive already contains a health claim — "met extra aandacht voor een duurzame leefstijl om burn-out of welvaartsziektes te voorkomen zoals depressie" — and a learning claim, "een open klaslokaal, waar we leren van de natuur, van elkaar en van de diverse planten" [C, `../meaning/note.md` §3]. Which of those was the practice, and which was the offer's framing?
2. **Who came, and who stopped coming?** This is the question no retrieved source answers for any Eindhoven green practice [U], and the one that decides H1, H2 and H5.
3. **Would you describe what happens as teaching?** If yes, who teaches, what do they teach, and what happens when they are not there — the single-person dependency this repository has already documented twice.
4. **Would being listed as an activity be welcome, and would being a partner be unwelcome?** The route is documented and free [D, `C6`]; the appetite is **[U]** and must not be assumed.
5. **What would you refuse to do?** A garden that turns down a health framing is making a defensible choice, and the note should be able to record a "no".
6. **For the health partners: what would make you change something because of what a community practice told you?** The reciprocal-learning question, asked of the side that has the power to act on the answer [D for the absence, `D` §6].
7. **New: which of the two Fontys themes would a garden actually meet?** The health-literacy theme and the green-environment theme are both public and neither has a garden in it [D, `E5`]. Asking which one a garden is relevant to is a cheaper question than asking whether to collaborate.

## 12. What this means for the main question and the three pillars

**For the main question** ("human and ecological health through shared care"): the health half of that phrase should be carried by **access, relationship and the practice of asking**, with **food capability** as the one learning claim that has a neighbouring evidence base — and with the *possibility* of interactive health literacy recorded as a live, testable hypothesis rather than foreclosed. **v1 said "not by health literacy"; this revision says "not by health literacy as an outcome of a garden, until someone measures it — and here is what measuring it would take."** That is a narrower claim than the commission's framing in one direction and a wider one in the other, and it is compatible with the repository's finding that meaning and measured benefit come apart ([#19](../meaning/note.md) §1.4).

**Pillar 1, De Wielewaal as central green motor:** the health infrastructure offers no support for this pillar. The one green organisation inside the health movement is Wasven, not the Wielewaal [D, `C3`]; the CoE Health's green theme is about the *urban environment* and heat, not about a municipal estate; and the municipal green-health instrument is play and beweeg space [D, `C25`]. The pillar remains a hypothesis, as [#22](https://github.com/leonvanbokhorst/voedselbos/blob/research/22-climate-adaptation/research/climate/note.md) concluded from the climate side and [#15](../decisions/note.md) frames as a decision, now from a third direction.

**Pillar 2, a city-wide ecosystem:** the connective layer exists, is bigger than documented (193 partners), and is **thinner than its count** — no evaluation, no accounts, an operator dissolved, a one-year caretaker, funding ending in 2026, and gardens filed as meeting places [D, `C3`,`C25`,`C26`,`E2`]. "Connecting to the health movement" would currently mean joining a movement that is deciding what it is.

**Pillar 3, nature closer to everyday life:** this is where the retrieved evidence actually lands. The directory's 14 green/food listings, the eight €0–7 communal cooking and eating offers, the Iristuin's open-when-the-crew-is-there entrance rule, and the activity-submission route that forbids a referral requirement are all documented instances of **low-demand access to green and food** [D, `C6`,`C7`,`C9`,`E2`]. That is the pillar with evidence under it, and it does not need health literacy to justify itself.

**Community as foundation:** the health framing would *add* a condition to participation — and SDT says what that costs [D, `F4`]. The repository's own anchor case ran on a design in which "iedereen is vrij om de ene keer wel mee te doen en de andere keer niet" [C, `../meaning/note.md` §3]. **The intrinsic value of participation is preserved in this revision as a boundary (§1.3C), and it is now supported by a mechanism rather than only by an ethic.**

## 15. Uncertainties, and what would change the answer

**Not established, in the sources searched:** any adult garden used deliberately as a health-literacy intervention, anywhere; any measurement of *interactive* or *critical* health literacy in a green setting; any measurement of a garden's outcome after participation ends; any Dutch green-health programme with a controlled evaluation; any Dutch measurement of health literacy, learning or capability produced by a green programme; any **Dutch source pairing gezondheidsvaardigheden with buurttuin, volkstuin or voedselbos**; any application of COM-B or SDT to a community garden as a health intervention; any think-aloud or cognitive-interview source; an Eindhoven-specific health-literacy percentage; the GGD's September 2025 evaluation of the DE STAP neighbourhoods; DE STAP's legal form, accounts or whole-movement evaluation; whether any DE STAP listing leads to participation at all; whether a garden in Eindhoven has ever been approached by the health movement; whether the volunteers who met in 2025 still meet ([#16](../continuity/note.md)); and any study of people who were offered a garden and declined.

**What would change this answer most, in order:**

1. **A study that records achieved dose and measures household behaviour — not garden produce — after a garden intervention ends**, with a comparison. This is the specific study the issue needs and the literature does not have; it would either open or close the claim (§6.5).
2. **One adult community-garden or food-forest study with *interactive* health literacy as an outcome**, in any country. It would convert the central [U] into a finding either way — and it is the single most valuable missing study.
3. **A Dutch controlled evaluation of *Welzijn op Recept*** — the municipal pilot started on 2 June 2025 [D, `C25`]; a null on wellbeing or care use would harden the referral route into a negative.
4. **The GGD's evaluation of the DE STAP neighbourhoods** ([PI route](#13-investigation-and-access-log)) — the one document that would say whether a neighbourhood health approach with partners produced anything.
5. **A Dutch study or project document pairing health literacy with growing** — the pairing the Dutch search could not find.
6. **Participant-level data from any directory listing** — who comes, what they learn, what changes. It would turn the directory from a leaflet into practice, and it is the cheapest thing on this list.
7. **A source that would let a garden be assessed**: a garden-suitable capability measure with published properties (the gap `F9`/`F10` describe from the clinical end).

**What cannot be decided by this research:** whether a garden produces health literacy in Eindhoven — only a study with people could, and that requires the community's agreement, not the researcher's; whether anyone wants this collaboration; whether the herb garden is a place or a product ([#15](../decisions/note.md)); and whether the health framing is welcome at all.
