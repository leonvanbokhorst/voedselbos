## 1. The answer

**Short answer: a shared green practice is a plausible place for people to become more capable at *food* and at *asking*, and an implausible vehicle for *health literacy*; and the most defensible thing it does for health is to demand less of people than the health system demands.**

The issue's proposition has three links: participation → health literacy → self-directed behaviour change that carries into everyday life. Read against the retrieved evidence, the links fail differently, and the differences matter more than a verdict:

- **The middle link has no evidence at all.** Four recorded searches found **no study, anywhere, that uses an adult community garden, allotment, urban farm or food forest deliberately as a health-literacy intervention with health literacy as an outcome** [U, `A` §5]. "Green health literacy" is not an established construct; the nearest real family is environmental/climate/planetary health literacy, which is professional-facing and unvalidated — 28 studies, **17 distinct concepts**, "requires empirical validation" [D, `A27`]. A claim that a garden strengthens health literacy is an inference *across constructs*, not a documented effect.
- **The third link has one well-powered attempt, and it failed.** JArDinS followed 66 people entering a community garden against 66 pairwise-matched non-gardeners for twelve months, measuring household food supply, accelerometer physical activity, mental and social wellbeing: "no significant impact, **in spite of sufficient statistical power**" [D, `B7` — Lead-verified]. **No retrieved study measures anything after participation ends or a site closes** [U, `B` §4.3, `D` §4].
- **The first link is where the evidence is, and it is weaker and more design-dependent than the issue assumes.** Gardening programmes do produce small-to-moderate improvement in self-reported mood and social contact; the pooled estimate is carried by the weakest designs (before/after *d* = 0.60 against gardener/non-gardener *d* = 0.32), and the reviewers' own verdict is that "evidence quality overall was low" and effects "may be overestimated" [D, `B1`, `B4` — Lead-verified].

So the honest answer is not "yes, under conditions" or "no". It is that **the claim has to be re-specified**, because the construct the issue reaches for is not the one the evidence supports, and the region's own health platform already classifies gardens differently from the way the issue does.

Seven findings carry the answer.

1. **The headline number the issue inherits measures the wrong thing, and the record says so.** "About half of Dutch adults have limited health literacy" is **44.3%** (Nivel panel, HLS19-Q12, fieldwork November 2025) to **52.8%** (RIVM, HLS19-Q47, fieldwork 6 June–6 July 2025) [D, `A4`,`A6`,`E6` — Lead-verified]. The 8-point spread is mostly **instrument length**: the same 11,441 respondents score 52.8% on Q47 and 46.6% on Q12 [D, `A4`]. The step up from 29–36% (2012–2023, HLS-EU-Q16) is described by the national statistics portal as a consequence of the new instrument [D, `A7`]. The cut-offs are "**conceptueel vastgesteld**", not validated; the score is a rescaled mean of a four-point difficulty item, so 50 is the arithmetic neutral midpoint [D, `A4` — Lead-verified]; and RIVM states plainly that this "meet **ervaren moeilijkheid, niet objectieve vaardigheid**" and that the questionnaire was "hoogstwaarschijnlijk alleen ingevuld door mensen die kunnen lezen" [D, `A4` — Lead-verified]. It is a population indicator of felt difficulty; it is not a needs assessment for a garden and cannot be a baseline for one.
2. **The Dutch field has already moved to the demand side, which changes what a garden would even be doing.** RIVM's operational definition is relational — "een **dynamisch samenspel** tussen individuele capaciteiten en de complexiteit van het zorg- en informatiesysteem" [D, `A4`] — and Pharos states the doctrine without hedging: "Gezondheidsvaardigheden worden niet alleen bepaald door wat iemand zelf kan, maar ook door de eisen die de omgeving stelt" [D, `A1`, **Lead-verified on the factsheet**]; "Dat zoveel mensen beperkte gezondheidsvaardigheden hebben ligt niet aan hen" [D, `A2`]. If the deficit is substantially in the setting, then the garden's relevant property is not what it teaches but **how little it demands**: no referral, no form, no fluent reading, no registration, no measurement.
3. **Food literacy, not health literacy, is the construct that contains doing — and its gardening evidence base is real but is about children in schools.** Gardening is an established vehicle for **food literacy** (planning, managing, selecting, preparing): a preregistered Danish school-garden protocol, a Tasmanian kitchen-garden realist evaluation, and a systematic review of school food-and-nutrition-literacy interventions already organised by Nutbeam's functional/interactive/critical levels [D, `A28`,`A29`,`A31`]. The Dutch operationalisation is the **schooltuin**, and "Voedseleducatie is niet verplicht en daarmee geen structureel onderdeel van het Nederlandse curriculum" [D, `A26`]. Health literacy contains *appraising information*; food literacy contains *doing*. A food forest plausibly produces the second.
4. **Participation buys relationship, not capability, and the benefit is site-bound.** The Dutch evidence is modest and self-reported: eight to nine in ten volunteers in care-and-neighbourhood gardens say they met new people, but only **four in ten** agree that "in het afgelopen jaar durf ik meer mensen om hulp te vragen" — and asking is the one health-literacy-adjacent skill in the set [D, [`../climate/note.md`](../climate/note.md) §3.6 — verified there]. Coordinator burden is measured, not anecdotal: volunteer coordinators carry "the greatest burdens on their time and wellbeing … fatigue and feelings of over-commitment" [D, `B23`]. And where a site closed, the dependence shows: COVID closures had "disproportionate impacts … on vulnerable groups" [D, `B23`]. **Capability and benefit are attached to the place, and the repository's own anchor case is a place that ended.**
5. **The regional infrastructure is real, bigger and thinner than the issue says, and it is mid-handover.** The partner count is **193**, not 189 (the about page, the counter and 193 listed names agree) [D, `E1`,`C3` — Lead-verified]; growth ran 100 → 127 → 158 → 193 across municipal and Fontys documents [D, `C24`,`C25`,`C30`]. But DE STAP is not a foundation: its operator **Coöperatie Slimmer Leven was dissolved on 1 January 2026**, **GGD Brabant-Zuidoost holds a one-year penvoerderschap for 2026**, the relevant subsidies total **€1.2 million and end in 2026**, and "structurele borging van deze themalijnen bij een passende organisatie na 2026" is still to be decided [D, `C26` — Lead-verified]. There is **no public annual report, accounts, board or evaluation** of DE STAP [U, `C` §4]; the GGD's September 2025 evaluation of the DE STAP neighbourhoods is referenced by the municipality and is not public [D existence, `C25`; U content]. Among the 193 partners sit Fontys, GGD BZO, the municipality, IVN and — decisively for the main question — **Wasven**; **no food forest, community garden or citizen group appears in the list** [D, `C3`, absence].
6. **The directory shows what the movement already does with green and food, and it files it as meeting, not as health.** Of 147 Eindhoven listings, **14 are green or food (~10%)**, 99 are meeting-and-advice (~67%), and **every garden sits under the category *Ontmoeten*** [D, `E2`,`C7` — Lead-verified count]. The two listings the issue names resolve differently than expected: the Irisbuurt garden is **De Iristuin** at Buurthuis De Blokhut — a decade old, four named volunteers, herbs and tomatoes, and a neighbourhood pizza oven that uses the harvest — described on DE STAP with no address, no opening times, no cost and no contact [D, `C9`,`E3`]; and **'t Kruidenkafé is a GGzE day-activity venue** whose "Kruiden" is the *Kruidenbuurt*, offering yoga, Tai Chi, a volunteer-cooked Thursday meal and a clothing shop, with no growing of any kind [D, `C10`,`C11`,`C12`; the GGzE attribution is Line C's retrieval, not re-verified by the Lead]. The food activities are mostly **institutional kitchens that cook for and with residents** at €0–7; the green activities are maintenance and nature visits. The two rarely meet.
7. **The participation door is cheaper than the partnership door, and its own criteria contain the anti-qualification principle the issue asks for.** An activity can be listed by **anyone with a name and an e-mail**, free, subject to four published content criteria — one of which is "**Het is zonder verwijzing of indicatie toegankelijk**" [D, `C6`]. Partner status, by contrast, requires an *organisation* ("Naam organisatie") and leads to the Paco partner community; no fee, obligation or governance right is published anywhere [D, `C4`,`C5`]. So the route from a garden to visibility in the region's health movement is already open, free, and needs no entity — and the route into its governance is closed to anything that is not an organisation.

**What follows, stated as the note's inference rather than the sources' finding [I]:**

- **Do not run a health intervention in a garden.** The retrieved evidence gives no support for health literacy, dietary change or persistence, and the qualification-and-stigma evidence shows that gating an offer on a health problem deters exactly the people the offer claims to serve (§3.9).
- **Do not measure health literacy.** The instrument measures felt difficulty in a sample that excludes the least literate, on cut-offs that are conceptual; using it as an outcome for a garden would measure the instrument's properties, not the garden.
- **Claim the narrow things.** Food capability practised under supervision; the low-demand setting; the recurring occasion; and the one skill the Dutch evidence says is hardest and most protective — asking for help. Each of those can be described without borrowing a construct, and each can fail visibly.
- **Use the door that exists.** If visibility for Voedselbos 2.0 is the organising focus, an activity listing under a route that explicitly requires "geen verwijzing of indicatie" is a documented, free, reversible step — and it is a *listing*, not a collaboration, which is exactly what the record supports claiming.

## 3. The evidence

### 3.1 The number, and what it is a number of

The figure the issue inherits ("approximately 44–53% … depending on the 2026 survey and instrument") is three small errors away from the record, and all three matter for how it may be used [D, `A1`,`A4`,`A6`,`A7`; Lead read the Pharos factsheet and the RIVM report]:

- **The band is 44.3–52.8%**, and the factsheet itself is internally inconsistent — 52.8% in the headline range, 52.3% in its own explanatory paragraph.
- **Both surveys did fieldwork in 2025**, not 2026. RIVM fielded 6 June–6 July 2025 (n = 11,441); Nivel's panel in November 2025 (n = 733). 2026 is the publication year.
- **It is not two surveys giving a range.** It is one population survey read at two lengths — Q47 = 52.8%, Q12 = **46.6% in the same respondents** — plus one small panel on the short form (44.3%). The authors' own explanation, quoted by Pharos: the Q12 items "was gebaseerd op het conceptuele raamwerk en niet op basis van uitkomsten van de Q47", and "De Q12 is dus geen inhoudelijk equivalent van de Q47" [D, `A1`].

The rise from the pre-2024 series is the larger issue. The same national series reported 29% / 36% / 25% / 35% on HLS-EU-Q16 across 2012–2023 [D, `A4` p.29]. De Staat van Volksgezondheid en Zorg attributes the jump: "**Daardoor** is het percentage hoger dan in eerdere jaren, toen het percentage schommelde tussen 29 en 35% maar waar gebruik gemaakt werd van de HLS-Q16" [D, `A7`]. A series whose level changed when the instrument changed cannot carry a claim about Dutch society becoming less capable.

Two further properties bound what the number can be used for. The cut-offs are "**conceptueel vastgesteld** op basis van het oorspronkelijke HLS-EU-Q47 model" [D, `A4` p.18, Lead-verified], with no retrieved validation against behaviour or morbidity; and because the score is a rescaled mean of a four-point item anchored *heel moeilijk* → *heel makkelijk*, a score of 50 is the neutral midpoint between "difficult" and "easy", so "about half" is close to what a population rating itself near the midpoint would produce by construction **[I]**. And the measure is of felt difficulty, in a literate sample [D, `A4` p.31, Lead-verified].

**Consequence for this issue [I].** The number cannot be used in three of the ways such numbers are usually used: as a description of a neighbourhood (it is national, and no Eindhoven-specific percentage exists in any retrieved source [U, `C` §7.3]), as a diagnosis of the people a garden would reach, or as a baseline against which a garden could be credited with change. What it can support is a single, safer statement: **a large share of Dutch adults report finding health information hard to use, and the Dutch field's own reading of that is that the information and the system are hard.**

### 3.2 The demand side, which changes what a garden would be for

The Dutch operational definition is not a trait list [D, `A4` p.11, Lead-verified]:

> "Gezondheidsvaardigheden vormen een **dynamisch samenspel** tussen individuele capaciteiten en de complexiteit van het zorg- en informatiesysteem."

Nivel states the same as a mismatch between "persoonlijke vaardigheden en de complexiteit van de omgeving" [D, `A6`]. Pharos turns it into doctrine and into practice: "Gezondheidsvaardigheden worden niet alleen bepaald door wat iemand zelf kan, maar ook door de eisen die de omgeving stelt. Naarmate een situatie ingewikkelder is of meer stress veroorzaakt, kan het voor mensen moeilijker worden om hun vaardigheden optimaal te gebruiken, **ook als zij die vaardigheden in principe wel hebben**" [D, `A1`, Lead-verified]; and it operationalises the answer as ten characteristics of a *gezondheidsvaardige organisatie* — understandable information, easy to find, understandable conversations, extra support, inclusive research [D, `A3`].

The critique literature says the same thing from outside, and it is current rather than historical: Okuhara, Okada & Furukawa (2026) name "**health literacy reductionism**" — "the tendency to preferentially translate complex health communication and health promotion problems into a lack of individual health literacy among patients or the public" — which backgrounds relational, organisational and structural explanations [D, `A14`]. Chinn's older charge is that the concept "stretches the idea of 'literacy' to an indefensible extent" [D, `A9`].

**What this does to the issue's premise [I].** If the deficit is substantially in the demands, then a garden is not a place that supplies a missing skill to deficient people; it is a setting with a *different demand profile*. Its relevant properties become: no referral, no form, no registration, no fluent reading of institutional text, no measurement, no appointment, a person present, and permission to attend without producing anything (the last documented locally in [#20](../practices/note.md) §3). That is a real and defensible health claim — but it is a claim about *access*, not about *literacy*, and it is the opposite of a curriculum.

### 3.3 The construct that actually contains doing

Food literacy is defined in Dutch practice as "het hebben van kennis en vaardigheden om eten te kunnen plannen, regelen, kiezen en bereiden ten behoeve van een gezonde, veilige en duurzamere voedingsinname" [D, `A26`, citing Vidgen & Gallegos 2014 — the original was not retrieved]. Its Dutch instrument (SPFL, n = 755) reduces 50 items to 29 across eight domains including planning, managing and preparing [D, `A25`], validated on a sample that was **90.7% female and 59.1% highly educated** — a caution for exactly the population at issue **[I]**.

The difference is the one that decides this report's claim [I, from `A` §4.3]: **health literacy contains appraising; food literacy contains doing.** A garden plausibly produces the second. Claiming the first requires an extra step through information appraisal that the construct does not supply, and that no retrieved study has tested in a garden.

Where gardening does have a food-literacy evidence base, the population is children in schools: a preregistered Danish quasi-experimental protocol on six school-garden sites (FoodACT, NCT05839080) [D, `A28`]; a Tasmanian kitchen-and-garden realist evaluation [D, `A29`]; a systematic review of school food-and-nutrition-literacy interventions organised by Nutbeam's three levels [D, `A31`]. The Dutch route is the schooltuin, and it is not a structural curriculum requirement [D, `A26`]. **Nothing retrieved transfers this to an adult community garden in Eindhoven** [U].

One more admission, because it bounds the whole section: the issue asks for behaviour-change frameworks — COM-B and self-determination theory — to be used critically. **No line retrieved the primary sources for either, and this note therefore makes no claim from them** [U]. What the retrieved material supports instead is narrower and, for a garden, more useful: cooking interventions that report short-term success do so through "provide instruction" and "**prompt practice**" — supervised doing — and only 14 of 59 reported long-term outcomes [D, `D17`]; and the 2026 Bayesian re-analysis of social-prescribing trials locates the effect in "**successful participation, rather than referral alone**" [D, `D12`]. Both point at *opportunity and participation*, not at capability or motivation — which is the reading a COM-B audit would have to confront, not a substitute for running one.

### 3.4 What participation yields, and how strongly

The strongest evidence in the whole retrieval is that gardening programmes make people feel better while they are in them, and that the size of the finding depends on how it was measured [D, `B1` — Lead read Table 2]:

| Subgroup | Effect (Hedges' *d*) | Comparisons |
|---|---|---|
| All | 0.42 (95% CI 0.36–0.48); 0.35 after trim-and-fill | 76 |
| **Wellbeing** vs health variables | **0.47** vs 0.31 | 58 / 18 |
| **Horticultural therapy** vs non-therapy | **0.61** vs 0.31 | 33 / 43 |
| **Before/after** vs gardener/non-gardener | **0.60** vs 0.32 | 32 / 44 |
| **Patients** vs non-patients | **0.61** vs 0.32 | 28 / 48 |

The effect is not nothing; it is *design-dependent*. Every subgroup with less protection against regression to the mean, expectancy and enthusiasm carries the larger number, and the authors' own Egger test found publication bias, with trim-and-fill reducing the pooled estimate from 0.42 to 0.35 [D, `B1`].

The reviews that look hardest are blunt. Fifty-three studies: "Effects appeared positive for fruit and vegetable intake, some psychosocial and community outcomes, but mixed for physical health outcomes. **Evidence quality overall was low**" and "poor evidence quality suggests the effects of community gardening may be **overestimated**" [D, `B4`]. Fifteen studies: 13 of 15 carry selection bias, 3 of 15 used objective measurement [D, `B5`]. An all-RCT review of horticultural therapy found **four eligible RCTs** and "insufficient evidence" [D, `B11`]; a 27-study meta-analysis in older adults reports *g* ≈ 0.52 for self-efficacy and self-esteem but rates certainty "**very low**" [D, `B12`]. And the strongest dietary trial — 23 schools, 641 children, 18 months, 24-hour food diaries — found an adjusted effect of **−40 g/day** (95% CI −88 to 1; p = 0.06) and concluded there was "very little evidence" for the claim [D, `B16`].

The Dutch-specific result is the most useful single calibration: among 121 allotment gardeners and 63 neighbours, those **aged 62 and over** scored "significantly or marginally better on all measures of health and well-being", while younger gardeners "did not differ from younger neighbors" [D, `B9` — Lead-verified]. The authors add that "the findings may be limited by self selection" [D]. **In the one Dutch study of its kind, the effect is an age effect in a self-selected group, not a gardening effect with a general population.**

### 3.5 The transfer question, which is the pivot

This is where the issue lives or dies, and the retrieved literature is thin in a way that is itself the finding.

**The one study built to detect transfer found none.** JArDinS (Montpellier, 2018–2019) compared 66 people entering a community garden with 66 pairwise-matched non-gardeners over twelve months, on nutritional quality, environmental impact and cost of household food supplies, accelerometer-measured physical activity, mental and social wellbeing, food-waste sensitivity and connection to nature. Result: "Participating in the community garden had **no significant impact, in spite of sufficient statistical power**, on fruit and vegetables supplies (main outcome), nor on physical activity parameters, nor on others of the social/health, environmental and economic lifestyles components investigated" [D, `B7` — Lead re-read]. The authors' own conclusion is the sentence this report keeps coming back to: "The health benefits of community gardening previously reported by cross-sectional studies **might be confounded by selection bias**" [D, `B7`]. Their interviews add a further, humbling detail: some gardeners already had "pre-established health and environmental consciousness", and the reported barriers were "lack of time, lack of gardening knowledge, physical difficulty of gardening, health problems and conflicts with other gardeners" [D] — *participation* barriers, not health-literacy barriers.

**Nobody measures the after-the-end period.** Across the reviews read, follow-up is at the end of the intervention, with the garden still running [U, `B` §4.3]. The nearest adjacent evidence is that closures hurt most those with least: COVID closures had "disproportionate impacts … on vulnerable groups" [D, `B23`]. That is evidence of **dependence on the site** — the precise failure mode this repository documented when Voedselbos Eindhoven closed ([#16](../continuity/note.md)).

**The literature that would show transferable capability does not exist in retrievable form.** Situated learning's community-of-practice form has been reviewed in public health: twelve studies of ten communities of practice, changing practitioner practice where they offer "structured problem-solving, reflective practice and networking", and "**None of the studies described the impact of CoPs on public health outcomes**"; "the true impact of CoPs on population health remains unknown" [D, `D19` — Lead re-read]. In the one Dutch garden-and-kitchen case with participants in the record, the qualitative evaluation rates self-reliance at "+ (few examples from participant and supervisor)" and "finding opportunities and hobbies" as not found [D, `D6`].

**And the health-literacy half of the claim was never attempted.** Gardening as a *health-literacy* intervention does not appear in the literature at all: three abstract-level hits for health literacy + garden, none an intervention; a full-text sweep added nothing at title level [D/U, `A` §5.1–5.3]. The absence is not refutation — it is the absence of anyone having tried or published.

### 3.6 The regional layer: what exists, who owns it, and what it is for

**DE STAP naar gezonder** is, in its own words, "de naam van een **beweging**" with 193 organisational partners across Eindhoven and De Kempen [D, `E1`, Lead-verified]. It is also the city's own policy vehicle: "samen zetten we De STAP naar gezonder" (Gemeente Eindhoven, 2024) [D, `C24`]. What it is *not* is an organisation with a public existence: no legal entity, board, annual report, accounts or whole-movement evaluation was found [U, `C` §4]; the partner list contains "no food forest, community garden or citizen group" [D, absence, `C3`]; and its operational owner **Coöperatie Slimmer Leven was dissolved on 1 January 2026**, leaving **GGD Brabant-Zuidoost as penvoerder for one transition year** on **€1.2 million** of subsidies that run to the end of 2026, with "structurele borging … na 2026" requiring a decision that has not been taken [D, `C26`, Lead-verified]. The municipal health budget for 2026 sits inside a €4,229,000 package that is 85% SPUK-GALA, and the document says the cover for 2027 "valt veel lager uit" [D, `C25`].

**Fontys** is the complementary expertise the issue asks about, and it is current — with one important dating correction. The CoE Health page (live 2026) confirms the three themes verbatim: "**gezondheidsvaardigheden, gezonde fysieke en sociale leefomgeving en samenzorg voor de toekomst**", with health literacy defined around "informatievaardigheden en digitale vaardigheden" and "**inclusief onderzoek en co-creatie met gebruikers met lage gezondheidsvaardigheden**", and with a green-environment research line including "**Groene stedelijke leefomgeving** — Onderzoek naar de vraag: wat is het effect van een groene stedelijke omgeving op de gezondheid?" [D, `E5`, Lead-verified]. Its board is a public-private partnership of "**Slimmer Leven, Pharos, GGD-BZO, Transvorm, Fontys Mens en Gezondheid en Fontys Paramedisch**" [D, `E5`] — which places the Dutch health-literacy institute (Pharos) and the regional GGD one governance step from the university that owns DE STAP's learning workstream. Note two dating problems this creates: **Slimmer Leven is still listed as a board member months after its dissolution** [D, `E5`; `C` §6.2], and the 2023 article's claim that Fontys is "eigenaar van de bouwsteen 'lerend vermogen'" is **not repeated in any 2025–2026 source** [U, `C34`,`C35`].

Fontys' involvement continues in practice: the 2025 annual report and a 2 December 2025 BRON article name three professional learning workplaces — **Achtse Barrier, Eckart/Vaartbroek (Woensel) and Son en Breugel** — working "onder de vlag van" DE STAP on health, vitality and equal chances, with Toegepaste Psychologie among the student routes [D, `C34`,`C35`,`C33`]. **Tongelre, named in 2023, has since dropped out** [D, absence]. The funding is one-year at a time, described as a "pioniersfase" [D, `C34`].

**The municipality and the GGD** supply need and vocabulary but not an instrument for growing. Loneliness among adults rose 13% → 19%, stress 19% → 23%, and "voelt zich niet zo gelukkig of ongelukkig" 4% → 7% between 2020 and 2024, with healthy life expectancy flat at 70.6–71.9 years and inequality named by neighbourhood (Oud-Woensel, De Laak, Ontginning, Oud-Gestel, Doornakkers, Begijnenbroek) [D, `C25`]. The 2030 goals are "drie jaar langer in goede gezondheid" and a 20% reduction in health inequalities between neighbourhoods and groups [D, `C25`]. Green is a municipal health instrument **as space**: the "Gezonde Buurt" approach (Vlokhoven/Groene Long: play equipment, kunstgras, a walking path), beweegtuinen, calisthenics parks, and an adopted "**Groenarm**" indicator measuring distance to public green [D, `C25`,`C24`]. **No municipal document retrieved names community gardens, buurtmoestuinen, voedselbossen or urban food-growing as a prevention instrument** [D, absence, `C25`,`C24`]. The "Preventieakkoord" that once convened "ruim 50 partners" no longer exists as a programme; it was absorbed in 2024 [D, `C25`].

**Two consequences [I].** First, a food forest entering municipal health policy would be entering through a door that is not a prevention instrument: green space yes, cultivation no. Second, the regional movement it might join is *deciding what it will be after 2026* during 2026 — which is a reason to be a listing rather than a partner, and a reason not to build anything that depends on the movement's continuity.

**The pattern [I].** The movement's *green* offers are places to be and to help; its *food* offers are welfare and institutional kitchens that cook for and with people. A food forest sits in neither category, and the issue's premise — that these listings are already a health-literacy pathway — is not supported by their own classification. Being listed is **co-presence in a signposting instrument**, not a relationship: it tells you a submission was accepted, nothing about who runs the activity, whether it is current, or whether the movement and the garden have ever spoken. That is the same distinction this repository drew for Voedselbos Eindhoven's absent Trefpunt Groen membership ([`../network/note.md`](../network/note.md)).

The one genuinely encouraging structural fact is the submission route: four published criteria, one of which is "**Het is zonder verwijzing of indicatie toegankelijk**", no fee, no legal entity, editorial review [D, `C6`]. **The movement has already written the anti-qualification principle into its own intake.**

### 3.8 Frameworks, used critically

Three framework-level findings, each retrieved, and none of them licence a garden-as-intervention:

- **Positieve Gezondheid**, the vocabulary behind the region's six site sections (which are *not* the instrument's six dimensions [D, `C` §1.3]), is a conversation and agenda-setting tool, not an outcome measure. iPH itself points to a meetset "under development" and an Evaluatiewijzer still being built [D, `C37`]; a TSG methodological paper states "Dit instrument is niet ontwikkeld, en daardoor niet bruikbaar, als meetinstrument" and that content validity was "onvoldoende" [D, `C40`]; a psychiatric journal review states "de vragenlijst is niet gevalideerd en het wetenschappelijke bewijs dat positieve gezondheid 'werkt', ontbreekt tot nu toe" [D, `C41`]; and the concept carries a documented individualisation critique — "Wie ondanks alle positiviteit toch nog klachten houdt of er niet in slaagt zijn gedrag vol te houden, kan daar zelf voor verantwoordelijk worden gehouden" [D, `C42`]. **Any claim that a garden "improves positive health" inherits all four problems.**
- **Situated learning** is the right theory for what a garden does — and its health-promotion record is a change in *practice* with no demonstrated change in *outcomes* [D, `D19`], with the movement from periphery to core organised by the institution rather than the practice [D, `D20`] and power relations "displaced by a managerial preoccupation" with communities of practice [D, `D21`]. Read for a garden, the radical part of the theory is *who is allowed to move from the edge to the centre* — which is a governance question, not a curriculum.
- **Social prescribing** is the closest institutional analogue, and its best evidence is pre/post. The England-wide programme reached 8,339 people and improved all four ONS-4 items, with an SROI of £2.42 per £1 [D, `D9`]; its own evaluators record that "it is unknown how representative the data is" and that "**evidence for GSP is not sufficiently compelling or rigorous**" [D, `D9`]. The systematic-review layer is weaker still: 15 evaluations of link-worker schemes, "**All were rated as a having a high risk of bias**", and "current evidence fails to provide sufficient detail to judge either success or value for money" [D, `D11`]. There is **no NICE guideline recommending nature-based social prescribing** [U, `D` §2] — NG44 covers community engagement only [D, `D15`].

### 3.9 Exclusion, and the qualification trap

The issue asks this to be addressed directly, and the retrieved evidence supports a structural rather than attitudinal account.

- **The overlap is large and not mainly linguistic.** Stichting Lezen en Schrijven: 18% of Dutch adults (2.5 million) struggle with reading, writing and/or arithmetic; 25% of adults have limited health literacy; "**Alle laaggeletterden hebben beperkte gezondheidsvaardigheden**"; and "**Ongeveer 55% van de laaggeletterden heeft geen migratie-achtergrond**" [D, `D23`]. An *anderstaligen*-only framing would miss most of the group. (The factsheet's primary citations sit on a page not retrieved; treat 18/25% as second-hand until re-sourced **[I]**.)
- **Access to nature is stratified before anyone is invited.** In Natural England's MENE data (2009–2012, so dated), frequent nature visitors are 38% of ethnic-minority adults against 57%, 43% of the urban deprived, and 42% of people with a disability or long-term illness; "the demographic factors that account for most of the variation … are, in descending order: **whether or not someone has a disability**; whether people are members of the BAME population; socio-economic status; age" [D, `D24`]. **Disability is the strongest single factor** — and that is a design requirement (raised beds, paths, seating, a toilet), not an invitation.
- **Qualifying for the offer excludes.** Welzijn op Recept requires a GP referral for psychosocial complaints, and Dutch action research found it stalling even at experienced sites, with two of four drifting off the model's own definition and multi-problem clients better served by a broader offer [D, `D7`]. In UK qualitative work people decline because "they are afraid of being stigmatised" and because "Patients expect to be referred to an investigation, or a drug, not to SP!" [D, `D14`].
- **A free offer with an end date creates a cliff.** When free sessions stopped: "What is the point then if it's not free anymore?" [D, `D14`].
- **Burdens land on the person who hosts.** Volunteer coordinators carry the greatest time and wellbeing cost [D, `B23`]; a Dutch walking programme states "het aantal begeleiders heeft moeite dat bij te houden" [D, `D2`]; link workers' "capacity is stretched" [D, `D9`]; and the national directory of 1,300+ green-health initiatives, *Beter in het Groen*, went offline in 2020 [D, `D5`].

**Assembled from the retrieved evidence rather than quoted from one source, a genuinely low-threshold offer looks like this [I]:** free at the point of use; weekly and drop-in, so a missed week is not an exit; self-referral accepted; a person who makes first contact; no measurement burden; no end date; and physical access designed for people who cannot bend, stand long or travel far. **A garden can meet most of those conditions more cheaply than a programme can — and the two that it meets worst are the last two.**

## 4. Counter-evidence and tensions

1. **The strongest counter-evidence is that the garden may not be a health setting at all.** JArDinS, the only well-powered transfer test, is null [D, `B7`]; the strongest dietary RCT is null [D, `B16`]; the pooled gardening effect halves when designs improve [D, `B1`]; and where the Dutch evidence is strongest, the effect belongs to being over 62 rather than to gardening [D, `B9`]. The temptation to read the 0.47 wellbeing figure as a food-forest number is exactly the category error this note exists to prevent.
2. **The construct the issue wants may be the wrong construct, and the field's own move supports the correction.** Health literacy now means skill × demand [D, `A4`], the Dutch institute says the problem is not the person's [D, `A1`,`A2`], and the critique calls individualising it "reductionism" [D, `A14`]. Building a garden argument on a deficit reading would be using the construct in the sense the field has spent fifteen years abandoning.
3. **The regional partner is not the one the issue names.** Wasven — a staffed, care-contracted, ANBI-structured organisation — is a DE STAP partner [D, `C3`]; no food forest, community garden or citizen group is. The existing bridge runs through **institutionalised** green care, not through volunteer growing. That is a finding about which door is open, and it cuts against the assumption that a food forest's natural route is the health movement.
4. **The movement is weakest exactly where the issue needs it strongest.** Its operator dissolved, its coordination is a one-year transition, its money ends in 2026, it has no public accounts or evaluation, and it abandoned its own attempt to measure positive health [D, `C25`,`C26`; U, `C` §4]. A collaboration proposed in 2026 would be proposed to an organisation in the act of becoming something else.
5. **The municipal instrument is space, not cultivation.** Green appears as play, beweeg and a "groenarm" indicator — not as growing [D, `C24`,`C25`]. A food forest would be arguing for a category the policy does not have, which is the same asymmetry this repository found on the green side for the Lichtbos programme.
6. **The issue's own framing carries the risk it warns against.** Asking a garden to build health literacy makes participation conditional on a health goal, in a setting whose documented value is that it asks nothing. The Dutch evidence on stigma, on the free-offer cliff and on coordinator burden is the evidence for taking that warning literally [D, `D14`,`B23`].
7. **Evidence quality is the ceiling, and it is low by the reviewers' own verdicts.** "Evidence quality overall was low" [D, `B4`]; "certainty … very low" [D, `B12`]; "Generally the quantitative research was poor" [D, `B15`]; "All were rated as a having a high risk of bias" [D, `D11`]. Any confident sentence in this area is borrowing against a thin balance.
8. **A tension inside the Dutch numbers that could not be resolved.** Both 2025 measurements find **men** reporting more health-literacy problems than women (54.9% vs 50.9% in RIVM; the same direction at Nivel), against a policy framing that treats the issue as concentrated among women, older people and the lower-educated [D, `A4`,`A6`; unexplained, U]. It is a footnote, not a claim — but it is a reminder that this literature's headline patterns are not stable.
9. **The two survey figures disagree with each other in the record.** RIVM cites Nivel's panel figure as 45.2%; Nivel's own report, Pharos and Staat VenZ all give **44.3%** [D, `A4`,`A6`,`A1`,`A7`; U which is right]. The difference is small; the fact that four documents disagree about the same number is not.
10. **A contradiction preserved rather than repaired.** The issue brief states 189 partners; the page, its counter and its name list say **193**, while municipal and Fontys documents give 100 (early 2023), ">95" (June 2023), 127 (later 2023), 158 (2024–25) and "zo'n 150" (2025). The direction is consistent; **no figure is audited** [D, `E1`,`C3`,`C24`,`C25`,`C30`,`C35`].

## 5. Hypotheses and counterexamples

Four hypotheses, each written so that it could come back negative. These are **not** proposals and require no one's consent to be *false*; the first two can be tested on public evidence alone.

**H1 — Task capability.** A recurring, free, bounded session with one task and one person who knows produces **task capability** ("I can do this here, with someone around") but not portable capability, and the difference shows up as soon as the person is asked to do it alone. *If it worked:* people who have attended three times do the task unaided in a different setting. *If it failed:* people are confident and helpful inside the session and never do it at home — which is the VTTB finding [D, `D6`] and the situated-learning verdict [D, `D19`]. *Falsifier:* a garden whose participants report doing the task at home without prompting, verifiable without asking them to fill anything in.

**H2 — The demand profile is the health contribution.** The health-relevant property of a shared green practice is that it is a **lower-demand setting** than the health system: no referral, no form, no reading, no measurement, no appointment. *If it worked:* the people who come include people who have declined or dropped out of referred health offers. *If it failed:* the participants are the same already-engaged, already-educated group as the volunteer base in the Dutch evaluation, which was "about half … university or HBO" against 35–37% of surrounding residents [D, [`../climate/note.md`](../climate/note.md) §3.6]. *This is the one hypothesis that can be checked retrospectively from a garden's own participant description, without a new study.*

**H3 — The one learnable health-literacy skill is asking.** If anything health-literacy-adjacent is produced, it is **interactive** literacy: asking a question, getting help, navigating a person rather than a system. The Dutch evidence says this is the hardest, not the easiest: only 4 in 10 volunteers agree they now dare ask more people for help, against 8–9 in 10 who have met new people [D, [`../climate/note.md`](../climate/note.md) §3.6]. *If it worked:* an increase in "I asked someone for help" would be the only defensible indicator — and it is a **giving/asking asymmetry**, so the garden would be producing givers, not askers. *If it failed:* the garden reproduces the asymmetry. *Note the direction of this hypothesis: it predicts the garden is worse at the health-literacy skill than at the social one.*

**H4 — The listing, not the partnership.** An activity listing (free, anyone, four criteria, one of which forbids a referral requirement) reaches more of the intended population than partner status (organisation-only, governance-facing, obligations unpublished) — and it costs nothing. *If it worked:* a listed activity with no organisational form behind it gets enquiries from people who are not already in the network. *If it failed:* listing generates no contact, and the "movement" is a directory with no traffic. *Falsifier:* enquiry data, which the movement would have to hold; **not obtainable** [U].

**Five counterexamples to the premise, from the retrieved record:**

1. **The Vergeten Tuin** has run for twenty-five years with ~180 members and ~25 core volunteers, a green zoning since 2007 and a management agreement, and it makes **no health claim at all** [D, [`../practices/note.md`](../practices/note.md) §3.1]. If long-lived gardens produced health literacy, the practice most likely to have noticed has not.
2. **'t Kruidenkafé** is named for herbs and contains no herbs: a GGzE day-activity venue whose participants are mental-healthcare clients [D, `C10`,`C12`]. It is the clearest local case of the name doing the argument's work, and it is genuinely valuable as a *low-demand social venue* — which is the different and better claim.
3. **The eight cooking listings** already exist, cost €0–7, and are classified as meeting, not learning [D, `E2`,`C7`]. If cooking together produced food literacy that anyone could see, the region's own health platform would have a reason to say so.
4. **Fontys' professional learning workplaces** in Achtse Barrier and Eckart/Vaartbroek are the region's documented instance of an institution entering a neighbourhood to learn — and the learning runs **from** the institution **to** the neighbourhood (student routes, research, "versterken van gezondheid … en gezondheidsvaardigheden") [D, `C33`,`C34`]. Reciprocity is documented in participatory *research* [D, `D22`], not in this programme class [U, `D` §6].
5. **The England-wide green social prescribing programme** — the best-resourced attempt anywhere, 8,339 people, £5.77m — is pre/post only, and its own evaluators say the evidence is "not sufficiently compelling or rigorous" [D, `D9`]. A volunteer food forest in Eindhoven cannot outperform the design of a national programme with professional link workers.

## 6. Open questions for a later, separately agreed conversation

Not questions for a stakeholder to answer on the researcher's behalf; questions whose answers would change the note. **No one has been asked any of them.**

1. **What does "health" mean in the practice's own words?** The Voedselbos archive already contains a health claim — "met extra aandacht voor een duurzame leefstijl om burn-out of welvaartsziektes te voorkomen zoals depressie" — and a learning claim, "een open klaslokaal, waar we leren van de natuur, van elkaar en van de diverse planten" [C, `../meaning/note.md` §3]. Which of those was the practice, and which was the offer's framing?
2. **Who came, and who stopped coming?** This is the question no retrieved source answers for any Eindhoven green practice [U], and the one that decides H1 and H2.
3. **Would you describe what happens as teaching?** If yes, who teaches, what do they teach, and what happens when they are not there — the single-person dependency this repository has already documented twice.
4. **Would being listed as an activity be welcome, and would being a partner be unwelcome?** The route is documented and free [D, `C6`]; the appetite is **[U]** and must not be assumed.
5. **What would you refuse to do?** A garden that turns down a health framing is making a defensible choice, and the note should be able to record a "no".
6. **For the health partners: what would make you change something because of what a community practice told you?** The reciprocal-learning question, asked of the side that has the power to act on the answer [D for the absence, `D` §6].

## 7. What this means for the main question and the three pillars

**For the main question** ("human and ecological health through shared care"): the health half of that phrase should be carried by **access and relationship**, with food capability as the one learning claim the evidence permits — not by health literacy, dietary change or positive-health scores. That is a narrower claim than the commission's framing and a more defensible one, and it is compatible with the repository's existing finding that meaning and measured benefit come apart ([#19](../meaning/note.md) §1.4).

**Pillar 1, De Wielewaal as central green motor:** the health infrastructure offers no support for this pillar. The one green organisation inside the health movement is Wasven, not the Wielewaal [D, `C3`]; the CoE Health's green theme is about the *urban environment* and heat, not about a municipal estate; and the municipal green-health instrument is play and beweeg space [D, `C25`]. The pillar remains a hypothesis, as [#22](../climate/note.md) concluded from the climate side and [#15](../decisions/note.md) frames as a decision, now from a third direction.

**Pillar 2, a city-wide ecosystem:** the connective layer exists, is bigger than documented (193 partners), and is **thinner than its count** — no evaluation, no accounts, an operator dissolved, a one-year caretaker, funding ending in 2026, and gardens filed as meeting places [D, `C3`,`C25`,`C26`,`E2`]. "Connecting to the health movement" would currently mean joining a movement that is deciding what it is.

**Pillar 3, nature closer to everyday life:** this is where the retrieved evidence actually lands. The directory's 14 green/food listings, the eight €0–7 communal cooking and eating offers, the Iristuin's open-when-the-crew-is-there entrance rule, and the activity-submission route that forbids a referral requirement are all documented instances of **low-demand access to green and food** [D, `C6`,`C7`,`C9`,`E2`]. That is the pillar with evidence under it, and it does not need health literacy to justify itself.

**Community as foundation:** the health framing would *add* a condition to participation. The repository's own anchor case ran on a design in which "iedereen is vrij om de ene keer wel mee te doen en de andere keer niet" [C, `../meaning/note.md` §3]. A health-literacy goal is not compatible with that design unless it is kept entirely off the invitation.

## 10. Uncertainties, and what would change the answer

**Not established, in the sources searched:** any adult garden used deliberately as a health-literacy intervention, anywhere; any measurement of a garden's outcome after participation ends; any Dutch green-health programme with a controlled evaluation; any Dutch measurement of health literacy, learning or capability produced by a green programme; an Eindhoven-specific health-literacy percentage; the GGD's September 2025 evaluation of the DE STAP neighbourhoods; DE STAP's legal form, accounts or whole-movement evaluation; whether any DE STAP listing leads to participation at all; whether a garden in Eindhoven has ever been approached by the health movement; whether the volunteers who met in 2025 still meet ([#16](../continuity/note.md)); and any study of people who were offered a garden and declined.

**What would change this answer most, in order:**

1. **A study measuring household behaviour — not garden produce — after a garden intervention ends**, with a comparison. This is the specific study the issue needs and the literature does not have; it would either open or close the whole claim (§3.5).
2. **A Dutch controlled evaluation of *Welzijn op Recept*** — the municipal pilot started on 2 June 2025 [D, `C25`], and a null on wellbeing or care use would harden the referral route into a negative.
3. **One adult community-garden or food-forest study with health literacy as an outcome**, in any country. It would convert the central [U] into a finding either way.
4. **The GGD's evaluation of the DE STAP neighbourhoods** ([PI route](#8-investigation-and-access-log)) — the one document that would say whether a neighbourhood health approach with partners produced anything.
5. **A municipal document naming community growing as an instrument** in the gezonde leefomgeving programme — it would open a policy door §3.6 says is closed.
6. **Participant-level data from any directory listing** — who comes, what they learn, what changes. It would turn the directory from a leaflet into practice, and it is the cheapest thing on this list.
7. **A retrieval of COM-B and self-determination theory**, so that the framework audit the issue asks for can be made rather than deferred.

**What cannot be decided by this research:** whether a garden produces health literacy in Eindhoven — only a study with people could, and that requires the community's agreement, not the researcher's; whether anyone wants this collaboration; whether the herb garden is a place or a product ([#15](../decisions/note.md)); and whether the health framing is welcome at all.
