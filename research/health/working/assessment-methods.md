# Assessable or not? Instruments, context-sensitive methods, and what a community should not have to carry

**Line:** revision line (v2), serving issue #25. **Author:** health-reviser. **Retrieved:** 26 November 2026.
**Status:** raw working material for [`../note.md`](../note.md). Not evidence in itself. Marks as elsewhere.

**Why this file exists.** v1 merged two different claims: (i) *population instruments are unsuitable as a garden's outcome measure* and (ii) *therefore no appropriate assessment exists*. (ii) does not follow from (i). This file separates them and records what context-sensitive assessment is retrievable, what each method could establish, what burden it puts on participants, and what a community could reasonably do **without becoming a research site**.

---

## 1. Claim (i): why the population instruments cannot be a garden's outcome measure

This part is already established in v1 from retrieved sources and is not re-argued here; it is restated so the two claims stay apart.

| Property | Evidence | Consequence for a garden |
|---|---|---|
| Measures **felt difficulty**, not skill | "Dit meet **ervaren moeilijkheid, niet objectieve vaardigheid**" (`A4` p.31, Lead-verified) | A garden cannot move a self-report of difficulty with *hospital forms* without moving the hospital forms |
| Cut-offs are **"conceptueel vastgesteld"**, not validated against behaviour or morbidity | `A4` p.18, Lead-verified | Any "improvement" is movement across an unvalidated boundary |
| Score is a rescaled mean of a 4-point item; 50 is the arithmetic neutral midpoint | `A4` p.18 + arithmetic [I] | Baseline will sit near the midpoint whatever the garden does |
| Sample **excludes the least literate** — self-completion | `A4` p.31 | The people the claim is about are the least likely to be in the data |
| Short forms are **not content-equivalent** to the long form | `A1` (Q12 "geen inhoudelijk equivalent van de Q47"); Q47 52.8% vs Q12 46.6% in one sample, `A4` p.24 | Comparing a garden's Q12 score to a national Q47 figure is a category error |
| Q12 subscales have 3–4 items; skill-level differences "verschilt niet of nauwelijks" | `A4` p.30; `A6` sheet 15 | Domain/skill profiles from the short form are not stable enough to attribute to an activity |
| Rasch/DIF problems in the HLS-EU family; one-factor fit weaker outside older adults | `S23`, `S24` (via `A` working file) | Dimensional claims are instrument-dependent |

**Verdict: measure-unsuitable.** Using HLS19-Q47/Q12, HLS-EU-Q16 or (by extension, on the same self-report logic) HLQ/AAHLS as a garden's primary outcome would measure the instrument and the respondent's position, not the garden. That is a finding about **operationalisation**, not about whether a garden does anything.

## 2. Claim (ii): what context-sensitive assessment actually exists

Retrieved this revision; each row states what it could establish, its burden, and its evidential status.

| Method | Source | What it could establish | Burden on participants | Status / caveat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Teach-back** ("can you show/tell me how you would do it?") | **`F9`** Vick et al. 2026, *J Gen Intern Med*, systematic review + meta-analysis, 18 RCTs, n = 1 985 | Whether a person can *reproduce* an explanation or a task — a performance check, not a self-rating | Very low: one conversation, seconds; no literacy demand; can be done in the activity | **Established:** no clear effect on **knowledge** acquisition ("no clear pattern … (very low COE)"). Self-efficacy SMD **2.40** (95% CI 0.37–4.44), 5 RCTs, n = 416, **very low** certainty; self-reported short-term **adherence** SMD **1.04** (0.45–1.64), 7 RCTs, n = 571, **low** certainty; "large confidence intervals … substantial heterogeneity" [D, abstract]. **It is a clinical-communication method; its use in a garden would be an adaptation, not an application** |
| **Performance-based, context-specific instruments** | **`F10`** Poureslami et al. 2023, *Respir Care* 68(5):638–648 (VAHLT) | Whether a person can perform disease-specific tasks — built *with* patients and professionals, disease-specific | High: 320 recruited across 6 speciality clinics, plus spirometry | **Exists, and is expensive.** Construct validity preliminary; **no significant association between tool scores and disease severity or quality of life** [D, abstract]. A garden version would be a research project, not a leaflet |
| **Short forms as process measures with stated limits** | `A4`, `A6`, `S23`, `S24`; HLS-EU-Q6 validation | Direction of travel on felt difficulty, if the same instrument is used throughout and the limits are stated | Low (6–12 items) but reading-based | **Usable as a process measure with explicit caveats; not usable as an outcome claim** |
| **Food-literacy instrument (SPFL)** | `A25` Poelman et al. 2018, 29 items, 8 domains | Self-perceived planning, managing, selecting, preparing, eating | Moderate (29 items) | Validated on **90.7% female, 59.1% highly educated** adults [D, `A25`] — unknown behaviour in the population of interest [I]. It measures **self-perceived** capability |
| **Self-efficacy scales** | `B12` (self-efficacy measured as an outcome of horticultural therapy, *g* = 0.52, **certainty very low**); `F9` (teach-back raises self-efficacy) | Confidence in a specific task | Low–moderate | A defensible *secondary* measure; it is the construct most responsive to relational teaching, and the least informative about whether anything was learned |
| **Qualitative interview / ethnographic observation** | `F5` (30 interviews, two countries, SDT-framed); `F8` (Veen: two years of participant observation in 7 Dutch gardens + interviews + questionnaires); `D6` (WUR MSc thesis, 16 interviews/17 people); `F7` (participatory work with residents) | **Meaning, mechanism, barriers, and what people say they do** — the only method retrieved that surfaces asking-for-help, shame, conflict and non-use | High in time, low in literacy demand; requires consent, transcription, and someone who can analyse | **Established as feasible** — done repeatedly in Dutch settings. Cannot establish effect size; `F5` and `B7` are the **same cohort**, so qualitative and quantitative agreement there is not independent corroboration |
| **Participatory / co-created evaluation** | `D22` Jagosh et al. (realist evaluation of 11 CBPR partnerships); `F7` | Whether the *partnership* changed, and for whom; ripple effects | High: co-governance, time, trust | Reciprocal learning is documented here and **only** here (`D22`); it is not documented for green-health programmes (`D` §6) |
| **Process / realist evaluation** | `F7` (realist review + participatory action research); `F9`/`B12` give the outcome side | **For whom, under what conditions** — the question the issue actually asks | Moderate | `D19` supplies the warning: CoP evaluations "lack rigour; thus, the true impact of CoPs on population health remains unknown" [D] |
| **Think-aloud / cognitive interviewing** | — | Whether a text or a task is understood in the way its author intended | Low–moderate | **[U] No source retrieved.** I do not describe the method or claim its properties. Recorded as a gap |

## 3. What a community could reasonably do without becoming a research site

This is the practical payload, marked as **inference assembled from the retrieved methods**, not quoted from any one source [I].

1. **One question, asked by a person, not a form.** Teach-back is the only retrieved method that is simultaneously low-burden, literacy-free and usable inside an activity — and its evidence is strongest for self-efficacy and short-term adherence, weakest for knowledge (`F9`). If a garden checks anything, "show me how you'd do this at home" is the cheapest defensible check, and it produces a conversation rather than a dataset.
2. **Count what the practice already produces.** Who came, how often, who came back, who stopped, how much was harvested, who asked for help. `B7`'s own dose finding — **half of the gardeners harvested nothing in a year** — came from data the garden itself would hold. Counting attendance and lapsed attendance is not a measurement regime; it is memory.
3. **Refuse the battery.** No HLS19, no SPFL, no pre/post questionnaire as a condition of taking part. The retrieved evidence gives three reasons: the instruments do not measure what the claim needs (§1); the burden falls on the people least able to carry it (`F2`'s Equity criterion; `D` §5.3 on data-collection burden in GSP); and imposed measurement is a **controlling context** in SDT terms, which predicts reduced intrinsic motivation (`F4`).
4. **If a study is wanted, let it be co-created and let it be someone else's burden.** The Dutch precedent exists (`F7`), the CBPR evidence on what it takes exists (`D22`), and the cost is documented: "co-design activities … proved more time consuming than expected" and in some cases "limited meaningful engagement of people with relevant lived experience" (`D9`).
5. **Say what the check cannot show.** A garden that counts attendance and asks one teach-back question can say "people came, people came back, people could show me the task". It cannot say "health literacy improved". The distance between those two sentences is the whole of §1.

## 4. Not established

- **[U]** Any think-aloud or cognitive-interview source; any application of these methods in a garden or food setting.
- **[U]** Any validated instrument measuring *interactive* or *critical* health literacy that is suitable for a community setting. The AAHLS exists but its subscale structure is contested (`S33` via `A`), and no retrieved study uses it in a garden.
- **[U]** Any Dutch instrument measuring learning or capability as an outcome of a green programme (`D` §10 item 3; unchanged by the Dutch search in `nl-literature.md`).
- **[U]** The measurement properties of `F7`'s instruments — the project record reports findings and products, not psychometrics; the underlying scientific publication was named but not retrieved.
- **[U]** What the JArDinS correction (BMC Public Health 21:15, 4 January 2021, doi 10.1186/s12889-020-10010-1) changed. The correction record was retrieved but the substantive change was not isolated in the retrieved text, and no claim here rests on a number the correction might have touched.
