## 1. COM-B / the Behaviour Change Wheel — the primary statement

**Source `F1`.** Michie S, van Stralen MM, West R. "The behaviour change wheel: A new method for characterising and designing behaviour change interventions." *Implementation Science* 6:42. Published 23 April 2011. doi 10.1186/1748-5908-6-42. Open access. Retrieved 2026-11-26T21:09:52Z, `text/html` (served from link.springer.com after a redirect carrying `?error=cookies_not_supported`), sha256 `10c5623e031848f6…`. **Depth: full text, first 30 000 of 57 204 extracted characters, read directly** (abstract, introduction, methods, COM-B definition, framework review, reliability testing). The latter part of the discussion was truncated and is not relied on.

### 1.1 What the model is

The paper searched 1 267 articles and 19 existing frameworks, found that "None of the frameworks reviewed covered the full range of intervention functions or policies, and only a minority met the criteria of coherence or linkage to a model of behaviour", and built a framework whose hub is a three-condition "behaviour system":

> "At the centre of a proposed new framework is a 'behaviour system' involving three essential conditions: **capability, opportunity, and motivation** (what we term the 'COM-B system')." [D, F1, abstract]

Definitions, verbatim [D, F1, §"Develop a new framework" / behaviour system]:

> "Capability is defined as the individual's psychological and physical capacity to engage in the activity concerned. It includes having the necessary knowledge and skills."
> "Motivation is defined as all those brain processes that energize and direct behaviour, not just goals and conscious decision-making. It includes habitual processes, emotional responding, as well as analytical decision-making."
> "Opportunity is defined as all the factors that lie outside the individual that make the behaviour possible or prompt it."

Subdivisions: physical vs psychological capability; physical vs social opportunity; reflective vs automatic motivation [D, F1].

### 1.2 The three properties that matter for this issue

1. **COM-B is not a deficit model, and the authors say so in as many words.** [D, F1]
   > "This system places no priority on an individual, group, or environmental perspective — intra-psychic and external factors all have equal status in controlling behaviour. However, for a given behaviour in a given context it provides a way of identifying how far changing particular components or combinations of components could effect the required transformation. For example, with one behavioural target the only barrier might be capability, while for another it may be enough to provide or restrict opportunities…"

   **[I]** This is the strongest single support in the retrieved record for the correction this revision makes: on COM-B, "the garden cannot build health literacy" is not a finding about capability at all — it is a claim about one of three conditions, and it is false as a general statement because opportunity is a co-equal condition. A garden is, in COM-B terms, primarily an **opportunity** intervention (a physical and social setting that makes a behaviour possible and prompts it), and secondarily a capability one.

2. **The framework is a design and description tool, not a theory of effectiveness.** [D, F1]
   > "Interventions and policies to change behaviour can be usefully characterised by means of a BCW comprising: a 'behaviour system' at the hub, encircled by intervention functions and then by policy categories. **Research is needed to establish how far the BCW can lead to more efficient design of effective interventions.**"

   **[I]** So a note may use COM-B to *audit* what a garden does and does not supply, and may not use it to predict that a garden works.

3. **Capability is explicitly knowledge *and* skills, and skill is not literacy.** [D, F1] The framework's "capability" is psychological and physical capacity; the intervention function that maps to it is "training … increasing psychological or physical skills", distinguished in the paper from "education … imparting knowledge and developing understanding" [D, F1]. **[I]** For a garden, this split is the useful part: a garden plausibly supplies training (doing a task with someone who knows), not education (imparting a model of health information).

### 1.3 The nearest retrieved application to a green setting

**Source `F2`.** Allison AL, Frost R, Murtagh N. "Promoting planting in front gardens: a systematic approach to intervention development." *UCL Open: Environment* 6:e3147 (24 May 2024). doi 10.14324/111.444/ucloe.3147. PMC11152043. Retrieved 2026-11-26T21:10:09Z, `text/html`, sha256 `fb8b67df8c8e1984…`. **Depth: full text, first 25 000 of 108 577 characters** — abstract, introduction, framework description, methods; the results/recommendation detail beyond that was not read.

What it establishes [D]:

- The BCW **has been applied to gardening**, but to *front gardening by householders*, and the authors state the novelty explicitly: "To the best of our knowledge, it has had no application within the context of promoting gardening."
- It is a **design** study, not an evaluation: "The next steps include evaluating the proposed interventions in practice." Twelve intervention recommendations were produced; none was tested.
- The stakeholder preference is informative for this issue: "Stakeholder feedback revealed a preference for **educational and supportive (social and practical) strategies** (e.g., community gardening workshops, front gardening 'starter kits') over persuasive and motivational approaches (e.g., social marketing, motivational letters from the council to householders)."
- The behavioural system around gardening is multi-actor: "Householders' front gardening behaviour is complex and influenced by the behaviour of many other community actors. It also needs to be understood as a step in a continuum of other behaviours (e.g., clearing land, gardening, waste disposal)."
- It applies the APEASE criteria, including **Equity** ("a consideration of whether the intervention reinforces disparities between different sectors of society") [D].

**[I]** Two consequences. (a) The one retrieved COM-B application to gardening is about *getting people to garden*, not about what gardening does to them — the direction of the behaviour is opposite to this issue's. (b) Even there, the intervention functions that stakeholders preferred are the ones a community garden already provides informally (workshops, starter kits, social support) — which is a hypothesis worth stating, not a finding.

### 1.4 The critique, and why it is not a dismissal

**Source `F3`.** Ogden J. "Celebrating variability and a call to limit systematisation: the example of the Behaviour Change Technique Taxonomy and the Behaviour Change Wheel." *Health Psychology Review* 10(3):245–250 (Sept 2016). doi 10.1080/17437199.2016.1190291. Retrieved 2026-11-26T21:10:36Z via the Europe PMC REST API, `resultType=core`, sha256 `bfcd7554069212b2…`. **Depth: abstract only.** Six commentaries in the same issue are indexed (PMIDs 27118520, 27180642, 27185472, 27189788, 27216695, 27248444) and were not retrieved.

> "This paper argues that although the call for better descriptions of what is done is useful for clarity and replication, this systematisation may be neither feasible nor desirable. In particular, it is suggested that the gaps inherent in the translational process from coding a protocol to behaviour will limit the effectiveness of reducing patient variability, that theory variability is necessary for the health and well-being of a discipline and that practice variability is central to the professional status of our practitioners. It is therefore argued that we should celebrate rather than remove this variability…" [D, F3, abstract]

**[I]** For this issue the critique cuts two ways and both ways are honest. It disciplines *this note*: a COM-B audit of a garden performed at a desk, by someone who has never seen the garden run, is exactly the "coding a protocol" move Ogden says loses the behaviour. And it disciplines the *health partners*: a garden that resists being systematised into intervention functions is not being uncooperative; it is protecting the variability that makes it a practice.

## 2. Self-determination theory — the primary statement

**Source `F4`.** Ryan RM & Deci EL. "Self-Determination Theory and the Facilitation of Intrinsic Motivation, Social Development, and Well-Being." *American Psychologist* 55(1):68–78, January 2000. doi 10.1037/0003-066X.55.1.68. Retrieved 2026-11-26T21:10:22Z from the authors' own document repository (selfdeterminationtheory.org), `application/pdf`, sha256 `e14779081a999b49…`. **Depth: full text, first 30 000 of 79 248 extracted characters** — abstract, CET, the motivation continuum figure, the start of organismic integration theory. The health-care/education applications at the end were truncated and are not relied on.

Core statements [D, F4]:

> "The findings have led to the postulate of three innate psychological needs — **competence, autonomy, and relatedness** — which when satisfied yield enhanced self-motivation and mental health and when thwarted lead to diminished motivation and well-being."

> "Cognitive evaluation theory (CET) was presented by Deci and Ryan (1985) as a subtheory within SDT … framed in terms of social and environmental factors that facilitate versus undermine intrinsic motivation … In other words, it will flourish if circumstances permit."

> "…research revealed that not only tangible rewards but also **threats, deadlines, directives, pressured evaluations, and imposed goals diminish intrinsic motivation** because, like tangible rewards, they conduce toward an external perceived locus of causality. In contrast, choice, acknowledgment of feelings, and opportunities for self-direction were found to enhance intrinsic motivation…"

> "…people will be **intrinsically motivated only for activities that hold intrinsic interest for them**, activities that have the appeal of novelty, challenge, or aesthetic value. For activities that do not hold such appeal, the principles of CET do not apply, because the activities will not be experienced as intrinsically motivated to begin with."

**[I] This is the most directly usable finding in the whole revision for the "intrinsic value" instruction.** SDT predicts that attaching an imposed health goal, a measurement regime or a behavioural target to a garden is a *controlling* context in the technical sense, and that controlling contexts diminish intrinsic motivation and persistence. The theory therefore supports the boundary the PI asked to preserve — the garden must not become a compulsory behavioural intervention — *on motivational grounds*, not merely on ethical ones. It is a prediction, not a measured effect in a garden, and it is falsifiable (see §4).

## 3. The empirical application to community gardening

**Source `F5`.** Sachs A, Tharrey M, Darmon N, Alaimo K, Boshara A, Beavers A, Litt J. "'To me, it's just natural to be in the garden': A multi-site investigation of new community gardener motivation using Self-Determination Theory." *Wellbeing, Space and Society* 3:100088 (2022). doi 10.1016/j.wss.2022.100088. Retrieved 2026-11-26T21:10:41Z from the HAL-INRAE repository (author-deposited submitted version), `application/pdf`, sha256 `c7a39044e7863168…`. **Depth: full text, first 40 000 of 76 719 characters** — abstract, methods, results on competence, autonomy and relatedness. The discussion/limits section was truncated.

**Access note.** The publisher's own copy at `sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666558122000173` returned **403 with a Cloudflare challenge** (`cf-ray a60be0c9084f23e3-AMS`). The DOI resolves to the same host. The route that worked was Unpaywall (`api.unpaywall.org/v2/10.1016/j.wss.2022.100088`) → a HAL repository copy. **The article is CC BY-NC-ND gold open access; no control was bypassed.** The version read is the **submitted** version, not the publisher's typeset version — page numbers therefore cannot be cited.

What it found [D, F5]:

- Design: **30 new community gardeners** (15 Montpellier, 15 Denver), semi-structured interviews after their first year, thematically coded inductively and then organised deductively under SDT's three needs.
- **Relatedness carried the data at both sites:** "Relatedness as a motivational feature carried through the interview data at both sites. Participants' success or failure to relate to other gardeners was a major influence for how autonomous and competent as a community gardener they expressed feeling" [D, abstract].
- Competence **developed through practice and through other people:** "Participants in both countries expressed feeling competent in their gardening skills after engaging regularly and habitually with gardening… Competence frequently resulted from feeling related to others" [D]. Instruction and workshops were the mechanism named: "When instruction was readily available via engaged leaders and instructional classes and events, new community gardeners described feelings of relatedness and enjoyment."
- **Asking for help is the friction point, in this literature too:** "Others also described feeling shy and hesitant to ask for help. However, those who could draw on relatedness with others were more likely to share that CG was enjoyable and worthy of their time" [D]. This is an independent echo of the Dutch 4-in-10 finding (`E8`) — with the same caveat, that it is a qualitative observation, not a measurement.
- The authors' own conclusion is a *reach* claim, not an effect claim: "With the presence of ongoing, friendly support from others, more individuals may adhere to this socially connecive, nature-based practice" [D, abstract — "connecive" as printed].

**A critical point about the witness count.** This paper is **nested in the same two studies it draws participants from**: the Montpellier interviews come from the JArDinS cohort (`B7`), the Denver interviews from the Litt et al. randomised trial (291 participants, wait-list control) [D, F5, §2.1.1–2.1.2]. **It is therefore the same research programme as the null quantitative result, not a second, independent witness.** Where the qualitative material reports a participant saying "we do our shopping differently, and we really eat differently" [D, F5, §3.2] and the quantitative paper reports no change in household food supply at 12 months (`B7`), the reader is looking at a **disagreement inside one study family** — and the honest resolution is that self-reported change and measured change diverge, which is precisely why the report does not treat either as decisive on its own.

## 4. What each framework predicts for a garden, what it does not, and what would test it

Kept apart deliberately: **theory (predictions)** vs **empirical application (findings)**.

| Framework | What it predicts for a shared green practice | Status of that prediction | What it does **not** predict | What a test in Eindhoven would require |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **COM-B** | The garden is primarily an **opportunity** intervention (physical: a place, tools, a task; social: other people, permission, norms) and only secondarily a capability one (training, not education). Barriers are likely to sit in opportunity, not motivation. | Theory [D, F1]. No retrieved application to community gardening as a health-literacy intervention [U]; the one gardening application is about promoting gardening, not about outcomes [D, F2]. | That any capability gained transfers; that motivation is the binding constraint; that a garden changes behaviour outside itself. | A COM-B audit of one garden's actual offer (what is available, to whom, when, with whom), performed **with** the garden rather than at a desk; then a test of whether removing an opportunity barrier (opening hours, a person present, no registration) changes who comes. |
| **SDT** | Relatedness is the load-bearing need in a garden; competence develops through doing and through instruction by someone who knows; autonomy is protected by keeping the health goal off the invitation. Imposed goals, directives and measurement are controlling and predictably diminish intrinsic motivation. | Theory [D, F4]; one qualitative application consistent with it [D, F5] which is **nested in the same cohort as the null trial** — one witness family. | That need satisfaction produces health-literacy gains, behaviour change, or persistence after the site closes. Nothing in SDT specifies *what* is learned, only *why* someone keeps doing it. | A measure of the three needs in a garden population with a comparator (the Basic Psychological Needs scales would be the instrument; **not retrieved here** [U]); plus a follow-up after exit, which no retrieved study has (`B` §4.3). |
| **Both** | That the *organisational form* of the practice — who supervises, whether it is weekly and routine, whether a task is shared — is a condition of any learning effect. | Prediction [I] from F1+F4; consistent with the JArDinS authors' own reading that "multicomponent programs are more likely to modify health outcomes" [D, B7 full text] and with Veen's finding that the garden's organisational type determines whether produce reaches the plate [D, F8]. | Which form, at what dose, for which population. | An organisational-form comparison — volunteer commons vs staffed/structured programme, same outcomes, same period — which the retrieved literature explicitly lacks (`B` §12 item 3). |

### Where the SDT↔construct mapping is informative, and where it is only analogical

**Informative** [I, from D]:

- **Competence ↔ self-efficacy ↔ capability.** SDT's competence is a *need* (a felt condition); self-efficacy is a *belief about a specific task*; COM-B's capability is a *capacity*. They are not the same construct, and the difference is measurable: teaching-back a task raises self-efficacy (SMD 2.40, very low certainty, `F9`) without reliably raising knowledge. A garden plausibly moves the first and the third and leaves the second untouched.
- **Autonomy ↔ the intrinsic-value boundary.** SDT supplies the mechanism by which a health framing could *harm*: controlling contexts undermine intrinsic motivation. That is the theory behind the report's refusal to make participation conditional.
- **Relatedness ↔ interactive health literacy.** Nutbeam's interactive level requires social skills — asking, negotiating, discussing with others. SDT's relatedness is the motivational precondition for exercising them. This is the closest thing to a *mechanism* linking a garden to a health-literacy-adjacent outcome, and it is the one the report now treats as a hypothesis (H5).

**Analogical only** [I]:

- Reading "autonomy" as "self-directed behaviour change" collapses a motivational construct into a behavioural outcome. SDT's autonomy is about *why* someone acts, not about *what* they do when nobody is watching. The issue's phrase "self-directed changes" is closer to self-regulation/internalisation, which SDT treats as a separate continuum (external → introjected → identified → integrated regulation) [D, F4] — and which no retrieved garden study measures.
- Reading "relatedness" as "social support" and then as "social prescribing" imports an institutional referral model the theory says nothing about.

## 5. What I could not establish

1. **[U]** Any application of COM-B or the BCW to a **community garden, allotment, urban farm or food forest as a health-literacy or health intervention**. The one retrieved application is to front gardening *promotion* (`F2`). The v1 claim that "no line retrieved the primary sources for either [framework]" is now resolved for the primary sources and remains true for this specific application.
2. **[U]** Any retrieval of the six commentaries on Ogden (`F3`), or of Ogden's own reply. The critique is recorded at abstract level.
3. **[U]** Any retrieved measurement of SDT's three needs in a gardening population. `F5` uses SDT as an analytic frame on interviews, not as a measured construct.
4. **[U]** Any retrieved test of the SDT prediction that a health goal imposed on a garden reduces participation. It is a prediction with a plausible design (compare an open-entry and a health-framed entry to the same activity) and no retrieved instance.
5. **[U]** Bandura's primary statement of self-efficacy. Self-efficacy is discussed here as (a) an outcome measured in horticultural therapy (`B12`, *g* = 0.52, very low certainty) and (b) an outcome of teach-back (`F9`). **No claim is made about the construct's original formulation.**
