# Voedselbos Eindhoven — evidence brief, network map, experiments, open questions

Desk research, 26 November 2026. Public sources only; no stakeholder contact; no implementation. Marks: **[D]** documented in a retrieved source, **[C]** claimed by one source, **[I]** inference, **[U]** not established, **[P]** provided by the commissioner (direct account, not public documentation). Workings and URLs in the notes listed in the appendix.

## Executive summary

1. **The closure is documented; the sale is a direct account.** On 28 July 2026 the site closed "met onmiddellijke ingang" after more than twelve years — a planned wind-down that became abrupt. Private ground at Elburglaan 8, 0.7 ha, edge of De Wielewaal. The commissioner reports that the landowner sold the land **[P]**; the buyer, date and terms are **[U]**, and the public record does not name the owner or the reason.
2. **What ended was an operator, not a membership.** No legal entity exists in the record. Programme, public face, products and coordination ran through one person's practice; the audience survives as a mailing list, the ~30 volunteers' survival is **[U]**.
3. **The municipal asymmetry is a portfolio difference, not a refusal.** Money follows land the city owns: €9.8 m Lichtbos and €600 k a year for 145 ha around De Wielewaal **[D]**, against no instrument at all for a twelve-year food forest on private land beside it **[D/I]**. Both documented municipal green instruments are Didam single-candidate awards to capitalised professional partners; one admits the city is "onvoldoende geëquipeerd om grote aantallen vrijwilligers te bereiken en te begeleiden" **[D]**. The *Beleidsregel groen en water 2026* writes the gate down: Article 10 refuses community management of "middel of hoog" green.
4. **The network is thin, and "2.0" is one word.** The food forest was never in Trefpunt Groen's *achterban*; its only documented edges are co-presence at a 2024 market and a Supportpunt-built website **[D]**. De Wielewaal is adjacency only, the TAC garden unverified, Bij Albrecht unresolved. Publicly, 2.0 is a workshop invitation and a place "als een kruidentuin" **[D]** — *not a food forest* is settled, *place or product* is open.
5. **Structure is necessary and insufficient; four uncertainties are critical; three experiments are cheap.** Ecodorp Boekel owns its ground and funds food work from rent on a thirty-year loan, and still scores 1/5 on backup skills and collective memory after fifteen years **[D/SA]**. Critical: written tenure, a named second keeper, herb garden as place or product, what volunteers do when asked concretely. The experiments need no software, entity or budget line.

## 1. The brief

### 1.1 Question and focus

The commission asks how existing communities, green spaces and local organisations in Eindhoven can form a mutually supportive ecosystem contributing to human and ecological health through shared care. Research took Voedselbos Eindhoven — the one named starting point that has publicly closed — as entry point, treating De Wielewaal as one adjacent node and a municipal-estate model, not the organising centre.

The question assumes an existing base to connect. The record supports a corrected version: the base exists as *audiences, sites and one-off contacts*, not as *member organisations able to commit each other*. Of the named starting points, one closed without a legal successor, one is a municipal project with no operator, one a municipal estate run by a national NGO, two are staffed organisations with payroll and reporting duties **[D]**. Only Trefpunt Groen holds a city-wide mandate, and it is advisory **[D]**.

### 1.2 Method and evidence standard

Public sources only: municipal publications and council information, gemeenteblad announcements, open data, initiative websites, regional and national press, and the sibling evidence base at `../eindhoven-evidence-base`, whose claims were used as leads, not findings. No stakeholder contact; no willingness to collaborate assumed. Access boundaries are recorded, not worked around: the council questions-half-hour on the closure and the municipal Lichtbos page returned Cloudflare 403 and remain unread.

Three things are held apart that are usually collapsed: what a source says a party *does* (documented), what it says it *intends* (claimed), and what the pattern *suggests* (inference). Self-descriptions of award-winning initiatives and of the municipality were treated identically — as claims tested against instruments and dates.

### 1.3 What the research found

**Closure and continuity.** Tenure, not volunteer energy, was the fail point: a twelve-year system was lost because the initiative owned nothing **[D/I]**. Documented drivers: insecure private land and operator concentration; the commissioner reports the landowner sold the land **[P]**, though public sources do not name the buyer, date or terms. Not supported: municipal policy change, funding cuts, volunteer collapse (a full volunteer group met in November 2025) **[D]**. De Wielewaal's professionalisation is **context, not cause** **[I]**.

**Network and operating models.** Four archetypes, not one network: a volunteer commons on borrowed land; professionalised care enterprises with foundation governance; public estates with institutional operators; an advisory intermediary. They are not peers. Shared care strains on tenure, liability (visiting volunteers are a different risk class from care participants), rhythm, and mandate — Trefpunt represents an *achterban*, not these initiatives **[D/I]**.

**Participation and exhaustion.** Participation has a price, and it is mostly administrative **[D]**. Organiser burnout is a documented cause of community-garden decline, with causes — constant engagement, task switching, funding anxiety, unclear roles — that map onto the local material **[D, second-hand]**. A one-season grant produces a one-season garden **[I]**. The *network* outlived the *place*; the place-bound community did not survive as a body.

**The municipal interface.** Enabler of a narrow kind (owner-only greening subsidy, €2,500 cap, ten-year maintenance duty), gatekeeper at the top (Article 10), absent partner where it matters: no instrument for a food forest on private land exists beside a nine-figure programme for the municipal estate **[D]**. The greening-wishes register — 124 records, 56 still wishes, 14 with a planned year — shows a feedback loop the city itself calls weak **[D]**.

**Complementarity and reciprocity.** Almost nothing reciprocal is documented: co-presence, not exchange **[D]**. Reciprocity's preconditions — repeated interaction, visible contribution, an exit option, someone bearing coordination — are absent, and asymmetries are large: 24 staffed hectares against 0.7 volunteer hectares **[D/I]**. Hence the thin, mirrored experiments in §3.

**Ecological limits.** No municipal carrying-capacity standard exists, and four producers give four different green totals **[D]**. Limits must be constructed site by site with the site's manager, not asserted. The tension is distributional: green-poor neighbourhoods may need access most while sensitive sites absorb it least **[I]**.

**Visibility and 2.0.** Visibility is a function with a cost, not an organising purpose **[I]**. Three documented deficits: absent from the 2026 national open-day map, absent from the city's own green network, an audience held personally rather than collectively **[D]**. Visibility cannot fix tenure or operator concentration, and a successor that is again one person's offering raises the stakes of that concentration **[I]**.

**The comparator.** Boekel's transferable assets are structural: community-controlled ground, a property vehicle with an external member, food work cross-subsidised by rent, labour attached to membership, hosting as a discipline. Non-transferable: residence, rural land economics, bankability, founder capacity, novelty **[D/I]**.

### 1.4 Against the three pillars

**Pillar 1, De Wielewaal as motor:** the capital and the public opening are real — 145 ha, €9.8 m, €600 k a year, a centre with ~100 volunteers and 4 staff **[D]** — but it motors a municipal portfolio, not a community network. No operating relation to the food forest exists (adjacency only), "motor" names no driver, and the community is a passenger unless invited **[D/U/I]**. Treat the pillar as a hypothesis, not a premise.

**Pillar 2, a city-wide ecosystem:** a connective layer exists — Trefpunt Groen's Groen Cafés and network map, Supportpunt's back office — but it is advisory, and the closed node was never in it **[D]**. What is available now is thin, bounded, mirrored exchange, not shared infrastructure.

**Pillar 3, nature closer to everyday life:** the strongest documented base — Dunantpark's residents' food forest, Groen Cafés, the English-language International Green Café, 124 greening wishes **[D]** — with one unresolved conflict: access versus protection is a governance choice the city has not made, and no local carrying-capacity evidence exists **[D/U]**.

**Community as foundation:** the community outlived the place as an *audience* and a *practice*, but not as a *body* — no entity, no channel, no second keeper. Taken literally, the premise demands exactly what this case lacked **[D/I]**.

## 3. Three experiments

Design only; nobody has been asked; all consent-gated. Hypotheses, stop rules and estimates in full: [`research/experiments/note.md`](experiments/note.md).

| | **A — Half-day swap** | **B — "Twaalf jaar op één A4"** | **C — Shared hosting** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Parties** | Wasven × the Voedselbos community | Supportpunt × Trefpunt Groen × the community | International Green Café × Wasven; room at LAB-1 |
| **Hypothesis** | Labour transfers only if a host absorbs strangers into an existing routine without creating a supervision task | A closure can be converted into transferable knowledge at near-zero cost, and others use a checklist they did not commission | Co-hosting distributes cost rather than multiplying it; each party gives under four hours |
| **Success signals** | ≥5 volunteers each way; host asks for a second date unprompted; ≤1 coordination day per party | ≥1 initiative adopts a checklist item within six months (a lease, a second person, a status page); linked by another body | All three want a second evening; ≤4 hours prep each; ≥30% of attendees new to all three |
| **Stop rules** | Induction >45 min per person; coordinator >half a day; a task must be invented; a safety question unanswered | Community declines; authorship contested; drift from "what we learned" to "what we achieved" | Prep exceeds a day for any party; pricing excludes neighbours; only the already-converted attend; content promotes one organisation |
| **Rough cost** | ~12 volunteer hours, €0–50, no software | ~8 h Supportpunt, ~6 h author, ~4 h Trefpunt, €0–150 | Room hire (real, **[U]**, perhaps €150–400, possibly in kind), ~4 h per party, ~€50 |
| **Consent gate** | Group's existence **[U]** — must run as Wasven × named individuals; no photos, no names, no ecologically managed zones | Two consents: to be a case, and to be named. If refused, another case runs | Each party's own communications decision; pair an English-first event with a Dutch walk; no filming without consent |

**Rejected:** joint funding bid; umbrella foundation (the municipality is already designing it); shared depots (site, keyholder, liability); care-farm volunteer exchange (safeguarding red line); taking over municipal green under the *uitdaagrecht* (months, adversarial, refusal likely); and Voedselbos × Dunantpark, the obvious pair, because its organisers, form and tenure are **[U]**. Software: a roster and a text thread suffice; the only defensible AI use is transcription with a human checking names **[I]**.

## 4. Open questions for Maartje and stakeholders

**Four decision-critical uncertainties** ([`research/decisions/note.md`](decisions/note.md)):

1. **Written tenure — where, what term, who holds it?** Resolve with a written request under the Beleidsregel groen en water 2026, which covers requests to manage, buy or lease municipal green, plus what the June 2026 wethouder conversation produced. *Enough:* a written answer naming a parcel and a term, or a written no. *Flips if:* the parcel is "middel of hoog" — then Article 10 points to partnership with a qualified manager.
2. **A named second keeper, accountable for twelve months?** Ask two specific people, with a written role and a stated time cost. *Enough:* two names and a fallback, or an honest no. *Flips if:* nobody says yes — do not incorporate; run a hosted programme.
3. **Is the herb garden a place, a product line, or a metaphor?** One page: in twelve months, what exists that does not exist today. *Enough:* a place (or none), a rhythm, a price, a person. *Flips if:* a product line — tenure stops being critical and a host venue suffices.
4. **What do volunteers do when asked concretely?** A dated invitation, a stated time cost, an easy exit; count who returns twice — not a survey. *Enough:* two consecutive gatherings with returning faces. *Flips if:* under ~5 return — the successor is a practice, not a commons.

**Order.** Ask 3 before 1 (it may remove the need for a site); 2 before any formalisation or funder conversation; 4 before designing anything that assumes a group. Do not ask the municipality for a location before there is a legal person and a keeper — it will be filed and answered with "meedenken". Do not ask funders before a named object and someone to account for it. Do not ask peers for solidarity before correcting the claim that Eindhoven has no food forest — one exists in het Henri Dunantpark **[D/disagree]**.

**Conversation guide.** (1) In twelve months, what exists that does not exist today: a place, a programme, or a product? (2) If you were ill for three months, who would be answerable? (3) What did the June conversation with the wethouder actually produce? (4) Has anyone asked, in writing, for a specific parcel — and do you know the deadline? (5) What are you willing to stop doing to make room? (6) **What would make you decide to stop entirely?** (7) Which invitation is concrete enough to visit? (8) What should the volunteers decide, and what must stay yours? Listen for whether "kruidentuin" comes with a place or a product; whether the mailing list is offered as a commons or kept as an asset; whether a second name appears unprompted.

**What research cannot decide.** Whether to continue at all; appetite for risk, income and visibility; whether a parcel will be granted; whether volunteers will follow; whether a herb garden can carry a livelihood.

## 5. Not established, and what would change the picture

**Not established:** the buyer, date, price and terms of the sale (the fact of sale is a direct account from the commissioner **[P]**, not a public document); whether any legal entity existed; whether the ~30 volunteers persist; what the 26 November workshop produced; whether any invitation is a site offer; whether the herb garden has a site; whether the International Green Café is active; whether Dunantpark has an agreement; TAC's garden, access rules and manager; LAB-1's room cost; Bij Albrecht's role; whether the *uitdaagrecht* has been used since 30 June 2026; and the council's position, whose file is blocked and unread.

**What would change the picture most, in order:** a written municipal answer allocating or refusing a parcel with a term; a second named keeper; a public statement that the herb garden is or is not a site; a KVK or ANBI registration after the November workshop; a 2026 Trefpunt Groen report showing a food-forest node entering its *achterban*; evidence that the volunteers still meet; a second keeper at the Dunantpark initiative, which would make it the movement's actual Eindhoven node and the obvious partner.
