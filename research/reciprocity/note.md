# What makes reciprocity between initiatives durable rather than extractive?

**Issue:** [#5](https://github.com/leonvanbokhorst/voedselbos/issues/5) · **Status:** provisional overall; no claim here is admitted, and admission is the reviewer's act under the repository's provenance practice.
**Research date:** 26 November 2026. Desk research; public sources only; no stakeholder contact; no fieldwork; no implementation. All retrievals are mine unless marked "inherited" (see [`sources.md`](sources.md) §2).
**Marks:** **[D]** documented in a retrieved source · **[C]** claimed by one actor or self-description · **[I]** inference of this note · **[U]** not established in the sources searched · **[P]** commissioner-provided.

**Lens — who bears the coordination cost.** This note reads every reciprocal arrangement as a cost-allocation question before it reads it as a goodwill question. Reciprocity that looks free is usually paid for by someone: in paid hours, in a host's supervision time, in a funder's programme budget, or in the unpaid evening of the person who answers the e-mail. Where a note would normally ask "is there trust?", this one asks "who is paying for the trust to be maintained, and what happens when they stop?" §5 answers that question with numbers wherever the record gives them.

---

## 1. The answer in short

**1. Almost nothing reciprocal is documented between Eindhoven's green *initiatives* as organisations.** The city's documented exchange sits at three other levels: **vertical** (individual ↔ a host institution that pays for supervision — Wasven, De Vergeten Tuin, De Buurtwerf) **[D]**; **material and horizontal but not organisational** (seed and plant exchange between gardeners, hosted by a venue or a hobby association) **[D]**; and **brokered** (a third party with its own interest pays the coordination bill — the municipality and Trefpunt Groen, a developer and Struikroven, a funder and 23 gardens) **[D]**. What the commission's question calls "mutual support between initiatives" is, in the retrieved record, mostly *vertical* or *brokered*, and calling it mutual obscures who pays.

**2. The one natural experiment says peer exchange does not happen by itself, even when it is funded.** A Dutch funder put 23–27 care-and-neighbourhood gardens into one programme for three years with a shared closed Facebook group, annual knowledge days and paid project leaders. The evaluators' verdict is unambiguous: *"Van een lerende gemeenschap is gedurende de drie jaar van Groen Verbindt alleen sprake geweest op de kennis- en inspiratiedagen. Verder hebben de projecten elkaar bijna niet opgezocht"* — a learning community existed only on the days the funder convened it; beyond that the projects hardly sought each other out **[D, R2]**. A funder can buy an occasion. It did not buy reciprocity.

**3. The reciprocity that exists is paid for, and the payer is identifiable in every durable case.** Trefpunt Groen spends **20% of its paid hours** on the relational work itself (15% *afstemming achterban* + 5% *netwerk intensiveren*) out of a **€232,000** turnover **[D, R1]**. Wasven supervises **eleven volunteer working groups** through teamleiders **[D, R5]**. Each Groen Verbindt garden ran on **three to four paid staff, about twelve paid hours a week**, and when the programme ended "in veel gevallen komen er minder betaalde uren beschikbaar voor de coördinatie" **[D, R2]**. Struikroven's free plants to residents are paid for by the developer or corporation that hires it **[D, R6]**. The seed library's cool dry cellar belongs to an arts venue, not to a garden **[D, R8]**. **No retrieved case of sustained exchange in Eindhoven has no payer.** That is the note's central empirical claim.

**4. The sharpest measured local fact is a giving/asking asymmetry, and it is the mechanism by which extraction happens without a villain.** In the same annual survey, year after year: **9 in 10** volunteers report meeting new people in the neighbourhood, **5 in 10** that they stand ready for others more, and **4 in 10** that they dare ask more people for help **[D, R2]**. The evaluators name the asymmetry themselves: *"Om hulp vragen is altijd moeilijker dan hulp bieden, zeker voor vrijwilligers"* **[D, R2]**. A network built on people's willingness to *give* will accumulate offers and never accumulate **claims**. The party that can ask — because it has a protocol, a legal person, a letterhead — will do the asking; the party that cannot ask will do the giving. Nobody has to intend extraction for it to occur.

**5. Asymmetry is not the same as extraction, and the evidence supports two tests rather than one verdict.** **(a) Recording:** is the weaker party's contribution written down somewhere it could point to? The advisory protocol records the *advice* and makes tree advice binding, and describes the achterban's input as "een vitale bijdrage aan de kwaliteit van het advies" **[D, R1]** — the contribution is named, though never attributed per contributor. **(b) Withholding:** can the weaker party stop contributing without losing its own ground? An advisory *achterban* can walk away from an intermediary; a volunteer food forest with no entity and no land could not walk away from a landowner, and when the land went, everything went **[D/I, R11, R7]**. **Dependence, not size, is the extraction risk** — which is why the 24-hectare partner is not automatically the extractor.

**6. What initiatives refuse to share is almost undocumented, and the two documented refusals are institutional rather than interpersonal.** The city refuses community management of green of "groenwaarde middel of hoog" **[D, R16]**. The repository's own experiment design refuses to move care participants between organisations' care regimes on safeguarding grounds **[D/I, R14]**. The one Eindhoven motion that would have created shared **(tuin)gereedschap** lending was **withdrawn on an alderman's commitment**, with no published outcome **[D, R10]**. No documented instance was found of one green initiative declining another's request **[U]** — and §6 explains why that absence must not be read as harmony.

**7. Four design conditions separate durable from extractive exchange in this evidence, and three more are plausible but untested here.** Durable: **a named payer of the coordination load**, **a recurring bounded slot**, **an exit that costs nothing**, and **a record of contribution rather than of offers**. Plausible, not established locally: a divisible object or a bounded task, rotation with redundancy, and a conflict route. **Formalisation is not one of the conditions** — the informal cases survive and the funded ones lose their coordination when the funding stops **[D, R2, R19]**.

**What the typology shows, stated as an inference [I] and not as a finding.** The rows that work without trust (1, 13) exchange a *divisible, storable, low-value* object or an *administered* risk. The rows that require trust and are not documented (2, 3, 6, 7) exchange *labour, obligation or money between organisations*. The rows that are documented but do not produce exchange (4, 5, 10) exchange *presence*. **The evidence is consistent with a simple rule: the harder the thing is to divide, store and return, the more the arrangement needs a payer and a record — and Eindhoven has payers and records for material and for vertical care, but not for labour between organisations.** That rule is a hypothesis the evidence fits; it is not tested against a case that breaks it.

## 4. Where the honest answer is narrower than the question

The commission asks what forms of mutual support **already exist between** the initiatives. Three findings force a narrower answer.

**4.1 The strongest documented exchange is inside one organisation, not between two.** Wasven's volunteer page is the richest reciprocity document retrieved in this research: eleven working groups, expert guidance, fixed rhythms for each group, a named teamleider per team, and — in a volunteer's own words — a rota that absorbs absence:

> "Als iemand een keer niet kan, staat er gelijk een ander klaar om jouw dienst over te nemen. Hierdoor is er ruimte om je eigen tijd in te delen. Een van ons rijdt bijvoorbeeld één keer in de 14 dagen zijn rit en een ander rijdt wel 5 keer per week." **[D, R5]**

That is durable reciprocity, it is *generalised* rather than balanced (one driver at 1×/fortnight, another at 5×/week), and it is **vertical**: individuals give to an institution that allocates, remembers and substitutes. It is not a model for exchange *between* initiatives; it is a model for what an initiative has to be able to do before it can host anyone else's people.

**4.2 The horizontal exchange that exists is horticultural and hosted, not organisational.** The seed library is the clearest reciprocity *design* in the city, and it is worth reading closely because of how deliberately it lowers the trust requirement:

> "Kom langs om zaden en plantjes te ruilen, lenen en/of te doneren. Breng je eigen zaden, plantjes of stekjes mee of als je die niet hebt, **iets anders wat je zou willen ruilen**." **[D, R8]**

Three design choices in one sentence: **lending with return** (the seeds come back), **swapping** (equivalence is not required — anything exchangeable will do), and **donating** (no return at all). Add a basement that keeps seed cool and dry, a few opening days a year, and a workshop or film on those days, and the arrangement needs neither trust nor a relationship: the *store* holds the obligation, not the people. Its own stated purpose is to connect "de tuinen en tuinmensen van Eindhoven" **[D, R8]** — gardeners, not organisations.

**4.3 The co-presence that exists does not convert.** Eight Groen Cafés a year across five partners is real, repeated, programmed collaboration **[D, R1]**. Fourteen organisations on one erf in October 2024 is real co-presence **[D, R11]**. Neither produced a documented exchange afterwards, and the one programme that tried to convert co-presence into a learning community concluded that it had not **[D, R2]**. This is the point at which the network reading and the reciprocity reading diverge: **the connective layer is documented; the exchange layer is not.**

**What the lens changes.** Three things.

**(a) The scarcity is coordination hours, not goodwill.** The record is full of goodwill: thirteen co-present organisations, a mailbox of invitations at a closure, eleven volunteer groups, 193 health partners, 147 directory entries **[D, R11, R7, R5, R20]**. What the record is short of is *paid or hosted hours to convert goodwill into exchange*, and the one place where those hours are counted (Trefpunt Groen) they are one fifth of the organisation's time **[D, R1]**. A design that adds another occasion without adding hours adds work to whoever already answers the e-mail.

**(b) The end of the payer is the documented failure mode, and it is a funding design, not a volunteer failure.** Groen Verbindt's gardens were not abandoned by their volunteers; their **paid coordination hours** were forecast to fall when the programme closed **[D, R2]**. The same shape appears in this repository's own material: a one-season grant, a "supervrijwilliger" holding three offices because nobody will take over, a treasurer post advertised for over a year **[D, R15, R17]**. **Durability is a property of the funding horizon at least as much as of the relationship.**

**(c) The unpaid side of the ledger is systematically unrecorded.** Trefpunt Groen's hours statement records *its own* hours, not the achterban's. The gardens' evaluations record volunteers' self-reported change, not the hours members spend answering the intermediary's requests. The experiments note's estimate of a half-day swap is an estimate **[U, R14]**; the €300,000 and the €32,000 are budget lines, but nobody has costed what a volunteer group actually spends on being networked. **The strongest statement the evidence licenses is: the coordination cost is visible where an institution pays it, and invisible where a volunteer bears it.** That asymmetry in *accounting* is itself a mechanism of extraction, because a cost nobody records cannot be negotiated.

## 6. The asymmetry question: does the strong draw on the weak?

**6.1 The sizes are not in dispute.** Wasven: ~24 hectares, three ANBI foundations, paid staff, care contracts, a member of the regional health movement's 193-partner list **[D, R12, R20]**. Trefpunt Groen: €232,000 turnover, three paid staff, a protocol mandate and **binding** tree advice **[D, R1]**. Voedselbos Eindhoven: 0.7 hectares of private land, ~30 volunteers, one coordinator, **no legal entity found**, never in Trefpunt's *achterban*, absent from the national open-day map, and closed on 28 July 2026 **[D, R11, R7]**.

**6.2 But the record does not show the strong taking from the weak. It shows the strong being *listed* and the weak being *unlisted*.** The regional health movement's partner list contains Wasven and not a single food forest, community garden or buurttuin **[D, R20]** — and it requires an organisation name. The free listing route requires only a name and an e-mail, and the closed initiative still does not appear **[D, R20]**. Absence from a public list is not exclusion, and this note does not claim it is. What it does show is that **the entry ticket is administrative form, not willingness** — and that is a far more mundane and more fixable asymmetry than capture.

**6.3 The one place where labour flows uphill is documented, and it is not obviously unfair.** Trefpunt Groen's own account of the exchange:

> "Wij coördineren de inspraak van lokale natuur- en milieugroepen en andere betrokkenen uit onze achterban, die allen **een vitale bijdrage leveren aan de kwaliteit van het advies**." **[D, R1, p. 25]**

and, in the protocol summary, TGE "coördineert de inbreng, meningen en adviezen van natuur-, groen-, en andere aangesloten groeperingen … en is verantwoordelijk voor een goede communicatie met haar achterban" **[D, R1, p. 26]**. So: **the achterban supplies expertise and legitimacy unpaid; TGE supplies coordination, paid, and converts the input into advice that is binding in the case of tree felling.** The city's side of the bargain is stated in the same document as "het vergroten van het maatschappelijk draagvlak en de kans op bezwaarprocedures verkleinen" **[D, R1, p. 26]** — the city is buying reduced objection risk with advisory power.

This is a real asymmetry, and it is also a real trade. Whether it is extraction turns on two questions the record cannot answer: **is the achterban's input attributed** (it is named as vital, never itemised per contributor) **[D/I]**, and **could a member leave and still be heard** (plausibly yes — the achterban is not dependent on TGE for land or money) **[I]**. The repository has no participant voice from the achterban **[U]**. That absence is the single largest gap in this note, and §10 names what would close it.

**6.4 The extraction risk that *is* documented runs the other way: a strong party asking a voluntary one to guarantee a public function.** Article 10 of the green policy refuses community management of high-value green and directs it to nature managers **[D, R16]**. That refusal can be read as gatekeeping; it can equally be read as the city declining to hand a volunteer group a duty it could not staff. The repository's participation note documents the same shape from the volunteer side: the burden that breaks people is administrative, and a "supervrijwilliger" is holding three offices because nobody will take over **[D, R15]**. **On this evidence the likelier extraction is not a big initiative borrowing a small one's credibility; it is an institution — municipal or organisational — assigning a small one an obligation that looks like partnership and functions as unpaid public service.**

**6.5 Two tests, and what would settle them.** The note proposes, as an inference rather than a finding, that a reviewer can apply:

- **Recording test.** Is the weaker party's contribution written down in a form it could point to six months later? The protocol passes on *advice*; the directory fails on *contribution* — 147 entries record offers, and nothing records who contributed to what **[D, R1, R20]**.
- **Withholding test.** Can the weaker party stop contributing without losing its own ground? The advisory achterban can; the landless food forest could not, and its exchange ended with its tenure **[D/I, R7, R11]**.

Where a contribution fails both tests, the arrangement is extractive in structure whether or not anyone intends it. Where it passes both, asymmetry is legitimate — and §7 gives the cases where the evidence positively supports that reading.

## 7. Counter-evidence and tensions

A note with no tensions is a note that did not look. Seven, in order of how much they cost the argument.

**7.1 Asymmetry can be legitimate, and the local evidence for it is good.** Wasven's transport rota runs with one driver once a fortnight and another five times a week, and the volunteer describing it calls this freedom, not unfairness: "Hierdoor is er ruimte om je eigen tijd in te delen" **[D, R5]**. Care work cannot be pooled across organisations at all: moving participants between care regimes is refused on safeguarding grounds in this repository's own design work **[D/I, R14]**. **Some asymmetry is not a failure of reciprocity; it is what makes a rota robust, and some of it is a hard boundary.**

**7.2 Informality can be the durable form.** De Block Acker, 100 m² on loan from a temporary platform with no entity, no lease and no rent, was handed "overgedragen aan de vrijwilligers zelf" and coordinated in a WhatsApp group **[D, R19]**. De Vergeten Tuin has run 25 years on a members' association and a core of ~25 **[D, R17]**. Against that, the formal, funded, evaluated programme is the one that lost its coordination hours **[D, R2]**. **The issue explicitly forbids assuming formal agreements beat informal ones, and the local evidence agrees** — with one caveat the same material supplies: Block Acker's present status is unknown, and its host platform's domain is now parked **[D, R19]**. Informality survived the platform's hospitality; whether it survived the platform's disappearance is **[U]**.

**7.3 Equal exchange may not be the goal, and the design of the seed library says so.** "Breng je eigen zaden … of als je die niet hebt, iets anders wat je zou willen ruilen" **[D, R8]** deliberately refuses equivalence. The gift is not accounted. If the goal of a network is care rather than barter, imposing a ledger may destroy the thing it measures — and the literature on gift exchange is a *lens* here, not evidence: it warns that an unrepayable gift creates obligation rather than equality, but Eindhoven has no case measured against that warning **[I]**.

**7.4 Co-presence may be enough for the outcome the commission actually wants.** Nine in ten volunteers report meeting new people in the neighbourhood **[D, R2]** — an outcome the main question cares about ("human and ecological health through shared care"). Demanding that a garden also become an exchange partner imports a requirement the participants were never asked about, and would load a group that already has a treasurer vacancy **[D, R17]**. **The counter-argument to this whole note is that it asks a company to become a market.**

**7.5 A new venue can crowd out the exchange it was built to host.** The strongest single piece of counter-evidence in the repository's fragmentation note: more cooperation in one collaborative venue has been found associated with *less* cooperation elsewhere **[D, R21]** — an association across five regions, abstract-level, not a mechanism. A "reciprocity broker" is a new venue. This note's §5 recommendation and this finding are in direct tension, and the tension is not resolved here.

**7.6 A sibling note's "coordination infrastructure already exists, cheaply" does not survive retrieval.** [`../experiments/note.md`](../experiments/note.md) §1 lists the Klusbus as a scheduled municipal neighbourhood circuit and counts it among existing coordination infrastructure. My retrieval of the municipality's own raadsinformatiebrief identifies the Eindhovense Klusbus as an **energy-poverty service**: a national SPUK for "de aanpak van energiearmoede", run "samen met de woningcorporaties in het Duurzaamheidspact", which in 2023 provided "ruim 9.000 huishoudens" with small energy-saving measures **[D, R4]**. It is not tool lending and it is not green-initiative infrastructure. The correction matters beyond the detail: **the repository's inventory of "cheap existing infrastructure" contains at least one item that is not what it was taken to be**, and §3 row 2 (tool sharing) is empty partly because nobody checked.

**7.7 The strongest evidence in this note is not from Eindhoven.** Groen Verbindt is a national programme; its 23–27 gardens were not Eindhoven gardens. It is the best available evidence on what a funder's attempt at peer reciprocity actually produces, and it is the same country, the same practice type and a comparable funder logic **[I]**. It is not a measurement of Eindhoven, and this note does not present it as one. What transfers is the *mechanism* — paid coordination hours, success-display burying questions, exchange only on convened days — not a local magnitude.

## 8. Not established, and what would change the answer

**Not established in the sources searched [U]:**

- Whether **any** tool, equipment or material sharing exists between two named Eindhoven green initiatives. The typology's row 2 is empty after a search, not after an assumption.
- Whether any **volunteer swap** between two initiatives has ever happened, or been refused.
- Whether any initiative has **refused** another's request, and on what grounds. The literature on refusal (safeguarding, liability, competence) is available; the local record is silent.
- The **terms of De Vergeten Tuin's 2025 beheersovereenkomst** — the one documented formalised quid pro quo between a volunteer green group and the city. Whether it obliges, pays or merely recognises is unknown.
- Whether the **paraplustichting, flexpool or central confidential adviser** were ever established. The plan was to report first results in Q3–Q4 2025 and to evaluate in 2026 **[D, R3]**; no such report was retrieved.
- Whether **Zadenbibliotheek De Nootzaak** still opens. The retrieved page is the 19 April 2025 opening.
- Whether the **Voedselbos volunteer group** still exists, and whether any of the closure's offers became anything.
- Whether **Trefpunt Groen's achterban** experiences the exchange described in §6.3 as fair. There is no participant voice in the record.
- What a **volunteer group actually spends** on being networked. The cost is recorded only when an institution pays it.
- Whether **asymmetric exchange with a care organisation** is experienced by the weaker party as support or as obligation. Wasven reports that "het contact met andere deelnemers … voor veel deelnemers lastig" is **[D, R12]**; that is about participants, not peer organisations.

**What would change the answer, in order:**

1. **One participant account from Trefpunt Groen's achterban** — what the relationship costs in hours and what it delivers. It would settle §6.3 either way, and it is the single largest gap.
2. **The text of De Vergeten Tuin's management agreement.** It is the only local document that could show a volunteer group obtaining something durable from the city in exchange for care.
3. **A 2026 status check on the seed library and on the paraplustichting.** Both would move a row from "designed" to "operating", and the second would test the municipality-as-payer hypothesis directly.
4. **One documented instance of a refusal, either direction.** It would turn §6 from inference into evidence.
5. **A costed volunteer hour.** Any initiative that recorded what it spends answering external requests would make the invisible side of §5 visible.

## 10. Adjacent questions noticed, not researched

- **#4 complementarity and conflict.** This note treats exchange of *like* for *like*. Exchange of *unlike* things — a care farm's legitimacy for a garden's produce, an intermediary's access for a group's knowledge — is #4's territory, and §6.3 sits right on the boundary.
- **#9 who is the community.** Every exchange needs two parties. In these cases "the volunteers", "the members", "the achterban" and "the buurt" are four different bodies, and none is documented as deciding collectively whether to enter an exchange [R17].
- **#11 experiments.** Experiment A (the half-day swap) is the direct test of typology row 3, and the Klusbus correction in §7.6 slightly weakens its "cheap infrastructure already exists" premise.
- **#12 AI.** The only defensible software in this material is a roster and a record of contribution — which is to say, the *substrate* of reciprocity rather than a substitute for it.
- **#7 continuity.** Payer withdrawal is a continuity risk of the same class as tenure, and the continuity register does not yet carry it.
- **#3 participation.** The giving/asking asymmetry (§1.4) is a participation finding as much as a reciprocity one: a programme that asks people to give will recruit, and a programme that needs them to ask will not.
- **#27 fragmentation.** The three entry doors and the missing fourth are the same question as this note's §6.2, seen from the health system's side.

## 11. Implications for pillar 2 — "connect existing initiatives into a city-wide ecosystem"

1. **The connector is a payer, not a platform.** The evidence for a connective layer in Eindhoven is evidence of paid intermediaries: an advisory stichting at 20% of its hours, a staffed care estate, a funded programme, a commissioned foundation **[D, R1, R5, R2, R6]**. Pillar 2 should therefore be costed as coordination hours before it is designed as a network. That is a different proposal from the one the pillar's wording implies.
2. **The cheapest thing that could actually be built is already in the city and does not need a new organisation**: the seed-library pattern — a store, a few opening days, a host with a cellar and a reason to open it **[D, R8]**. It is the one reciprocal design in Eindhoven that works without trust between the parties, and it could carry a *record of contribution* at almost no cost.
3. **Do not build a tool library first.** The only political attempt to do it was withdrawn **[D, R10]**, and the failure modes (site, keyholder, liability) are the ones the repository already identified **[R14]**. The same money in coordination hours would buy more.
4. **Design for asking, not for giving.** If 9 in 10 volunteers report new contacts and 4 in 10 report daring to ask for help **[D, R2]**, a network that invites people to contribute will grow, and a network that requires them to request will not. Any invitation should therefore be phrased as an offer with a stated time cost and an easy exit — which is what the participation note's Brabant material says volunteers want **[D, R15]**.
5. **Record the payer.** Whatever pillar 2 becomes, it should publish one line: who pays for the coordination, for how long, and what happens on the day it stops. On this evidence that line is more predictive of durability than any statement of shared values.
6. **Keep the red lines.** Four from this evidence, each with a source: do not move people between care regimes **[R14]**; do not let a volunteer practice guarantee a public function it cannot staff **[R16, R15]**; do not create a coordination role with no named payer after the pilot **[R2]**; do not take a weaker party's legitimacy without recording its contribution **[R1, R20]**.

## 12. Limits of this note

- **No participant voice.** Everything here about cost, fairness and experience is read from documents produced by institutions, funders and intermediaries. The one exception is a volunteer quoted on Wasven's own website **[D, R5]** and a coordinator quoted by an evaluator **[D, R2]** — both selected by the party publishing them.
- **No observation.** Nothing here records an exchange happening. Every claim is about what is written down.
- **The dating problem.** Several key pages are undated (R5, R6, R8, R9) and one is frozen at its opening event (R8). The practices note found the same problem across this city's green record; this note inherits it.
- **The strong evidence is one programme in another city.** §7.7.
- **The literature is a lens, and is used as one.** Concepts from gift exchange, social exchange theory, commons governance and trust research appear in this note only where the *local* evidence raised the question; none is presented as evidence about Eindhoven. Where a lens shaped an inference, the inference carries **[I]**.
- **This note cannot decide.** It cannot say whether a reciprocity layer is worth building, whether anyone would join it, or whether the asking asymmetry is a stable feature of volunteering or an artefact of one questionnaire. Those are decisions and measurements, not documents.
