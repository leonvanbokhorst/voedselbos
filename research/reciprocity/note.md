# What makes reciprocity between initiatives durable rather than extractive?

**Issue:** [#5](https://github.com/leonvanbokhorst/voedselbos/issues/5) · **Status:** provisional overall; no claim here is admitted, and admission is the reviewer's act under the repository's provenance practice.
**Research date:** 26 November 2026. Desk research; public sources only; no stakeholder contact; no fieldwork; no implementation design. Sources are numbered in [`sources.md`](sources.md); where a source was retrieved by a delegated line and then re-read by me, `sources.md` says so.
**Marks:** **[D]** documented in a retrieved source · **[C]** claimed by one actor or self-description · **[I]** inference of this note · **[U]** not established in the sources searched · **[P]** commissioner-provided.

**Lens — who bears the coordination cost.** This note reads every reciprocal arrangement as a cost-allocation question before it reads it as a goodwill question. Reciprocity that looks free is paid for by someone: in paid hours, in a host's supervision time, in a funder's programme budget, or in the unpaid evening of whoever answers the shared Gmail inbox. Where a network note would ask "is there trust?", this one asks "who is paying for the trust to be maintained, and what happens on the day they stop?" §6 answers that with numbers wherever the record gives them.

---

## 1. The answer in short

**1. Almost nothing reciprocal is documented between Eindhoven's green *initiatives* as organisations.** The city's documented exchange sits at three other levels. **Vertical:** an individual gives labour to a host institution that pays to supervise, remember and substitute — Wasven's twelve working groups under teamleiders, De Vergeten Tuin's ~25 core volunteers of ~180 members, De Buurtwerf's free plots with shared maintenance **[D]**. **Material and horizontal but not organisational:** seed and plant exchange between gardeners, hosted by an arts venue and a hobby association **[D]**. **Brokered:** a third party with its own interest pays the coordination bill — a housing-corporation pact and a fee behind Struikroven, a protocol behind Trefpunt Groen, a funder behind 23 gardens **[D]**. What the commission calls "mutual support between initiatives" is, in the retrieved record, mostly *vertical* or *brokered*; calling it mutual obscures who pays.

**2. The one natural experiment says peer exchange does not happen by itself, even when it is generously funded and deliberately designed.** A national funder put 23–27 green-social gardens into one programme for three years. At application it required two parties (one institutional, one neighbourhood), a written **samenwerkingsovereenkomst** covering "eigenaarschap en gebruik van de grond", a mandatory **Alliantielab** to "de wederzijdse verwachtingen afstemmen", co-financed staff time, a three-year horizon, annual exchange meetings, and an explicit duty to pass knowledge to newcomers — plus up to €75,000 per partnership and a €5,000-a-year team budget **[D, R20]**. Three years later the evaluators wrote: *"Van een lerende gemeenschap is gedurende de drie jaar van Groen Verbindt alleen sprake geweest op de kennis- en inspiratiedagen. Verder hebben de projecten elkaar bijna niet opgezocht"* — a learning community existed only on the days the funder convened it **[D, R2]**. **That is the most honest available estimate of what design can and cannot buy.** It is a national programme with no Eindhoven project among its 24 documented participants **[D, R21]**, so it is a mechanism, not a local measurement.

**3. The reciprocity that exists is paid for, and the payer is identifiable in every durable case.** The city itself says so, in a lease notice for a building on the same street as the food forest's former site: *"**De Gemeente is onvoldoende geëquipeerd om grote aantallen vrijwilligers te bereiken en te begeleiden**"* — and leases the building to Natuurmonumenten, which has "ruim 7.000 vrijwilligers" and a POM status **[D, R22]**. Trefpunt Groen spends **20% of its paid hours** on the relational work itself (15% *afstemming achterban* + 5% *netwerk intensiveren*) out of **€232,000** turnover **[D, R1]**. Each Groen Verbindt garden ran on **three to four paid staff, about twelve paid hours a week**, and when the programme ended "in veel gevallen komen er minder betaalde uren beschikbaar voor de coördinatie" **[D, R2]**. Wasven's working groups each get an annual discretionary budget "aan het verhogen van kennis, kunde en **binding** van de groep" **[D, R11]**. **No retrieved case of sustained exchange in Eindhoven has no payer.**

**4. The sharpest measured asymmetry is between giving and asking — and it is the mechanism by which extraction happens without a villain.** In the same annual survey, year after year: **9 in 10** volunteers affirm meeting new people in the neighbourhood, **5 in 10** that they stand ready for others sooner, **4 in 10** that they dare ask more people for help **[D, R2]**. The evaluators name it: *"Om hulp vragen is altijd moeilijker dan hulp bieden, zeker voor vrijwilligers"* **[D, R2]**. **Three cautions travel with that figure, and this note takes all three seriously:** the unit is the *individual volunteer*, the object is "mensen" in general rather than other organisations, and the gap is ten points, not a collapse **[D/I, R2]**. It is therefore **not** evidence that Eindhoven organisations hesitate to ask each other for help. What it does license is weaker and still useful: **a network built on people's willingness to give will accumulate offers before it accumulates claims.** The organisation-level version is claimed in Eindhoven's own words by a board network: *"Besturen worstelen vaak met precies dezelfde vraagstukken, maar weten dat niet van elkaar"* **[D, R24]**.

**5. Asymmetry is not extraction, and the evidence supports two tests rather than one verdict.** **(a) Recording:** is the weaker party's contribution written down somewhere it could point to? The advisory protocol records the *advice* and makes tree advice binding, and describes the achterban's input as "een vitale bijdrage aan de kwaliteit van het advies" **[D, R1]** — named as vital, never itemised per contributor. **(b) Withholding:** can the weaker party stop contributing without losing its own ground? An advisory *achterban* can walk away from an intermediary; a landless volunteer food forest could not walk away from a landowner, and when the land went, everything went **[D/I, R13, R7]**. **Dependence, not size, is the extraction risk** — which is why the 24-hectare partner is not automatically the extractor, and why the sharper local danger runs the other way: an institution assigning a volunteer group a duty that looks like partnership and functions as unpaid public service (§7.4).

**6. What is refused is documented mostly at the institutional level, not between peers.** The city refuses community management of green of "groenwaarde middel of hoog" **[D, R16]**. The council **withdrew** the one motion that would have created shared lending space including "**(tuin)gereedschap**", on an alderman's commitment with no published outcome **[D, R10]**. Two partnership projects in the funded programme declined to continue in coalition form — "Een project gaat niet in coalitievorm verder"; "Twee projectleiders geven aan zelfstandig verder te gaan, zonder partners" **[D, R2]**. **No documented instance was found of one green initiative declining another's request [U]** — and §8 explains why that absence must not be read as harmony.

**7. Four conditions separate durable from extractive exchange in this evidence; four more are plausible and untested here.** Durable: **a named payer of the coordination load**, **a recurring bounded slot**, **an exit that costs nothing**, and **a record of contribution rather than of offers**. Plausible: a divisible object or bounded task, rotation with redundancy, a conflict route, and a small discretionary budget held by the group itself. **Formalisation is not one of the conditions** — the informal cases survive and the funded ones lose their coordination when the funding stops **[D, R2, R17]**.

**What the typology shows, as an inference [I] and not a finding.** The rows that work without trust (**1**, **13**) exchange a *divisible, storable, low-value* object or an *administered* risk. The rows that need trust and are missing (**3**, **6**, **7**) exchange *labour, obligation or money between organisations*. The rows that are documented but do not produce exchange (**4**, **5**, **10**) exchange *presence*. **The evidence is consistent with one rule: the harder a thing is to divide, store and return, the more the arrangement needs a payer and a record.** Eindhoven has payers and records for material, for risk, and for vertical care; it has neither for labour between organisations. The rule is a hypothesis the evidence fits; no retrieved case breaks it, which is not the same as its having been tested.

## 4. Where the honest answer is narrower than the question

**4.1 The strongest documented exchange is inside one organisation, not between two.** Wasven's volunteer page is the richest reciprocity document retrieved: twelve named working groups, "onder deskundige leiding", fixed rhythms per group, a teamleider per team, and — in a volunteer's own words — a rota that absorbs absence:

> "Als iemand een keer niet kan, staat er gelijk een ander klaar om jouw dienst over te nemen. Hierdoor is er ruimte om je eigen tijd in te delen. Een van ons rijdt bijvoorbeeld één keer in de 14 dagen zijn rit en een ander rijdt wel 5 keer per week." **[D, R5]**

That is durable reciprocity. It is *generalised* rather than balanced in Sahlins' terms — unequal contribution with no accounting — but that classification is an analogy, licensed only because the relation pre-exists the exchange, which is the condition Sahlins attaches to generalised reciprocity **[I, R30]** (see §13.2). And it is **vertical**: individuals give to an institution that allocates, remembers and substitutes. It is not a template for exchange *between* initiatives; it is a description of what an initiative must already be able to do before it can host anybody else's people.

**4.2 The horizontal exchange that exists is horticultural, hosted, and deliberately low-trust.** The seed library is the clearest reciprocity *design* in the city, and the design is the point:

> "Kom langs om zaden en plantjes te ruilen, lenen en/of te doneren. Breng je eigen zaden, plantjes of stekjes mee of als je die niet hebt, **iets anders wat je zou willen ruilen**." **[D, R8]**

Three choices in one sentence: **lending with return**, **swapping with no equivalence required**, and **donating with no return at all**. Add a cellar that keeps seed cool and dry, a few opening days, and a workshop or a film on those days, and the arrangement needs neither trust nor a relationship: **the store holds the obligation, not the people.** Its stated purpose is to connect "de tuinen en tuinmensen van Eindhoven" **[D, R8]** — gardeners, not organisations.

**4.3 The one thing in Eindhoven that looks like a reciprocity *institution* is a pact among capitalised parties.** The Duurzaamheidspact's struikroof norm is a *prospective, collective* obligation: every demolition or renovation project is to be stripped of plants first **[D, R23]**. It has survived since at least 2021, it is described by the municipality as having become "een gewoonte", and it is backed by a paid broker **[D, R6]**. **Note what makes it different from everything else in this note: the parties are four housing corporations, a tenants' federation and the city. Each can afford to comply, each has a continuing interest, and none of them is a volunteer.** No green initiative is documented as a party **[U]** — which is the central asymmetry this note can actually evidence.

**4.4 Co-presence does not convert.** Eight Groen Cafés a year across five partners is real, repeated, programmed collaboration **[D, R1]**. Fourteen organisations on one erf is real co-presence **[D, R13]**. Neither produced a documented exchange afterwards, and the programme that tried hardest to convert co-presence into a learning community concluded that it had not **[D, R2]**. **The connective layer is documented; the exchange layer is not.** That distinction is the whole answer to the issue in one line, and it is why the network map in this repository should not be read as a reciprocity map.

**4.5 What would count as a counterexample.** So that a later pass can overturn this note rather than argue with it, here is what would do it, in order of force.

1. **A documented horizontal exchange with no payer.** Two named Eindhoven initiatives, exchanging labour or materials over more than one occasion, with no host, funder, broker or municipal channel behind it. Any one of §3 rows 3, 6 or 7 filled this way would falsify P7 outright and would be the most valuable single find for this issue.
2. **A post-event collaboration traceable to a convened occasion.** The 2024 market put fourteen organisations on one erf **[D, R13]**; a joint planting, a shared seed stock or a mutual referral traceable to a conversation held there would falsify P19 and would make the co-presence layer far more important than this note says.
3. **A green initiative as a party to the Duurzaamheidspact.** That would move §3 row 14 from "a pact among capitalised parties" to something a volunteer group can join, and would change what pillar 2 should copy.
4. **A volunteer group that obtained something durable from the city in exchange for care.** The two candidates are De Vergeten Tuin's 2025 management agreement and Wasven's beheerovereenkomst **[R17, R11]**; either text, published, could show a reciprocal settlement that this note could not see.
5. **A documented refusal between peers, with reasons.** It would replace §7's inference about why horizontality is missing with something an actor said.
6. **An operating paraplustichting.** It would show the municipality bearing the back-office cost at arm's length, and would test the "who pays" claim in its strongest available form **[R3]**.

**What would *not* count.** More co-presence, another market, another network map or another directory listing. On this evidence those are the things Eindhoven already has in quantity **[D, R1, R13, R18]**, and their presence is the premise of the question rather than an answer to it.

## 5. Two documented micro-reciprocities worth keeping

Small, but they are the only places in the material where a *reciprocal arrangement between persons* is described rather than asserted **[D, R26]**.

**(a) The alternating street.** A volunteer describing how she and a neighbour share an adopted street: *"Sindsdien zorgen we samen voor de volledige Fransebaan. Op even dagen doe ik de ene helft en op oneven dagen de andere. En hij doet dat andersom."* **[D, R26]** A division of labour, a rhythm, a partner, and a recognition loop — *"Regelmatig wordt er getoeterd en steken mensen hun duim op"* **[D, R26]**. No agreement, no entity, no money.

**(b) The sharing rule that tells the city what not to do.** Adopted green gets a sign, and the sign has a function: *"Als er onderhoud plaatsvindt weten zij dat er een stukje groen is geadopteerd. Dan weten ze dat ze daar niet moeten schoffelen."* **[D, R26]** A visibility device that protects a contribution by instructing the stronger party — the cheapest conflict-prevention mechanism in the whole record, and it costs a sign.

**What these two have in common, as an inference [I]:** both are *pairwise*, both have a **fixed rhythm**, both produce **visible evidence of contribution**, and neither needs a ledger. They are the smallest possible durable arrangements, and they suggest that the unit of durability may be smaller than "the network" — a pair and a rhythm, not a platform and a membership.

**Three things the lens changes.**

**(a) The scarcity is coordination hours, not goodwill.** The record is full of goodwill: fourteen co-present organisations, a mailbox of invitations at a closure, twelve volunteer groups, 193 health partners, 147 directory entries **[D, R13, R7, R5, R18]**. What is short is *paid or hosted hours to convert goodwill into exchange* — and where those hours are counted, they are a fifth of an organisation's time **[D, R1]**. A design that adds an occasion without adding hours adds work to whoever already answers the e-mail.

**(b) The end of the payer is the documented failure mode, and it is a funding design, not a volunteer failure.** Groen Verbindt's gardens were not abandoned; their **paid coordination hours** were forecast to fall when the programme closed **[D, R2]**. The same shape recurs: a one-season grant, a "supervrijwilliger" holding three offices because nobody will take over, a treasurer post advertised for over a year **[D, R15, R17]**. And the city's own back office is being built on money that ends in 2026 **[D, R3]**. **Durability is a property of the funding horizon at least as much as of the relationship** — which is why the host with the most volunteers in this material is itself running a deficit with a continuity reserve under 1.5× annual costs **[D, R11]**. The payer may be fragile too.

**(c) The unpaid side of the ledger is systematically unrecorded.** Trefpunt Groen's hours statement records *its* hours, not the achterban's. Programme evaluations record volunteers' self-reported change, not the hours members spend answering an intermediary's requests. **The strongest statement the evidence licenses: the coordination cost is visible where an institution pays it, and invisible where a volunteer bears it.** That accounting asymmetry is itself a mechanism of extraction, because a cost nobody records cannot be negotiated **[I]**.

## 7. The asymmetry question: does the strong draw on the weak?

**7.1 The sizes are not in dispute.** Wasven: ~24 hectares, three ANBI foundations, paid staff, care contracts, a partner in the regional health movement's 193-name list **[D, R13, R18]**. Trefpunt Groen: €232,000 turnover, three paid staff, a protocol mandate and **binding** tree advice **[D, R1]**. Voedselbos Eindhoven: 0.7 hectares of private land, ~30 volunteers, one coordinator, **no legal entity found**, never in Trefpunt's *achterban*, absent from the national open-day map, closed on 28 July 2026 **[D, R13, R7]**.

**7.2 But the record does not show the strong taking from the weak. It shows the strong being *listed* and the weak being *unlisted*.** The regional health movement's partner list contains Wasven and no food forest, community garden or buurttuin **[D, R18]**, and it requires an organisation name. The free *listing* route requires only a name and an e-mail, and the closed initiative appears in neither **[D, R18]**. Absence from a public list is not exclusion, and this note does not claim it is. What it shows is that **the entry ticket is administrative form, not willingness** — a more mundane and more fixable asymmetry than capture.

**7.3 The one place where labour flows uphill is documented, and it is not obviously unfair.** Trefpunt Groen's own account:

> "Wij coördineren de inspraak van lokale natuur- en milieugroepen en andere betrokkenen uit onze achterban, die allen **een vitale bijdrage leveren aan de kwaliteit van het advies**." **[D, R1, p. 25]**

and, in the protocol summary, TGE "coördineert de inbreng, meningen en adviezen van natuur-, groen-, en andere aangesloten groeperingen … en is verantwoordelijk voor een goede communicatie met haar achterban" **[D, R1, p. 26]**. So: **the achterban supplies expertise and legitimacy unpaid; TGE supplies paid coordination and converts the input into advice that binds in the case of tree felling.** The city's side is stated in the same document — "het vergroten van het maatschappelijk draagvlak en de kans op bezwaarprocedures verkleinen" **[D, R1, p. 26]**: the city buys reduced objection risk with advisory power.

That is a real asymmetry and also a real trade. Whether it is extraction turns on two questions the record cannot answer: **is the contribution attributed** (named as vital, never itemised) **[D/I]**, and **could a member leave and still be heard** (plausibly yes — the achterban depends on TGE for neither land nor money) **[I]**. There is **no participant voice from the achterban** in the repository **[U]**. That is this note's largest gap.

**7.4 The likelier extraction is institutional, not interpersonal.** Article 10 of the green policy refuses community management of high-value green and directs it to nature managers **[D, R16]** — readable as gatekeeping, and equally as the city declining to hand a volunteer group a duty it could not staff (the volunteer practice that closed had no entity at all **[D, R13]**). The participation note documents the same shape from the volunteer side: the burden that breaks people is administrative, and the "supervrijwilliger" holds three offices because nobody will take over **[D, R15]**. **On this evidence the sharper danger is an institution — municipal or organisational — assigning a small one an obligation that looks like partnership and functions as unpaid public service.** The one documented case where care was extinguished by an institutional handover is about residents, not initiatives: a D66 faction asked the council about residents "**worden gevraagd om zelf aangebracht groen te verwijderen**" in a street being delivered by a developer, and whether they could adopt it "op dezelfde wijze als elders in Eindhoven" **[D that the questions were tabled; C on the facts, which rest on a paywalled article; outcome [U], R28]**.

**7.5 Two tests, offered as inference rather than verdict.** (i) **Recording:** is the weaker party's contribution written down where it could point to it? The advisory protocol passes on *advice*; the health directory fails on *contribution* — 147 entries record offers and nothing records who contributed to what **[D, R1, R18]**. (ii) **Withholding:** can the weaker party stop contributing without losing its own ground? The advisory achterban can; the landless food forest could not, and its exchange ended with its tenure **[D/I, R7, R13]**. Where a contribution fails both tests, the arrangement is extractive in structure whether or not anyone intends it. Where it passes both, asymmetry is legitimate — and §8.1 gives the cases where the evidence positively supports that.

## 8. Counter-evidence and tensions

Nine, in order of how much they cost the argument.

**8.1 Asymmetry can be legitimate, and the local evidence is good.** Wasven's rota runs one driver at 1×/fortnight and another at 5×/week, and the volunteer describing it calls this freedom, not unfairness **[D, R5]**. Care work cannot be pooled across organisations at all: moving participants between care regimes is refused on safeguarding grounds in this repository's own design work **[D/I, R14]**. **Some asymmetry is what makes a rota robust; some of it is a hard boundary.**

**8.2 Informality can be the durable form.** De Block Acker — 100 m² on loan, no entity, no lease, no rent — was "overgedragen aan de vrijwilligers zelf" and coordinated in a WhatsApp group **[D, R17]**. De Vergeten Tuin has run 25 years on a members' association and ~25 core volunteers **[D, R17]**. Against that, the formal, funded, evaluated programme is the one that lost its coordination hours **[D, R2]**, and two of its projects explicitly declined coalition form **[D, R2]**. The issue forbids assuming formal agreements beat informal ones; the local evidence agrees. The caveat is that Block Acker's present status is unknown and its host platform's domain is parked **[D, R17]**.

**8.3 Equal exchange may not be the goal, and the design says so.** "iets anders wat je zou willen ruilen" deliberately refuses equivalence **[D, R8]**. If the point is care rather than barter, imposing a ledger may destroy what it measures — and the gift-exchange literature is a *lens* here, not evidence: it warns that an unrepayable gift creates obligation rather than equality, but no Eindhoven case has been measured against that warning **[I]**.

**8.4 Co-presence may be enough for the outcome the commission wants.** Nine in ten volunteers report meeting new people **[D, R2]** — an outcome the main question cares about. Requiring a garden to become an exchange partner imports a demand its participants were never asked about, onto a group that already has a treasurer vacancy **[D, R17]**. **The strongest objection to this note is that it asks a company to become a market.**

**8.5 The best-designed reciprocity package in the Dutch record did not fix asking.** The Oranje Fonds required a written agreement on land ownership and use, a facilitated start, co-financed staff time, three years of money, annual exchange meetings and a knowledge-sharing duty **[D, R20]** — and still measured 4 in 10 daring to ask for help after three years **[D, R2]**. Worse for any design optimism: the same package required that the initiative be run "overwegend op vrijwilligers" and transferred to volunteers, *while* paying for the coordination that made it work **[D, R20]**. **A funder can require the paperwork of reciprocity and cannot require the relationship.** Any recommendation in §12 must be read against this.

**8.6 A new venue can crowd out the exchange it was built to host.** The repository's fragmentation note records that more cooperation in one collaborative venue has been found associated with *less* cooperation elsewhere **[D, R19]** — an association across five regions, abstract-level, not a mechanism. A reciprocity broker is a new venue. §12's recommendations and this finding are in direct tension, and the tension is not resolved here.

**8.7 A sibling note's "coordination infrastructure already exists, cheaply" does not survive retrieval.** [`../experiments/note.md`](../experiments/note.md) §1 lists the Klusbus as a scheduled municipal neighbourhood circuit counting toward existing coordination infrastructure. The municipality's own documents identify the Eindhovense Klusbus as an **energy-poverty service**: a national SPUK for "de aanpak van energiearmoede", run with the housing corporations, which visited "15.759 huishoudens" before it stopped — "De Klusbus stopt maar krijgt wel een vervolg in een ander jasje" **[D, R4, R23]**. Its dataset's 22 "locations" are **neighbourhoods**, not depots **[D, R23]**. The correction matters beyond the detail: the repository's inventory of cheap existing infrastructure contains at least one item that is not what it was taken to be.

**8.8 The strongest evidence in this note is not from Eindhoven.** Groen Verbindt is national; none of its 24 documented projects is in Eindhoven **[D, R21]**. It is the best available evidence on what a deliberate attempt at peer reciprocity actually produces — same country, same practice type, comparable funder logic **[I]** — and it is not a local measurement. **What transfers is the mechanism** (paid coordination hours, success-display burying questions, exchange only on convened days), never a local magnitude.

**8.9 The one arrangement that is unambiguously generous is the one nobody would call reciprocity.** The city gives away plants, bulbs, topsoil and mulch with no return duty, and lends clean-up kit free for a single day **[D, R26]**. It is a **one-way transfer**, and it is the cheapest and least fragile thing in this entire note — no trust, no record, no coordination, no payer beyond the city's own budget. Against that, the instrument that *does* attach an obligation, the Groensubsidie with its ten-year upkeep duty, reaches only owners of ground and therefore excludes most of the initiatives in this repository **[D, R13]**. **The uncomfortable implication is that the durable, reciprocal designs this note has spent its length describing are harder, costlier and more fragile than simply giving things away** — and that a network built to produce exchange may be solving a problem the city has already solved, differently, by not asking for anything back.

## 12. Implications for pillar 2 — "connect existing initiatives into a city-wide ecosystem"

1. **The connector is a payer, not a platform.** The evidence for a connective layer in Eindhoven is evidence of paid intermediaries: an advisory stichting at 20% of its hours, a staffed care estate, a funded programme, a commissioned foundation, and a city that leases a building to buy volunteer-coordination capacity it says it lacks **[D, R1, R5, R2, R6, R22]**. Pillar 2 should therefore be costed as coordination hours before it is designed as a network.
2. **The cheapest thing that could actually be built is already here and needs no new organisation**: the seed-library pattern — a store, a few opening days, a host with a cellar and a reason to open it **[D, R8]**. It is the one reciprocal design in Eindhoven that works without trust between the parties, and it could carry a record of contribution at almost no cost.
3. **Do not build a tool library first.** The one proposal for it was withdrawn **[D, R10]**, and its failure modes — site, keyholder, liability — are the ones this repository already identified **[R14]**. The same money in coordination hours buys more.
4. **Design for asking, not for giving.** If volunteers report giving more readily than asking **[D, R2]**, a network that invites contribution will grow and one that requires requests will not. Every invitation should carry a stated time cost and a free exit — which is what Brabant volunteers say they want **[D, R15]**. **But read this against §8.5:** the best-designed Dutch package did not move the asking figure, so treat this as a design principle to test, not a lever.
5. **Give the group its own money.** The one documented device for group durability is Wasven's annual discretionary amount per working group, for "kennis, kunde en binding" **[D, R11]**. It is small, internal and unglamorous, and it is the only line in this material that a volunteer group can spend on its own cohesion without asking anyone's permission.
6. **Record the payer.** Whatever pillar 2 becomes, it should publish one line: who pays for the coordination, for how long, and what happens the day it stops. On this evidence that line predicts durability better than any statement of shared values — and the city's own back office is currently funded "incidenteel … tot en met 2026" **[D, R3]**.
7. **Keep the red lines.** Five from this evidence, each with a source: do not move people between care regimes **[R14]**; do not let a volunteer practice guarantee a public function it cannot staff **[R16, R15]**; do not create a coordination role with no named payer after the pilot **[R2]**; do not take a weaker party's legitimacy without recording its contribution **[R1, R18]**; and do not require the paperwork of reciprocity and call it reciprocity **[R20, R2]**.

**What not to conclude from this note.** Four temptations, each of which the evidence refuses.

1. **"So we need a reciprocity broker."** That is a *new coordination role with no named payer*, which §6(b) identifies as the failure mode and §8.6 records a documented crowding-out risk for. If someone builds it, the first question is who funds the coordination in year two — not what the platform does.
2. **"So the initiatives should formalise."** §8.2 finds the informal cases surviving and the funded programme's paid coordination ending. Formalisation is not among the four supported conditions.
3. **"So the strong should give more to the weak."** §7.2 finds asymmetry operating as *listing*, not capture, and §8.1 finds some asymmetry legitimate. A transfer would not change who can fill in a form or whose contribution is recorded.
4. **"So nothing should be built."** The four supported conditions are cheap: a recurring slot, a free exit, a record of contribution, and one line naming the payer. The seed library is the existence proof that all four can be had for a cellar and a few afternoons **[D, R8]**. If a later pass retrieves nothing else, it should retrieve how that one arrangement is actually run.

## 13. The literature as lens — and where it is only analogy

The issue names four bodies of literature. Eight items were retrieved at source for this note (full texts or OCR texts except where stated; read depth and transfer limits are in [`sources.md`](sources.md) §5). **None of them is evidence about Eindhoven,** and the rule applied below is that a lens may *name a question* the local evidence then has to answer, and may not answer it.

**13.1 Reciprocity as pattern and as norm — Gouldner.** Gouldner separates reciprocity as a describable *pattern of exchange* from a *norm* with two minimal demands: help those who helped you, and do not injure them **[D, R29]** — read in a Spanish translation of the 1960 article, because the English original was closed; it may not be quoted in English here. **Licenses:** the diagnostic question "is this a pattern or a norm?" — which is exactly the question §3 forces on the Duurzaamheidspact (a norm) versus the 2024 market (a pattern). **Analogy only:** the paper is a programme for functional analysis with no data, so it cannot support the claim that norms produce durability. **Local finding it sharpens:** Eindhoven's one durable multi-party arrangement is a *norm with a broker behind it* (§3 row 14), not a pattern that grew.

**13.2 Generalised, balanced, negative — Sahlins.** Three points on a continuum whose position is fixed by social distance, and the causal arrow runs differently at each end: *"It is notable of the main run of generalized reciprocities that the material flow is sustained by prevailing social relations; whereas, for the main run of balanced exchange, social relations hinge on the material flow"* **[D, R30]**. **Licenses:** a prediction this note can state and has partly confirmed — a city-wide network of *distant* peers should default to **balanced** (explicit equivalence, or nothing), which is what §3 rows 3, 6 and 7 show: no exchange at all rather than a generalised flow. **Analogy only, and it forbids something:** Sahlins' cases are kin-based, non-monetary societies with no organisations, no state and no money. So **volunteer labour must not be called "generalised reciprocity"** on this evidence — including the Wasven rota, which §4.1 describes as generalised *only* in the narrower sense that the relation pre-exists the exchange inside a working team. **Where the note is exposed:** if a later reader wants the rota classified, that classification is an analogy, not a finding.

**13.3 The gift that cannot be repaid — Mauss.** The obligations to give, receive and repay, and the asymmetry inside them: *"To give is to show one's superiority, to show that one is something more and higher, that one is master. To accept without returning or repaying more is to face subordination, to become a client and subservient"* **[D, R31]** (Cunnison translation, OCR text). **Licenses:** proposition P9 — the legitimacy currency in §3 rows 12 and 14 cannot be returned, so it creates rank. **Analogy only:** the empirical base is Polynesian, Melanesian and Northwest Coast, and the explanatory apparatus includes the *hau*, which must not be imported. What travels is an obligation *structure* as an ideal type. **Local finding it sharpens, and complicates:** the Struikroof recipient owes nothing documented **[D, R23]** — by Mauss that should create subordination of residents to the pact's members, and no retrieved source tests whether it does. The note therefore reports it as a structural risk, not a finding.

**13.4 Power is dependence — Emerson.** *"The power of A over B is equal to, and based upon, the dependence of B upon A"* **[D, R32]**, with dependence made of motivational investment × lack of alternatives. **Licenses:** the *withholding test* in §7.5, and its remedy — alternatives, not goodwill. **Analogy only:** a dyadic theory of calculating actors that cannot describe care and was measured on none of these organisations; **Blau and Homans were not read and nothing is attributed to them.** **Local finding it sharpens:** the advisory *achterban* has alternatives (it depends on Trefpunt Groen for neither land nor money **[I]**), which is why §7.3 does not call that relationship extraction; the landless food forest had none, which is why its exchange ended with its tenure **[D, R7, R13]**.

**13.5 Commons design principles — Ostrom, and her critics.** The principles most relevant here are monitoring by monitors *"accountable to the appropriators"*, graduated sanctions, low-cost conflict arenas and nested units **[D, R33]** — and Ostrom's own disclaimer that the list "is still quite speculative" **[D, R33]**. **Licenses:** a list of durability conditions to *look for*, which is how §1.7's third and fourth conditions (record, conflict route) were framed. **Analogy only, and this is the sharpest limit in the section:** a network of initiatives is **not** a common-pool resource — there are no resource units, no appropriator boundary and no subtractable stock — so the eight principles cannot be used as a checklist, which is precisely the critique the commons literature itself makes **[D, R33, R34]**. **Local finding it sharpens:** Eindhoven has one arrangement with a monitoring-and-signalling device that is genuinely Ostrom-shaped — the Adopteer een Straat sign that tells municipal workers not to hoe **[D, R26]** — and it is a *pairwise* arrangement, not a commons.

**13.6 Thin and thick trust.** "Thick" can be operationalised as track record, partner-specific predictability, risk and interdependence, and identification, with the conditions stated as *"Two conditions of trust are risk and interdependence"* **[D, R35]**; generalised trust, by contrast, is an early-learned and stable outlook — *"Trust must be learned, not earned. Generalized trust reflects our outlook on the world and is stable over time"* **[D, R36]**. **Licenses:** a hard limit on what a network can do. A convened occasion can build *particularised* trust between specific people; it cannot manufacture generalised trust, and it cannot build either without risk and interdependence. **Analogy only:** none of this is measurable from documents or interviews, and it locates trust in persons and dyads — there is no such thing as network-level trust in this material. **Local finding it sharpens:** eight Groen Cafés a year produce repeated, programmed contact **[D, R1]**; on this lens that is the raw material of particularised trust and nothing more, which is consistent with the finding that co-presence did not convert (§4.4).

**13.7 Care webs and mutual aid.** The normative claim is that durable care is *negotiated and bounded* — consent, capacity, a real "no" — and that its failure modes are urgency-driven collapse, burnout-as-conflict and co-optation through funding: *"Burnout is created or worsened when we feel disconnected from others, mistreated, misunderstood, ashamed, overburdened, obsessed with outcomes, perfectionist, or controlling"* **[D, R37]**. **Licenses:** the exit and refusal conditions in §1.7 and the red lines in §12, and it explains why a *volunteer* group's no must be free — a point the local evidence reaches independently through what Brabant volunteers say they want **[D, R15]**. **Analogy only:** this is normative political writing with no samples and no outcomes, in a US disability-justice and anarchist context where the state and nonprofits are antagonists — an odd fit for a Dutch municipality that funds the sector. It constrains reciprocity; it does not describe it here.

**13.8 What could not be retrieved, and what that costs.** The coordination-labour literature was **not obtainable at source**: Star & Strauss is abstract-only behind a subscription gate, and the abstract supports only the framing claim that visibility is contextual, **not** the proposition that coordination work is invisibly borne here **[U, R38]**. That proposition is therefore carried entirely by case evidence in §6(c) — the hours statement that records the institution's hours and not the achterban's — and a reviewer should treat it as the weakest supported claim in the note. Also unobtained: Gouldner in English, Blau, Homans, Lewicki & Bunker, and the optional diverse-economies material.

**The net effect of using the literature this way** is that it does three jobs and no more: it names the distinctions the local evidence needed (pattern vs norm; generalised vs balanced; power as dependence; particularised vs generalised trust); it forbids two over-claims (calling volunteer labour generalised reciprocity; using Ostrom as a checklist); and it predicts one thing the evidence partly confirms (distant peers default to balanced exchange, i.e. to explicit equivalence or to nothing). **It does not explain why Eindhoven is like this, and this note does not claim it does.**

## 14. Limits of this note

- **No participant voice.** Everything here about cost, fairness and experience is read from documents produced by institutions, funders, intermediaries and one municipality. The exceptions are two volunteers quoted on a scheme's own webpage **[D, R5, R26]** and a coordinator quoted by an evaluator **[D, R2]** — each selected by the party publishing it.
- **No observation.** Nothing here records an exchange happening. Every claim is about what is written down.
- **The dating problem.** Several key pages are undated (R5, R6, R8, R9, R26) and one is anchored to a single event (R8). The practices note found the same problem across this city's green record; this note inherits it.
- **The strong evidence is one national programme.** §8.8.
- **The literature is a lens and is used as one.** Gift exchange, social exchange theory, commons governance, trust research and care-ethics writing appear only where the *local* evidence raised the question; none is presented as evidence about Eindhoven, and where a lens shaped an inference the inference carries **[I]**. What each lens licenses, and what it does not, is set out in §13; one lens (coordination labour) could not be retrieved at source and therefore carries no claim at all.
- **This note cannot decide.** It cannot say whether a reciprocity layer is worth building, whether anyone would join it, or whether the asking asymmetry is a stable feature of volunteering or an artefact of one questionnaire. Those are decisions and measurements, not documents.
