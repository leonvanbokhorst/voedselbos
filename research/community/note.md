# Who is "the community" — who is on the record, who is missing, and what it would mean to research *with* it

Issue #9. Desk research, 26 November 2026. Public sources only; no fieldwork, no stakeholder contact, no method rollout, no implementation. Marks as in the rest of the repository: **[D]** documented in a retrieved source, **[C]** claimed by a party or a self-description, **[P]** provided by the commissioner (direct account, not public documentation), **[I]** inference of this note, **[U]** not established in the sources searched. Source ids `N1`–`N15` (local records) and `M1`–`M8` (literature lens) are resolved in [`sources.md`](sources.md); the counts are reproducible from [`working/datasets.md`](working/datasets.md); the delegated Dutch line is in [`working/dutch-line.md`](working/dutch-line.md).

**Lens declared.** *Who is structurally absent, and what the record can and cannot show about them.* The working assumption that follows from the lens, stated up front so a reviewer can test it: **a participation map built from producer-side data is evidence about the producer as much as about the participants.** §3 and §4 test that sentence; §7 is where it can fail.

---

## 1. The answer in short

1. **No participant is on the record in Eindhoven's green data.** In the city's greening register every field *about people* is a staff coding; there is no name, household, contact or verbatim voice anywhere in its 124 records **[D, `N1`,`N2`]**. The city-wide count of "who participates" that this repository has been quoting is therefore a count of *records about* participation, made by the party that also decides what counts as participation **[D/I]**. The sharpest existing finding in this repository — that no participant voice is on the record — is here confirmed for the green *data* as well as for the green *stories*, and that extension is this note's first contribution **[D]**.

2. **Where participation is counted, the counter is the municipality.** 31 of 124 greening records (25%) entered as a residents' initiative, 39 (31%) through an area coordinator, and 54 (43%) through a municipal colleague, an "other", an external party, a building team, or nobody recorded **[D, computed from `N2`]**. Of the 19 records rated "high" for resident involvement, **13 came in through an area coordinator** and 4 from residents **[D, computed]**; and the rating itself is never defined — the catalogue describes the field as "laag, redelijk, of hoge betrokkenheid" while the data also contain `matig` 26 times **[D, `N1`]**. The field that is meant to measure residents' involvement co-varies most with *who entered the record* **[I]**.

3. **Authority is granted by legal form and by editorial control, and assumed by whoever writes the record.** Five documented modes (§4): a foundation's board; a coordinator's intake coding; a platform's editorial desk; one person's mailbox; an intermediary's claim to speak for an *achterban* **[D]**. In no retrieved Eindhoven case is there a documented body of residents that decides something collectively and records the decision **[D — negative finding]**.

4. **The city's own grammar of participation is top-down, and it says so.** The participation ordinance defines *inwonersparticipatie* as "het op initiatief van een bestuursorgaan betrekken van…" — the initiative belongs to a municipal body by definition — and defines its strongest rung, *meebeslissen*, as decisive influence exercised by participants who "zijn vanuit hun rol weliswaar niet formeel bevoegd om besluiten tot nemen" **[D, `N10`]**. A neighbourhood that wants to *start* something must use another door: the greening register (which re-codes it), a gebiedsgericht subsidy open to "natuurlijke- en rechtspersonen" (so renters qualify), or the *uitdaagrecht*, which requires the challenger to supply "een indicatie van het draagvlak onder belanghebbenden" **[D, `N12`,`N10`]**.

5. **Absence from the record is not absence from the place — and in three retrieved documents the exclusion is stated, not merely undocumented.** The municipality's own commissioned evaluation of a participation process records that people with a physical disability "zijn als groep met specifieke belangen wat betreft de toegankelijkheid van de openbare ruimte niet actief benaderd" **[D, `N13`]**; a national survey of green volunteers states that it "niet is ingevuld door mensen die de Nederlandse taal niet machtig zijn" **[D, `N14`]**; and the nearest comparator study reports that Moroccan and Turkish residents were heavily under-represented in its own sample, while quoting residents who name the participants as "a select club of hip white vegans" **[D, `N15` — comparator, not Eindhoven]**. For Eindhoven's gardens themselves, the absence of renters, migrants, low-income households, youth, older people, disabled people and people without Dutch is **[U]**: undocumented, which is not the same as excluded **[I]**.

6. **Researching "with" is a set of costs, not a sentiment.** §5 costs six options by what they demand of participants and of organisers, what they return, and how they fail. The two cheapest are not methods at all — returning the record to the people in it, and asking its producers to correct it — and neither can manufacture the voices of people who never came **[I]**. The most expensive, co-research, is the only one that changes who holds the question; it also presupposes a body able to mandate representatives, which nothing retrieved shows exists here **[D/U]**.

7. **Ten guardrails in §6 are written to be adopted by the rest of the programme**, including the two rules this repository already carries (no identifiable personal stories without permission; no inferred motives or diagnoses) and three it does not: a minimum-cell rule for anything published by neighbourhood; the rule that an absent group is described as absent from the record and never as excluded; and the rule that "with" includes the right to refuse, including the right to refuse to be a case **[I, house standard applied]**.

## 2. Method, marks, and what this note is not

- **Scope taken from the issue**: who participates, who is missing, who speaks for whom, and what researching *with* would mean — methodological and ethical before empirical. Exclusions respected: no fieldwork, no stakeholder contact, no assumption that "the community" is one actor, no method chosen before the question is scoped **[D — issue text]**.
- **What was retrieved, and how.** `source_read` with signed Web Bot Auth requests, 26 November 2026, plus two municipal open-data exports downloaded whole and counted locally with `sha256` recorded, because a rendered table cannot be recounted by a reviewer **[D, [`working/datasets.md`](working/datasets.md)]**. Access boundaries are recorded in §10, not worked around; the robots.txt exception was not needed for anything cited.
- **Read depth is stated for every source** in [`sources.md`](sources.md). Two items that carry argument are **abstract-level only** (`M2` White 1996; `M5` Spada & Peixoto 2025) and are used only for what their authors say they found. One is a **reprint** of Arnstein 1969 (`M1`), not the 1969 journal setting, with faulty OCR; quotations follow the reprint. One is a **relay**: Fricker's definitions are quoted from a peer-reviewed article that quotes them with page numbers (`M3`), not from the book.
- **Two method sources were only partly secured.** Walking interviews rest on a 2008 full-text methods article (`M11`), not the 2011 *Applied Geography* paper this note first sought, which stayed at record depth; community mapping rests on a 2011 full-text project account (`M12`), while the canonical "whose map?" critique (`Chambers 2006`) is **not established** — title verified, text blocked — so that particular argument is **not** used. §5 says which row rests on which depth.
- **This note is itself a representation act.** It maps people who did not ask to be mapped, for a commission they did not commission. §6 rule 9 exists because of that; §13 states what the note cannot do about it.

### 3.3 The counts, and what they are counts of

**(a) The greening register.** 124 records; catalogue `modified` 2026-11-20 **[D, `N1`,`N2`]**. Status: "Kans of wens" 56, "Lopend" 21, "Uitgevoerd" 20, empty 16, "Afgevoerd" 9, "On hold" 2 **[D, computed]**. Intake channel: `geco` 39, `bewonersi` 31, empty 21, `collega` 11, `anders` 11, `buitenBet` 10, `bouwteam` 1 **[D, computed]**. Only one code is defined in the catalogue — "**geco = gebiedscoordinator**" — and the rest carry no published definition, so "31 residents / 10 external parties" is an **inference from the token**, not a documented label **[D for the absence of definitions; I for reading `bewonersi`]**. Resident involvement: hoog 19, redelijk 24, matig 26, laag 22, empty 33 **[D]**, against a field description that omits `matig` and publishes no criterion for any level **[D, `N1`]**. Planned year: **20** records (2025: 11, 2023: 6, 2024: 3), not the 14 this repository previously reported; 2024 + 2025 = 14, the likeliest arithmetic of the earlier figure **[D/I]**. Intake dates run 2023-05-23 to 2026-09-15 across 90 dated records **[D]**.

**(b) The cross-tabulation that matters.** Of the 19 "high involvement" records, 13 arrived via an area coordinator, 4 via a resident initiative, 1 via a building team and 1 with no origin; of the 31 resident-initiated records, 20 are rated only "matig" **[D, computed]**. Three readings are compatible with those numbers: coordinators may enter precisely the wishes where residents are already strongly engaged; the rating may track how well the coordinator knows the file; or the rating may be an artefact of a scale that is nowhere defined **[I, unresolved]**.

**(c) A contradiction inside one dataset.** Its description says the 13 "Actief ontharden" locations are "aangedragen door bewoners"; in the data, 6 have no intake channel, 5 are `geco`, 1 an external party and 1 a resident **[D, `N1`,`N2`]**. The register's prose and the register's codes are two different statements about the same records **[I]**.

**(d) The green-poor register.** 70 records across nine neighbourhoods; the indicator is defined as "Buurten met minder dan 75m² openbaar groen per woning" **[D, `N3`]** — a distributional measure with a unit, a denominator (per dwelling) and a threshold, and the only such measure found in this research **[D]**. It contains one residents' initiative verbatim (Limbeek-Zuid) and two management arrangements: a "beheerovereenkomst Gemeente en Stichting Bloemenbuurt Noord en bewoners" for a Bloemenplein garden, and a Tivoli garden "door buurtbewoners gerund, grond van Woonbedrijf" **[D, `N4`]**. Coding caution: Limbeek-Noord appears both as "Limbeek-Noord" and "Limbeek Noord", so any per-buurt count is fragile **[D]**.

**(e) What none of these counts contains.** No participant, attendee, age, tenure, income, household, language or contact; no field in which a resident speaks; no field for "was the person who asked involved in the execution" **[D]**. No record contains an e-mail address **[D]**. Ten of the 124 free-text descriptions contain literally "??" **[D, delegated line, re-countable from `N2`]**.

### 3.4 Who is structurally absent, and what the record can and cannot show

| Group | What the Eindhoven record shows | Documented exclusion? |

| **Renters** | Nothing. No green register records tenure. **The honest statement, in one sentence:** for Eindhoven's green places these groups are absent from the record because the record was not built to see them, and the three documented exclusions found anywhere are a subsidy that requires ownership, a national survey that excludes non-Dutch speakers, and a participation process that did not approach disabled residents **[D]**; treating any of that as proof that a particular Eindhoven garden excludes anyone would be an over-claim **[U]**.

**A second-order absence that changes the map: the people who said no.** Nothing retrieved records a refusal — not a declined invitation, not a lapsed volunteer, not a neighbour who objected **[D — negative]**. The nearest thing is a Bennekel resident who had to remove parts of her street garden after neighbours complained: "wat voor de één een groene oase is, voor de ander een obstakel of rommel" **[D, cross-ref [`../practices/note.md`](../practices/note.md)]**. A participation map built from initiative records will always over-represent consent **[I]**.

### 3.5 The comparator, read closely: what Rijnvliet's own participants say about who is missing

This is the only retrieved study in which residents of an edible neighbourhood are quoted, and it is therefore the best available picture of what a participation map looks like when the mapper does reach people — and of what it still misses. It is a comparator, not evidence about Eindhoven **[D, `N15`]**.

- **The method, stated plainly.** Survey, 134 responses, of which 106 valid after cleaning; recruitment by "Convenience, Snowball, and Voluntary Response Sampling"; flyers, digital outreach and in-person contact; interviews with five residents plus two conducted by a colleague, four of the seven recruited from survey respondents who volunteered; all interviews in English **[D, `N15`]**.
- **The method's own reach failure, recorded in its own tables.** The survey was offered in five languages and "no responses were submitted in Arabic, Turkish, or Ukrainian" **[D]**; only one respondent was not a resident and was excluded, so the "open-access" neighbourhood's non-resident users are absent from a study designed to include them **[D]**; and the population comparison shows Moroccan residents at 38.7% of the neighbourhood against 3.7% of the sample, Turkish at 19.7% against 3.7% **[D]** — a table whose population column sums to 125%, so at least one figure is a coding error the thesis does not reconcile **[D/I]**.
- **The participant voice.** Open-ended answers name the exclusion in the residents' own words: "**Involving everyone, actively. Not just a select club of hip white vegans.**"; "**Social cohesion; mixing rich and poor in the neighborhood has not succeeded. The school actively helped with segregation.**"; "**On an equal opportunity basis, I would prioritize social rent people with picking from the gardens. Nb this is not about me; I have enough resources and live in owner-occupied housing.**" **[D, `N15`]**
- **And the mechanism, from an interview:** "**Now maybe we need to connect all the different people, because the street ambassadors are only the highly educated white people. Maybe we need more connection—it's not only about the people who are living here.**" (I4, middle-aged Dutch woman) **[D, `N15`]**
- **The informational gatekeeper.** Harvesting knowledge travelled through "Residents WhatsApp group" (19% of respondents) and word of mouth, with a measured association between valuing community and using those channels (p = 0.022 and p = 0.025) **[D, `N15`]**; one resident said plainly: "**That many people, like us, still don't know when you can and may pick/eat what. Information boards would be very nice for this, as I don't feel the need to be in all kinds of WhatsApp groups.**" **[D, `N15`]**
- **And the finding that cuts against this note's own thesis.** 56.2% of respondents had no interest in being involved and 59% chose "not at all involved" for a defined role **[D, `N15`]**. Presence in a place is not a wish to govern it **[I]**.

**What this changes.** The Rijnvliet study did the thing this note says is missing — it asked people — and it still under-reached the residents who were not socially connected, could not answer in one of five languages, or were not residents at all **[D/I]**. The lesson for method is not "ask people" but "the channel decides the sample", and the residents themselves say which channel that was **[I]**. The issue's phrase "the Rijnvliet study" should also be corrected: it is an MSc thesis, not a peer-reviewed article **[D, `N15`]**.

### 4.2 The municipal grammar, read closely

- **Participation is defined as something a municipal body starts.** *Inwonersparticipatie* = "het op initiatief van een bestuursorgaan betrekken van inwoners, bezoekers, bedrijven, maatschappelijke organisaties, overheidsorganisaties of belanghebbenden bij de voorbereiding, uitvoering of evaluatie van gemeentelijk beleid" **[D, `N10`, art. 1]**. A residents' initiative is therefore not "inwonersparticipatie" in the ordinance's sense at all; it is something the ordinance does not name **[I]**.
- **The strongest rung is explicitly not decision-making power.** *Meebeslissen*: "deelnemers zijn vanuit hun rol weliswaar niet formeel bevoegd om besluiten tot nemen, maar hebben een doorslaggevende invloed op de besluitvorming" **[D, `N10`, toelichting]**. The four terms are imported from *Factor C*, a national **communication** framework **[D, `N10`]** — a plausible explanation for why the ladder tops out below Arnstein's partnership rung **[I, `M1`]**.
- **Who counts as a resident is tenure-neutral; who counts as a participant is not.** "inwoners: ingezetenen als bedoeld in artikel 2 van de Gemeentewet" — registration, not ownership **[D, `N10`]**. But the one green instrument that reaches a household *as* a household is the de-paving subsidy, and it is owner-only **[D, cross-ref [`../participation/note.md`](../participation/note.md)]**.
- **The green rules gate on the land, not on the people.** A request to manage municipal green of "groenwaarde middel of hoog" "wordt afgewezen" unless management passes to "natuur- en terreinbeherende instanties of partijen voor wie de groen- en natuurwaarde vooropstaat" with demonstrably guaranteed quality **[D, `N11`, art. 10]**. Nothing in the article asks who the requesting community is; the test is the parcel's green value and the manager's professional standing **[D/I]**. The parcel classification sits on a *groenwaardenkaart* (bijlage 2) **[D, `N11`]** — a map, not a conversation.
- **Evaluation is scheduled but not yet possible.** The ordinance is evaluated "vierjaarlijks" (art. 8) and entered force 30-06-2026, so no evaluation under it can exist yet **[D, `N10`]**. What does exist is a *project-level* evaluation the city itself commissioned — the one that records the disability exclusion **[D, `N13`]**. "No evaluation found" is a finding about the instrument's age, not about the city's practice **[I]**.

### 4.3 What a producer-side record does to "who speaks" — and a correction to the record

The Woensel-West platform is the clearest case, and it cuts both ways. It is run by Stichting Woensel-West, which also "beheert de Buurtinfowinkel in het Spilcentrum Boschdijk" **[D, `N7`]**. Its directory of 47 entries includes **Gemeente Eindhoven, Woonbedrijf, WIJeindhoven, Trudo and Wijk GGD** alongside nine "buurtgroepen" **[D, `N7`]**, and its history timeline is written around a housing corporation's programme ("1995 Trudo komt in de wijk"; "2011 … Trudo brengt samen met bewoners, bewonersorganisaties, gemeente en collega-corporaties de Visie Woensel-West uit") **[D, `N6`]**. That makes it, exactly as the issue says, a participation instrument and an interested party, not a neutral observer **[D/I]**. It is also the place where a resident's story becomes public at all — seven stories, 16-05-2025 to 13-10-2026, several about named individuals, with an explicit invitation to submit one **[D, `N5`,`N8`]**. Both things are true, and a note reporting only one of them is not describing the platform **[I]**.

**A correction offered because it bears on method.** The sibling evidence base recorded (26 October 2026) that the platform's residents' story section was **empty** **[C, provisional, `../../eindhovenbase` report 22 — used as a lead only]**. At retrieval on 26 November 2026 the section held seven items **[D, `N8`]**. Either content was added in the intervening month or the earlier retrieval saw a filtered view; this note uses the November state and flags the disagreement rather than silently updating it **[D/I]**. It is a small demonstration of how quickly a map of "who is on the record" goes stale.

**And an internal inconsistency of the same producer:** the timeline dates the primary school's "best school of Brabant" recognition to **2013** **[D, `N6`]**, while the news item dates it to **2015** **[D, `N9`]**. Two pages, one editor, one fact, two dates — the ordinary condition of a community platform, and a reason not to treat its claims as a record of what happened **[I]**.

### 4.4 What is not there at all

No minutes, no member vote, no election, no budget decision by residents and no conflict record were found for any Eindhoven green initiative in the sources searched **[D — negative]**. Where a decision is documented, it is documented as one person's or one board's **[D]**. This is the same finding as the continuity and meaning notes, arrived at from the representation side, and it is why §5's most ambitious method is also the least feasible here **[I]**.

## 5. Method options, costed

Read as a menu of costs, not a recommendation. Each row states what the method demands of participants, what it demands of organisers, what it returns, and its characteristic failure mode. The literature is a lens on method design; none of it is evidence about Eindhoven **[M-series]**.

| Method | Demands of participants | Demands of organisers | What it returns | Failure modes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Co-research / participatory action research** | sustained involvement in framing, collecting, interpreting and publishing — weeks, not hours; exposure to disagreement with neighbours | a paid or protected coordinator, a data agreement, a dispute route, and willingness to be corrected in public | a question the group owns; findings it will use; capacity that outlasts the project | invitation without power — the classic "empty ritual of participation" that "maintains the status quo" **[D, `M1`]**; participation that "may take place for a whole range of unfree reasons" and can entrench existing relations **[D, `M2`]**; co-production practised "in an extractive way: knowledge is treated as data that can be aggregated and understood in abstract and universal form" **[D, `M13`, abstract]**; the community partner bearing recruitment and cost while the institution keeps the data and the lead role — "Universities are in a position of power in co-produced research … they have often secured and hold the funding for any project, and it is likely that an academic will be the project lead" **[D, `M14`]**, with remedies proposed in the helicopter-research literature (subawards, shared resources, PI credit) **[D, `M4`]** |
| **Walking interviews / go-along** | 45–90 minutes outdoors at a time they choose; being recorded while moving; if the route is fixed, walking someone else's route | route design (the studied example designed the route to give "locations for sheltering from adverse weather"), two-person teams, transcription, a reason the walk is not surveillance | place-specific knowledge, routes, hazards and memories a map does not hold; recurring data about the same locations | the trade-off is explicit: "Fixed routes do, however, lose the empowering element that Carpiano notes with the participant-led 'go-along'" **[D, `M11`]**; recording location creates "an uncomfortable 'Big Brother is watching you' total surveillance quality … questions about the power relations between interviewer and interviewee" **[D, `M11`]**; and the exclusion this note cares about — mobility impairment — is **not covered** by the retrieved source **[U]** |
| **Photovoice** | photographing their own place over weeks, then selecting and captioning; showing their view to an audience | devices, **consent for people who appear in the photographs**, a curation session, an exhibition decision, storage and deletion | the participants' own framing of "their community's strengths and concerns", critical dialogue in groups, and a route to policymakers **[D, `M9`, abstract]** | "Photovoice projects often present unique challenges related to subjects who appear in photographs and ensuring that consent is collected from them" **[D, `M10`]**, alongside vulnerability, recruitment and retention difficulty, interpretive complexity, and the emotional impact on researchers **[D, `M6`]**; image ownership **[U]** |
| **Community mapping** | an evening or two of drawing, naming and arguing over a map | facilitation, base material, and a decision about what happens to the map afterwards | a competing account of the neighbourhood's green, including places the register lacks; the retrieved account reports that participatory GIS "does not merely document community knowledge, but can promote mutually beneficial co-learning" **[D, `M12`]** | the same source names the cost: "Such partnerships are challenging for the same reasons they are powerful: the joining of parties with different incentive structures, bases of accountability and cultures of knowledge" **[D, `M12`]**; a tidy map that looks like consensus and is then used as a plan without the mappers **[I]**; the canonical "whose map?" critique could not be retrieved, so that specific argument is **not** made here **[U]** |
| **Deliberative session / mini-public** | a day or more, plus reading; deliberation with strangers | recruitment, venue, facilitation, payment, and a written answer to the recommendations | a considered judgement with reasons, and a record of where opinions moved | representativeness the method cannot support: "small sample sizes, group effects …, sampling biases from population lists, and low acceptance rates" **[D, `M5`]**; cost; an answer that arrives after the decision **[I]** |
| **Record-return (cheapest; a proposal, not a validated method)** | reading one page about themselves and saying what is wrong | one letter or visit per record-producer; a correction log | verified names, dates and corrections at near-zero cost to residents; a test of whether the register is wrong | reaches only people already in the record, so it cannot find the absent; may read as an audit; produces corrections, not relationships **[I]** |

**Three constraints that bind any choice, from the evidence rather than from taste:**

1. **There may be no body to co-research with.** The case at the centre of this programme produced an audience and a practice, not a membership **[D, cross-ref [`../meaning/note.md`](../meaning/note.md), [`../continuity/note.md`](../continuity/note.md)]**. Co-research presupposes a group able to mandate representatives; nothing retrieved shows one exists **[U]**.
2. **The cheapest methods are the ones the record can bear.** A record-return exercise asks people to correct a public record about a *place*, creates no new personal data about them, and can be stopped at any point **[I]**. Photovoice and walking interviews create new personal data and new obligations, and should not come first **[I]**.
3. **The method with the strongest equity claim carries the highest exclusion risk.** Walking, photovoice and evening deliberations all select for mobile, Dutch-speaking, time-flexible people — the profile that the national education skew and the local disability exclusion already describe **[D, `N14`,`N13`]**. A method that selects the same people again will produce a confident map of the already-visible **[I]**.

**What the local record adds to the costing, from the city's own evaluation of a participation process:** residents asked for "meer tijd … voor achterbanraadpleging" and the municipal team concedes it worked to its own timetable rather than the city's **[D, `N13`]**; a residents' sounding board disagreed with the minutes of its own meetings, so "de verslagen nooit vastgesteld werden" — a dispute about the record itself **[D, `N13`]**; and the team concludes that "de diversiteit van meningen binnen 1 doelgroep groter [is] dan tussen 2 verschillende doelgroepen" **[D, `N13`]**. That last sentence is the most useful single line in the file for anyone about to build a "community" category **[I]**.

### 5.1 What each option would have to change to reach the people who are absent

None of the six reaches the structurally absent as it is normally practised, and saying so is cheaper than discovering it in the field. Stated per option, as a design condition rather than a plan **[I]**:

- **Co-research** would have to be constituted with people who are not already organised — which means paying for time, meeting where people already are, and accepting that the group may not be representative of anything. The risk is manufacturing a group and then calling it the community **[I]**.
- **Walking interviews** would have to hand the route to the participant, provide transport and an interpreter, and be paired with people who cannot walk far or at all. The retrieved study's own design shows the trade-off: a fixed route buys comparability and loses "the empowering element" of the participant-led walk **[D, `M11`; I]**.
- **Photovoice** would have to settle, before the first photograph, who may appear in a picture and who owns it — the published difficulty is precisely "subjects who appear in photographs and ensuring that consent is collected from them" **[D, `M10`]**. It reaches people who will not speak in a meeting, and exposes them **[I]**.
- **Community mapping** would have to be run in more than one language and with materials people can actually use, and it would have to publish what it did *not* map. Its documented strength is co-learning between parties with "different incentive structures, bases of accountability and cultures of knowledge" **[D, `M12`]**; that is also its documented difficulty **[D, `M12`]**.
- **Deliberation** would have to recruit by sortition rather than by invitation, and to answer in writing. The representativeness it is usually credited with is exactly what the method's critics say it cannot deliver **[D, `M5`]**.
- **Record-return** cannot reach the absent at all. That is its honest limit, and the reason it is cheap **[I]**.

## 6. Ethical guardrails for the rest of the programme

Rules a reviewer can check a future note against. 1–2 restate the repository's existing rules because they bind every method above; 3–10 are new here.

1. **No identifiable personal story without permission.** No names, roles or addresses in any output unless the person agreed, in writing, to that specific publication **[I, house standard]**.
2. **No inferred motives or diagnoses.** What a person says is evidence of what they say; any conclusion about why is marked **[I]** or not written **[I, house standard]**.
3. **Absence is reported as absence from the record, never as exclusion from the place.** Use "not established in the sources searched"; never "excluded" without a document that says so **[I, §3.4]**.
4. **A minimum-cell rule for anything published by neighbourhood.** No count below five, and no combination of attributes that identifies a household, in any table published from this programme — because a street of twenty households plus a role plus a date is an identification **[D, `M7` deductive disclosure; I for the threshold]**.
5. **Consent for the *research*, separately from consent for the *activity*.** Being in a garden's photographs, WhatsApp group or mailing list is not consent to be a research participant, and the reverse is also true **[I]**.
6. **Nobody is asked to represent a constituency they do not control.** If a keeper is interviewed, the note says "a keeper", not "the community"; if a board speaks, it speaks for the organisation — the ladder's own author warns that "neither the have-nots nor the powerholders are homogeneous blocs" **[D, `M1`; I]**.
7. **Benefit-sharing is specified before collection, not after.** What participants get — a corrected record, a paid facilitator, a shared meal, a copy they can veto — goes into the plan, and unpaid participant labour is a cost to be justified rather than assumed **[D, `M4`; I]**.
8. **Data minimisation and a deletion date.** No audio beyond transcription; no image of a third party without that person's own consent; no list of "who did not participate" in any form **[D, `M7`; I]**.
9. **The map is a representation, and the note says so.** Any participation map this programme publishes carries a line stating who produced it, from which records, and who is not in it **[I, §2]**.
10. **The right to refuse, including the right to refuse to be a case.** A group may decline to be researched, withdraw afterwards, and veto the use of its name; a refusal is a result and is reported as one **[I]**.

**A caution about importing the CARE principles.** CARE (Collective benefit, Authority to control, Responsibility, Ethics) is written for Indigenous data governance and states that "the current movement toward open data and open science does not fully engage with Indigenous Peoples' rights and interests" **[D, `M8`]**. Its principles are a useful *analogy* for a neighbourhood seeking control of its own data, and rule 7 borrows the idea of collective benefit from it. It is not a framework this programme may claim to apply, and using its vocabulary for a Dutch neighbourhood would be a category error unless a community asked for it **[I]**.

## 7. Counter-evidence and tensions

1. **The most visible voice is not the most powerful, and the record cannot tell them apart.** Trefpunt Groen is the city's most visible green intermediary, holds the only city-wide mandate, and is advisory; the Wielewachters "hebben meegedacht" **[D]**. Visibility and power run in different directions, and a map built from visibility will rank Trefpunt first **[I]**.
2. **The register's coordinator-heavy pattern has an innocent explanation.** Area coordinators exist to convert residents' wishes into municipal records, so `geco` entries with "high involvement" may be evidence of a working intake rather than substituted voice **[I]**. What would settle it: the intake procedure, or one coordinator's account of one record **[U]**.
3. **Non-participation may be a preference, not a barrier.** In the nearest comparator, 56.2% of survey respondents said they had no interest in being involved and 59% chose "not at all involved" for a defined role **[D, `N15`]**. If that generalises, "who is missing" is partly a question about people who do not want the thing, and a programme treating absence as a deficit will patronise them **[I]**. The counter-consideration sits in the same study: residents also complain that "It is often a select group that participates in activities and makes themselves heard" **[D]** — the wish for wider participation and the wish not to participate coexist **[D]**.
4. **A foundation platform can be genuine participation and an interested party at once.** §4.3 documents both. The tempting correction — to prefer informal, resident-only channels — would remove the only place where a resident's story becomes public at all **[D/I]**.
5. **"With, not on" is itself a demand on people's time, and the literature has said so for decades.** Arnstein records residents "demanding a fee for research interviews" because "Nothing ever happens with those damned questions, except the surveyor gets $3 an hour, and my washing doesn't get done that day" **[D, `M1`]**. White's warning is stronger: participation "may also be the means through which existing power relations are entrenched and reproduced", and it "may take place for a whole range of unfree reasons" **[D, `M2`, abstract]**. The local instance is the Stadhuisplein evaluation's finding on reaction time and *achterbanraadpleging* **[D, `N13`]**.
6. **Inviting the organised strengthens the organised.** Every route in §4 rewards the party that can produce a legal person, a board, a registration or a *draagvlak* statement **[D, `N10`,`N12`]**. That is a structural bias toward the already-constituted, and it is how a participation map becomes self-fulfilling **[I]**.
7. **Documentation and privacy pull against each other.** The most useful participation map is a map of who is absent, and in a street of twenty households that map is an identification **[D, `M7`; I]**. Rule 4 in §6 is a partial answer, not a solution.
8. **The research can itself be the tokenism it diagnoses.** A programme that publishes "the community was consulted" with no decision attached sits on Arnstein's consultation rung, where participation is "a window-dressing ritual" and people are "primarily perceived as statistical abstractions" **[D, `M1`]**. The only protection available is that this programme's outputs are falsifiable and its non-findings published — which is what this note tries to be **[I]**.

## 9. Who should be asked first — and who should not be asked yet

A sequencing proposal with reasons, not a contact plan. Nothing here has been sent to anyone **[D — scope]**.

**Ask first, in this order:**

1. **The record-producers, about their own record.** The area coordinators who entered 39 of 124 wishes, and the colleague who maintains the register: not "who is the community" but "what do your codes mean, and may we quote your definitions". *Why first:* they hold the only city-wide artefact, they can answer at zero risk to residents, and their answer bounds every later claim **[I]**.
2. **The intermediary whose boundary is the finding.** Trefpunt Groen: what is the *achterban*, and would a food forest be eligible? *Why:* it is the documented edge of the city's green network, and the answer is a fact about the network rather than about a person **[I, cross-ref [`../network/note.md`](../network/note.md)]**.
3. **The platform's editorial desk.** Stichting Woensel-West: what does the redaction do to a submitted story, and what does it hold that it has not published? *Why:* it is a documented channel for a resident voice, and asking the editor is asking about a procedure, not about a person **[I, `N5`–`N9`]**.
4. **The keepers already named in public records**, one at a time, with one question about the record rather than about themselves (the record-return in §5). *Why fourth:* it needs a scoped question and a consent route this phase has not designed **[I]**.

**Do not ask yet, with reasons:**

- **Individual volunteers and neighbours of the closed food forest.** No scoped question exists, the group's existence is **[U]**, and the experiments note already requires a consent gate that has not been built **[D, cross-ref [`../experiments/note.md`](../experiments/note.md); I]**.
- **Care participants at Wasven or Phood.** A different risk class under care-quality regimes; a research conversation is not a day activity, and consent inside a care relationship is not straightforwardly free **[D, cross-ref [`../network/note.md`](../network/note.md); I]**.
- **People who never came.** They have no channel that does not run through the gatekeeper whose reach is the thing in question; asking them via the garden reproduces the gatekeeping **[I]**.
- **The closed groups** (Facebook, WhatsApp). Login-walled, members-only, and out of scope by rule; the exclusion is a boundary, not a gap to be worked around **[D, §10]**.
- **Anyone named only in a municipal record.** Being the subject of a staff coding is not consent to become a research participant; approaching them would convert an administrative record into a contact list **[I, §6 rule 5]**.
- **"The community" as the commission addresses it.** No documented body could receive that address, and treating the commissioner's own network as the community would import exactly the sampling problem §3.4 describes **[I]**.

## 12. Implications for the research programme

1. **"Community as the foundation" should be restated as three communities, because the record keeps three apart:** the participants (largely undocumented for Eindhoven), the record-producers (documented, and decisive for what the record says), and the intermediaries (documented, and not representative of the gardens) **[D/I]**. A programme that says "community" while meaning the third will build on the wrong foundation **[I]**.
2. **Any participation figure this programme publishes should carry its producer and its register.** "31 residents" is a count of staff codes; "19 with high involvement" is a count of an undefined rating **[D, `N1`,`N2`]**. That is a citation rule, not a caveat.
3. **The three experiments in the BRIEF need a return step, not a bigger sample.** Each already has a consent gate; each should add what the participants get and how they can correct the record **[I, cross-ref [`../experiments/note.md`](../experiments/note.md)]**.
4. **The cheapest next move is a correction, not a method.** Ask the register's producers what their codes mean and whether the Limbeek-Zuid record is right; ask the platform's redaction what it holds. Both are questions about documents, answerable without touching a resident, and both bound the map **[I]**.
5. **Do not let the programme's own outputs become the next "community" record.** If a later note cites this map as evidence of who participates, this note will have manufactured the artefact it warned about **[I, §6 rule 9]**.

## 13. Limits

- **One city, one month, public records.** Everything here describes what Eindhoven's public record says in November 2026, and records go stale within weeks — §4.3 is the demonstration **[D]**.
- **No participant voice, by design and by scope.** The note can say what is missing; it cannot describe it. Where it characterises absence, it does so as **[U]** **[I]**.
- **The comparators are comparators.** The Rijnvliet case differs in tenure and scale — a 15-hectare municipal edible neighbourhood against a 0.7-hectare food forest on private land — and the study the issue calls "the Rijnvliet study" is a **Wageningen MSc thesis of 27 March 2025** (supervisors Sovová and Pilarová, part of the CULTIVATE project), not a peer-reviewed article **[D, `N15`]**. That matters for the weight it can carry, and the thesis's own population-comparison table sums to 125% across categories, so at least one figure is a coding error it does not reconcile **[D, `N15`]**. A peer-reviewed Rijnvliet study was searched for and not found **[U]**.
- **The literature is a lens, and two lenses are missing.** §5's failure modes rest on what was retrieved; the care-ethics lens (Tronto) and the canonical "whose map?" critique (Chambers) were both blocked, so neither is used. Two sources are abstract-level only (`M2`, `M13`) and two are abstracts of a paper whose full text exists (`M5`, `M9`); the Fricker definitions are second-hand (`M3`) **[D/U, §10]**.
- **The note is written by an outsider for a commissioner.** It cannot substitute for the account the group would give of itself, and it should be read as a map of the mapmakers **[I]**.
