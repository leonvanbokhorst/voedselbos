# Which open questions would change the decisions — #15

Drawn from `../continuity/note.md`, `../network/note.md`, `../participation/note.md`, `../visibility/note.md` (incl. `cultural-intermediaries.md`) and `../comparators/ecodorp-boekel.md`, with the documents those notes cite. No stakeholder contact. **[D]** = documented; **[disagree]** = the notes or sources conflict; unmarked judgements are this note's reasoning.

## 1. Whose decision, and what is on the table

Three decision-makers, not one. **The coordinator**, who holds the audience, the practice and the invitation list. **The volunteer group** — it exists, has no channel, and its continuation is unverified. **Counterparties** — municipality, funders, peers — whose decisions the first two can only invite.

What is publicly settled is less than it looks. A November 2026 newsletter under the practice's brand announces a new place "als een kruidentuin" and a workshop on 26 November 2026 at Student Base, High Tech Campus. Nothing found creates an entity, names a site, or uses the name "2.0" **[D]**. One decision is already taken in public: *not a food forest*. The open one is what "herb garden" means.

## 2. The test

A question earns research only if the decision is **expensive or slow to reverse** and the evidence could **flip** it. Most listed items fail the first test: joining a network, listing on a map, co-hosting an evening, requesting a parcel in writing are cheap and reversible, so they should be *done*, not studied. Research value sits in land, keepers and legal form.

**Which experiments to support.** One whose cost is below the cost of being wrong about the decision it tests, and whose result is visible within a season. A Groen Café edition tests D5 cheaply; a workshop at a corporate campus tests a professional audience, not a neighbourhood; a herb-garden pilot on borrowed ground tests nothing about tenure and repeats the failure.

## 4. Decision-critical, and merely interesting

**Critical (four):** ground with written tenure; a second keeper; herb garden as place or product; what volunteers do when asked concretely. The rest is downstream.

**Interesting, not decision-relevant yet:** the movement's visibility and identity (presupposes a collective that does not exist); Dunantpark's organisers and tenure (only if D5 becomes a network decision); Trefpunt Groen's internal governance; Boekel's full legal architecture (only the property-holding split matters, and only at D3); the burnout literature, already sufficient to act on; "who should be the face of the movement" — a question with no addressee.

**Settle one disagreement first.** The newsletter says "Eindhoven heeft géén voedselbos meer"; the record documents a residents' food forest in het Henri Dunantpark — a defensible distinction or an error **[disagree]**. A network option begins with credibility among peers.

## 5. What not to ask yet

- **"What should the movement become?"** — no collective exists to answer; you get preferences about a brand.
- **Volunteers, "what should 2.0 be?"** before options with costs exist — wishes, not commitments. Ask for a specific act.
- **The municipality for a location** before there is a legal person and a named keeper; the request will be filed and answered with "meedenken".
- **Funders**, before there is an object to fund and someone to account for it.
- **Whether to formalise**, before knowing whether two people will serve.
- **Peers for solidarity**, before correcting the claim about the city having no food forest.

## 6. A conversation guide

Eight questions, in order, for Maartje and whoever else turns out to be deciding:

1. In twelve months, what exists that does not exist today: a place, a programme, or a product?
2. If you were ill for three months, who would be answerable?
3. What did the June conversation with the wethouder actually produce — a name, a parcel, a promise?
4. Has anyone asked, in writing, for a specific parcel — and do you know the decision deadline?
5. What are you willing to stop doing to make room?
6. **What would make you decide to stop entirely?** — the question nobody asks, and the one that changes every other answer.
7. Which of the invitations you received is concrete enough to visit?
8. What should the volunteers decide, and what must stay yours?

Listen for three things: whether "kruidentuin" comes with a place or a product; whether the mailing list is offered as a commons or kept as an asset; whether a second name appears unprompted.

## 7. What research cannot decide

Whether to continue at all; appetite for risk, income and visibility; whether a parcel will be granted (political); whether volunteers will follow (only a real invitation tests it); whether a herb garden can carry a livelihood; whether the movement should have a face. Research can name instruments and deadlines, report what comparable initiatives did with them, and count what happened. It cannot choose.
