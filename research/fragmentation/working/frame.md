# Research frame — issue #27

**Question (one sentence).** To what extent is the health-promotion and prevention landscape in Eindhoven and Eindhoven–De Kempen fragmented, in what consequential ways, and what explains it; and under what conditions can autonomous initiatives contribute to shared outcomes, recognise each other's work and build reciprocal relationships without growing their own organisation or surrendering local identity?

**What a useful answer would change.** Whether Voedselbos 2.0 treats "connect to the health movement" as a joining problem, a coordination problem or a boundary problem; whether a new platform/alliance is the default response; and how the repository reads the regional "movement" it has already documented as thin (health note §3.6). It would also give the PI a defensible way to say "not established" about resident-level fragmentation instead of inferring it from a count of initiatives.

**Lens (one line).** Distinguish plurality from harmful fragmentation, and test the mechanisms at the level where the evidence sits, rather than carrying a national finding into Eindhoven.

## Propositions the answer must establish, contest or bound

- **P1 — Definition.** Fragmentation is not a count of initiatives. It is a set of observable failures: duplicated offers without a population/access difference; broken referrals or handovers; disconnected knowledge; incompatible eligibility or reporting; temporary pilots without continuity; unmet need despite overlapping offers; transaction costs borne by residents and small organisations.
- **P2 — Locus.** In this region the documented fragmentation is concentrated in the **financing and coordination architecture** (short-term, compartmentalised, project-shaped money; legal-person/consortium gates; reporting tied to a governance club), not demonstrably in duplicated front-line offers.
- **P3 — Access asymmetry.** The regional health movement offers small autonomous initiatives a **low-threshold listing door** (anyone with a name and e-mail; one criterion forbids referral/indication) and a **high-threshold partner/governance door** (organisation required; PACO; Regioraad; penvoerderschap). There is little documented in between that supports reciprocal, ongoing relationship.
- **P4 — Binding constraint.** The binding constraint on shared impact is the **funding horizon and accountability design** (money ends 2026; attribution and activity reporting; coordination is the first cost cut), not an absence of goodwill or a refusal to collaborate.
- **P5 — Plurality is real and partly beneficial.** The directory's 147 activities, 14 green/food offers and eight communal cooking/eating offers are a form of distributed access; a count is not evidence of harmful duplication.
- **P6 — Autonomy is a reason, not an excuse.** The RIVM records municipalities resisting regional agreements to protect local autonomy and because SPUK money is temporary. That is a rational response to the incentive structure, not a character flaw.
- **P7 — Conditions.** The evidence supports a contribution-based, polycentric model: shared outcome frames without shared ownership of every activity; coordination funded as a shared service with a horizon longer than the projects; lightweight participation routes that do not require legal personality; and explicit permission to hand off, decline or stop.

## Independent subquestions (delegated where independence helps)

1. **National financing/accountability.** What do the GALA/IZA/SPUK/AZWA documents establish about compartmentalisation, shared ownership and the 2026 cliff? (Line A.)
2. **Regional/local money and rules.** What does the Eindhoven–De Kempen architecture require, who is eligible, and is there award/implementation evidence? (Line B.)
3. **Mechanisms and models.** What does empirical research establish about fragmentation mechanisms and about polycentric/collaborative alternatives, including negative cases? (Line C.)
4. **The local two-door structure and its consequences.** Retrieved by the Lead: the DE STAP listing vs partner routes, the GGD's five pathways, the municipal budget, the subsidy scheme, the directory classification.

## Evidence that would settle or falsify

- **Settle P2:** a published inventory of local offers showing near-duplicate activities serving the same population/access conditions; or an audit of referrals showing repeated handover failures. **Falsify P2:** documented duplication or broken referral chains at the local level.
- **Settle P3:** an award list or application data showing small initiatives entering the consortium route; or a documented intermediate route (e.g. a neighbourhood network with shared referral). **Falsify P3:** evidence that informal groups routinely obtain IZA subsidy or governance rights.
- **Settle P4:** a funding decision extending coordination beyond 2026, or an accountability design that rewards handoff/stopping. **Falsify P4:** evidence that cooperation is rewarded and coordination survives the cliff.
- **Settle P5:** participant/usage data showing the low-demand offers reach people outside the already-engaged. **Falsify P5:** evidence that the offers duplicate each other and reach the same small group.
- **Settle P6:** a region that made long-term agreements despite the same national rules. **Falsify P6:** evidence that autonomy is not the stated reason.

## Source families

- **Official/primary:** Tweede Kamer (letter + GALA MTR), RIVM reports (2025-0062, 2026-0060), zorgakkoorden.nl, VNG/DUS-I SPUK documents, gemeenteblad/CVDR, Eindhoven municipal policy and budget, GGD BZO board papers, DE STAP/IZA monitor.
- **Independent:** academic empirical literature on fragmentation, collaborative/polycentric governance, projectification, resource dependence, psychological ownership, and integration outcomes (incl. negative cases).
- **Counterevidence:** regional self-reports of improving collaboration; cases of successful cross-boundary cooperation; evidence that plurality is beneficial; evidence that coordination bodies add layers.
- **Lived experience:** not available within the exclusions; the note must say so rather than infer it.

## Scope boundaries

- **Geography:** Eindhoven municipality and the IZA region Eindhoven–De Kempen (15 municipalities), with national policy only as the framework that sets the local rules.
- **Period:** 2023 (GALA/IZA signed; Regioplan December 2023) to November 2026. Earlier evidence only where it explains the current architecture.
- **Population/denominator:** organisations and arrangements, not individuals; no profiling, no named-person motive attribution.
- **Edition/version:** the subsidy scheme as in force 17-03-2026 (CVDR721954); the 2026 amendment; the 2026 monitors; the 2025 GALA MTR (April 2025).
- **Exclusions:** no stakeholder contact, no fieldwork, no private network data, no clinical advice, no institutional ranking, no accusation of motive, no inference of absence from public silence, no new organisational/software design.
