## 1. The answer

**Short answer: the region's health-promotion landscape is not best described as a swarm of disconnected initiatives. It is a layered coordination architecture — national agreements, a regional plan and council, a GGD-coordinated prevention infrastructure, a municipally mandated SPUK and a 193-partner "movement" — that is real, recent and partly working. What fragments is not the offer but the conditions under which the offer can be joined up: money that is time-limited and, until October 2025, compartmentalised, coordination that is a project rather than a service, and three doors — a free listing, a light partner affiliation, and a legal-person consortium route — with almost nothing between the light affiliation and the heavy one.**

That answer has three parts, and they should not be collapsed.

**First, the fragmentation that is documented is architectural, not behavioural — and the architecture moved while this report was being written.** The GALA mid-term review (April 2025) states that the broad SPUK had "15 gescheiden onderdelen waarvan de daaraan verbonden middelen beperkt uitwisselbaar zijn" and "een intensieve administratieve last", and that because part of the money is temporary, "gemeenten zelf aan zet zijn voor het opzetten van een langlopende aanpak" [D, `MTR` p.6]. The first half of that diagnosis was acted on within six months: from 29 October 2025 "De schotten tussen de verschillende onderdelen zijn vervallen", municipalities can spend the total allocation across all themes, and per-part SiSa reporting ended [D, `STCRT`]. The second half was not: the same amendment cut the 2026 budget by 10%, and the regeling lapses on 16 July 2027 [D, `STCRT`]. From 2027 a new ontschotte SPUK AZWA/HLO (€1.53bn for 2027–2029, one integral budget, conditional on a college-approved *werkagenda*) replaces it [D, `SPUK27`]. **One fragmentation mechanism was dismantled; the other was tightened.** In Eindhoven–De Kempen the municipal prevention budget is **€4,229,000 in 2026, of which €3,582,000 (85%) is SPUK-GALA**, and **€739,000 in 2027**, because "er zijn geen structurele verplichtingen ten laste van de SPUK-GALA die doorlopen na 2026" [D, `EIND` §D]. The region's coordination operator was dissolved on 1 January 2026; the GGD holds a **one-year** penvoerderschap for 2026 on **€1.2 million** that ends that year, with "structurele borging … na 2026" still requiring a decision [D, `GGD`]. These are not symptoms of institutions defending turf. They are the predictable output of a system that funds projects for fixed terms and counts activity rather than relationship — and that has now removed one accounting barrier while leaving the time horizon in place.

The region says so itself. Its 2026 *Herijking regiobeeld* records, as a lesson from the 2023 Regioplan: "we zien ook veelheid aan activiteiten belegd in verschillende thema's (**fragmentatie**) … Met verschillende samenwerkingsverbanden en governance structuren … Zonder werkelijke focus op de uitkomsten en hoe deze bijdragen aan het grote doel van het regioplan" [D, `HERIJKING` slide 2]. That is not a researcher's inference from a distance; it is the region's own project group, whose participants include the GGD, CZ, VGZ, the municipality, care providers, a village cooperative and the care-sector employers' association Transvorm, diagnosing the architecture it built [D, slide 3]. It is the single most important local source in this report.

**Second, the local record does not establish harmful duplication or broken referral chains.** What it establishes is a large, diverse offer: 147 Eindhoven entries in the regional directory, 52 of them *Ontmoeten* and 47 *Hulp en advies*, with nine communal cooking/eating entries and four garden/plant entries, three of them gardens, all filed under *Ontmoeten* [D, `EHV` — counted]. That is a lot of activity, and it is not evidence of failure. The directory's own access page requires no organisation, no referral and no indication — one of its four criteria is "Het is zonder verwijzing of indicatie toegankelijk" [D, `LIST`]. For a resident, that is a genuinely low-demand route. Whether the offers duplicate each other, reach the same small group, or leave needs unmet is **not established in the sources searched** [U]. A count of initiatives is not a finding of fragmentation, and this note does not treat it as one.

**Third, the binding constraint on shared impact is the funding and accountability design, not the absence of goodwill.** The regional movement's own monitors report growing collaboration and, in the same period, a decline in regional agreements, shared decision-making and financial agreements between municipalities and insurers; the RIVM's 2026 synthesis says municipalities and insurers "nog weinig keuzes en afspraken maken over wie waarvoor verantwoordelijk is" [D, `GALA25` table 2.1; `RIVM26`]. The 2025 GALA monitor reports that regional and local relationships are "onder druk" from national developments, producing "terughoudendheid om voor de lange termijn samenwerkingsrelaties aan te gaan" [D, `GALA25` p.11]. The region's own conclusion for 2026 asks for "het consistent verankeren van de regio-ambities/impactdoelen in keuzes en monitoring", "het versterken van de data-infrastructuur" and "versterk hiervoor de samenwerking tussen thematrekkers, projectleiders en monitoringteam" [D, `STAPCON`]. Those are coordination tasks with no dedicated long-term home.

**The regional money is not a crowded competition; it is a closed door.** In spring 2025 the region received exactly **three** applications — GGD Brabant-Zuidoost, Stroomz Zorgt and Coöperatie Slimmer Leven — totalling €2.5 million against a €2.2 million ceiling, and the ceiling was raised to fit them. The advice note is explicit that the raise is tied to those applications and that "geen andere partijen een beroep kunnen doen op de ophoging" [D, `AWARD25`]. The same operation was repeated for 2026 [D, `GMB`]. So the scheme did not ration scarce money among many suitors; it accommodated the incumbents. **The exclusion, if any, is at the door — the requirement to be a legal-person penvoerder of a Regioplan-derived consortium — not in the queue.** That distinction matters: a claim that the region's money "competes away" small initiatives would be unsupported. A claim that the entry rule is built for incumbents is documented.

**What follows for the second half of the question.** The evidence supports a **contribution-based, polycentric** answer rather than a new umbrella: shared outcome frames that do not require shared ownership of every activity; coordination funded as a shared service with a horizon longer than the projects it coordinates; participation routes that do not require legal personality; and explicit permission to hand off, decline or stop without being scored as failing. That is an inference from the retrieved architecture and the empirical governance literature (§3.7), not a demonstrated local model. The evidence does not support creating a new alliance, platform or merger as the default response — and it does not support the opposite conclusion either, that the existing plurality is already a working ecosystem. The honest finding is narrower: **the connective layer exists, is easier to enter through a listing than through a funded partnership, and is currently financed in a way that makes it unlikely to outlast the projects it connects.**

**What is established, contested and not established:**
- **Established on retrieved evidence:** the time-limited character of the national money and the removal of its compartments in October 2025; the 2026→2027 municipal funding cliff; the one-year regional coordination transition; the legal-person/consortium/penvoerder gate on the regional subsidy; the open, referral-free listing route; the partner route's organisation requirement; the directory's classification of gardens as meeting places; the absence of a food forest or community garden from the 193-name partner list.
- **Contested:** whether regional collaboration is improving or weakening. The RIVM's summary says cooperation improved; its own table shows several agreement indicators declining between 2024 and 2025; the regional monitor says cooperation is growing. The disagreement is partly about object (operational collaboration vs formal regional agreements), partly about method (self-report), and it is itself a finding about how the system knows itself.
- **Not established in the sources searched:** harmful duplication of local offers; broken referral or handover chains experienced by residents; resident navigation burden or repeated information requests; whether the 193 partners do anything together beyond being listed; whether any small initiative has tried and failed to enter the consortium route; whether the region's collaboration would survive the 2026 cliff.
- **Not knowable with the methods used:** whether named actors want territory, prestige or control. The exclusions forbid inferring motive, and nothing retrieved would support it.

## 3. The evidence

### 3.1 What would count as fragmentation, and what would not

The issue asks for the observation to be defined before it is tested, and this is the right order. The retrieved record supports seven observable manifestations, each of which can fail visibly:

1. **Duplicated activities without a justified population or access difference.** Two offers are duplicative when they serve the same people, in the same way, at the same time, and neither adds access, language, disability, age or geography.
2. **Missing or broken referrals and handovers.** A person is eligible for a next step that no one is responsible for making happen.
3. **Disconnected knowledge and learning.** The same lesson is discovered in several projects and not transferred; monitoring data are not comparable across themes.
4. **Incompatible eligibility or reporting requirements.** A small organisation must hold a legal form, a consortium agreement and a quarterly reporting rhythm to be funded, while a large one already has them.
5. **Repeated temporary pilots without continuity.** The activity ends when the subsidy ends, and the relationship ends with it.
6. **Unmet needs despite overlapping offers.** The offers exist, but the people with the highest need are not in them.
7. **Transaction costs borne by residents and small organisations.** The effort of finding, entering and staying in the system is shifted onto the least resourced.

The same record also supports four reasons why a plurality of initiatives is not a failure: local specificity, culturally and linguistically appropriate offers, distributed experimentation, and multiple entry points. The question is therefore not "how many initiatives are there" but "which of the seven failures can be observed, for whom, and at what cost". **This note applies that test and finds that the local record documents manifestations 4 and 5 most clearly, 3 partially, and does not document 1, 2, 6 or 7 at all.** That distribution is itself the answer.

### 3.2 National: an architecture that produced compartments — and then removed them

The GALA mid-term review is the primary document the issue asks for, and it is narrower and more careful than the parliamentary letter that transmits it. It says plainly that it is "nog te vroeg om conclusies te trekken over behaalde resultaten" [D, `MTR` p.3]. What it does conclude concerns the conditions for collaboration:

> "De brede SPUK kent 15 gescheiden onderdelen waarvan de daaraan verbonden middelen beperkt uitwisselbaar zijn en kent ook een intensieve administratieve last. Daarnaast is een deel van de GALA middelen tijdelijk, waardoor gemeenten zelf aan zet zijn voor het opzetten van een langlopende aanpak." [D, `MTR` p.6]

and, earlier:

> "de wijze van financieren (brede SPUK met diverse verantwoordingsregels) belemmert de integrale uitvoering in gemeenten." [D, `MTR` p.4]

**That was the April 2025 position. It is no longer the October 2025 position.** On 29 October 2025 the brede SPUK was amended: "De schotten tussen de verschillende onderdelen zijn vervallen"; municipalities can now use "het totaal toegekende uitkeringsplafond per gemeente … voor alle onderdelen en thema's", and "Bij de SiSa-verantwoording hoeven dus ook niet meer de financiële gegevens per onderdeel ingevuld te worden" [D, `STCRT`]. The same amendment cut the 2026 budget by 10%, froze indexation, added €43 million from AZWA/HLO, and left the regeling to lapse on 16 July 2027 [D, `STCRT`]. From 2027 a new, ontschotte SPUK AZWA/HLO — €1.53 billion for 2027–2029, "één integraal budget", allocated ambtshalve but conditional on a college-approved *werkagenda* due 8 December 2026 — replaces it [D, `SPUK27`]. **One fragmentation mechanism, the compartments, was dismantled within six months of the MTR; the other, temporariness, was not.** The RIVM's 2025 conclusion is that "de bekende knelpunten rondom incidentele middelen voor preventie en structurele borging, nog niet zijn opgelost" [D, `GALA25` p.90].

**The compartmentalisation verdict is a citation chain, not an independent finding.** The MTR attributes it to the VNG's 2024 *uitvoeringstoets*, based on interviews and two sessions with 14 municipalities and one samenwerkingsverband; RIVM's monitor relays it [D, `MTR` p.6; `GALA25`]. The VNG's own summary is sharper than the MTR's: "Helaas sluit de financiering via een tijdelijke specifieke uitkering (SPUK) met 15 onderdelen en beperkte ruimte voor verschuiving en flinke verantwoordingslasten hier niet bij aan", and "Rechtmatigheid lijkt belangrijker dan doelmatigheid" [C, `VNGTOETS`]. That is a documented policy judgement by an interested party, not an independent measurement. It is still evidence — of what the implementers experienced — but it cannot carry the weight of an observed effect.

Two further passages matter for the mechanism. The first is about professional worlds: "Partijen functioneren binnen verschillende systeemwerelden met elk hun eigen manier van financieren en eigen regelgeving" [D, `MTR` p.4]. The second is about mutual dependency in the five ketenaanpakken: "Wanneer één van beide domeinen haar aandeel niet levert of kan leveren, dan stokt al snel de inrichting van de keten" [D, `MTR` p.4]. That is a structural account of why a chain can fail without anyone behaving badly.

The review is also explicit that it does not prescribe a structure: "Het GALA (en IZA) geeft geen blauwdruk hoe deze samenwerkingsstructuur er uit moet zien. Elke regio kan deze inrichten passend bij de regio" [D, `MTR` p.5]. This is the design decision that produces variation between regions — and, in a region without a strong convening capacity, may produce fragmentation by default rather than by intention [I].

The parliamentary letter adds a finding that the GALA MTR text does not itself contain. It says the picture "komt overeen met de bevindingen vanuit de MTR IZA", and that "in sommige regio's ontbreekt het aan gedeeld eigenaarschap op het eindresultaat" [D, `LETTER`]. **The issue brief attributes that shared-ownership finding to the GALA mid-term review; the retrieved GALA MTR does not contain the phrase, and a full-text check of the IZA MTR's covering letter (Kamerstuk 31765 nr. 852) did not find it either. The IZA MTR itself was not retrieved, so where the phrase originates is not established [U].** This is a small provenance correction with a larger methodological point: the sentence that travels furthest in policy summaries is the one whose underlying document is least often read. It is also why this note treats "compartmentalised" and "shared ownership" as two different claims from two different documents, not as one finding.

The RIVM's 2025 monitor gives the mechanism a direction and a cost. It reports both sides in the same summary: collaboration has strengthened, "onder andere doordat partijen uit verschillende domeinen een gezamenlijk doel hebben, taken en verantwoordelijkheden duidelijker of beter belegd zijn en doordat leerervaringen worden uitgewisseld"; and "regionale en lokale samenwerkingsrelaties staan onder druk door landelijke ontwikkelingen. Dit leidt tot terughoudendheid om voor de lange termijn samenwerkingsrelaties aan te gaan en stagnatie bij ketenaanpakken en andere GALA-activiteiten" [D, `GALA25` p.11]. The same report records municipalities "die terughoudend zijn in het maken van regionale afspraken. Zij houden vast aan hun eigen autonomie en lokaal beleid … of vanwege de tijdelijkheid van de SPUK-middelen" [D, pp.25–26].

The most striking evidence in the 2025 monitor is a table the summary does not lead with. Among mandated municipalities, the proportion agreeing that decision-making on the design and implementation of agreements is done jointly fell from **67% to 36%**; agreement on dividing responsibilities from **55% to 43%**; on who coordinates implementation from **60% to 48%**; and on financial agreements from **52% to 41%** (n=42 in both waves) [D, `GALA25` table 2.1]. Over the same period, agreements with frontline professionals about responsibilities rose from **37% to 46%**, and about referrals and coordination from **27% to 39%** [D, p.24]. **The pattern is not "collaboration is collapsing". It is "operational collaboration is improving while formal regional governance is thinning".** That is a more precise and more consequential finding than either the optimistic or the pessimistic summary, and it is the kind of tension the repository's method exists to preserve.

The 2026 third measurement, read at abstract level because the full PDF could not be retrieved (see §5), sharpens the same point: "Regionaal is te zien dat gemeenten en zorgverzekeraars nog weinig keuzes en afspraken maken over wie waarvoor verantwoordelijk is. Dit vraagt om meer daadkracht van de partijen." It adds that people with health problems in difficult circumstances (e.g. debt) remain hard to reach, that municipalities face uncertainty about structural funding, and that the advice differs by level: "Op landelijk niveau zijn rust, stabiliteit en duidelijkheid gewenst. Regionaal is regie, coördinatie en een gezamenlijke visie nodig. Lokaal is het belangrijk de plannen gaan uitvoeren, waarbij maatwerk mogelijk is." [D, `RIVM26`, abstract].

One further national finding belongs here because it names the felt consequence for the smaller actor. Asked what VWS could do, interviewees asked for "het bundelen van versnipperde subsidies en middelen om meer effect te sorteren en om te voorkomen dat regio's ondersneeuwen in allerlei losse projecten en trajecten" [D, `GALA25` p.34]. That is a direct, documented statement that the project form itself is experienced as fragmentation — not by critics, but by the regions implementing it.

### 3.3 Regional: a coordination architecture that is real, and project-shaped

Eindhoven–De Kempen has built more coordination than the "thin network" finding in the repository's earlier notes might suggest. The Regioplan was signed in December 2023 by 15 municipalities, care and welfare partners and the insurer; a **Regioraad** was established to steer the agreements; Eindhoven is the mandated municipality and penvoerder for the IZA-SPUK; and a programme office was set up [D, `EINDSUB`; `EIND` §B]. The GGD states that "In de regio Eindhoven – de Kempen heeft de GGD de taak gekregen om dit proces regionaal vorm te geven en te coördineren" for the five ketenaanpakken [D, `GGD5`]. A regional monitor tracks nine themes across activity, result, impact and transition layers [D, `STAPLW`].

The map's most important feature is what it leaves out: the volunteer green commons is represented by one closed initiative, and the health architecture's green members are all staffed organisations. That is not a complete network map — it is a map of the **publicly documented** architecture, and the absence of the commons from it is the finding.

That architecture has several features that matter for fragmentation.

**(a) The money is a consortium instrument, and the consortium is pre-defined.** The subsidy scheme funds only "een penvoerder, zijnde een rechtspersoon, namens een samenwerkingsverband voortkomend uit het Regioplan" [D, `CVDR` art. 3]. The municipal application page is blunter: "Aanvragen is alleen mogelijk voor IZA-partners, die nu al penvoerder zijn in de samenwerking voor het Regioplan en zich verbonden en verenigd hebben rondom één van de genoemde onderdelen" [D, `EINDSUB`]. The scheme does say a consortium "staat te allen tijde open voor het aansluiten van nieuwe partijen" [D, `CVDR` art. 1], but the entry condition is to be already inside. **For a community garden or a volunteer group, this is not a door with a high threshold; it is a door on the other side of the building.**

**(b) The obligations are governance obligations.** A funded consortium must "actief aansluiten bij de partner community (Paco) van DE STAP naar gezonder", participate in Regioraad meetings and their preparation, deliver a quarterly progress report, cooperate with monitoring, and cooperate with the municipality's IZA-SPUK accountability [D, `CVDR` art. 10]. These are reasonable conditions for a regional programme. They are also a second job for a small organisation, and they help explain why the route is used by organisations that already have the capacity [I].

**(c) The horizon is the project horizon.** The scheme funds activities from 1 January 2024 to 31 December 2026 and "vervalt op 1 januari 2027" [D, `CVDR` arts. 5, 14]. The ceilings are €2.07m (2024), €2.5m (2025) and €2.5m (2026), allocated first-come-first-served, with a lottery for same-day oversubscription [D, art. 9]. The first-come rule is efficient; it also rewards applicants who are already prepared, which is another way of saying it rewards incumbents.

**(d) Coordination is the first thing that becomes temporary.** The region's operator, Coöperatie Slimmer Leven, was dissolved on 1 January 2026. The GGD board paper records that "Met het opheffen van de Coöperatie Slimmer Leven per 1 januari 2026 komt het penvoerderschap van de subsidieprogramma's Vitale Communities en Bewustwording te vervallen", that the GGD takes it over "voor een overgangsperiode van één jaar (2026)", on "een subsidie van € 1,2 miljoen", with subsidies running "tot eind 2026", and that "structurele borging van deze themalijnen bij een passende organisatie na 2026" is still to be worked out [D, `GGD`]. The municipal budget confirms the cliff: €4,229,000 for 2026 (85% SPUK-GALA) against €739,000 for 2027, with "geen structurele verplichtingen ten laste van de SPUK-GALA die doorlopen na 2026" [D, `EIND` §D].

**(e) Concentration without competition.** The award record is the part of the architecture that rules alone cannot show. In spring 2025 the region received three applications — GGD ("Programma Gezond opgroeien en leven voor iedereen"), Stroomz Zorgt ("De STAP naar Samen Precies") and Coöperatie Slimmer Leven ("Vitale communities en bewustwording") — totalling €2.5 million against the €2.2 million ceiling. The advice note proposing the raise says the increase "is direct gekoppeld aan de ingediende aanvragen … geen andere partijen een beroep kunnen doen op de ophoging van het plafond" [D, `AWARD25`]. The 2026 amendment repeats the operation in a single article, raising the 2026 ceiling to €2.5 million, with no explanatory note [D, `GMB`]. **Three payees in 2025, a ceiling raised to fit them, and a repeat raise for 2026.** This is the opposite of a competitive grant market. It is a small club of implementing partners whose plans set the budget.

**(f) The region diagnoses its own fragmentation.** The 2026 *Herijking regiobeeld* is a working document of the regional project group (GGD, CZ, VGZ, the municipality, care providers, a village cooperative and the care-sector employers' association Transvorm), dated 20 April 2026. Its second slide states the lesson of the 2023 Regioplan: "Maar we zien ook veelheid aan activiteiten belegd in verschillende thema's (fragmentatie) — Met verschillende samenwerkingsverbanden en governance structuren — Zonder werkelijke focus op de uitkomsten en hoe deze bijdragen aan het grote doel van het regioplan" [D, `HERIJKING` slide 2]. Slide 4 proposes "een nieuw samenwerkingsmodel waarbij een bestuurlijke groep de lange lijnen bepaalt (strategie) en de uitvoering is belegd bij een uitvoeringsorganisatie" [D, slide 4]. **The region's answer to its own fragmentation is further concentration of strategy and execution — a centralising move, not a pluralist one.** That is a legitimate response to the problem it names, and it is also the move the issue warns against making the default. The two readings are not reconciled in the document.

**(g) A vertical split by design.** The GGD and STROOMZ form a regional programme team of five project leaders and a programme manager, working on "de regionale kaders waarin we streven naar samenhangend aanbod voor alle inwoners van de regio". Then: "**Gemeenten pakken de implementatie op basis daarvan zelf op** of ontwikkelen verder door in samenwerking met betrokken partijen" [D, `GGDUITLEG`]. This is a deliberate division of labour, and it explains why municipalities defend local autonomy: they are the layer that is supposed to adapt the regional frame to local circumstances. It also creates the seam where fragmentation lives — between a regional frame and fifteen local implementations, with no shared instrument for what happens across the seam.

**(h) Money returned while coordination is unmet.** The 2025 municipal accounts record realised IZA baten of €3,371k against an entitlement of €4,436k, with unspent money flowing back to the Rijk; the retrieved figures do not fully reconcile, and this note treats the exact amount as provisional [D, `JAARSTUKKEN` via line B; U]. The direction is nonetheless consequential: **the region can underspend a ring-fenced prevention budget in the same year its own monitor says the connective work — shared goals, data infrastructure, collaboration between theme leads — is what remains undone.** Absorptive capacity, not only money, is the constraint.

The regional monitor's own conclusion is consistent with this picture, though it reads it more positively. It reports "Samenwerking groeit", "Gezamenlijke verantwoordelijkheid groeit zichtbaar", and that "de regio voortgang boekt op de kritische bouwstenen van een transitie". For 2026 it asks for "het consistent verankeren van de regio-ambities/impactdoelen in keuzes en monitoring, het opschalen van initiatieven en het versterken van de data-infrastructuur", and "Versterk hiervoor de samenwerking tussen thematrekkers, projectleiders en monitoringteam" [D, `STAPCON`]. **The tasks that remain undone are exactly the connective tasks: shared goals, shared data, and coordination between the people who lead the themes.** And the monitor is produced by the GGD, which is also the coordinator [D, `STAPCON` footer] — a self-report, not an independent evaluation.

### 3.4 Local: three doors, and very little in between

The most useful local finding is a contrast between three ways of entering the regional health movement, all documented on the same website on the same day.

| Who can enter | "Naam (verplicht)" and "E-mail (verplicht)"; no organisation field, no legal form [D, `LIST`] | "Naam organisatie" required; no fee or obligation published [D, `PARTNER`] | A legal person as penvoerder of a Regioplan-derived consortium, already an IZA partner [D, `CVDR` art. 3; `EINDSUB`] |

The listing route is genuinely good news, and it is the one place where the movement has written the anti-qualification principle into its own intake. But a listing is **co-presence, not relationship**. The partner route is lighter than it looks — an organisation name and an e-mail, no published fee or duty — and that is precisely its limit: it carries affiliation without obligation, recognition or resource. The penvoerder route carries money and governance, but requires a legal person, a Regioplan-derived consortium and a quarterly reporting rhythm. **There is no documented route in between in the retrieved sources: something that carries a referral, a reciprocal obligation or a recognised contribution without either being a logo on a page or becoming a funded consortium.** That missing middle is the most concrete answer this research can give to "what enables shared impact without surrendering local autonomy" — the region has built a very light door, a very heavy door, and not much in between.

The directory count makes the same point from the other side. Of 147 Eindhoven entries, the gardens are filed as places to meet:

- **Buurtmoestuin Irisbuurt** — *Ontmoeten*
- **De Vergeten Tuin** — *Ontmoeten*
- **Buurttuin** — *Ontmoeten*
- **'t Kruidenkafé Kalmoesplein** — *Ontmoeten* (a GGzE day-activity venue named for the *Kruidenbuurt*, not for cultivated herbs [D, health note §3.7])
- **Adopteer een stukje groen** — *Klussen*

and all nine cooking/eating entries — "Samen eten", "Samen koken in het kookatelier", "Samen koken en eten" (twice), "Soep koken", "Open soep", "Samen aan tafel bij de Buurtkeuken", "Kookclub voor mannen", "Koekjes bakken" — are *Ontmoeten* [D, `EHV` — counted]. No garden is categorised as health, learning or prevention. That is not a criticism of the directory; *Ontmoeten* may be the honest category. It is evidence that **the region's public classification system has no slot for "growing food as a health-relevant practice"**, which is exactly the slot a food forest would need.

The partner list shows the same boundary from the institutional side. Of 193 named partners, the green organisations present are IVN natuur educatie, Het Bos In, De Groote Heide and Wasven; **no food forest, community garden or buurttuin appears** [D, `PARTNERS` — list read and searched]. Wasven — a staffed, care-contracted, ANBI-structured organisation — is a partner; the volunteer commons is not. The existing bridge runs through **institutionalised** green care, not through volunteer growing. Absence from a public list is not absence of collaboration, and this note does not claim that no garden has ever spoken to a health partner. It claims something narrower and more useful: **the public architecture of the movement lists no volunteer green commons, and the green organisations inside it are the ones that already look like institutions.**

### 3.5 What is plurality, not fragmentation

The same evidence that could be read as fragmentation reads as plurality, and the difference is not rhetorical. The directory is large (147 entries), varied (eight categories), local (sub-neighbourhood locations), and cheap or free ("meestal gratis" [D, `EHV`]). It includes offers for specific groups — Iranian/Farsi community, LGBTQ+, people with a disability, informal carers, parents, older people — that a single programme would not produce. The parliamentary letter's own observation that "er bestaat vaak geen 'one size fits all' aanpak" [D, `LETTER`] supports reading this as distributed access rather than duplication.

The counterweight is that **variety at the offer level coexists with uniformity at the access level**: almost everything is a place to meet, a walk, a course or a repair café, delivered by an organisation with a room and a time. The offers that require the least — a garden you can enter when the crew is there, a communal meal at €0–7 — are the ones least visible as health. **The plurality is real; what is missing is not more offers but a way of recognising the low-demand ones as part of the health infrastructure.**

The region has tested the plurality hypothesis itself, cautiously. The 2026 Herijking analyses three citizen initiatives — Zorgcoöperatie Hoogeloon (20 years), Dorps Coöperatie Meer Riethoven (about 10 years) and the Steensel initiative (2 years) — against their municipalities, the region and national data. It finds that "De drie dorpen scoren positief op kenmerken van sociale samenhang bij volwassenen tot 65 jaar", while the picture on health behaviours is negative or mixed [D, `HERIJKING` §8.1–8.2]. It then states the limit plainly: "Binnen de reikwijdte van dit onderzoek is het niet mogelijk om vast te stellen of er een causaal verband is tussen de inwonerinitiatieven en het verschil in uitkomsten omdat grotendeels alleen openbare data beschikbaar was op dorp, gemeente en regioniveau." It adds that a structurally better score for longer-running initiatives "geeft dit een indicatie van het mogelijke positieve effect" [D, §8.1]. **That is the right way to read the beneficial-plurality case: an indication, from ecological data, that long-lived local initiatives may be associated with social coherence — not evidence that they produce health, and not evidence that a new initiative would.**

The health note's finding that the region's health platform files gardens under *Ontmoeten* and classifies no listing as health education or literacy [D, health note §3.7, P23] is therefore not an anomaly. It is the same boundary seen from the classification side. The question "why does health promotion fragment into separate initiatives" has a partial answer here: **it fragments because the categories that organise the money and the directory are not the categories in which the practice actually creates health.** Growing food is filed as meeting; meeting is funded as social base; social base is a different budget line from prevention. The practice crosses the boundary; the instruments do not.

### 3.6 Consequences for residents: what is documented, and what is not

The issue asks for actual consequences: ease of discovering and accessing opportunities, referral continuity, trust, choice, unmet needs, repeated requests for the same information, and the effort of navigating the system.

**What is documented:**
- **Reach is partial and uneven.** In 2024, valpreventie reached 2,013 residents with a risk test and 106 with a preventive exercise intervention; the GLI started 95 of 148 registrants and 19 moved on to regular sport; Welzijn op Recept, started on 2 June 2025, had 31 referrals by the time of writing, of which 16 turned out to involve more than a single question and were referred on to WIJeindhoven, 5 already had a generalist, and 10 were helped by a phone call or a referral to the social base [D, `EIND` §B]. These are reach and throughput figures, not navigation or unmet-need figures.
- **The hardest-to-reach are named as hardest to reach.** The 2026 RIVM synthesis says it "blijft vooral lastig om mensen met gezondheidsproblemen die in moeilijke omstandigheden leven, bijvoorbeeld door schulden, mee te laten doen aan de initiatieven" [D, `RIVM26` abstract]. That is a national finding, not an Eindhoven measurement.
- **Residents are involved in planning, less afterwards.** The RIVM interviews find that "Inwoners worden wel geleidelijk meer betrokken bij de planvorming, maar minder in de fasen daarna", and that regions are still searching for how to involve a representative group beyond informing and advising [D, `GALA25` p.32].
- **The municipal evaluation cannot attribute health gain.** It scores projects 0–8 on "gezondheidswinst" and says explicitly that "het meten van gezondheid(swinst) is complex … moeilijk om het causale verband aan te tonen tussen een bepaalde interventie en het resultaat en/of effect op de gezondheid van de inwoner" [D, `EIND` §B]. The scores are self-assessed against eight indicators, not observed effects.
- **One instrument did not reach residents at all for months.** The fitcoins project started on 1 July 2025 after a European tender, but "Vanwege technische problemen is het platform nog niet gerealiseerd. Hierdoor kunnen inwoners zich nog niet aanmelden in de app" [D, `EIND` §B]. That is a concrete delivery gap, not a fragmentation finding, but it is the kind of cost that a resident experiences as "the system does not work".

**What is not documented:** whether residents experience the system as fragmented; whether they are asked for the same information twice; whether a referral from a GP to Welzijn op Recept, or from a neighbourhood team to a garden, actually completes; whether the 147 offers reach beyond the already-engaged; and whether unmet need is concentrated where offers are densest. **These are not knowable from the documents retrieved, and they are precisely the questions the issue says should decide the answer.** The note therefore reports them as a gap, not as a negative finding.

The one indirect local signal worth naming is the health note's finding from the *Groen Verbindt* evaluation: year after year 9 in 10 volunteers agreed they had met new people in the neighbourhood, 5 in 10 that they were more ready to help others, and only **4 in 10** that they dared ask more people for help [D, health note §3.4, its source `E8`]. If a health-promotion system's weakest outcome is asking for help, then a fragmented landscape's cost is not only navigation; it is that people who most need to ask are least likely to. That is an inference across a different programme and population, and it is labelled as one.

### 3.7 What the empirical literature establishes about the mechanisms

The literature answers the two halves of the question with unequal strength, and the inequality is itself a finding. **The conditions under which collaboration is more or less likely to work are documented extensively. The effects of collaboration on health or organisational outcomes are documented thinly, and several of the strongest reviews say so in their own conclusions.** Any sentence of the form "research shows partnership works" is unsupported; a sentence of the form "research identifies these conditions, and the effect evidence is weak and mixed" is supported. **Verification note:** every row except Corbin is abstract-level and child-reported; the Lead re-read only the Corbin full text. The status column describes the state of the literature, not this run's verification of each paper.

Its value is the demonstration that exclusive attribution is not empirically available even with a large budget and a research team. |

**What this means for the question.** Three mechanisms are directly relevant to Eindhoven's architecture and are supported at the right level:

1. **Projectification** explains why a system that funds time-limited projects struggles to maintain permanent coordination. The local scheme is a three-year project instrument; the coordination it depends on is a one-year bridge. The literature says this is a design mismatch, not a people problem.
2. **Coordination burden** explains why collaboration dissolves even when everyone values it. The local obligations — consortium agreement, quarterly reports, Regioraad meetings, monitoring, IZA-SPUK accountability — are exactly the "ex-post interdependence" the literature identifies as the cost that ends collaborations. The answer is to streamline the burden, not to add commitment.
3. **The weak outcome evidence** explains why the region's monitors measure process and disclaim attribution. That is not a failure of the region's evaluators; it is the state of the field. It also means that no one can promise that a new coordinating body would improve health.

The individual and group mechanisms (intrinsic motivation, psychological ownership, status, trust) are best read as **reasons to design for autonomy and recognition**, not as explanations of named actors. The local record does not measure any of them, and this note does not infer them.

### 3.8 What would enable shared impact without surrender — the conditions the evidence supports

The evidence supports designing for conditions, and it supports **contribution-based accounting** rather than exclusive attribution. It does not support a claim that any coordination model reliably produces shared outcomes. Stated as conditions, with the evidence behind each:

1. **Bridge the time frames.** Coordination must be funded on a horizon at least as long as the work it coordinates. The projectification literature is explicit that temporary implementation inside permanent organisations fragments continuity unless there are "interlinking mechanisms"; the local record shows the opposite — a three-year subsidy scheme and a one-year coordination bridge. **Condition: a coordination budget that does not expire before the relationships it supports.** [D, Nilsen & Feiring; Godenhjelm et al.; local: `CVDR` art. 14; `GGD`]
2. **Relieve the coordination burden.** The difference between sustained and dissolved collaborations in the inter-municipal evidence was management that streamlined the burden of joint working [D, Elston et al.]. The local scheme adds obligations at every layer (consortium agreement, quarterly report, Regioraad, monitoring, IZA-SPUK). **Condition: the administrative cost of participating is proportional to the size of the participant, and shared back-office functions are provided rather than required.** [I, from the local obligations + the inter-municipal finding]
3. **Build trust through repeated interaction, and use more than one management strategy.** Trust is the strongest measured network lever, especially for process outcomes, and combined strategies outperform single ones [D, George et al.]. The local record shows operational trust improving while formal agreements thinned; that is an asset to protect, not a substitute for a written settlement. **Condition: repeated, low-stakes joint work alongside the formal agreement.** [D/I]
4. **Protect intrinsic motivation; do not tie recognition to output counts.** The individual-level evidence says output-tied incentives can crowd out intrinsic motivation [D, Cerasoli et al.]. The local scheme ties money to a plan of aanpak with KPIs and quarterly reports [D, `CVDR` art. 7]. **Condition: accountability for contribution and learning, not only for countable output; and no requirement that a volunteer practice become a programme to be recognised.** [I, from the level caveat + the local design]
5. **Make contribution visible, not only activity.** The attribution literature shows that exclusive attribution is not empirically available [D, Greenhalgh et al.], and the partnership literature shows that process evaluations dominate [D, Corbin et al.; Panjwani et al.]. The local directory makes an *offer* visible but records nothing about who contributed to what [D, `EHV`; `LIST`]. **Condition: a recognition layer that records contribution and handoff, not just a listing.** [I]
6. **Allow plurality and watch for masking.** Polycentric density can coexist with declining effectiveness, and complexity can mask failure [D, Morrison]. The region's 193 partners and 147 offers are not evidence of health; they are evidence of density. **Condition: monitor a small number of population outcomes independently of the network's own self-report.** [D/I]
7. **Permit non-collaboration, handoff and ending.** Avoidance has rational grounds and is under-reported in the collaboration literature [D, Gazley]; stopping low-value practice is an established practice with active change interventions [D, Niven et al.]; dissolution can be the rational response to ex-post interdependence [D, Elston et al.]. The local scheme rewards continuation (reporting on activities) and has no published route for handing off or ending [I, from `CVDR` art. 10]. **Condition: a funder that accepts a legitimate "no", a handoff or a closure as a successful outcome.** [D/I]

**The local application, stated as inference.** The region has a light listing door, a light partner affiliation and a heavy consortium door. The conditions above point to the missing middle: a route that carries **recognition of contribution, a referral, or a reciprocal obligation** without requiring a legal person, a consortium agreement and a quarterly report. That is not a proposal for a new organisation; it is a description of the function the current architecture does not perform. Whether it can be performed by an existing body — the GGD, the Regioraad, a neighbourhood network, or the directory itself — is a decision the research cannot make. **The evidence says the function is missing; it does not say who should hold it.**

**Recognition, in this evidence, is not a feeling; it is a record of who contributed to what, plus a repeated occasion on which that contribution is used.** The region has the first (a directory that records offers) without the second (a record of contribution), and it has repeated occasions (Regioraad, PACO, the monitor) that are organised around projects rather than around the practices that make them work. That is the precise sense in which autonomous initiatives cannot currently recognise each other's work: there is no shared record and no recurring occasion that is not also a reporting obligation.


### 3.9 Cases: cooperation that works, and disconnection that costs

The issue asks for concrete cases rather than a verdict. These are the retrieved ones; each is labelled by what it demonstrates and what it does not.

**Working cooperation.**

1. **The valpreventie chain has a real handover.** In 2024, 16 of Eindhoven's 20 neighbourhoods ran valpreventie activities; 2,013 residents took a fall-risk test and 106 entered a preventive exercise intervention. The municipality records that "voor het doorverwijzen/overdragen naar structureel beweegaanbod [wordt] samengewerkt met Eindhoven Sport" [D, `EIND` §B]. This is the clearest local instance of a chain with a named next step and a named partner. What it does not show is how many of the 2,013 were offered the next step, how many accepted, or what happened to those who declined. **It is a functioning referral route, not evidence of referral continuity.**
2. **Municipalities already blend streams in practice.** The RIVM monitor records that 76% of municipalities combine GALA money with other budgets, and gives two concrete examples: SPUK plus management budget for dementia-friendly walking routes, and a sewer project plus SPUK plus provincial subsidy for a play-and-meeting place [D, `GALA25` p.73]. **The compartments constrained accounting, not local ingenuity** — which is why removing them in October 2025 is a real simplification.
3. **The regional kader is a working coordination product.** The GGD and STROOMZ have a five-project-leader programme team and a regional framework per pathway, which municipalities then implement locally [D, `GGDUITLEG`; `GGDJR`]. That is a division of labour that produces something reusable, and it is the strongest argument for the vertical split. **It is a framework, not a delivery record.**
4. **The three-payee consortium has continuity of a kind.** GGD, Stroomz and Slimmer Leven each hold a defined programme; when Slimmer Leven dissolved, the GGD took over its penvoerderschap for 2026 and the partners signed a *garantstelling* [D, `GGD`]. **The layer bent rather than broke — by concentrating further.**

**Consequential disconnection.**

5. **The coordination bridge is one year long.** The GGD holds the penvoerderschap for 2026 only, on €1.2 million that ends that year, with structural anchoring after 2026 undecided [D, `GGD`]. The municipal prevention budget falls from €4,229,000 to €739,000 in 2027 [D, `EIND` §D]. **The person who coordinates the region's prevention infrastructure has a one-year mandate; the relationships they coordinate do not.**
6. **The local scheme has no door for a community practice.** The subsidy requires a legal-person penvoerder who is already an IZA partner leading a Regioplan consortium [D, `CVDR` art. 3; `EINDSUB`]. A garden can be listed, and can be a partner, but cannot hold the pen or receive the money on its own terms. **The disconnection is structural: there is no route that carries money, referral or co-governance to an informal practice.**
7. **An instrument can be funded and not reach anyone.** The fitcoins project started on 1 July 2025 after a European tender; by the time of the evaluation the platform was not built, so "inwoners zich nog niet kunnen aanmelden in de app" [D, `EIND` §B]. A separate cardiovascular approach was still in conversation among all parties about whether to start [D, `EIND` §B]. **These are delivery failures, not fragmentation failures — but they are what a resident experiences as a system that does not work.**
8. **The directory cannot see the practice as health.** The gardens are filed under *Ontmoeten*; no listing is categorised as health education or prevention [D, `EHV` — counted]. **The classification is the disconnection**: the practice is visible, but not as something the health system would refer to.
9. **The partner list contains the institutional green bridge, not the volunteer one.** Wasven, IVN, Het Bos In and De Groote Heide are partners; no food forest or community garden is [D, `PARTNERS`]. **The existing bridge runs through organisations that already have staff and contracts.**

**What the cases add up to [I].** The region is good at building **frameworks, consortia and directories** — the products of coordination — and weak at sustaining **the relationships and handovers** that make them work for a resident or a small practice. That is the same pattern the mechanisms literature predicts: projectification produces products, not continuity; coordination burden falls on the smallest participant; and the outcome evidence is too weak to tell whether the products work.


## 4. Counter-evidence and tensions

1. **Collaboration improved; formal agreements thinned.** The RIVM summary and the regional monitor both report stronger collaboration in 2024–2025, and the same RIVM report's table shows declines in joint decision-making, responsibility division, coordination agreements and financial agreements among mandated municipalities [D, `GALA25` p.11 and table 2.1]. The reconciliation is not a contradiction in the sources: the improving items are operational (agreements with frontline professionals rose from 37% to 46%, and on referrals from 27% to 39% [D, p.24]); the declining items are governance (who decides, who is responsible, who pays). **Operational cooperation can improve while the formal settlement weakens.** That is worse for continuity than either trend alone, because the relationships that work are the ones without a written home. Three qualifications matter. First, the unit is the *gemandateerde gemeente* (one per IZA region, n=42), not the individual municipality. Second, on the two hardest items the parties move in opposite directions: municipal respondents report fewer agreements on dividing responsibilities (55%→43%) and fewer financial agreements (52%→41%), while insurers report **more** financial agreements (44%→60%) [D, table 2.1]. Third, both waves are self-report by contact persons whose representativeness RIVM itself says is unknown, and RIVM records the possibility of "een rooskleurige voorstelling van de voortgang" [D, `GALA25` p.93]. **The direction of the municipal governance items is consistent; the meaning of the divergence between municipalities and insurers is not established, and it is the same divergence the MTR says it cannot explain.**

2. **The compartments were porous in practice, even before they were removed.** The MTR described a compartmentalised SPUK; the RIVM monitor found that **76% of municipalities** ran projects combining GALA funding with other streams or existing policy, and gives examples (SPUK plus management budget for dementia-friendly walking routes; a sewer project plus SPUK plus provincial subsidy for a play-and-meeting place) [D, `GALA25` p.73]. So the compartmentalisation was a real constraint on accounting, not an absolute barrier to local blending. **The fragmentation was in the accountability, not necessarily in the delivery.** This weakens any claim that the money could not be used integratively; it strengthens the claim that integrating it cost extra administrative work.

3. **The scheme is closed at the door but open in its text.** Article 1 says a consortium "staat te allen tijde open voor het aansluiten van nieuwe partijen"; the municipal page says only IZA partners who are "nu al penvoerder" may apply [D, `CVDR` art. 1; `EINDSUB`]. Both are true: new parties can join someone else's consortium, but cannot hold the pen. **The barrier is representation, not membership.** This is a softer finding than "excluded", and a more actionable one.

4. **There was no competition to lose.** Three applications, a ceiling raised twice to fit them, no refusals [D, `AWARD25`; `GMB`]. A narrative in which small initiatives are outcompeted by large ones is not supported by the award record. The likely mechanism is earlier: small initiatives do not apply, because the entry rule and the reporting obligations make the route unavailable or unattractive. That is an inference [I], and the record does not show whether any small initiative considered applying. **Absence of refusals is not evidence of open access.**

5. **The region's own remedy is centralisation, not pluralism.** The 2026 Herijking diagnoses fragmentation and proposes a model in which "een bestuurlijke groep de lange lijnen bepaalt (strategie) en de uitvoering is belegd bij een uitvoeringsorganisatie" [D, `HERIJKING` slide 4]. That is a direct challenge to this note's inference that a new umbrella should not be the default. The honest position is that **the evidence does not decide between strengthening the centre and strengthening the middle**: the region's diagnosis is compatible with both, and the choice depends on whether the problem is a lack of strategic focus (centre) or a lack of low-threshold connective routes (middle). The region is choosing the centre, and the literature on coordination bodies (§3.7) records both successes and cases where the new layer became another island.

6. **Plurality's benefits are indicated, not established.** The Herijking's three-village analysis finds positive social-coherence signals and explicitly disclaims causal attribution [D, `HERIJKING` §8.1]. It is ecological data at village level, with two of the three initiatives in municipalities that also provide village support. **It is a reason not to treat plurality as a failure by default; it is not a reason to claim that a new local initiative will produce health.**

7. **The monitor is a self-report by the coordinator.** The DE STAP IZA monitor is "een initiatief van DE STAP naar gezonder, uitgevoerd door de GGD Brabant-Zuidoost", and the GGD is also the regionally mandated coordinator of the prevention infrastructure [D, `STAPCON` footer; `GGD5`]. That does not make it wrong; it makes it interested. The Herijking is produced by a broader project group, but it is still a regional self-assessment. **No independent evaluation of the region's prevention infrastructure was found** [U].

8. **The partner list is broad, so absence is about category, not size.** The 193 partners include sole traders, coaching practices and small foundations alongside hospitals and municipalities [D, `PARTNERS`]. A small organisation can be a partner. The absence of gardens is therefore not evidence that small actors are excluded by size; it is evidence that **community growing is not in the partner category at all**. That is a different finding and a more precise one.

9. **The national finding is not the local finding.** The compartmentalised SPUK was a national design; the 2026→2027 municipal cliff and the one-year coordination transition are local documents. The step from "the national money is compartmentalised" to "Eindhoven's landscape is fragmented" is an inference, supported by the region's own diagnosis but not measured by it. **This note keeps the two levels separate and does not use the national finding as proof of the local one.**

10. **The policy summary is more confident than the document it summarises.** The GALA MTR says it is "nog te vroeg om conclusies te trekken over behaalde resultaten" [D, `MTR` p.3]; the parliamentary letter and the issue brief speak of a landscape that "obstructs" and lacks "shared ownership". The shared-ownership phrase is the letter's; it is absent from the GALA MTR and from the IZA MTR covering letter, and its origin is not established [D, `LETTER`; `MTR` and `KST31765` absence; U]. **The sentence that travels is not the sentence in the document.** That is a finding about how this policy field knows itself, and it should temper any confident claim built on a summary.

11. **The municipal evaluation's "all actions executed" is a status convention.** It states that "alle voorgenomen acties uit de uitvoeringsagenda Gezondheid 2024-2025 zijn uitgevoerd", while the same document records that the fitcoins platform was not live, that the cardiovascular approach was still in conversation, and that a green play-and-sport location is expected only after 2027 [D, `EIND` §B]. "Executed" means the action was started or handled, not that residents received it. **Delivery language and delivery are different objects.**

12. **The directory count cannot settle the plurality question.** 147 entries is a lot; 52 *Ontmoeten* and 47 *Hulp en advies* could be read as duplication or as choice. Without population, access and usage data, the count supports neither reading. **The note's refusal to treat the count as a finding is itself the finding.**

13. **The Regioraad's decision rights are opaque.** The scheme requires participation in Regioraad meetings and quarterly reporting to it [D, `CVDR` art. 10], but no public minutes, membership list or decision rules were retrieved [U]. A governance layer whose decisions are not publicly traceable is a fragmentation risk in its own right — not because it is illegitimate, but because residents and small initiatives cannot see how priorities are set.

14. **The funding cliff may not arrive as a cliff.** The municipal document says 2027 cover "valt veel lager uit" and that there are no structural SPUK-GALA obligations after 2026 [D, `EIND` §D], but it also says the 2027 figure is the *current* projection and that the AZWA discussions are ongoing. A new national agreement could change it. **The cliff is documented as a budget line, not as an observed outcome.**

15. **A contradiction preserved rather than resolved.** The 2025 GALA monitor reports a decline in "onderlinge waardering" between municipalities and insurers [D, p.11], while the regional monitor reports growing shared responsibility [D, `STAPCON`]. They concern different objects (national survey vs regional self-report), and the note does not average them. If anything, the disagreement is the point: **the region's experience of itself and the national measurement of the region are not the same instrument.**

16. **The national diagnosis is time-stamped, and the reform is partial.** The MTR's compartmentalisation finding was true in April 2025 and was addressed in October 2025: the schotten were removed. But the same amendment cut the 2026 budget by 10% and left the regeling to lapse in July 2027 [D, `STCRT`]. **The de-compartmentalisation and the austerity arrived in the same instrument.** A report that cites the MTR's critique without the amendment would be describing a system that no longer exists; a report that cites the amendment as a solution would be ignoring that the time horizon, the accountability for outcomes, and the local 2026 cliff are unchanged. Both errors are easy, and this note tries to avoid both.

17. **The new 2027 SPUK adds a procedural condition that may re-centralise what ontschotting decentralised.** The money is allocated ambtshalve — no application — which lowers the entry cost. But it is conditional on a college-approved *werkagenda* due 8 December 2026 [D, `SPUK27`]. That is a new planning document, approved at municipal level, in a fixed national format and on a fixed deadline. It may reduce fragmentation by forcing one local plan; it may also reproduce the project logic at a higher level. **The evidence does not yet say which.**

18. **The national instruments are almost silent on autonomous citizen initiatives.** In the AZWA's description of the "basisinfrastructuur", "Burgerinitiatieven" appears once, as an unelaborated list item alongside inloopvoorzieningen and wijkteams, without money, rights or role [D, `SPUK27`/`LOKET`]. The national layer neither enables nor obstructs local autonomy directly; it is largely indifferent to it, and its accountability runs through municipalities and insurers. **That is a finding about the instrument's blind spot, not about the intentions of the people who wrote it.**

19. **The strong version of the "autonomy" explanation is not supported locally.** The RIVM records autonomy as a *reason* municipalities give for resisting regional agreements [D, `GALA25` pp.25–26]. The local Herijking records a region choosing a stronger centre [D, `HERIJKING` slide 4]. Neither document shows an actor defending a domain for its own sake. The psychological-ownership literature documents territorial behaviour at employee level [D, `C`], but using it to explain a named local actor would be an unwarranted level jump and a motive attribution the exclusions forbid. **Autonomy is documented as a structural rationale; it is not documented as a motive.**

Their findings are presented as **child-reported, verified only against the child's verbatim excerpts and study table**, not re-retrieved. The report's argument uses them for *conditions* and *mechanism plausibility*, not for a pivotal local finding. The access failures behind them (two T&F Cloudflare 403s; publisher-elided abstracts for the founder-identity studies; PubMed cookie wall; Europe PMC 403) are recorded in §5 and in the child's file.

**How integration changed the answer:**
1. **Line A made the national finding time-stamped.** Before it, the draft presented the 15 compartments as current. The amendment of 29 October 2025 removes them, cuts 2026 by 10%, and sets up a new ontschotte SPUK for 2027. The answer now distinguishes *what was true when the MTR was written* from *what is true now*, and identifies the transition seam between the local scheme's end (1 January 2027) and the new SPUK's first beschikkingen (1 February 2027).
2. **Line A turned the compartmentalisation verdict into a citation chain.** The MTR relays the VNG's own uitvoeringstoets (14 municipalities and one samenwerkingsverband). The note now labels it a documented policy judgement by an interested party, not an independent measurement.
3. **Line B changed the competition reading.** The draft inferred that the consortium door excluded small initiatives from scarce money. The award record shows three applications and a ceiling raised twice to fit them — **concentration without competition**. The exclusion is at the door (penvoerderschap), not in the queue. This is a sharper and better-supported claim.
4. **Line B supplied the region's own diagnosis.** The 2026 Herijking's "veelheid aan activiteiten … (fragmentatie)" and "zonder werkelijke focus op de uitkomsten" became the central local source, and its proposed centralising remedy became the main counter-argument to this note's "missing middle" inference.
5. **Line B explained the autonomy finding structurally.** The vertical split — GGD/STROOMZ set regional kaders, municipalities implement locally — makes municipal autonomy a role requirement, not a turf instinct.
6. **Line C prevented an overclaim.** Its central warning — conditions are documented, effects are not — forced §3.8 to state conditions rather than a model, and forced the note to say that no coordination model can be sold on its results. It also supplied the negative cases (integration not delivering intended changes; coordination burden dissolving collaborations; polycentric density masking decline) that §4 needed.

**What was rejected or qualified:**
- Line B's "the scheme is open to new parties" was qualified: the text says a consortium is open, but the municipal page says only existing penvoerders may apply. Both are recorded.
- Line B's "underspend" was not accepted as a clean figure; it is carried as provisional with the reconciliation problem stated.
- Line C's founder-identity and recognition findings were **not established** and are not used. The psychological-ownership finding is kept at employee level and explicitly not used to explain any local actor.
- Line A's "schotten abolished" was accepted for the financial compartments, but qualified: the 15 parts remain as goals, and the 2026 cut is part of the same instrument.
- Line C's Greenhalgh case was **not** used as a "failed integration" example: the authors explicitly refuse an overall verdict, and the note says so.

**Degradation.** All three lines were time-boxed and read much of their material at abstract level. The Lead re-read the local and national primary documents in full, but not the academic literature except Corbin. The report's local findings are therefore better verified than its mechanism literature, and §7 states the consequence.


## 7. Important uncertainties and limits

**Not established in the sources searched:**
- Any local instance of harmful duplication: two offers serving the same population in the same way with no access difference. The directory's eight categories and 147 entries are consistent with both duplication and choice; no population or usage data were retrieved.
- Any broken referral or handover experienced by a resident. The documents describe incomplete *ketenaanpakken* and missing regional agreements; they do not trace an individual referral.
- Resident navigation burden, repeated information requests, or trust. No resident-level data exist in the retrieved public record.
- Whether the 193 partners do anything together beyond being listed. Partner status publishes no obligations and no activity record.
- Whether any small initiative has considered, attempted or been discouraged from the consortium route. The record shows three applications and no refusals; it does not show who did not apply.
- Whether the region's collaboration would survive the 2026 cliff. The cliff is a budget line; its effect is a forecast.
- The Regioraad's membership, decision rules and minutes. No public record was found.
- The per-applicant split of the 2025 awards beyond Slimmer Leven's €1.2m; the 2024 awards; the exact subsidy-vs-opdracht split of the SPUK.
- Any independent evaluation of DE STAP, the IZA monitor or the regional prevention infrastructure.
- Whether the directory is current: a listing is an editorial acceptance, not a live service.

**Not knowable with the methods used:**
- Whether any named actor seeks territory, prestige, funding or control. The exclusions forbid inferring motive and nothing retrieved would support it.
- What residents experience, want or would refuse. That requires consent-based inquiry, which is outside this mandate.
- Whether a garden, a meal or a walking group produces health. The health note establishes that the evidence for that is weak and design-dependent; this note does not add to it.

**Unavailable:** the Regioplan PDF (404), the IZA MTR PDF (not exposed), the full RIVM 2026 report (connection aborted; abstract only), the GGD's September 2025 evaluation of the DE STAP neighbourhoods (not public), a consolidated award register (not published), Precies' 2025 progress report (connection aborted), and the Regioraad's minutes. Each is recorded in §5 with what it could have established.

**Measurement limits.** The regional monitor is a self-report by the coordinator; the RIVM monitor is a self-report survey of municipalities and insurers; the municipal evaluation's health-gain scores are self-assessed against eight indicators and explicitly not causal; the Herijking's village analysis is ecological and explicitly non-causal. **No measurement in this note observes a resident, a referral or a health outcome.** The strongest local evidence is documentary: rules, budgets, awards, dates and the region's own diagnosis. That is enough to establish the architecture and not enough to establish its effects.

**Three further limits, stated because they qualify the national and mechanism sections.** First, the RIVM 2025 monitor has a 96% municipal response (327 of 342) and a 92% insurer response (35 of 38), but RIVM itself says it does not know whether the respondents are representative, whether they consulted colleagues, or whether they gave "een rooskleurige voorstelling van de voortgang" [D, `GALA25` p.93]. The apparent decline in regional agreements could be a change in respondents or in candour; the direction is consistent but the magnitude is not audited. Second, the 2026 RIVM report is abstract-level only; its method and limitations are unread. Third, the scholarly literature is mostly abstract-level in this run, and only Corbin was re-read by the Lead. The mechanism findings are therefore weaker evidence than the local documents, and the note uses them accordingly.

**Temporal limit.** This is a snapshot at 26 November 2026. The 2026 cliff has not yet been observed; the 2027 budget is a projection; the AZWA's local implementation is not yet observable; the Herijking's proposed model is not yet adopted. A report written after the 2027 budget would be a different report.

## 8. What would change the answer

In order of how much it would change:

1. **A consolidated award and delivery register** for the regional subsidy: who received what, for which activities, with what reach and what output. It would turn the money trail into an implementation trail and settle whether the three-payee structure delivers or merely absorbs.
2. **The Regioplan text and the Regioraad's minutes and decision rules.** They would establish the intended division of responsibility and whether the governance layer exercises real choices or ratifies them. The 404 on the Regioplan is the single most consequential retrieval failure in this run.
3. **A resident-level study of navigation and referral**, with consent: how people find an offer, what they are asked twice, where a handover fails, who gives up. It is the only evidence that could establish or falsify the issue's central concern. It is also the evidence this mandate cannot produce.
4. **The GGD's September 2025 evaluation of the DE STAP neighbourhoods.** It is the one document that would say whether a neighbourhood health approach with partners produced anything. It is referenced by the municipality and not public.
5. **The 2027 funding decision.** If coordination is funded structurally and the municipal prevention budget is restored, the cliff hypothesis weakens; if the GGD's one-year bridge ends without a successor, the discontinuity finding hardens.
6. **A documented intermediate route that works** — a referral partnership, a shared recognition scheme, a light mutual-obligation arrangement between a health partner and a community practice. One working example would change the "missing middle" from an absence to a design option.
7. **A local case of failed integration or of a coordinating body adding a layer.** The Herijking's proposed execution organisation is the obvious candidate to watch. If it centralises without improving reach, the note's preference for the middle over the centre gains support; if it improves both, the preference is wrong.
8. **The full RIVM 2026 report**, for its method and limitations and its regional breakdown. The abstract is clear; the report may qualify it.
9. **Evidence that the directory's offers reach people outside the already-engaged.** Enquiry or participation data held by DE STAP would convert the low-demand-access claim from a design property into an observed one.
10. **An independent evaluation of the regional monitor.** If the self-report is accurate, the collaboration trend is real; if it is not, the note's reading of the region's self-diagnosis needs reweighting.
11. **The 2027 SPUK AZWA/HLO regeling text and the first municipal werkagendas (due 8 December 2026).** They would establish whether the new ontschotte money changes the *door* — who can hold it, who writes the plan, who is accountable — or only the *pot*. If the werkagenda becomes the new penvoerderschap, the local gate survives the national reform, and the "missing middle" finding holds for the next period too.
12. **The Herijking's proposed execution organisation, if adopted.** It is the live test of the centralisation path: whether a stronger centre improves reach and coordination, or becomes another layer.

## 10. Implications for the main question and the three pillars

**For the main question** ("how can existing communities, green spaces and local organisations form a mutually supportive ecosystem … through shared care"): the ecosystem is not missing. It is **governed, funded and monitored — and governed in a way that makes the community practice a listed activity rather than a party.** The shared-care question is therefore not "how do we connect?" but "which door, with what obligation, for how long?". The evidence supports a narrow, defensible role for a community practice: be visible through the listing route, be useful through a concrete local relationship, and do not mistake either for membership of the health system.

**Pillar 1, De Wielewaal as central green motor:** no support from this evidence. The green organisations inside the health movement include Wasven, IVN, Het Bos In and De Groote Heide; the Wielewaal is not among them; the regional frame is about care pathways, not a municipal estate; and the municipal prevention budget is a care-and-social-base budget, not a green-estate budget. The pillar remains a hypothesis, as the health and climate notes also concluded from their directions.

**Pillar 2, a city-wide ecosystem:** the connective layer is real and larger than the repository's earlier "thin network" finding: 193 partners, a regional plan, a Regioraad, five GGD-coordinated pathways, a monitor and a directory. But it is **a consortium plus a directory**, with a light affiliation in between and no route that carries obligation without legal personality. "Connecting to the health movement" should therefore be specified as one of three acts — a listing, a partnership, or a consortium — because they carry different rights, costs and risks. The evidence does not support a new platform; it supports using the existing light doors deliberately and asking for a middle route where a real handover is needed.

**Pillar 3, nature closer to everyday life:** this is where the evidence lands most strongly. The directory's 147 offers, the €0–7 communal meals, the gardens open when the crew is there, and the listing criterion that forbids a referral requirement are all documented instances of **low-demand access to green and food**. They are the pillar's asset. The classification gap — growing filed as meeting, no slot for food-growing as health-relevant — is the specific thing that could be changed without building anything: a category, a description, a recognition, not a programme.

**Community as foundation:** the health architecture's categories do not contain the community's practice. That is not a reason to change the practice; it is a reason to be precise about what the practice can and cannot claim from this system. The community's logic — low demand, no referral, no measurement, permission to attend without producing — is an asset the health system says it wants and has no instrument to recognise. **The most useful contribution Voedselbos 2.0 could make to the health question is not to become a health initiative; it is to remain a place the health system can list, refer to and learn from without turning it into a programme.** That is an inference from the retrieved architecture, not a recommendation the research can authorise.

**The 2027 reform changes the pot, not necessarily the door.** From 2027 the money is ontschot, allocated ambtshalve to municipalities, and conditioned on a college-approved werkagenda. That removes the per-theme compartments and the application queue — both real simplifications. But the municipal layer remains the gatekeeper of the plan, and the community practice still enters through a municipal werkagenda or not at all. **The reform may reduce fragmentation between national themes; it does not by itself create a route for an autonomous initiative to hold money, co-govern, or be recognised for a contribution.** That is the specific thing to watch in the 2026 werkagendas and the 2027 beschikkingen.

**On the "new umbrella" question:** the evidence does not support making one the default, and it does not support the opposite dogma either. The region is already moving toward a stronger centre (Herijking slide 4). The open question is whether the missing middle — a light, reciprocal, referrable relationship — can be built without a new organisation. The research cannot answer that; it can say that the current architecture has not tried it.

### Open questions for a later, separately agreed conversation

Not questions for a stakeholder to answer on the researcher's behalf; questions whose answers would change this note. **No one has been asked any of them.**

1. **For the region and the GGD:** which of the three doors would you want a community practice to use, and what would it have to show for the relationship to be worth its administrative cost to you?
2. **What would make a handoff, a decline or a closure count as a good outcome** in your reporting, rather than as a failure to deliver?
3. **Which coordination task in the current architecture is nobody's paid job** — and what would it cost to make it one, for longer than a year?
4. **If you had to fund one thing for four years instead of four things for one year, what would you protect?**
5. **For the municipality:** what would a *werkagenda* need to contain for a community practice to be a named contributor rather than a listed activity?
6. **For a community practice:** what would you accept as recognition without becoming a programme, and what would you refuse?
7. **For the monitor:** what would it take for the regional monitor to see a garden as health-relevant without turning it into an intervention?
8. **For everyone:** what would you stop doing to make room for a middle route — and who would notice if you did?
