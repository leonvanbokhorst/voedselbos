## 1. The answer

**Short answer, in one sentence: this is a layered coordination architecture that is real, recent and partly working; the evidence documents its arrangements and the experiences reported inside it, and it does not demonstrate that its architecture causes harm — and the specific thing it once appeared to demonstrate, that the money falls away in 2027, turns out to be an accounting transition that the successor instrument has since resolved.**

The second half of the question — under which conditions autonomous initiatives can contribute to shared outcomes without surrendering identity — has a narrower answer than v1 gave. The evidence supports **design conditions** and the absence of one specific **route** (a light, reciprocal, referrable relationship that carries obligation without legal personality). It does not establish that the route's absence causes fragmentation, and it does not establish that building it would help.

**Five findings carry what is established, and they sit at different evidential depths (§2).**

**1. The architecture is documented, is layered, and moved twice while this research ran.** *(Documented arrangement.)* A national agreement and its mid-term review; a broad SPUK that was compartmentalised and then de-compartmentalised on 29 October 2025; a regional plan, a Regioraad, a GGD-coordinated prevention infrastructure, a municipal consortium subsidy, and a 193-partner movement with a free listing route [D, `MTR`; `STCRT`; `CVDR`; `PARTNERS`; `LIST`]. The compartments were real: the MTR records "15 gescheiden onderdelen waarvan de daaraan verbonden middelen beperkt uitwisselbaar zijn en … een intensieve administratieve last" [D, `MTR` p.6]. They were removed within six months [D, `STCRT`]. **A report that cites the MTR's critique without the amendment is describing a system that no longer exists.**

**2. The regional money's continuity was uncertain in December 2025 and is now documented to 2029.** *(Documented arrangement — this corrects v1.)* v1 reported a cliff: the municipal prevention budget "falls from €4,229,000 (2026) to €739,000 (2027)" [D, `EIND` §D]. The document says that, but it says it about a year that contains a one-off national envelope, written before the successor instrument existed, and it hedges with "**(mogelijk)** wegvallen". The successor instrument was published on 21 July 2026 and its per-region amounts are **inside the regeling** [D, `SPUKAZWA`, Bijlagen 1–4]: for 2027 the Eindhoven–De Kempen region receives **€5,968,460**, Eindhoven receives **€3,103,887** locally plus **€529,106** for Woensel Zuid, and GGD Brabant-Zuidoost receives **€155,110** — €9,601,453 addressed to Eindhoven in total. The municipality's own October 2026 budget Q&A confirms the figure independently: the AZWA money "zijn niet opgenomen in de begroting 2027 t/m 2029", and adding it raises both sides by "**ca. 9,6 miljoen**", leaving the balance unchanged [D, `TV2027` q.44]. **Two documents reconcile to within rounding, from different directions.** So the 2026→2027 movement is a *budget composition at a moment when the successor allocation was neither known nor booked*; it is **not an established reduction in prevention capacity**, and v1 was wrong to present it as one. What survives is narrower and still consequential: the 2026 envelope is cut 10% and the old regeling lapses in July 2027 [D, `STCRT`]; the successor runs 2027–2029 and structural continuation beyond that "lijkt pas richting 2029/2030" to become clear, contingent on evidence [D, `VNGQA`]; and outside four named ketenaanpakken "blijven er geen structurele middelen over vanuit GALA" [D, `VNGQA`]. **Temporariness survives as a finding; the cliff does not.**

**3. Reported experience inside the architecture is mixed in a specific, informative way.** *(Reported experience, not measurement.)* The GALA monitor reports stronger operational collaboration and, in the same table, thinning formal regional agreements: joint decision-making 67%→36%, division of responsibilities 55%→43%, coordination agreements 60%→48%, financial agreements 52%→41%, while agreements with frontline professionals rose 37%→46% and on referrals 27%→39% (n=42) [D, `GALA25` table 2.1, p.24]. The region's own 2026 *Herijking* diagnoses "veelheid aan activiteiten … (**fragmentatie**)" and work "zonder werkelijke focus op de uitkomsten" [D, `HERIJKING` slide 2], and proposes a stronger centre: "een bestuurlijke groep [die] de lange lijnen bepaalt (strategie) en de uitvoering … bij een uitvoeringsorganisatie" [D, slide 4]. **These are self-reports by the actors, including by the coordinator that produces the monitor.** They establish what the field says about itself. They do not establish an effect.

**4. The mechanism most often implied — that actors defend domains — is not established locally, and the evidence that would establish it was never collected.** *(Insufficient evidence to adjudicate.)* v1 wrote that the strong "autonomy" explanation "is not supported locally". That phrasing closed a question the record cannot close. What the record shows is: municipalities give autonomy as a *reason* for restraint in regional agreements [D, `GALA25` pp.25–26]; the vertical split makes local discretion a role requirement rather than a preference [D, `GGDUITLEG`]; and no retrieved document measures ownership, identity threat, recognition or territoriality among local actors [U]. The organisational-psychology literature documents these mechanisms at employee and group level, with real counter-evidence and live disputes about transfer (§4.7). **The honest status is open, not refuted.** Local evidence is insufficient to adjudicate, and §4.10 states what would adjudicate it.

**5. The choice between a stronger centre and a stronger middle is live, and the evidence does not settle it — but it does settle what to look at.** *(Plausible mechanism, both sides.)* The region proposes a stronger centre [D, `HERIJKING` slide 4]; the funding instrument already centralises [D, `SPUKAZWA`]; and the one comparative study that speaks to the proposal finds that a corporate structure supported by regionalisation was "the most direct means" to collaboration, **with the alignment of budget authority and service planning reported as the lever that made it bind** — where the money and the plan sat in different places, the network "lacked a key governance lever" [D, line E]. Against that, the polycentric literature's own advantages are "posited" rather than demonstrated, and the sharpest single piece of counter-evidence in this report cuts against the middle: **more cooperation in one collaborative venue has been found to be associated with *less* cooperation elsewhere** [D, line E; child-reported at abstract level, cross-sectional — treated as an association, not a mechanism] **No retrieved study tests the two head-to-head, in health promotion or in any high-income health system.** Beyond that, the note offers a **synthesis, labelled [I]**: five conditions associated with documented outcomes in the literature reviewed — authority aligned with resources, completeness of implementation, coordination burden, the number of separate accounts, and whether competitive allocation sits in the same body as coordination (§4.9). It is a better frame than "centre or middle"; it is not a validated checklist. **And the reconciliation of accountability with autonomy holds on evidence and fails on authority: you can have light accounts and local autonomy, or binding coordination and local discretion over resources, but the same body cannot credibly offer both** (§4.8, P40).

**What is established, contested and not established:**
- **Established (documented arrangement):** the compartmentalisation and its October 2025 removal; the 10% 2026 cut and the July 2027 lapse of the old regeling; the successor instrument's existence, ambtshalve character, 2027–2029 horizon, published regional and local amounts, werkagenda condition, and named funding of regional coordination; the legal-person/consortium/penvoerder gate on the *local* regional subsidy; the open, referral-free listing route; the partner route's organisation requirement; gardens filed under *Ontmoeten*; no food forest or community garden among 193 partners.
- **Established (documented arrangement), and it corrects v1:** the 2027 municipal figure does not represent a decided reduction; the 2027 **local** allocation is at least as large as the 2026 GALA envelope it replaces, and the regional allocation is additional resource administered by the city — while both are addressed to municipalities and GGDs, not to community practices. **A regional before-and-after cannot be stated: no retrieved document gives the region's prior IZA/regional allocation, so the regional figure has no verified baseline.**
- **Reported experience, unresolved by design:** whether collaboration is improving or weakening. The national survey says operational collaboration improved and formal agreements thinned; the regional monitor says collaboration is growing. Different objects, different instruments, both self-report.
- **Plausible mechanism, not established locally:** projectification, coordination burden, output-tied incentives, psychological ownership and territoriality, identity and recognition, resource dependence, institutional isomorphism, goal ambiguity. §4.7 states what the literature establishes and at which level; §4.10 states what would be needed locally.
- **Not established in the sources searched:** harmful duplication of local offers; broken referral or handover chains experienced by residents; resident navigation burden or repeated information requests; whether the 193 partners do anything together beyond being listed; whether any small initiative tried and failed to enter the consortium route; whether the region's collaboration would survive a funding transition; whether *any* consequence of fragmentation for residents has been demonstrated.
- **Not knowable with the methods used:** whether named actors want territory, prestige or control. The exclusions forbid inferring motive, and nothing retrieved would support it. This is a limit of the method, not a finding about the actors.

## 2. How to read this note: four strata and four evidence states

v1's most serious defect was not a wrong fact but a wrong *kind* of claim in the wrong place: a documented budget line, a reported experience and a causal mechanism were marshalled into one verdict. This revision separates them and labels every substantive claim twice — by the stratum it comes from and by the state of the evidence about it.

### 2.1 Four strata

| Stratum | What it contains | What it can support | What it cannot |
|---|---|---|---|
| **S1 — Documented institutional arrangement** | Statutes, subsidy schemes, budgets, award decisions, board papers, governance rules, published amounts, dates | That a rule, amount, obligation or date exists; who is addressed by an instrument; what it requires | That anyone behaves accordingly; that the arrangement produces any outcome |
| **S2 — Reported experience** | Survey responses from municipalities and insurers; monitors produced by the coordinator; the region's self-diagnosis; stated reasons in interviews | What actors say about the system, and that they say it | Independent measurement; magnitude; whether the report is accurate |
| **S3 — Plausible causal mechanism** | Empirical and theoretical literature; this note's inferences from structure | That a mechanism operates in the settings where it was measured; that it is a candidate here | That it operates here; that it explains a named local outcome |
| **S4 — Demonstrated consequence** | Observed effects on residents, communities, organisations, in this region | That fragmentation causes the effect | — |

**The finding that matters most in this revision is a distributional one: this report has almost nothing in S4.** No retrieved source observes a resident, a referral or an organisational outcome in Eindhoven–De Kempen in a way that can be attributed to the arrangement of the system. v1's answer used S1 evidence to carry S3 claims and read like S4. The corrected distribution is:

| Stratum | v1's implicit weight | v2's explicit weight | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 documented arrangement | High | **High, and now stronger** (the successor instrument is read at source) | §4.2–4.4 |
| S2 reported experience | High, often unlabelled | **Labelled as such** | §4.2, §4.3(f), §4.6 |
| S3 plausible mechanism | High, sometimes as finding | **Presented as candidate mechanisms with level caveats** | §4.7, §4.10 |
| S4 demonstrated consequence | Low, but implied | **Stated as absent** | §4.6, §8 |

### 2.2 Four evidence states

Every proposition in §3 is also tagged with its state:

- **Supports** — retrieved evidence is consistent with the proposition and would be unlikely under its negation.
- **Contradicts** — retrieved evidence is inconsistent with it.
- **Insufficient to adjudicate** — the relevant evidence does not exist, or exists at the wrong level, or the proposition has not been operationalised here. **This is the correct state for most mechanism claims, and it is not a verdict.**
- **Operationalisation unsuitable** — the measure used cannot answer the question asked, whatever its result. (Example: a population health-literacy instrument cannot serve as a garden's outcome measure; see the health note.)

**The distinction v1 collapsed**, and which the PI's correction requires: *"no study found"* is a statement about search coverage; *"the evidence contradicts it"* is a statement about the world; *"the measure cannot answer this"* is a statement about instruments. v1 wrote the first where it sometimes meant the second, and the third where it sometimes meant the second. This revision keeps them apart, and §2.3 records what that does to the answer.

### 2.3 What changes when the distinction is applied

- **The money finding reverses direction.** A budget movement that looked like a cut was an artefact of comparing a year with a national envelope to a year without one, before the successor was known. Reading the successor instrument at source moves the proposition from "established reduction" to "documented re-sourcing with changed conditions" (§4.3(d), P12).
- **The coordination finding softens but survives.** "Coordination is a project, not a service" becomes: the *funding horizon* for regional coordination is now three years and named in the instrument [D, `SPUKAZWA` art. 2.1(2)(c)], while *whose job it is* after the GGD's one-year bridge remains undecided. The institutional question is open; the financial one has partly closed.
- **The mechanism findings become open rather than refuted.** Psychological ownership, autonomy, identity, recognition and territoriality are retained as **live hypotheses with no local measurement**, not as explanations and not as exonerations (§4.7, §4.10).
- **The "missing middle" finding survives on S1 ground and loses its causal claim.** The *absence of a route* is documented: the new instrument is addressed to municipalities and GGDs, and onward funding of third parties is governed by procurement and state-aid rules [D, `SPUKAZWA` art. 2.1, 3.1, toelichting "Staatssteun"]. That the absence *causes* fragmentation, or that filling it would help, is not established.
- **The recommendation set narrows to conditions and to one testable claim.** §4.8 lists conditions the evidence supports; §4.9 states the rival that would make the missing-middle reading wrong; §8 states what would decide between them.

## 4. The evidence

### 4.1 What would count as fragmentation, and what would not

The issue asks for the observation to be defined before it is tested. The retrieved record supports seven observable manifestations, each of which can fail visibly:

1. **Duplicated activities without a justified population or access difference.**
2. **Missing or broken referrals and handovers.**
3. **Disconnected knowledge and learning.**
4. **Incompatible eligibility or reporting requirements.**
5. **Repeated temporary pilots without continuity.**
6. **Unmet needs despite overlapping offers.**
7. **Transaction costs borne by residents and small organisations.**

It also supports four reasons why plurality is not failure: local specificity, culturally and linguistically appropriate offers, distributed experimentation, and multiple entry points.

**v1 applied this test and concluded that the local record documents manifestations 4 and 5 "most clearly", 3 "partially", and "does not document 1, 2, 6 or 7 at all", calling that distribution "itself the answer".** The first half was right and the second was over-corrected in revision. The precise distribution is: **two of the seven are documented as arrangements — 4 (incompatible eligibility and reporting requirements) and 5 (temporary instruments without a named long-term owner) — one partially (3, disconnected knowledge and learning, of which the monitor's own data-infrastructure gap is one instance), and the other four not at all.** What the record does *not* contain is an **observed consequence** for any of the seven: nobody has measured a resident turned away by incompatible rules, or a handover that failed. So the finding is: *the arrangements that could produce fragmentation are documented for two of the seven; the consequences are documented for none.* That is more useful than either v1's "only two" pessimism or the revision's first draft, which said the occurrence of none was documented — which was false on its own evidence, since the reporting burden and the unowned coordination function **are** the occurrence, at the level of arrangement.

### 4.2 National: an architecture that produced compartments, removed them, and then re-sourced the money

The GALA mid-term review is narrower and more careful than the parliamentary letter that transmits it: it says plainly that it is "nog te vroeg om conclusies te trekken over behaalde resultaten" [D, `MTR` p.3]. What it does conclude concerns the conditions for collaboration:

> "De brede SPUK kent 15 gescheiden onderdelen waarvan de daaraan verbonden middelen beperkt uitwisselbaar zijn en kent ook een intensieve administratieve last. Daarnaast is een deel van de GALA middelen tijdelijk, waardoor gemeenten zelf aan zet zijn voor het opzetten van een langlopende aanpak." [D, `MTR` p.6]

> "de wijze van financieren (brede SPUK met diverse verantwoordingsregels) belemmert de integrale uitvoering in gemeenten." [D, `MTR` p.4]

**That was the April 2025 position; it is no longer the October 2025 position.** On 29 October 2025 the brede SPUK was amended: "De schotten tussen de verschillende onderdelen zijn vervallen"; municipalities may use "het totaal toegekende uitkeringsplafond per gemeente … voor alle onderdelen en thema's"; per-part SiSa financial lines ended [D, `STCRT`]. The same instrument cut the 2026 budget by 10%, added €43 million from AZWA/HLO, and left the regeling to lapse on 16 July 2027 [D, `STCRT`]. **v1 also said it froze indexation. That is withdrawn:** the amendment's budget tables carry the 2024 loon- en prijsontwikkeling, but no freezing provision was located, and the reviewer was right to say the inference was ours rather than the instrument's (§10).

**And then the successor arrived, which v1 did not read.** The *Regeling specifieke uitkering AZWA en HLO* was adopted on 14 July 2026 and published as Staatscourant 2026, 25830, in force from 22 July 2026 [D, `SPUKAZWA`]. Its design answers, in legal text, several questions v1 could only infer:

- **No application, no competition.** Allocations are ambtshalve to every mandaathouder, every municipality and every GGD, with the amounts fixed in four annexes (P12a, P21a).
- **A three-year horizon, in the instrument.** 2027–2029, with 70% carry-over, and a legal end date of 16 July 2030 [D, arts. 5.2, 8.2].
- **Coordination is a named funded activity.** Art. 2.1(2)(c) lists "regionale coördinatie van gemeenten in de samenwerkingsregio" among the regional activities; art. 2.1(3) gives each GGD a 2027 allocation for "versterking van de regionale kennis- en adviesfunctie en coördinatie van de ketenaanpakken" [D].
- **A condition, not an eligibility test.** Art. 4.1 makes the regional **werkagenda** a precondition; the VNG states that "de inhoud van de werkagenda wordt niet inhoudelijk beoordeeld" [D, `VNGQA`].
- **Money reaches municipalities and GGDs.** Nothing in the regeling addresses a non-public body; onward funding of third parties is governed by procurement and state-aid rules, and the toelichting is explicit that municipalities may not pass on "ongeoorloofde staatssteun" directly or indirectly [D, `SPUKAZWA`, toelichting "Staatssteun"]. **This is the structural fact behind the "missing middle", and it is now documented in the successor instrument rather than inferred from the old one.**

**What did not change.** The 2026 cut and the 2027 lapse of the old regeling are facts [D, `STCRT`]. The VNG states that "behalve de middelen (90 mln) voor genoemde ketenaanpakken … blijven er geen structurele middelen over vanuit GALA", and that clarity on structural funding after the temporary money "lijkt pas richting 2029/2030 [te] ontstaan, afhankelijk van de verzamelde bewijslast" [D, `VNGQA`]. **So temporariness persists — it has moved from 2026 to 2029, and it is now explicitly conditional on evidence of effect.** That is a different and, for a community practice, no less consequential finding than the one v1 made: the horizon that a garden would have to plan against is three years and a proof burden, not one year and a cliff.

**The compartmentalisation verdict is a citation chain, not an independent finding.** The MTR attributes it to the VNG's 2024 *uitvoeringstoets*, based on interviews and two sessions with 14 municipalities and one samenwerkingsverband; the RIVM monitor relays it [D, `MTR` p.6; `GALA25`]. The VNG's own summary is sharper: "Helaas sluit de financiering via een tijdelijke specifieke uitkering (SPUK) met 15 onderdelen en beperkte ruimte voor verschuiving en flinke verantwoordingslasten hier niet bij aan", and "Rechtmatigheid lijkt belangrijker dan doelmatigheid" [C, `VNGTOETS`]. That is a documented policy judgement by an interested party — evidence of what implementers experienced (S2), not an observed effect (S4).

**One sentence in the record deserves its own line, because it is the only place where the project form is named as the problem by the people implementing it.** Asked what VWS could do, interviewees asked for "het bundelen van versnipperde subsidies en middelen om meer effect te sorteren en om te voorkomen dat regio's ondersneeuwen in allerlei losse projecten en trajecten" [D, `GALA25` p.34]. That is reported experience, and it is the strongest S2 support in the record for a projectification mechanism.

The map's most important feature is what it leaves out: the volunteer green commons is represented by one closed initiative, and the health architecture's green members are all staffed organisations. That is not a complete network map — it is a map of the **publicly documented** architecture. Whether the commons is absent from the *work* or only from the *record* is not established [U]; absence from a public directory is not absence of relationship.

That architecture has several features that matter.

**(a) The old money was a consortium instrument with a pre-defined consortium.** The local scheme funds only "een penvoerder, zijnde een rechtspersoon, namens een samenwerkingsverband voortkomend uit het Regioplan" [D, `CVDR` art. 3]. The municipal page is blunter: "Aanvragen is alleen mogelijk voor IZA-partners, die nu al penvoerder zijn …" [D, `EINDSUB`]. The scheme says a consortium "staat te allen tijde open voor het aansluiten van nieuwe partijen" [D, `CVDR` art. 1], but the entry condition is to be already inside. **Note the tense: this describes an instrument that expires on 1 January 2027** [D, `CVDR` art. 14]. It is a fact about the past three years, not about the next three.

**(b) The successor money is not a consortium instrument at all.** Its addressees are public bodies; its allocation is ambtshalve; its amounts are fixed. **The gate that created incumbent advantage has been removed at the top and replaced by a condition (the werkagenda) that municipalities and regions satisfy collectively.** For a community practice, the practical consequence is not exclusion and not inclusion but *indirectness*: any resource reaching it would come through a municipality's own spending, subject to procurement and state-aid rules [D, `SPUKAZWA` toelichting "Staatssteun"]. **That is a documented structural constraint. It is not evidence that anyone intends to keep a garden out, and this note does not claim it is.**

**(c) The obligations of the old scheme were governance obligations.** A funded consortium had to join the PACO partner community, participate in the Regioraad, deliver a quarterly report, cooperate with monitoring and with the municipality's IZA-SPUK accountability [D, `CVDR` art. 10]. Reasonable conditions for a regional programme; also a second job for a small organisation — and a plausible reason why the route was used by organisations that already had capacity [I].

**(d) The horizon — corrected.** The old scheme funded 2024–2026 and expired on 1 January 2027 [D, `CVDR` arts. 5, 14]. The successor runs 2027–2029 [D, `SPUKAZWA` art. 2.1]. **The coordination-bridge finding of v1 therefore needs restating rather than repeating.** The GGD board paper is accurate about 2026: the Coöperatie Slimmer Leven was dissolved on 1 January 2026, the GGD took the penvoerderschap "voor een overgangsperiode van één jaar (2026)" on "een subsidie van € 1,2 miljoen", and "structurele borging … na 2026" was still to be worked out [D, `GGD`]. **From 2027 the funding position changes: regional coordination is a named funded activity for three years (P11a), but no retrieved document assigns it to a named organisation beyond the GGD's one-year bridge.** The institutional question — *whose job is coordination in 2027?* — is open. The financial question — *is there money for it?* — is answered, and the answer is yes, for three years [D, `SPUKAZWA` art. 2.1(2)(c)]. **Since the region's werkagenda was not found in the public record at retrieval (§6), where that money lands is unresolved.**

**(e) Concentration without competition — and its end.** In spring 2025 the region received **three** applications — GGD, Stroomz Zorgt and Coöperatie Slimmer Leven — totalling €2.5 million against a €2.2 million ceiling, and the advice note proposing the raise says the increase "is direct gekoppeld aan de ingediende aanvragen … geen andere partijen een beroep kunnen doen op de ophoging van het plafond" [D, `AWARD25`]. The 2026 amendment repeated the operation in a single article with no explanatory note [D, `GMB`]. **This is the strongest S1 evidence in the report for incumbent advantage under the old scheme, and it is time-stamped: there is no queue to be excluded from under the successor, because there is no queue.**

**(f) The region diagnoses its own fragmentation — and prescribes a stronger centre.** The 2026 *Herijking regiobeeld* is a working document of the regional project group (GGD, CZ, VGZ, the municipality, care providers, a village cooperative and Transvorm), dated 20 April 2026. Slide 2 gives the lesson of the 2023 Regioplan: "Maar we zien ook veelheid aan activiteiten belegd in verschillende thema's (fragmentatie) — Met verschillende samenwerkingsverbanden en governance structuren — Zonder werkelijke focus op de uitkomsten en hoe deze bijdragen aan het grote doel van het regioplan" [D, `HERIJKING` slide 2]. Slide 4 proposes "een nieuw samenwerkingsmodel waarbij een bestuurlijke groep de lange lijnen bepaalt (strategie) en de uitvoering is belegd bij een uitvoeringsorganisatie" [D, slide 4]. **§4.9 treats this as a serious rival rather than as a tension to be noted.**

**(g) A vertical split by design.** The GGD and STROOMZ form a regional programme team of five project leaders and a programme manager, working on "de regionale kaders waarin we streven naar samenhangend aanbod voor alle inwoners van de regio". Then: "**Gemeenten pakken de implementatie op basis daarvan zelf op** of ontwikkelen verder door in samenwerking met betrokken partijen" [D, `GGDUITLEG`]. This is a deliberate division of labour, and it makes municipal autonomy a role requirement [I]. It also creates the seam where fragmentation would live — between a regional frame and fifteen local implementations — and no retrieved document describes a shared instrument for what happens across that seam [U].

**(h) A possible 2025 underspend — provisional.** The 2025 municipal accounts, as read by line B, record realised IZA baten of €3,371k against an entitlement of €4,436k, with a repayment to the Rijk; the retrieved figures do not fully reconcile, and this note treats them as **provisional** [D, `JAARSTUKKEN` via line B; U]. **If real**, it would be consequential: the region would have underspent a ring-fenced prevention budget in the year its own monitor says the connective work is undone, and absorptive capacity rather than money would be the binding constraint. **This is precisely the alternative explanation that competes with the "missing middle" reading (§4.9), and it should not be dismissed: it is a single, unreconciled retrieval, and a reviewer should weigh it as such.** Note also that the successor instrument's ambtshalve, allocation-fixed design removes the application step that most plausibly produced underspend.

**(i) The region's own monitor is a self-report by the coordinator.** It reports "Samenwerking groeit", "Gezamenlijke verantwoordelijkheid groeit zichtbaar", and asks for 2026 for "het consistent verankeren van de regio-ambities/impactdoelen in keuzes en monitoring, het opschalen van initiatieven en het versterken van de data-infrastructuur" [D, `STAPCON`]. The remaining tasks are the connective ones: shared goals, shared data, coordination between theme leads. The monitor is produced by the GGD, which is also the coordinator [D, `STAPCON` footer] — a self-report, and labelled as one here.

### 4.4 Local: three doors, the successor's missing fourth, and what "exclusion" would require

The most useful local finding is a contrast between three ways of entering the regional health movement, documented on the same website on the same day.

| | **Activity listing** | **Partner affiliation** | **Penvoerder / consortium** (to 1 Jan 2027) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who can enter | "Naam (verplicht)" and "E-mail (verplicht)"; no organisation field, no legal form [D, `LIST`] | "Naam organisatie" required; no fee or obligation published [D, `PARTNER`] | A legal person as penvoerder of a Regioplan-derived consortium, already an IZA partner [D, `CVDR` art. 3; `EINDSUB`] |

| What it proves | Co-presence in a signposting instrument | Affiliation | Membership of the funded governance club |
| What it cannot carry | Referral, recognition of contribution, reciprocal obligation, shared outcome | Money, governance rights, referrals, obligations | Informality, a volunteer group without a legal person |

The listing route is genuinely good news, and it is the one place where the movement has written the anti-qualification principle into its own intake. But a listing is **co-presence, not relationship**. The partner route is lighter than it looks — an organisation name and an e-mail, no published fee or duty — and that is precisely its limit: affiliation without obligation, recognition or resource. The penvoerder route carried money and governance until 1 January 2027.

**The successor instrument adds a fourth column, and it is the important one.**

| | **AZWA/HLO allocation (2027–2029)** |
|---|---|
| Who can enter | Mandaathouders, municipalities and GGDs only [D, `SPUKAZWA` arts. 2.1, 3.1, 2.1(3)] |
| What it gets you | A fixed, ambtshalve amount for three years, with a 100% voorschot and 70% carry-over [D, arts. 2.2, 5.1, 5.2] |
| What it costs | A regional werkagenda approved by every college, by 1 March 2027 [D, art. 4.1] |
| What it proves | That the region has a plan and the colleges have signed it |
| What it cannot carry | Any direct relationship with a non-public body: onward funding is bounded by procurement and state-aid rules [D, toelichting "Staatssteun"] |

**So the "missing middle" is not a gap in the old scheme that the reform might have closed; it is a property of both.** The old instrument required legal personality and a consortium; the new one requires a municipality. What the new instrument *does* remove is the queue, the application and the ceiling-raising club. **An initiative that would previously have been structurally unable to apply is now not required to: the money arrives.** It simply does not arrive at the initiative.

**On exclusion, the record supports a precise and much narrower statement than v1 gave.** v1 wrote that "the exclusion, if any, is at the door" and that the entry rule "is built for incumbents". Both are inferences about intent dressed as findings. What is documented is: (i) a rule that requires legal personality and prior penvoerderschap; (ii) an award record showing three applicants and no refusals; (iii) a ceiling raised to fit those applicants and reserved to them; (iv) a successor instrument addressed to public bodies; (v) state-aid and procurement rules that bound onward funding. **What is not documented: any application refused, any initiative discouraged, any actor's reasoning about the door, or any instance of a garden being told no.** The correct statement is P22: **eligibility restrictions and incumbent advantage are documented; demonstrated exclusion and intentional gatekeeping are not — insufficient evidence to adjudicate.** The distinction is not pedantry: the first implies a design change (open the route, simplify the rules); the second implies a conflict, and acting on it would be acting on an accusation the record does not support.

**The directory count makes the classification point.** Of 147 Eindhoven entries, the gardens are filed as places to meet (Buurtmoestuin Irisbuurt, De Vergeten Tuin, Buurttuin, 't Kruidenkafé — all *Ontmoeten*; "Adopteer een stukje groen" under *Klussen*), and all nine cooking/eating entries are *Ontmoeten* [D, `EHV` — counted]. No garden is categorised as health, learning or prevention. *Ontmoeten* may be the honest category. The finding is that **the region's public classification has no slot for "growing food as a health-relevant practice"**. That is an S1 fact about a taxonomy. Whether the taxonomy causes anything is S3, and not established.

**The partner list shows the same boundary from the institutional side.** Of 193 named partners, the green organisations present are IVN, Het Bos In, De Groote Heide and Wasven; no food forest, community garden or buurttuin appears [D, `PARTNERS`]. Wasven — staffed, care-contracted, ANBI-structured — is a partner; the volunteer commons is not. The 193 partners also include sole traders and small foundations, so absence is about **category, not size** [D, `PARTNERS`]. Absence from a public list is not absence of collaboration, and this note does not claim no garden has ever spoken to a health partner.

### 4.5 What is plurality, not fragmentation

The same evidence that could be read as fragmentation reads as plurality. The directory is large (147 entries), varied (eight categories), local, and cheap or free ("meestal gratis" [D, `EHV`]). It includes offers for specific groups — Iranian/Farsi community, LGBTQ+, people with a disability, informal carers, parents, older people — that a single programme would not produce. The parliamentary letter's own observation that "er bestaat vaak geen 'one size fits all' aanpak" [D, `LETTER`] supports reading this as distributed access rather than duplication.

The counterweight: **variety at the offer level coexists with uniformity at the access level.** Almost everything is a place to meet, a walk, a course or a repair café, delivered by an organisation with a room and a time. The offers that require least — a garden you can enter when the crew is there, a communal meal at €0–7 — are the ones least visible as health.

The region has tested the plurality hypothesis itself, cautiously. The 2026 Herijking analyses three citizen initiatives — Zorgcoöperatie Hoogeloon (20 years), Dorps Coöperatie Meer Riethoven (~10 years) and the Steensel initiative (2 years). It finds that "De drie dorpen scoren positief op kenmerken van sociale samenhang bij volwassenen tot 65 jaar", while the picture on health behaviours is negative or mixed [D, `HERIJKING` §8.1–8.2]. It then states the limit plainly: "Binnen de reikwijdte van dit onderzoek is het niet mogelijk om vast te stellen of er een causaal verband is … omdat grotendeels alleen openbare data beschikbaar was op dorp, gemeente en regioniveau", adding that a structurally better score for longer-running initiatives "geeft dit een indicatie van het mogelijke positieve effect" [D, §8.1]. **An indication, from ecological data, that long-lived local initiatives may be associated with social coherence — not evidence that they produce health, and not evidence that a new initiative would.**

### 4.6 Consequences for residents: what is documented, what is not, and why

The issue asks for actual consequences. This is where the report is thinnest, and the revision's task is to say so precisely rather than to fill the gap with plausible reasoning.

**Documented (S1/S2):**
- **Reach is partial and uneven.** In 2024, valpreventie reached 2,013 residents with a risk test and 106 with a preventive exercise intervention; the GLI started 95 of 148 registrants, 62 completed and 19 moved on to regular sport; Welzijn op Recept, started 2 June 2025, had 31 referrals, of which 16 involved more than a single question and were referred on to WIJeindhoven, 5 already had a generalist, and 10 were helped by a phone call or a referral to the social base [D, `EIND` §B]. Reach and throughput, not navigation or unmet need.
- **The hardest-to-reach are named as hardest to reach.** The 2026 RIVM synthesis says it "blijft vooral lastig om mensen met gezondheidsproblemen die in moeilijke omstandigheden leven, bijvoorbeeld door schulden, mee te laten doen" [D, `RIVM26` abstract]. National, not Eindhoven.
- **Residents are involved in planning, less afterwards.** "Inwoners worden wel geleidelijk meer betrokken bij de planvorming, maar minder in de fasen daarna" [D, `GALA25` p.32].
- **The municipal evaluation cannot attribute health gain**, and says so: "het meten van gezondheid(swinst) is complex … moeilijk om het causale verband aan te tonen" [D, `EIND` §B]. Its 0–8 scores are self-assessed against eight indicators.
- **One instrument did not reach residents at all for months.** The fitcoins project started 1 July 2025, but "Vanwege technische problemen is het platform nog niet gerealiseerd. Hierdoor kunnen inwoners zich nog niet aanmelden in de app" [D, `EIND` §B].
- **One indirect local signal.** From the *Groen Verbindt* evaluation: year after year 9 in 10 volunteers agreed they had met new people in the neighbourhood, 5 in 10 that they were more ready to help others, and only **4 in 10** that they dared ask more people for help [D, health note §3.4, `E8`]. If a health-promotion system's weakest reported outcome is asking for help, the cost of a fragmented landscape may not be navigation but the failure to ask — **an inference across a different programme and population, labelled as one [I]**.

**Not documented — and this is the corrected finding:**

| Consequence the issue asks about | Status | Why it is not established |
|---|---|---|
| Duplicated activities serving the same people in the same way | **Insufficient evidence** | No offer inventory with population, access and usage data exists in the public record |
| Broken referrals / handovers experienced by a resident | **Insufficient evidence** | No referral audit; the valpreventie chain shows a *working* handover to Eindhoven Sport, not a broken one |
| Repeated requests for the same information | **Insufficient evidence** | No resident-level data of any kind |
| Resident navigation burden or trust | **Insufficient evidence** | Requires consent-based inquiry, outside this mandate |
| Unmet need concentrated where offers are densest | **Insufficient evidence** | No SES join; the health note records the distributional question as unexamined |
| Whether the 147 offers reach beyond the already-engaged | **Insufficient evidence** | The directory has no usage data |
| Whether fragmentation, if present, *causes* any of the above | **Not established, and not establishable with documents** | Requires a design that observes residents over time |

**The honest summary: the local record contains no demonstrated consequence of fragmentation for residents — not because none exists, but because the instruments that would show one are not public and this method cannot produce them.** v1 treated that absence as a negative finding. It is not one. It is a statement about the evidence base, and it is the strongest argument in this report for the consent-based inquiry the issue itself anticipates.

### 4.7 What the empirical literature establishes about the mechanisms — and at which level

The literature answers the two halves of the question with unequal strength. **The conditions under which collaboration is more or less likely to work are documented extensively. The effects of collaboration on health or organisational outcomes are documented thinly, and several of the strongest reviews say so in their own conclusions.** Any sentence of the form "research shows partnership works" is unsupported; "research identifies these conditions, and the effect evidence is weak and mixed" is supported.

2. **Coordination burden** explains why collaboration dissolves even when everyone values it. The old local obligations — consortium agreement, quarterly reports, Regioraad meetings, monitoring, IZA-SPUK accountability — are the "ex-post interdependence" the literature identifies. **The successor's obligations are lighter: one regional werkagenda, SiSa verantwoording, monitoring cooperation, no application and no per-theme accounting.** By the literature's own logic, that should reduce the dissolution risk. 3. **The weak outcome evidence** explains why the region's monitors measure process and disclaim attribution. That is the state of the field, not a failure of the region's evaluators, and it means nobody can promise that a new coordinating body would improve health.

### 4.8 What would enable shared impact without surrender — stated as conditions, with the tension named

The evidence supports designing for conditions, and it supports **contribution-based accounting** rather than exclusive attribution. It does not support a claim that any coordination model reliably produces shared outcomes. **Nor does it support the reverse claim — that recognition and accountability can always be reconciled with autonomy.** §4.8 states the conditions; P40 states the tension honestly.

1. **Bridge the time frames.** Coordination must be funded on a horizon at least as long as the work it coordinates. *Evidence:* projectification literature; and now the local instrument itself, which funds regional coordination for 2027–2029 [D, `SPUKAZWA` art. 2.1(2)(c)]. *What remains open:* whose job it is, and what happens in 2030.
2. **Relieve the coordination burden.** The difference between sustained and dissolved collaborations in the inter-municipal evidence was management that streamlined the burden [D, Elston et al.]. The successor instrument reduces the burden (no application, one werkagenda, light SiSa) but adds a new planning document with a hard deadline [D, `SPUKAZWA` art. 4.1; `VNGQA`]. *Condition: the administrative cost of participating is proportional to the size of the participant.* [I]
3. **Build trust through repeated interaction, and use more than one management strategy** [D, George et al.]. The local record shows operational trust improving while formal agreements thinned; that is an asset to protect, not a substitute for a written settlement.
4. **Protect intrinsic motivation; do not tie recognition to output counts** [D, Cerasoli et al.]. *Condition: accountability for contribution and learning, not only for countable output; and no requirement that a volunteer practice become a programme to be recognised.* [I]
5. **Make contribution visible, not only activity** [D, Greenhalgh et al.; Corbin et al.]. The directory makes an *offer* visible but records nothing about who contributed to what [D, `EHV`; `LIST`]. *Condition: a recognition layer that records contribution and handoff.* [I]
6. **Allow plurality and watch for masking** [D, Morrison]. *Condition: monitor a small number of population outcomes independently of the network's own self-report.* [D/I]
7. **Permit non-collaboration, handoff and ending** [D, Gazley; Niven et al.; Elston et al.]. The successor instrument has no published route for handing off or ending — but neither does it score continuation, since it does not require an application or a per-theme result. *Condition: a funder that accepts a legitimate "no", a handoff or a closure as a successful outcome.* [D/I]

**The tension, stated plainly — and split into what can and cannot be reconciled.** Line E's finding is that the answer is not one thing. **Accountability-with-autonomy can be reconciled on the evidentiary dimension and cannot be reconciled on the authority dimension**, and conflating the two is what makes the debate unresolvable.

*What can be reconciled:*

- **The claim type.** Attribution ("your organisation produced this outcome") is not empirically available for multi-actor work; contribution ("your work was a contributory cause, neither necessary nor sufficient") is a specified alternative (Mayne's contribution analysis). A funder can require contribution claims without requiring exclusive attribution [D, line E]. **This is the strongest reconciliation the literature offers, and it is a real one.**
- **The purpose of the account.** Accountability theory distinguishes democratic, constitutional and **learning** perspectives, which generate different deficits. An account designed for learning — what happened, what should change — is not the same design as one designed for control — did you deliver what you promised. If the centre's decisions are about what to fund next and what to stop, a learning account suffices; if they are about sanction, it does not [D, line E].
- **Who bears the transaction cost.** Reporting is a fixed cost and therefore regressive, and stacking funders' formats multiplies it. One shared account, one format, one deadline, and the consortium rather than the smallest partner bearing the assembly cost are supported as *design directions* — though the only measured saving retrieved is 8 hours to 6 per funder, which is real but small [D, line E].
- **Recognition's realistic job.** Recognition reliably moves **documented conformity** far more than measured quality (a Cochrane review of accreditation: a 30-percentage-point gain in documented compliance against a 2.4% median gain in quality indicators, very low certainty), and it can be run in a way that supports participants — fewer participants, more support. **It should not be expected to move practice or to substitute for resources** [D, line E].

*What cannot be reconciled:*

1. **Binding requires authority.** If a centre is to make any decision that changes what partners do, someone must hold resource authority; where planning and budget were split, the arrangement did not bind and members were not held accountable [D, line E]. **Autonomy in the sense of "no decision by anyone else about my resources" is incompatible with a centre that coordinates anything. No reporting design removes that transfer of discretion.**
2. **Measurement changes behaviour in the direction of the measure.** The stronger the consequence attached to an account, the more the account becomes the work — "efforts to improve ranking factors without improving the characteristics the factors are designed to measure". **A contribution regime with hard consequences is a target regime with extra steps** [D, line E].
3. **Fixed compliance costs are regressive, and unrestricted money does not automatically build capacity.** Small organisations pay proportionally more for the same account; and a large-N study of unrestricted gifts finds recipients kept their indirect-cost ratio and reserves constant rather than investing in infrastructure — **undercutting the trust-based-funding assumption that removing restrictions builds capacity** (a preprint, large US nonprofits, not transferable in magnitude, and cited here only to refuse the claim) [D, line E].
4. **Competitive funding corrodes collaboration, including the collaboration the funder says it wants.** Documented in health promotion directly: "competitive tendering often undermined it in practice" [D, line E]. **A body that both coordinates and allocates competitive funds reproduces this contradiction inside itself.**

**The honest bottom line, and it is a sentence the report should be judged on.** *You can have light accounts and local autonomy, or binding coordination and local discretion over resources, but the same body cannot credibly offer both.* That is an inference from the retrieved evidence, not a finding of any single study [I]. **A scheme that gives recognition without resources converts a contribution into a reputational gesture while leaving the cost with the contributor; a scheme that ties resources to attributable outcomes penalises exactly the low-demand, relationship-based practices whose value is hardest to attribute (§4.6).** Neither is designed away by good intentions, and a report that promised the reconciliation would be selling something the evidence does not stock.

**Recognition, in this evidence, is not a feeling; it is a record of who contributed to what, plus a repeated occasion on which that contribution is used.** The region has the first (a directory that records offers) without the second (a record of contribution), and it has repeated occasions (Regioraad, PACO, the monitor) organised around projects rather than around the practices that make them work. **That is a design observation, not a diagnosis of motive.**

### 4.9 Centralisation as a serious rival — and the finding that neither reading is currently determined

v1 treated the region's centralising remedy as a tension to be noted ("the evidence does not decide between strengthening the centre and strengthening the middle"). That was correct as far as it went, and it did not go far enough: **centralisation is not merely the region's preference, it is a developed alternative with its own literature, its own diagnosis of the problem, and a plausible claim to be the better answer here.** This section states its case as strongly as the evidence allows, states the rival as strongly, and then says what the evidence actually decides — which is less than either side would like.

Line E retrieved and appraised 16 sources in three families for this section. Two of its findings govern everything below, and both cut against the way v1 framed the choice.

**First, the typology everyone borrows from is not itself a finding.** Provan & Kenis's three network-governance modes are the standard reference for "a region this size needs an administrative organisation". **That article could not be read at any level** — no abstract deposited at Crossref, OUP returned HTTP 403, the Tilburg repository copy returned a Cloudflare 403 (`cf-ray a60be4a17929db60-AMS`). What *can* be documented is worse for the borrowed claim than its absence: the same research programme treats mode-appropriateness as contingent on form, voluntary-versus-mandated inception and developmental stage; insiders describe the empirical literature applying the typology as suffering "persistent conceptual ambiguity" and a "lack of discernment between governance and management"; and whole-network reviewers call that empirical literature "modest". **Anything of the form "research shows an administrative organisation coordinates better" is unsupported.**

**Second, the one comparative study that speaks directly to the regional proposal supports the centre — conditionally, and not for the reason the report first gave.** A study of ten Canadian mental health networks (three governance forms, 96 key informants) finds that "a corporate structure supported by regionalization offered the most direct means for local governance to attain inter-organizational collaboration". **The corporate structure is the mechanism the authors identify.** What the paper then reports is how it bound: corporate-governed networks in regionalised provinces were "held accountable for ensuring mental health service coordination", and the study's own section heading is "Aligning budget authority with service planning". Where the province held the budgets and the network held the plan, the networks "lacked a key governance lever". **So budget/planning alignment is a reported enabling condition within the corporate model, not a substitute for having a centre — and the accurate inference is conditional: a strategy board whose planning is not matched by resource authority is reported to lack the lever that made the Canadian model bind.** That is a documented limitation of an arrangement, not a measured outcome failure, and the earlier draft of this paragraph overstated it in both directions.

**The centralising case, at its strongest and as the evidence supports it.**

- **The problem it names is documented by the region itself.** The Herijking's diagnosis — "veelheid aan activiteiten … zonder werkelijke focus op de uitkomsten" — is a diagnosis of *diffusion*, not of missing connections. If activity is spread across themes and governance structures without shared outcome focus, the remedy is a body that sets strategy and owns execution [D, `HERIJKING` slides 2, 4].
- **A corporate structure supported by regionalisation was reported as the most direct route to collaboration, and the study reports budget authority sitting with service planning as the condition under which it bound.** The Canadian comparison is the closest analogue retrieved, and it is a real finding, not a typology [D, line E].
- **Centralisation with binding standards and complete implementation improves outcomes at scale.** London's full-coverage hyperacute model reduced 90-day mortality; Greater Manchester's partial model did not, and the authors' own explanation is "the lower level of adherence in Greater Manchester". This is service reconfiguration rather than governance, but it establishes that a centre can work when it binds the pathway [D, line E].
- **The funding architecture has already centralised.** The successor instrument hands one three-year regional allocation to one mandaathouder, conditions it on one regional werkagenda approved by all colleges, and names "regionale coördinatie van gemeenten in de samenwerkingsregio" as a funded activity [D, `SPUKAZWA` arts. 2.1(2)(c), 4.1]. **A report arguing for a polycentric middle is arguing against the grain of the instrument that will fund the next three years.**
- **Local precedent.** When the Coöperatie Slimmer Leven dissolved, the layer bent rather than broke — by concentrating further, into the GGD, with a garantstelling from partners [D, `GGD`]. That is a small natural experiment in which centralisation was the working response to a coordination failure.

**The limits of the centralising case, which the same evidence supplies.**

- **Merging delivery organisations does not deliver the promised gains, and it costs autonomy and access.** 102 English hospital mergers produced no gains beyond reduced activity, with financial performance down and waiting times up; nine NHS trust mergers undershot their savings target by a third and smaller partners' staff reported "a decrease in the autonomy of services and local decision making"; a realist synthesis finds that mandated collaboration shifts the basis of cooperation "from trust to contract" and produces "collaborative inertia" [D, line E]. *These are organisation-level findings about merger; they establish the mechanism and its costs, not the magnitude for a coordinating board.*
- **The democratic cost is real but contingent.** Larger jurisdictions reduce citizens' internal political efficacy in a causal study, but the equivalent trust effect is absent in Norway, and the authors attribute the difference to implementation. "Bigger unit ⇒ weaker local voice" is a documented risk, not a law [D, line E].
- **The coordination-body failure mode is documented as a pattern, not as a measured causal claim.** Platforms that "operate in isolation"; 34% of states running multiple fragmented mechanisms with minimal linkage (with an internal inconsistency the reviewer should note: 9 of 47 is 19.1%, not the 23% the abstract states); and the crowding-out finding below. **No retrieved study measures a coordination layer causing worse coordination than its absence [U].**
- **Bargained, not designed.** Administrative-organisation size and remit follow power dependencies, including dependence on external non-members. **The centre is an actor in the system, not a neutral fixer of it** — and the regional proposal presents the board as an instrument [D, line E].

**The missing-middle / polycentric case, at its strongest and with its own limits.**

- **Density is not the problem the instruments measure.** 147 offers and 193 partners are not evidence of diffusion-without-focus; they are evidence of reach, and the Herijking's own three-village analysis finds positive social-coherence signals for long-lived local initiatives while disclaiming causal attribution [D, `HERIJKING` §8.1].
- **Ostrom did not show that polycentricity works; she showed that one specific charge against plurality did not hold.** Her lecture frames whether the centres form an interdependent system as "an empirical question in particular cases", reports that duplication in US metropolitan water and police services "rarely occurred", and closes by asking how diverse polycentric institutions "help or hinder" a list of outcomes. A 2019 review states that "systematic development of polycentricity, including its posited advantages, is lacking". **"Ostrom showed polycentricity works" is a misreading** [D, line E].
- **Polycentricity's own failure modes are documented**: overlap is "necessary — but not sufficient" for collective action; higher-level actors capture or dismantle lower-level centres and make cross-level linkages "deliberately nonfunctional"; and structural density can coexist with declining effectiveness, with complexity making failure harder to see [D, line E; Morrison].
- **The most important counter-evidence in this report cuts *against* the middle, not for it.** One retrieved study finds that **more cooperation in a collaborative venue is associated with less cooperation in other venues** — the exact opposite of the intended effect of building a convening space [D, line E; **child-reported at abstract level, cross-sectional across five California regions, and flagged for verification**]. It is an association, not a demonstrated crowding-out mechanism, and the report does not claim more than that. **A "missing middle" route is a new venue, and the one study that looks finds new venues can crowd out the cooperation they were meant to host.**
- **In health systems, the distributed-autonomy claim has not been shown.** The closest analogue — devolution with shared rules — yields "little rigorous evidence" and mixed results [D, line E].

**What the evidence actually decides — and this is the substantive result of the revision.**

**Neither reading is determined.** The centre–middle contest, as usually posed, is under-determined by the available evidence: no retrieved study tests a coordinating board against a light-middle arrangement head-to-head, in health promotion or in any high-income health system.

**What follows is this report's own synthesis, and it is labelled as one [I].** It is not a finding of any single study, the five items come from five different domains and levels, and no retrieved study tests the set as a set. Read as a synthesis, it is a more useful frame than "centre or middle": **the variables associated with documented outcomes are the same under either form.** They are:

1. **alignment of authority with resources** — a centre without budget authority does not bind (Canadian networks) [D];
2. **completeness of implementation** — partial coverage produces no measurable gain (London versus Greater Manchester) [D];
3. **coordination burden imposed on participants** — sustained collaborations are those where management "streamlined the coordination burden" [D, Elston et al.];
4. **the number of separate accounts a participant must render** — reporting reform in a well-resourced foundation field moved 8 hours to 6 per funder: real, but small, because the binding constraint is the number of funders [D, line E];
5. **whether competitive allocation and coordination sit in the same body** — documented in health promotion directly: "competitive tendering often undermined it in practice" [D, line E].

**So the practical question is not "centre or middle". It is "which of these five conditions will the arrangement satisfy, and which body holds the money".** That is a better question than either side of the v1 framing, and it is answerable from documents in a way that "centre versus middle" is not. **But it is a better question, not a proven answer** — and a reader who treats the five as a validated checklist would be making the same error v1 made with the architecture.

**Discriminating observations — what to look for next.** The table is short because only these observations distinguish the readings; the rest distinguish *design quality within* either reading, which is why they are listed above instead.

**What the evidence currently supports.** Not a verdict. It supports: (i) the region has chosen the centralising path [D]; (ii) the funding instrument is compatible with and arguably encourages it [D]; (iii) the conditions under which a centre works are documented, and the region's proposal does not yet state whether it satisfies the first of them [D/I]; (iv) the conditions under which a middle works are documented, and the one study that tests a new venue finds crowding out [D]; (v) no local observation yet distinguishes them, because the reform has not produced an observable consequence. **An honest report says the choice is live, states what would settle it, and does not smuggle a preference into the conclusion.** v1's phrasing ("what follows … a contribution-based, polycentric answer") did smuggle one, and this revision withdraws it.

**Counter-evidence that the report must carry, because it bears on the question's own premise.** Line D searched deliberately for evidence *against* the ownership/territoriality explanation, and found four findings that weaken or complicate it:

1. **Ownership predicted knowledge *sharing*, not hoarding.** In a survey of 342 doctors, ownership positively predicted both knowledge donating and knowledge collecting *(D11)*. The simple inference "ownership → withholding" is falsified at individual level. The dark side must be about *what* is shared and with whom — which is what the trust contingency predicts.
2. **Territorial behaviour is contingent, and high trust suppresses it** *(D09)*. This shifts the explanatory burden onto the *context*, which is designable, rather than onto the actor, which is not.
3. **Ownership adds nothing incremental for performance** *(D03)*. Any argument that ownership improves *outcomes* is not supported by this line's best multi-source study. Ownership buys citizenship behaviour and self-esteem, not effectiveness.
4. **Preserving subgroup identity can *increase* intergroup bias.** Two minimal-group experiments found that perceived continuation of a pre-merger identity strengthened identification with the superordinate group **and strengthened, rather than reduced, ingroup favouritism at the subgroup level** *(D21)*. **This is the most uncomfortable finding in the report, because it is the best experimental evidence against a premise the issue holds: that protecting local identity is the route to cooperation.** The design implication is not "merge anyway" — it is that identity preservation has to be paired with something that makes the shared category salient and the boundary permeable *(D19)*. **The literature does not reconcile "protect identity" with "achieve shared outcomes", and this report will not pretend it does.**

**What survives the level test.** Four findings from line D, and they are not the mechanism vocabulary the question reaches for: (i) organisational autonomy is **multidimensional** and legal status is a bad proxy *(D16)*; (ii) organisations **comply with institutional pressure without converging in practice** *(D31)*; (iii) **unity and diversity are jointly required and mutually contradictory** at network level, and their costs must be actively managed *(D17)*; (iv) recognition of the right to organise and low-cost conflict-resolution arenas are associated with durability across 91 commons studies *(D35, p = 0.032)*. **What does not survive: "the initiative is territorial", "the organisation feels ownership", "autonomy is a basic need of organisations".** Each is a level jump, and the first is also a motive attribution no retrieved instrument supports.

**The cheapest measurements this implies, in order of feasibility** *(line D §6.2)*. These are proposals for a later, separately agreed inquiry; none is undertaken here.

1. **Recognition versus funding, counted from documents already retrieved.** Two independent counts per organisation per year: *recognition* (named, invited, cited, listed as partner, given a governance or speaking role) and *funding* (euros awarded). **If recognition and funding diverge systematically in this region, then "being recognised" and "being funded" are separate goods, and the status mechanism becomes testable without fieldwork.** This is the highest-value measurement the line proposes and it requires no new data collection.
2. **Goal-ambiguity audit of the regional outcome set**, scored on four dimensions (mission, directive, evaluative, priority). If evaluative and priority ambiguity are high, the goal-ambiguity rival predicts coordination failure with no motivational mechanism at all *(D29)*.
3. **Contested-object log**: code each documented disagreement by object — a site, a target group, a referral route, a data set, a name, a budget line, a statutory duty. **This is what separates territoriality (control of a programme), place attachment (a site) and professional boundary work (a task)**, the three rivals that look identical in prose. In a food-and-green-space domain, place attachment is the most plausible confusion *(D34)*.
4. **Autonomy, six dimensions, from documents plus one short survey** — applying Verhoest's frame to a sample of local initiatives, with the explicit warning not to infer autonomy from legal form.
5. **Funding-exposure gradient and capacity gradient** from administrative data: does participation track budget share by stream, or staff capacity? These discriminate R1 and R5 from the psychological explanations.

**Two disciplines this section enforces.** First, **no level jumps**: every hypothesis is measured somewhere below the level the local question needs, and the transfer is stated rather than assumed. Second, **no motive attribution**: the exclusions forbid inferring any named actor's reasons, and nothing in the public record would support it. **The difference between "the record is consistent with H1" and "H1 explains the region" is the difference between an open hypothesis and a finding.**

**What this means for the answer.** The mechanism question is the part of the research question that a document-based method cannot close. That is not a failure of the research; it is why the issue itself asks for a later, separately agreed conversation. **v1 mistook the absence of local measurement for the absence of the mechanism — and, in one place, for evidence against it. The correction is to say what is missing, what the literature actually supports at its own level, and what it would take to close the gap.**

## 5. Counter-evidence and tensions

1. **The money finding reversed.** v1's central documentary support for "the coordination has no long-term home" was a municipal budget line that excluded a successor allocation decided seven months later. The successor is documented, its 2027 local allocation is at least as large as the 2026 envelope, and it is legally fixed to 2029 (P12, P12a–c). **No regional baseline exists in the retrieved record, so no regional before-and-after is claimed.** **The version of the temporariness finding that survives is different: not "the money disappears in 2027" but "the money is certain to 2029 and conditional thereafter, and outside four ketenaanpakken no structural GALA money remains".**

2. **A cut and an addition arrived in the same instrument.** The 29 October 2025 amendment removed the compartments, cut 2026 by 10%, added €43m and set the old regeling to lapse [D, `STCRT`]. The de-compartmentalisation and the austerity were one act. **A report that cites either without the other is describing half a reform.**

3. **Collaboration improved; formal agreements thinned.** The RIVM summary and the regional monitor report stronger collaboration in 2024–2025, while the same RIVM table shows declines in joint decision-making, responsibility division, coordination agreements and financial agreements among mandated municipalities [D, `GALA25` p.11, table 2.1]. The improving items are operational (agreements with frontline professionals 37%→46%; on referrals 27%→39% [D, p.24]); the declining items are governance. **Operational cooperation can improve while the formal settlement weakens.** Three qualifications: the unit is the *gemandateerde gemeente* (n=42), not the individual municipality; on financial agreements municipalities and insurers move in **opposite** directions (52%→41% and 44%→60%); and both waves are self-report whose representativeness RIVM itself questions, recording the possibility of "een rooskleurige voorstelling van de voortgang" [D, p.93]. **The direction is consistent; the magnitude and the meaning of the divergence are not established.**

4. **The compartments were porous in practice.** The RIVM monitor found **76% of municipalities** combined GALA money with other streams, with concrete examples [D, `GALA25` p.73]. The compartmentalisation constrained accounting, not local ingenuity. **This weakens any claim that integration was impossible and strengthens the claim that it cost extra administrative work.**

5. **The scheme was closed at the door but open in its text.** Art. 1 said a consortium "staat te allen tijde open voor het aansluiten van nieuwe partijen"; the municipal page said only existing penvoerders could apply [D, `CVDR` art. 1; `EINDSUB`]. Both true: new parties could join someone else's consortium but could not hold the pen. **The barrier was representation, not membership** — and under the successor this question is moot, because there is no consortium to hold.

6. **There was no competition to lose.** Three applications, a ceiling raised twice to fit them, no refusals [D, `AWARD25`; `GMB`]. A narrative in which small initiatives are outcompeted is not supported. The plausible mechanism is earlier — small initiatives do not apply [I] — and the record does not show whether any considered doing so. **Absence of refusals is not evidence of open access, and it is also not evidence of exclusion.** It is insufficient for either.

7. **The region's own remedy is centralisation, not pluralism** — and §4.9 now gives it its strongest form rather than treating it as an anomaly. The honest position is that **the evidence does not decide between strengthening the centre and strengthening the middle**, that the region is choosing the centre, that the funding instrument is compatible with that choice, and that the discriminating observations are listed in §4.9.

8. **Plurality's benefits are indicated, not established.** The Herijking's three-village analysis is ecological, at village level, with two of the three initiatives in municipalities that also provide village support, and it disclaims causality [D, `HERIJKING` §8.1]. **A reason not to treat plurality as failure by default; not a reason to claim a new initiative will produce health.**

9. **The monitor is a self-report by the coordinator** [D, `STAPCON` footer; `GGD5`]. No independent evaluation of the region's prevention infrastructure was found [U]. The Herijking is produced by a broader project group, but it is still a regional self-assessment.

10. **The national finding is not the local finding.** The compartmentalised SPUK was a national design; the successor's amounts are national; the werkagenda and the coordination assignment are local. **The step from "the national money is compartmentalised" to "Eindhoven's landscape is fragmented" is an inference**, supported by the region's own diagnosis and not measured by it.

11. **The policy summary is more confident than the document it summarises.** The GALA MTR says it is "nog te vroeg om conclusies te trekken over behaalde resultaten" [D, p.3]; the parliamentary letter and the issue brief speak of a landscape that "obstructs" and lacks "shared ownership". The phrase is the letter's; it is absent from the GALA MTR and the IZA MTR covering letter, and its origin is not established [U]. **The sentence that travels is not the sentence in the document.** The same caution now applies to v1's own cliff sentence, which is why it is withdrawn.

12. **"All actions executed" is a status convention.** The municipal evaluation states that "alle voorgenomen acties … zijn uitgevoerd", while recording that the fitcoins platform was not live, that the cardiovascular approach was still in conversation, and that a green play-and-sport location is expected only after 2027 [D, `EIND` §B]. **Delivery language and delivery are different objects.**

13. **The directory count cannot settle the plurality question.** 147 entries is a lot; 52 *Ontmoeten* and 47 *Hulp en advies* could be read as duplication or as choice. Without population, access and usage data, the count supports neither reading.

14. **The Regioraad's decision rights are opaque.** Participation and quarterly reporting are required [D, `CVDR` art. 10], but no public minutes, membership list or decision rules were retrieved [U]. **A governance layer whose decisions are not publicly traceable is a fragmentation risk in its own right** — not because it is illegitimate, but because residents and small initiatives cannot see how priorities are set. Under the successor, the werkagenda becomes the visible artefact; it was not public at retrieval.

15. **The 2027 SPUK adds a procedural condition that may re-centralise what ontschotting decentralised.** Allocation is ambtshalve, which lowers the entry cost; but it is conditional on a college-approved werkagenda by 1 March 2027, approved by fifteen colleges [D, `SPUKAZWA` art. 4.1; `VNGQA`]. It may reduce fragmentation by forcing one plan; it may reproduce the project logic at a higher level. **The evidence does not yet say which, and the document that would is the region's werkagenda.**

16. **The national instrument is near-silent on autonomous citizen initiatives.** In the AZWA's basisinfrastructuur, "Burgerinitiatieven" appears once, as an unelaborated list item without money, rights or role [D, `LOKET`]. The successor regeling is addressed only to public bodies [D, `SPUKAZWA` arts. 2.1, 3.1]. **The national layer is largely indifferent to local autonomy: it neither enables nor obstructs it, and its accountability runs through municipalities and insurers.** That is a finding about an instrument's blind spot, not about anyone's intentions.

17. **The "autonomy" explanation has a documented rationale and no documented motive.** The RIVM records autonomy as a *reason* municipalities give [D, `GALA25` pp.25–26]; the vertical split makes it a role requirement [D, `GGDUITLEG`]. Neither document shows an actor defending a domain for its own sake, and the psychological-ownership literature operates at a lower level *(line D)*. **Autonomy is documented as a structural rationale; whether it is also a motive is insufficient evidence to adjudicate (§4.10, H3).**

18. **A correction the record forces on the report's own framing.** v1's short answer said "the binding constraint on shared impact is the funding and accountability design, not the absence of goodwill". The successor instrument has materially changed the funding and accountability design — no application, three years, one plan, light accountability — **and no consequence is yet observable.** If fragmentation persists under the new design, the funding-constraint explanation is weakened and the capacity, centralisation and relational explanations gain. **The reform is therefore a natural experiment the report should name as such, rather than a solution it can predict.**

19. **The experimental evidence complicates the question's own premise about identity.** The issue's second half asks how initiatives can contribute "without having to grow their own organisation or surrender their local identity" — a formulation that assumes protecting identity is the route to cooperation. The best experimental evidence retrieved says otherwise: perceived continuation of a sub-group identity strengthened superordinate identification **and strengthened ingroup favouritism at the subgroup level** (two minimal-group experiments, *D21*). The best network evidence says diversity is a performance asset whose costs must be actively managed (*D17*). **These are not reconciled in the literature, and this report does not reconcile them.** The premise is not wrong, but it is an assumption the evidence partly contests, and a report that adopted it silently would be less trustworthy than one that names it.

20. **"Territoriality" is a label the evidence cannot carry locally.** No retrieved study measures territorial behaviour between organisations; the construct is measured at individual and small-group level, with a documented trust contingency (*D08, D09*). Using it to describe any real organisation's conduct in this region would be an unwarranted level jump and a motive attribution the exclusions forbid. **This is a limit on the report's vocabulary, not a finding about anyone.**

21. **The strongest single piece of counter-evidence in this report cuts against the report's own leaning.** A study of land-use and transportation planning institutions finds that "higher levels of cooperation in collaborative institutions are associated with lower levels of cooperation in other land-use and transportation planning institutions". **Adding a convening venue can crowd out the cooperation it was meant to host.** If a "missing middle" route were built, this is the documented failure it would most plausibly reproduce — and v1, which leaned toward the middle, did not have this finding [D, line E]. The second such finding is that removing restrictions on money does not by itself produce capacity investment in recipients (a preprint on 567 large unrestricted gifts; not transferable in magnitude, and cited only to refuse the claim that light funding builds capacity) [D, line E].

## 8. Important uncertainties and limits

**Not established in the sources searched:**
- Any local instance of harmful duplication, broken referral, resident navigation burden, repeated information request, or unmet need near dense offers (§4.6 table). **This is the report's largest gap and it is a gap in the evidence base, not a negative finding.**
- Whether the 193 partners do anything together beyond being listed.
- Whether any small initiative considered, attempted or was discouraged from the consortium route.
- Whether the region's coordination function is assigned to a named body from 2027. **The werkagenda would say; it was not public.**
- Whether the 2027 allocation changes *which activities* are funded, as distinct from *how much*. The instruments differ in scope (HLO added; four ketenaanpakken continued; other GALA themes not named) and no per-theme comparison exists [U].
- Whether the region's collaboration survives the reform. **The reform is a natural experiment that has not yet produced an observation.**
- The Regioraad's membership, decision rules and minutes.
- The per-applicant split of the 2025 awards; the 2024 awards; the subsidy-vs-opdracht split of the SPUK.
- Any independent evaluation of DE STAP, the IZA monitor or the regional prevention infrastructure.
- Whether the directory is current: a listing is an editorial acceptance, not a live service.

**Open rather than refuted — the correction this revision makes:**
- **H1–H5** (psychological ownership, territoriality, autonomy, identity, recognition): the literature documents them at employee and group level with real counter-evidence; **no local measurement exists in either direction** (§4.10). v1's "not supported locally" is withdrawn.
- **H6–H7** (projectification, coordination burden): established at the level of organisational form; **transfer to Eindhoven is a plausible inference, not a finding**.
- **The missing-middle reading and the centralisation reading**: both live; §4.9 lists the observations that would discriminate.

**Not knowable with the methods used:**
- Whether any named actor seeks territory, prestige, funding or control. The exclusions forbid inferring motive and nothing retrieved would support it.
- What residents experience, want or would refuse. That requires consent-based inquiry, outside this mandate.
- Whether a garden, a meal or a walking group produces health.

**Unavailable:** the Regioplan PDF (404, twice), the IZA MTR PDF (not exposed), the full RIVM 2026 report (connection aborted), the GGD's September 2025 neighbourhood evaluation (not public), a consolidated award register (not published), Precies' 2025 progress report (connection aborted), the Regioraad's minutes, and **the region's AZWA werkagenda (not yet published)**.

**Measurement limits.** The regional monitor is a self-report by the coordinator; the RIVM monitor is a self-report survey; the municipal evaluation's health-gain scores are self-assessed and explicitly non-causal; the Herijking's village analysis is ecological and explicitly non-causal. **No measurement in this note observes a resident, a referral or a health outcome.** The strongest local evidence is documentary: rules, amounts, dates, awards and the region's own diagnosis. That is enough to establish the architecture and not enough to establish its effects — a distinction v1 blurred and this revision enforces.

**Three further limits.** First, RIVM's 2025 monitor has a 96% municipal response (327 of 342) and 92% insurer response (35 of 38), but RIVM itself does not know whether respondents are representative, consulted colleagues, or gave "een rooskleurige voorstelling" [D, `GALA25` p.93]. The apparent decline in regional agreements could be a change in respondents or candour. Second, the 2026 RIVM report is abstract-level. Third, the scholarly literature is mostly abstract-level in this run; the mechanism findings are weaker evidence than the local documents, and the note uses them accordingly.

**Temporal limit.** This is a snapshot at 26 November 2026. The 2027 allocation is documented but unspent; the werkagenda was not public; the Herijking's proposed model is not adopted; the AZWA's local consequences are not observable. **A report written in 2027 will be a different report, and the discriminating observations in §4.9 are what it should look for.**

## 9. What would change the answer

In order of how much:

1. **The region's AZWA werkagenda.** It is now the single highest-value document: it would show whether regional coordination is assigned to a named body, whether a non-public contributor is named, and how the €5,968,460 is programmed. It resolves P11a, P39 and the "missing middle" question in one document. Due 8 December 2026; public shortly after, if the region publishes it.
2. **A consolidated award and delivery register** for the old regional subsidy: who received what, with what reach. It would settle whether the three-payee structure delivered or merely absorbed.
3. **A resident-level study of navigation and referral**, with consent. The only evidence that could establish or falsify the issue's central concern, and the evidence this mandate cannot produce.
4. **The 2027–2029 outturn.** If coordination is embedded and activity continues under the ambtshalve allocation, the temporariness finding weakens further; if the region underspends under an instrument with no application step, the **absorptive-capacity explanation becomes the leading one** and the architecture explanations lose weight.
5. **A documented intermediate route that works** — a referral partnership, a shared recognition scheme, a light mutual-obligation arrangement between a health partner and a community practice. One working example would turn the missing middle from an absence into a design option; one failed attempt would weaken it.
6. **The Herijking's proposed execution organisation, if adopted.** The live test of the centralisation path (§4.9). If it improves reach and coordination, the middle-first reading is wrong; if it adds a layer, it is right.
7. **Any local measurement of ownership, identity, recognition or territoriality** (§4.10). Even a small qualitative study with a behavioural measure would move H1–H5 from insufficient to adjudicable.
8. **The full RIVM 2026 report**, for its method, limitations and regional breakdown.
9. **Participation data from DE STAP.** It would convert the low-demand-access claim from a design property into an observed one, and would test the duplication question.
10. **A per-theme funding comparison** between the 2026 GALA envelopes and the 2027–2029 AZWA/HLO allocation. It would establish whether the composition change has losers, which the totals conceal.
11. **The Regioplan text** — still the unresolved retrieval from both runs. It would establish the intended division of responsibility the instruments implement.
12. **An independent evaluation of the regional monitor.** If the self-report is accurate, the collaboration trend is real; if not, the region's self-diagnosis needs reweighting.

## 11. Implications for the main question and the three pillars

**For the main question** ("how can existing communities, green spaces and local organisations form a mutually supportive ecosystem … through shared care"): the ecosystem is not missing, and the money has not disappeared. It is **governed, funded and monitored — and governed in a way that makes the community practice a listed activity rather than a party.** From 2027 the money is administratively easier to obtain (no application, three years) and structurally no more accessible to a non-public body (the addressees are municipalities and GGDs). **The shared-care question is therefore not "how do we connect?" but "which door, with what obligation, for how long, and who holds the money after 2029?".**

**Pillar 1, De Wielewaal as central green motor:** no support from this evidence. The green organisations inside the health movement are Wasven, IVN, Het Bos In and De Groote Heide; the Wielewaal is not among them; the regional frame is about care pathways; the municipal prevention budget is a care-and-social-base budget. The pillar remains a hypothesis.

**Pillar 2, a city-wide ecosystem:** the connective layer is real and larger than the earlier "thin network" reading: 193 partners, a regional plan, a Regioraad, five GGD-coordinated pathways, a monitor, a directory, and from 2027 a three-year regional allocation. But it is **a consortium plus a directory plus a municipal funding stream**, with a light affiliation in between and no route that carries obligation without legal personality. "Connecting to the health movement" should be specified as one of three acts — a listing, a partnership, or a municipal contribution — because they carry different rights, costs and risks. **The evidence does not support a new platform; it supports using the existing light doors deliberately, and asking where the 2027 coordination money lands.**

**Pillar 3, nature closer to everyday life:** the directory's 147 offers, the €0–7 communal meals, the gardens open when the crew is there, and the listing criterion that forbids a referral requirement are documented instances of **low-demand access to green and food**. The classification gap — growing filed as meeting, no slot for food-growing as health-relevant — is the specific thing that could change without building anything.

**Community as foundation:** the health architecture's categories do not contain the community's practice. That is not a reason to change the practice; it is a reason to be precise about what it can and cannot claim from this system. **The most useful contribution Voedselbos 2.0 could make would be to remain a place the health system can list, refer to and learn from without turning it into a programme.** That is an inference from the retrieved architecture, not a recommendation the research can authorise — and it is conditional on the region choosing the middle over the centre, which the evidence does not settle.

**The 2027 reform changes the pot and the conditions, not the addressee.** It removes the application, the queue and the per-theme compartments — real simplifications. It keeps the municipal layer as the gatekeeper of the plan and of any onward funding. **The reform may reduce fragmentation between national themes; it does not by itself create a route for an autonomous initiative to hold money, co-govern, or be recognised for a contribution.** Whether that matters depends on which rival in §4.9 is right, and the 2027 werkagenda and the 2028 outturn are where to look.

**On the "new umbrella" question:** the evidence does not support making one the default, and it does not support the opposite dogma either. **The region is already moving toward a stronger centre, with a funding instrument that fits that choice, and the case for the centre is stronger than v1 allowed.** The open question is whether the missing middle — a light, reciprocal, referrable relationship — can be built without a new organisation, and whether it would help. The research cannot answer that; it can say that both paths are live, that the region has chosen one, and what would show which was right.

### Open questions for a later, separately agreed conversation

Not questions for a stakeholder to answer on the researcher's behalf; questions whose answers would change this note. **No one has been asked any of them.**

1. **For the region and the GGD:** where does the 2027–2029 regional coordination money land, and who holds the function after the GGD's 2026 bridge?
2. **What would make a handoff, a decline or a closure count as a good outcome** in your reporting, rather than as a failure to deliver?
3. **If you had to fund one thing for four years instead of four things for one year, what would you protect?**
4. **For the municipality:** what would a *werkagenda* need to contain for a community practice to be a named contributor rather than a listed activity?
5. **For a community practice:** what would you accept as recognition without becoming a programme, and what would you refuse?
6. **For the monitor:** what would it take for the regional monitor to see a garden as health-relevant without turning it into an intervention?
7. **For everyone:** is the aim a stronger centre or a stronger middle — and what would you stop doing to make room for the one you choose?
