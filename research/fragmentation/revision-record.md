## 3. The PI's eight specific instructions, answered

### 3.1 Reconcile the 2026–2027 budget reduction with the AZWA/HLO successor arrangements

**What v1 did.** Quoted `EIND` §D: €4,229,000 (2026) → €739,000 (2027), and used it as documentary support for "the money is temporary and the coordination has no long-term home".

**What v2 found.** [`working/line-d-funding-transition.md`](working/line-d-funding-transition.md) records the full retrieval.

- The document **hedges**: the cause is "(mogelijk) wegvallen" — possibly falling away. It also scopes the 2026 figure: the costs include "alle GALA-enveloppen (**alleen voor 2026**)". It compares a year containing a one-off national envelope to a year without one, written **seven months before the successor instrument was published**.
- The successor instrument (`SPUKAZWA`, Stcrt. 2026, 25830, 21-07-2026) was **read in full**. Its annexes fix the 2027 amounts: region €5,968,460, Eindhoven local €3,103,887, Woensel-Zuid NPLV €529,106, GGD BZO €155,110. Sum for Eindhoven: **€9,601,453**.
- The municipality's own October 2026 budget Q&A (`TV2027` q.44) states that the AZWA money is "**niet opgenomen in de begroting 2027 t/m 2029**" and that adding it raises both sides by "**ca. 9,6 miljoen**", budget-neutral. **Two documents reconcile from different directions to within rounding.**
- Distinct statuses, as the PI required: **committed** (the 2026 municipal line, €647k own + €3,582k SPUK-GALA); **expiring** (brede SPUK to 16-07-2027; local scheme to 01-01-2027; GGD 2026 bridge); **prospective** (AZWA/HLO 2027–2029, amounts fixed, unspent); **actual expenditure** (2025: the provisional underspend; 2027–2029: none yet).
- **Conclusion:** the transition was incomplete, not a reduction. Like-for-like, 2027 local allocation ≈ 2026 GALA envelope. The temporariness finding survives in a different form (2029 horizon, evidence conditionality, four ketanpakken only).

**What remains open.** Whether any specific activity loses funding — the instruments differ in composition (HLO added; four ketanpakken continued; roken/alcohol and other GALA themes not named), and no per-theme comparison exists [U].

### 3.2 Separate arrangements, reported experience, mechanisms and demonstrated consequences

Answered by §2 (strata and states) and by §4.6's table of consequences. **The finding is that the fourth stratum is empty locally**, and the report now says so rather than implying otherwise.

### 3.3 Retain ownership, autonomy, identity, recognition and territoriality as open

Answered by P19a, §4.10 (seven hypotheses, each with its rival and its adjudication test) and §5.19–5.20. **No local measurement exists in either direction**, and the two sentences in v1 that closed it are withdrawn.

### 3.4 Strengthen the empirical organisational-psychology literature, with competing explanations and counter-evidence

Answered by `line-d` (36 sources; 2 full text, 22 verbatim abstract, 12 reconstructed abstract) and §4.7/§4.10. Key additions: the **"safety" antecedent** to ownership (justice, trust, relational closeness) makes ownership partly a product of how fair the arrangement feels; the **trust contingency** in territorial behaviour; **six dimensions of organisational autonomy** with legal status a poor proxy; the **permeability asymmetry** in mergers; **status conflict → information sharing**. Counter-evidence carried in full, including the finding that **preserving sub-group identity can increase ingroup bias** (§5.19) — which complicates the issue's own premise.

### 3.5 Eligibility restrictions versus demonstrated exclusion

Answered by P22 and §4.4. **Restrictions: documented. Exclusion: not documented — insufficient evidence.** v1's "the exclusion, if any, is at the door" and "built for incumbents" are inferences about intent presented as findings, and are withdrawn. The distinction matters practically: restriction implies a design change; exclusion implies a conflict, and acting on it would act on an accusation the record does not support.

### 3.6 Regional centralisation as a serious rival

Answered by §4.9 and P39: the centralising case at its strongest (the region's own diagnosis; network-governance typology; the accountability argument; the funding instrument already centralised; the Coöperatie's dissolution as local precedent), the missing-middle case at its strongest, and a **six-row table of discriminating observations**. **No verdict**: the evidence does not yet distinguish them, and v1's preference for the middle is withdrawn as a conclusion.

### 3.7 Shared accountability and contribution-based recognition with autonomy and financial sustainability

Answered by §4.8 and P40. Seven design conditions, each with its evidence and its level; and the tension stated plainly: **recognition without resources converts a contribution into a reputational gesture while leaving the cost with the contributor**, while resources tied to attributable outcomes penalise exactly the practices whose value is hardest to attribute. **The trade-off is real and the literature does not show it can be designed away for a volunteer practice.**

### 3.8 Which consequences for residents remain unestablished

Answered by §4.6's table: seven consequence types, all **insufficient evidence**, with the reason for each. The single indirect signal (the 4-in-10 asking-for-help finding) is retained and labelled an inference across a different programme.
