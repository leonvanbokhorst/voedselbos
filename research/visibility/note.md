# Voedselbos 2.0: what is visible, who is not, and whether visibility can organise it

Issue #17. Research date 26 November 2026; public sources only, no stakeholder contact, no implementation. Sources [S1]–[S12] in `sources.md`. Evidence marks: [D] documented, [C] claimed, [I] inference, [U] not established, [P] provided by the commissioner (direct account, not public documentation). Builds on `../network/note.md` (the closed food forest was absent from Trefpunt Groen's *achterban*; the Groen Cafés, TGE's network map and Supportpunt are the city's existing visibility infrastructure).

## 1. What is publicly known about "Voedselbos 2.0": almost nothing — and that is the finding

**There is no public entity, site, name or plan called "Voedselbos 2.0."** Searches in Dutch and English return no initiative, page, registration or press coverage under that name [S9]. What exists is a *live, thin, individually-held process* with four documented traces:

1. **A newsletter sent in November 2026 under the brand "Natuurlijk Maartje"** (Mailchimp campaign, publicly archived) titled "Voedselbos nu definitief gesloten". It says: "Onze tijd op Landgoed De Wielewaal is voorbij, maar ons verhaal gaat verder!" The stated future is to develop the new location **"als een kruidentuin"** — herb garden, cooking sessions, workshops, tea and conversation. It states **"Eindhoven heeft géén voedselbos meer"**, thanks people who offered places, says possibilities are "nog steeds … aan het verkennen", and invites readers to a **"Voedselbos Community – Workshop", 26 November 2026, 17:00–19:00, Student Base, High Tech Campus Eindhoven** to help shape "de toekomst van Voedselbos" [S1].
2. **The closure post** on the initiative's own site (28-07-2026) mentions invitations "om op nieuwe plekken voedselbossen te ontwikkelen" and "hopelijk opent zich ergens anders weer een nieuw pad" — no plan, no name, no date [S2].
3. **A wethouder conversation, end of June 2026**, recorded in the public agenda of Rik Thijs: he spoke with residents about "de toekomst van het voedselbos **en Natuurlijk Maartje**, dat een groot deel van de activiteiten rond het bos verzorgde" [S3].
4. **No new permanent location had been announced** as of 25-08-2026 [S3]; nothing found since.

So 2.0 is, publicly, a **workshop invitation and a stated intention to become a herb garden** — a smaller, experience-and-product-shaped successor, planned at a corporate innovation campus, convened through an individual's mailing list. It is not a relaunch, not a network, and not (yet) an organisation. The one thing that appears to be settled is subtraction: 2.0 is explicitly *not* a food forest.

Two contradictions are already visible, and both matter for #17:

- **"Eindhoven heeft géén voedselbos meer" is contestable.** A residents' food forest exists in het Henri Dunantpark (Woensel-Noord), made with several city organisations, focused on biodiversity, encounter and nature education [S3]. The closing initiative's narrative renders it invisible. Whether that is carelessness or a defensible distinction (a public programme site versus a park initiative) is not established — but the sentence is a claim about the movement, made by a node leaving it.
- **The visible representation outlived the substance.** In late August 2026, Natuurlijk Maartje's agenda still advertised a wildplukwandeling for 6 September **at Voedselbos Eindhoven, Elburglaan** — at a site permanently closed since July — and the public record could not establish whether it was cancelled, moved or went ahead [S3]. This is the issue's central risk, documented rather than hypothesised: a visibility layer that no longer refers to anything.

## 2. What "the movement" could mean — four referents, not one

The phrase collapses four different collectives with different visibility needs:

| Referent | Who is in it | Visible where | Who is absent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **National food-forest movement** | food forests across NL; Open Voedselbossendag on 5-09-2026, an initiative of the Natuur en Milieufederaties, organised by Boer&Buur met Natuur with Voedsel uit het Bos, with a public participant map and a "Meld je aan als voedselbos!" form [S4] | national map, one open day a year | **Eindhoven was not on the 2026 map** — neither the Elburglaan site (closed) nor the Dunantpark food forest [S3] |
| **Eindhoven green initiatives** | Wasven, Phood, TGE's *achterban*, Struikroven, Groen Bennekel, Voedselbosje bij De Splinter, Dunantpark; TGE's netwerk map distinguishes Partner / Bedrijf-instelling / Bewonerinitiatief [S5][S6] | TGE network map, Groen Cafés (8 in 2025 with Wasven, Bibliotheek, KNNV, IVN), Groenflits, Supportpunt's story pages [S5][S6][S7] | the closed Voedselbos was never in TGE's *achterban* (`../network/note.md`) |
| **The closed initiative's own community** | ~30 volunteers, workshop and wildpluk participants, newsletter subscribers, schools and companies who bought "Food Forest Experience" [S2][S8] | a dead website, an individual's Mailchimp list, Facebook page, an Instagram handle that is the person's | the volunteers as named actors; there is no collective channel |
| **The individual practice** | Natuurlijk Maartje: coaching, masterclasses, "Voedselbos Experience", a paid year-training "Leef bewust met de 4 seizoenen" (21-03-2026 → 20-03-2027, Eindhoven) [S8][S10] | its own site, Hipsy event listings, Instagram @natuurlijkmaartje | the community as author; the practice is a sole-trader-shaped offer |

The visibility question therefore has a prior question: *which* movement, and **who is entitled to be its face**. Today the answer, on the public record, is one person — the initiative's Instagram link points to `@natuurlijkmaartje`, its LinkedIn to one profile, and its newsletter list belongs to "Natuurlijk Maartje" [S1][S2]. That is not a criticism of the person who did the work; it is a structural fact about where the audience is held.

## 3. Audiences and purposes (what visibility is for, per audience)

| Audience | What visibility could do | What it cannot do | Governance need |
|---|---|---|---|
| Residents / neighbours | make sites findable and legible; convert curiosity into a Friday morning | create a site to visit | accurate opening status — the 6-09-2026 agenda failure shows the cost of not having it [S3] |
| Volunteers | recruitment and retention; recognition | substitute for coordination labour | named roles; consent for being shown; no "hero volunteer" framing |
| Schools, companies | sell experiences, fill the calendar | build a commons | clarity that paid experiences subsidise, not constitute, the community |
| Funders, philanthropy | legitimacy; a story that raises money | secure tenure | disclose that land is private (`§6`) |
| Municipality | demonstrate demand; support a location claim | bind the municipality | separate "we asked" from "they agreed" |
| Peer initiatives | mutual recognition; joint programming | create collaboration | reciprocity: show others as often as self |
| National movement | enrolment: being on the map and on the open day | fix a local absence | the map is a register, not a relationship [S4] |

## 4. Channels and formats, cheapest first (no new software)

1. **Enrol in what exists** — join the national participant map / Open Voedselbossendag [S4]; ask TGE to be listed on its netwerk map as a *bewonerinitiatief* or partner, with the correct status [S5]. Cost: an hour. Risk: a listing can outlive the thing it lists (see §1).
2. **One recurring public moment** — the Groen Café formula already works: 8 per year, jointly run by TGE, Wasven, Bibliotheek Eindhoven, KNNV and IVN [S5]. A "Voedselbos" edition is the lowest-cost route to an audience that is already assembled.
3. **A page that tells the truth** — one maintained page stating status (open/closed/moving), who decides, how to join, what it costs. The current site states "Permanent gesloten sinds juli 2026" while an event at that address was still advertised [S2][S3].
4. **Supportpunt-style story** — Supportpunt publishes member stories for Eindhoven foundations and already published the food forest's [S7]; a second story written *after* the closure is a rare and useful artefact.
5. **Signage / proof-of-concept site** — only if a site exists; visible infrastructure without a site is a poster for a place that is not there.
6. **Social channels** — currently the weakest and most extractive option: the audience is personal, the content is a person's, and a community cannot inherit it without a consent-based handover.

## 5. Governance and consent (the part that decides whether this is care or extraction)

- **Who speaks for the movement.** No one currently does; a 2.0 that claims to would need a mandate from named people (volunteers, participants), not from a mailing list.
- **Who is shown.** Volunteers, care participants (Wasven, Phood both work with people in care regimes — their images are a different category [S11]), children at schools, residents' gardens. Assume *no* implied consent from past photos.
- **Who is absent.** Publishing a map obliges you to explain omissions: Dunantpark is absent from the national map and from the closing narrative [S3].
- **Data.** The newsletter list is a marketing list held by one practice. Asking it to become a community list is a governance act (purpose, controller, unsubscribe, portability), not a technical one.
- **Failure stories.** The closure is the most informative thing the initiative produced. A visibility practice that can only show growth will misrepresent the movement it claims to serve.

## 6. What visibility can and cannot fix, tested against the closure

The documented fail points in `../network/note.md` were **land tenure** and **operator concentration**, not volunteer collapse. The public record documents the private-property vulnerability: the Elburglaan terrain is **private property**, "juist dat maakte de toekomst van het initiatief kwetsbaar" — the food forest used the ground for years, "maar die situatie bleek niet blijvend" [S3]. The commissioner reports the landowner sold the land **[P]**; the buyer, date and terms are [U].

- **Visibility cannot fix tenure.** No amount of attention converts private ground into secure ground. At best it creates the political demand that precedes an allocation.
- **Visibility cannot fix operator concentration.** "Een groot deel van de activiteiten" ran through one practice [S3]; if the successor is again one person's offering, visibility raises the stakes of that concentration.
- **Visibility cannot make a twelve-year system portable.** Food forests "hebben jaren nodig"; losing a location means you cannot simply resume elsewhere [S3]. A 2.0 that is a *herb garden* is a deliberate, honest response to that — and it is also a smaller claim than "food forest".
- **Visibility can fix**: absence from the national map; the recruitment funnel; the funder and municipal conversation; mutual recognition among initiatives; and institutional memory (an archive of what twelve years produced).
- **Therefore**: visibility is a *function with a cost*, not an organising purpose. It becomes organising only when it is bound to (a) a site with tenure and (b) a collective speaker — otherwise it organises a brand.

## 7. Metrics beyond reach

Count only what would survive the disappearance of the account: volunteers who return across seasons; new participants who come back a second time; sites with written tenure (owned, leased, or a formal municipal arrangement); partner commitments made *by others* (a Groen Café co-hosted, a school programme renewed); decisions changed (a location offered, a budget line); participant control (how many public statements were written or approved by the people shown); status accuracy (days the public page matched reality); unresolved enquiries answered. Anti-metrics worth naming: follower growth, impression counts, number of posts, "reach" of a closure announcement.

## 8. Verdict and what would need to be true

**Do not make "visibility of the movement" the organising purpose of 2.0.** It answers a real deficit (Eindhoven was absent from the national map; the initiative was absent from the city's own green network), but it is a *demand-side* function and 2.0's binding constraints are supply-side: land, a second responsible person, and a legal form. The defensible framing is: **2.0 = a small, honest, visible practice with secure ground and more than one keeper**, of which visibility is one function among four (site, tenure, keepers, voice).

What would need to be true for visibility to be the organising purpose: a site with tenure or a written municipal commitment; a named collective (board, cooperative, or volunteer council) able to consent to being represented; an agreed status page maintained by that collective; a partner willing to co-host recurring moments; and a decision that Dunantpark and other nodes are shown as peers, not background.

## 9. Research design (since the public footprint is thin)

Without contacting anyone, in this order: (1) monitor the public outputs of the 26-11-2026 Student Base workshop (newsletter archive, site updates, any new page); (2) check for a new legal entity (KVK, ANBI register) in the months after it; (3) re-check the Open Voedselbossendag map when 2027 registration opens — enrolment is a cheap, observable test of whether a node exists [S4]; (4) watch the municipality's public agenda and raadsinformatie for a location decision naming the food forest; (5) read TGE's 2026 annual report for whether a food-forest node enters its *achterban* — the network note's cleanest absence indicator; (6) map the Dunantpark initiative from public sources as the movement's actual Eindhoven node.
