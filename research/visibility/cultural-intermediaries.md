# Cultural intermediaries: LAB-1, Natlab, food design

Addendum to #17. 26 November 2026, public sources only. Sources [C1]–[C9] in `cultural-intermediaries-sources.md`. The lead's baseline facts (LAB-1's Expats strand and 2024 letter on a "huiskamer voor internationals"; Natlab as cinema/theatre in the former Philips Natlab; the food-design roster) are treated as verified and only extended here.

## The asset each one is

| Intermediary | What it is | What it can show that the initiatives cannot |
|---|---|---|
| **LAB-1** | cinema, club and events venue; strands for Expats, Indian Region Cinema, Kids, comedy, quiz, club, boardgames; Dutch Conversation Club weekly; venue hire [C1] | the habit of a **recurring English-language social slot**, in a room that already mixes cultural and civic publics — it hosted "The Sleepover", an action evening for ~100 students with housing problems [C2] |
| **Natlab** | cinema, theatre, café-restaurant in the former Philips Natuurlijk Laboratorium, Strijp-S; run by Stichting Film- en Theatercentrum Plaza Futura; summer courtyard films [C3][C4] | **legitimacy by lineage** — the building is Eindhoven's innovation origin story — plus an outdoor summer format and a kitchen |
| **Food design** | a discipline and its platforms: DAE's Food Non Food, the Embassy of Food, Marije Vogelzang, Studio CoPain [C5][C6][C7] | **craft and framing**: it can make produce, seasons and failure legible as an object or a meal, without a campaign |

## Audience fit — and the bridge that already exists

The strongest finding: the expat-facing green format is **already running**. Trefpunt Groen's **International Green Café** is "a group of sustainability-minded individuals who organize English-speaking events in Eindhoven, focused on the environment, ecology, and nature… guided nature walks, workshops, lectures and tours, **movie nights**, cleanups and gaming events", with an open call for co-organisers [C8]. So the gap is not format invention or audience research. It is (a) a **venue-scale partner** for the film-and-walk format, (b) **continuity** — the strand is volunteer-run, at the edge of the city's green advocacy body, and (c) the closed food forest was never on it (`../visibility/note.md`).

LAB-1 reaches **internationals, English-first cultural publics, quiz/club regulars**: the population most likely to have time and language and no inherited Eindhoven network, least likely to be reached by a Dutch neighbourhood flyer. Natlab reaches **film/theatre publics and the Strijp-S design workforce** — people who will accept a 90-minute evening about land if it is programmed well. Food design reaches **design publics, DDW visitors, funders and press** — the audience that can give an initiative a visual argument, and the one most at risk of consuming it.

## Existing food/green links (documented, thin)

- International Green Café runs English **movie nights** and nature walks [C8]: the only documented green↔cinema link in the city.
- LAB-1 hosted a **civic action evening** (housing) [C2]; no green or food programming found.
- Natlab's courtyard summer films [C3] are a format adjacency, not a green link.
- Food design has **no documented link to any Eindhoven green initiative** in the sources read; CoPain's bread work is covered locally as craft, not food-system critique [C7]. Unexplored territory, not a partnership waiting to be activated.

## Risks

1. **Programming versus community.** A venue programmes *events*; a food forest needs *weekly labour*. A good film night can eat the volunteer hours that would have gone into the beds — and venues count tickets and bar revenue, not maintained sites.
2. **Expat versus neighbourhood.** An English-first green strand serves internationals well and immediate neighbours poorly; the Elburglaan community was a Dutch-speaking Friday volunteer group. That is a real distributional choice and should be made explicitly.
3. **Aesthetics versus food justice.** Design framing rewards the photogenic. The food-justice actors sit elsewhere — Voedselbank Eindhoven publishes garden and park activities in its own register — and are unlikely to be in the design circuit. A programme showing only designed food would misrepresent the movement it claims to make visible.
4. **Representation and venue dependency.** Designers want subjects; the people shown are volunteers and, in care settings, participants. And being programmed by these institutions is visibility *rented* from someone else's infrastructure — the same structural position as appearing at Wasven's DDW market (`../network/note.md`).

## Three low-threshold formats

1. **Film + walk double bill with the International Green Café at LAB-1.** One English evening: a short walk, one film with a land/ecology theme, thirty minutes with the volunteer group in the room. Reuses an existing strand and an existing room; cost is a room slot plus a co-organiser. Mitigation for risk 2: bilingual title, a Dutch neighbour on the panel.
2. **A material session, not a campaign: "wat groeit er nog" with a food designer.** One afternoon turning real produce — the syrups, tea blends and kweeperenmoes the group already made for the 2024 market [C9] — into one object or one shared meal, documented rather than branded. The cheapest honest artefact of twelve years, and a design-public entry point that needs no site.
3. **A Natlab courtyard or Embassy of Food slot that tells the closure.** Twenty minutes inside an existing summer/DDW programme: what the food forest was, why private ground ended it, what the herb-garden successor is. Showing failure inside a design platform is unusual, cheap, and precisely the correction to "visibility without substance". Pair the panel with a food bank or Dunantpark organiser so the frame is food system, not aesthetics.

None of the three secures land, adds a second keeper, or creates a legal form — #17's verdict stands. But each costs a room slot rather than a year, and each tests whether an audience exists for the movement at all.
